# Psoriasis Comorbidities — A Mini‑Lecture

## Part 1. The Big Picture: Why We Even Talk About "Comorbidities" in a Skin Disease

Let me start with the single most important conceptual shift of this topic.

For most of the twentieth century, psoriasis was taught as a *disease of the skin* — thick red plaques with silvery scale, caused by keratinocytes dividing too fast. Treat the skin, cure the patient. Simple.

That model is now obsolete. Today we understand psoriasis as a **systemic immune‑mediated inflammatory disease that happens to be most visible in the skin**. The plaque is the tip of the iceberg; beneath the waterline sits a body-wide inflammatory state.

Here is the analogy I want you to hold onto for the whole lecture:

> **Think of psoriasis as a slow, smouldering bonfire.** The plaques are the visible flames. But a bonfire also throws sparks and smoke downwind. Those sparks land on the joints, the arteries, the gut, the liver, the eye, and the mind — and wherever they land, they can start a secondary fire. The skin is where you *see* the fire; the comorbidities are where the sparks landed.

This matters enormously in practice, because:

1. **Psoriasis patients die earlier.** Severe psoriasis is associated with a reduction in life expectancy of roughly 3–5 years, and the leading cause of death is *cardiovascular*, not dermatological.
2. **The comorbidity may be more dangerous than the rash.** A patient can live with plaques; they cannot live with a myocardial infarction.
3. **Comorbidities dictate treatment choice.** Every major antipsoriatic drug interacts with one comorbidity or another — so you cannot choose therapy rationally without knowing the patient's full systemic profile.
4. **Screening becomes part of dermatology.** The dermatologist is often the *only* doctor a young psoriasis patient sees. If you don't check the blood pressure, lipids, and mood, nobody will.

So: psoriasis is not a rash you treat. It is a systemic disease you *manage*.

## Part 2. The Mechanistic Core — *Why* Do These Comorbidities Cluster?

Before listing anything, let's understand the machinery. There are **five distinct routes** by which psoriasis connects to another disease. Most exam answers list comorbidities; a strong answer explains the *routes*.

### Route 1 — Shared Genetic Architecture (the "same blueprint" route)

Two diseases can co-occur simply because the same risk gene predisposes to both. The gene doesn't know it's supposed to stop at the skin.

| Genetic factor | Links psoriasis to… | What it actually does |
|---|---|---|
| **HLA‑Cw6** (HLA‑C*06:02) | Early‑onset (Type 1) psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis | An MHC class I molecule that presents a self‑antigen (e.g., **LL‑37/cathelicidin**, ADAMTSL5) to autoreactive CD8⁺ T cells → drives the psoriatic T‑cell response |
| **HLA‑B27** | **Axial** psoriatic arthritis, spondyloarthropathy, uveitis | Class I molecule linked to the whole spondyloarthritis family — explains why sacroiliitis, uveitis, and IBD travel together |
| **TNF‑α** pathway genes (*TNFAIP3*, *TNIP1*) | Psoriasis **and** psoriatic arthritis | Regulate NF‑κB signalling — the master inflammatory switch |
| **IL‑12/IL‑23 axis** (*IL12B*, *IL23R*, *IL23A*) | Psoriasis **and Crohn's disease** | Shapes the Th17 lineage; this is *why* one drug (ustekinumab) treats both diseases |
| **PSORS2, PSORS3, PSORS4** loci | Metabolic syndrome, Type 2 DM, hyperlipidaemia, cardiovascular disease | PSORS4 lies in the epidermal differentiation complex on 1q21; these loci overlap regions implicated in metabolic traits |

**The pharmacological proof of shared genetics:** if psoriasis and Crohn's disease were unrelated diseases that merely co-occurred by chance, a single antibody against IL‑12/23 could not possibly treat both. It does. That is genetics confirmed at the bedside.

### Route 2 — Shared Immune Pathways (the "same soldiers" route)

Psoriasis is driven by a **Th1 + Th17** dominated axis. Let me walk the chain carefully, because every downstream comorbidity hangs off it:

```
Trigger (infection, trauma, stress, drug)
        ↓
Keratinocyte stress → release of LL-37, dsDNA/RNA complexes
        ↓
Plasmacytoid dendritic cells → interferon-α
        ↓
Myeloid dendritic cells → IL-12 and IL-23
        ↓
   IL-12 → Th1 cells      IL-23 → Th17 / Th22 cells
        ↓                        ↓
   IFN-γ, TNF-α          IL-17A, IL-17F, IL-21, IL-22
        ↓                        ↓
        └──────── converge on keratinocytes ────────┘
        ↓
Keratinocyte hyperproliferation (acanthosis, parakeratosis),
neutrophil chemotaxis (Munro microabscesses),
angiogenesis (dilated dermal capillaries → Auspitz sign)
```

Now — **the key insight.** These cytokines are not confined to the skin. **TNF‑α, IFN‑γ, IL‑17, IL‑21 and IL‑23 are measurably elevated in the *serum* of psoriasis patients**, and they are found in high concentration in the affected tissues of the comorbid organ too. The skin lesion behaves like a cytokine factory venting into the circulation.

Follow each cytokine downstream and the comorbidity list writes itself:

- **TNF‑α** → insulin resistance (it serine‑phosphorylates IRS‑1, blunting insulin signalling), endothelial adhesion molecule expression, hepatic steatosis, synovitis, and — via the CNS — anorexia, fatigue and depressed mood.
- **IL‑17** → neutrophil recruitment into arterial plaque, smooth‑muscle activation, osteoclast activation in joints, and direct effects on gut epithelium.
- **IL‑23** → maintains pathogenic Th17 cells in skin, joint, gut, and eye alike.
- **IFN‑γ** → macrophage activation, foam cell formation in atheroma.

This is the concept sometimes called the **"psoriatic march"**: *systemic inflammation → insulin resistance → endothelial dysfunction → atherosclerosis → myocardial infarction and stroke.* Memorise that chain; it is the mechanistic spine of the whole answer.

### Route 3 — Shared Behavioural / Lifestyle Mediators (the "vicious cycle" route)

This route is bidirectional and often underappreciated. Psoriasis causes visible disfigurement and itch → social withdrawal, shame, poor sleep → reduced physical activity, comfort eating, smoking, alcohol → obesity and metabolic syndrome. And obesity, in turn, is *itself* pro‑inflammatory: adipose tissue secretes leptin, resistin, IL‑6 and TNF‑α while reducing protective adiponectin. So obesity worsens psoriasis, which worsens obesity.

**Analogy:** it's a feedback loop like a microphone held too close to a speaker — each side amplifies the other until the whole system screeches.

### Route 4 — Iatrogenic (the "our own treatment did it" route)

Some comorbidities are created or unmasked by therapy: PUVA causing skin cancer, ciclosporin causing hypertension and nephrotoxicity, acitretin causing hyperlipidaemia, methotrexate causing hepatic fibrosis, systemic steroids causing diabetes and rebound pustular flares.

### Route 5 — Psychosocial Burden (the "meaning of the disease" route)

This is not a mechanism of molecules but of human experience — visibility, stigma, perceived contagiousness, sexual dysfunction, career limitation. It is real, measurable, and carries mortality via suicide and substance misuse.

## Part 3. The Systematic Classification (Eleven Categories)

Now that you understand *why*, the list becomes logical rather than arbitrary.

### 1. Immune‑Mediated Inflammatory Diseases (IMIDs)

These are psoriasis's "siblings" — diseases sharing the IL‑23/Th17 and TNF‑α axis.

- **Psoriatic arthritis** — the flagship comorbidity
- **Crohn's disease** — shares the IL‑12/23 axis most strongly
- **Ulcerative colitis**
- **Uveitis**

*Why grouped together?* Because they form the **spondyloarthritis/IMID cluster** — the "SPINEACHE" family, if you like — where a shared cytokine backbone means one biologic can treat several.

### 2. Infections

Counterintuitive, isn't it? Psoriasis is an *over*active immune state — so why more infections?

- **Psoriatic plaques harbour *Staphylococcus***, because the barrier is broken. The stratum corneum is the body's cling‑film; in psoriasis it is parakeratotic, fissured, and shedding, so it no longer seals.
- **Flexural (inverse) psoriasis is the highest‑risk site** — occlusion, moisture, maceration and **fissures** in the depths of the groin, axilla, inframammary and gluteal cleft provide a direct portal of entry. Secondary bacterial and candidal infection is common here.
- Broader epidemiological risk of **pharyngitis, abdominal and respiratory infections.**

*Mechanism:* barrier failure + immune dysregulation (the immune system is *mis*directed, not simply strong) + **immunosuppressive therapy** (methotrexate, ciclosporin, biologics). Note too the two‑way street: streptococcal pharyngitis famously *triggers* guttate psoriasis via molecular mimicry between streptococcal M protein and keratin 17.

### 3. Cancer

Three overlapping explanations: chronic inflammation is mutagenic; treatments are carcinogenic or immunosuppressive; and lifestyle factors (smoking, alcohol) are shared.

- **Non‑melanoma skin cancer** — squamous cell carcinoma (dose‑dependent with PUVA) and basal cell carcinoma. *(Modern nomenclature increasingly prefers "keratinocyte carcinoma," but "non‑melanoma skin cancer" remains standard and acceptable.)*
- **Lymphoma** — notably cutaneous T‑cell lymphoma and Hodgkin lymphoma; risk rises with severity and with age (**>65 years**).
- **Other solid tumours** with reported excess risk: oral cavity, pharynx, oesophagus, pancreas, lung, bladder, kidney, breast.

⚠️ **Clinical caution:** early mycosis fungoides can masquerade as recalcitrant psoriasis. Any "psoriasis" that fails all therapy deserves a biopsy.

### 4. Metabolic Syndrome

The classic quartet:
- **Obesity** (especially central/visceral)
- **Hyperlipidaemia** (high triglycerides, low HDL)
- **Hypertension**
- **Diabetes / insulin resistance**

Metabolic syndrome is the **hinge** of this whole answer — it sits between the inflammation upstream and the cardiovascular catastrophe downstream. Prevalence is roughly doubled in psoriasis, and it scales with disease severity — a dose–response relationship that argues strongly for causation rather than coincidence.

### 5. Cardiovascular Disease

The downstream consequence — and the reason psoriasis shortens life.

- **Myocardial infarction** *(note: the correct term is inf**arction** — death of tissue from lack of blood supply; "infection" is a common typographical slip)*
- **Stroke**
- **Peripheral vascular disease**
- **Atrial fibrillation** (atrial inflammation and fibrosis create the electrical substrate)
- **Venous thromboembolism** (inflammation is prothrombotic: raised fibrinogen, platelet activation, endothelial tissue factor)

**Mechanism in one chain:** systemic TNF‑α/IL‑17 → endothelial dysfunction (reduced nitric oxide, raised adhesion molecules) → monocyte adhesion and transmigration → foam cells → atheromatous plaque → **plaque instability and rupture** → thrombosis → infarction.

Note that inflammation causes not just *more* plaque but *more unstable* plaque. **Analogy:** an atheromatous plaque is a blister on a road surface; inflammation is the frost that makes it crack and pothole. It is the cracking, not the size, that kills.

**The severity gradient is critical.** A 30‑year‑old with severe psoriasis carries a relative MI risk several‑fold above his peers. Because young people have low absolute risk, a *large relative* risk still means a modest absolute risk — but over 40 years of exposure, it accumulates into real events. This is why **Type 1 (early‑onset, HLA‑Cw6‑positive, familial) psoriasis carries the higher cardiovascular burden**: longer cumulative inflammatory exposure.

### 6. Hepatobiliary

- **Non‑alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD)** — the hepatic manifestation of metabolic syndrome. **NAFLD is the most common liver condition in Indian psoriatic patients.**
- **Alcoholic liver disease** — reflecting higher alcohol consumption in this population.

This has direct, immediate therapeutic consequence — see Part 5.

### 7. Psychiatric / Psychological

- **Anxiety**
- **Depression**

Not merely "reactive sadness." There is a biological arm: pro‑inflammatory cytokines activate **indoleamine‑2,3‑dioxygenase (IDO)**, diverting tryptophan away from serotonin synthesis toward kynurenine — a neurochemical contribution to depressed mood. Hence depression in psoriasis is both *psychological* (stigma, disfigurement, itch, sleep loss) and *inflammatory*. Suicidal ideation is measurably increased, and alcohol/substance misuse often follows as maladaptive coping.

### 8. Miscellaneous

- **Gout** — high cell turnover in psoriatic plaques increases purine load and serum urate; hyperuricaemia is common even without clinical gout.
- **Amyloidosis** — **secondary (AA) amyloidosis**, a rare but serious complication of any long‑standing inflammatory disease. Chronic IL‑6 drives hepatic serum amyloid A production; misfolded SAA fragments deposit in kidney, liver and spleen → nephrotic‑range proteinuria and renal failure.

### 9. Respiratory

- **Asthma**
- **COPD** — smoking is both a psoriasis risk factor (especially palmoplantar pustulosis) and the cause of COPD
- **Obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA)** — mediated by obesity; OSA then causes intermittent hypoxia → further systemic inflammation → worse psoriasis and worse cardiovascular risk. Another vicious cycle.

### 10. Adverse Pregnancy Outcomes

- **Preterm delivery**
- **Low birth weight**

*Mechanism:* systemic inflammation impairs placental vascular development (an inflamed, poorly perfused placenta is a small placenta), plus contributions from co‑existing hypertension, obesity and diabetes.

### 11. Ocular

- **Uveitis** — classically anterior, and part of the spondyloarthritis cluster; it is associated with **undifferentiated spondyloarthropathy, inflammatory bowel disease, and psoriasis** (the HLA‑B27 connection again). Presents as a painful red eye with photophobia — a genuine ophthalmic emergency, since untreated uveitis threatens sight.
- **Keratoconjunctivitis sicca (dry eye) is the *most common* ocular finding in psoriatic arthritis.**

Also seen: blepharitis, conjunctivitis, and psoriatic plaques on the eyelid margin causing ectropion and trichiasis.

⚠️ **Don't confuse these two:** *dry eye = most common; uveitis = most dangerous.* Examiners love that distinction.

## Part 4. The Major Physical Comorbidities in Depth (the classic ①–⑦)

### ① Psoriatic Arthritis

**The headline comorbidity — present in about 30% of psoriasis patients.**

- **Sequence:** skin usually precedes joints by about 10 years (roughly 70% skin‑first, 15% simultaneous, 15% joint‑first).
- **Pathology:** synovial and entheseal inflammation → pain, swelling, stiffness → erosion and, if untreated, **irreversible joint destruction** (the extreme being arthritis mutilans with "telescoping"/opera‑glass digits and the radiographic "pencil‑in‑cup" deformity).
- **Genetics:** **HLA‑B27** is implicated — specifically in *axial* psoriatic arthritis (sacroiliitis and spondylitis), whereas HLA‑Cw6 tracks more with skin disease and peripheral arthritis.
- **High‑risk markers for developing PsA:** nail involvement (nail matrix and the distal interphalangeal joint share the enthesis of the extensor tendon — anatomically the nail *is* an appendage of the joint), scalp psoriasis, intergluteal/perianal psoriasis, and obesity.
- **Why it's urgent:** joint damage is **permanent**. Skin lesions come and go; erosions do not. Delayed diagnosis of even 6 months produces measurably worse long‑term function. **Therefore every psoriasis patient at every visit should be asked about joint pain and morning stiffness**, ideally with a validated screening tool (PEST, PASE, ToPAS).
- **Distinguishing clue:** inflammatory joint pain is *worse with rest, better with movement*, with morning stiffness > 30 minutes — the mirror image of osteoarthritis.

### ② Metabolic Syndrome

- Higher incidence of **obesity, dyslipidaemia, and insulin resistance**, which then feed forward into cardiovascular disease.
- Frequently quoted figures: approximately a **4‑fold increase in diabetes** and a **3‑fold increase in myocardial infarction risk** in psoriasis patients (figures consistent with the meta‑analyses cited in Rook's and Bolognia; note that the magnitude depends heavily on disease severity and patient age — the relative risks are highest in young patients with severe disease).
- **Clinically:** measure waist circumference, BMI, blood pressure, fasting glucose/HbA1c and a lipid profile. Weight loss is genuinely disease‑modifying — bariatric surgery and significant dietary weight reduction improve PASI *and* improve response to biologics (partly because fixed‑dose biologics are relatively underdosed in heavy patients).

### ③ Cardiovascular Disease

- Chronic inflammation → **endothelial dysfunction** → accelerated **atherosclerosis** → increased **MI and stroke**.
- Risk is higher in **Type 1 (early‑onset) psoriasis** — younger onset, positive family history, HLA‑Cw6 positive, more severe and unstable course, and therefore decades more cumulative inflammatory exposure.
- Coronary artery inflammation can be visualised directly (FDG‑PET, coronary CT perivascular fat attenuation) and **falls when psoriasis is treated with biologics** — powerful evidence that controlling skin inflammation protects arteries.
- Practical point: standard risk calculators (Framingham, QRISK, ASCVD) **underestimate** risk here. Some guidelines suggest applying a **1.5× multiplier** for severe psoriasis, analogous to rheumatoid arthritis.

### ④ Gastrointestinal and Hepatic Disorders

- **Ulcerative colitis and Crohn's disease** are more prevalent, sharing the IL‑23/Th17 pathway. Ask about chronic diarrhoea, blood or mucus per rectum, abdominal pain and weight loss.
- ⚠️ **Crucial therapeutic warning:** **IL‑17 inhibitors (secukinumab, ixekizumab, brodalumab) can *precipitate or worsen* inflammatory bowel disease**, because IL‑17 is protective at the gut epithelial barrier. In a psoriasis patient with IBD, choose an **anti‑TNF or anti‑IL‑12/23** agent instead. This is the single most important pathway‑specific pearl in the field — it shows that shared pathways do not mean *identical* pathways.
- **NAFLD:** patients with both psoriasis and NAFLD are at **higher risk of methotrexate hepatotoxicity**. The already‑steatotic liver has no reserve — a second hepatic insult produces fibrosis far more readily. **Analogy:** methotrexate on a healthy liver is rain on dry soil; on a fatty liver it is rain on already‑saturated ground — it floods.
  - Practically: screen with liver enzymes, **serum procollagen III N‑peptide (PIIINP)** or transient elastography (FibroScan), take an honest alcohol history, use folic acid supplementation, and consider acitretin or a biologic instead where hepatic risk is high.
- **Coeliac disease** is also over‑represented; anti‑gliadin‑positive patients may improve on a gluten‑free diet.

### ⑤ Cancer

- **High‑dose PUVA** increases the risk of cutaneous **squamous cell carcinoma** (dose‑dependent, strongest evidence, particularly genital SCC in men — hence genital shielding is mandatory) and **basal cell carcinoma**. Risk begins to rise meaningfully above roughly 200 treatments or 1000 J/cm².
- Psoriasis patients **over 65 years** have an increased risk of **lymphomas**.
- Additional risk from ciclosporin (dose‑ and duration‑dependent; limit to ~1–2 years continuously), and from sequential therapy — e.g. PUVA followed by ciclosporin markedly amplifies skin cancer risk.
- **Practical:** lifetime annual skin surveillance for heavily phototherapy‑exposed patients; keep a cumulative PUVA dose record for every patient.

### ⑥ Infections

- **Psoriatic plaques harbour *Staphylococcus***, thanks to a defective barrier.
- **Flexural psoriasis is the special risk site** — moist, occluded, and prone to **fissures that become infected**.
- Increased risk of **pharyngitis and of abdominal and respiratory infections.**
- Add therapy‑related risk: screen for **latent tuberculosis, hepatitis B, hepatitis C and HIV before any biologic**, and remember that **anti‑TNF agents can reactivate latent TB** (with a tendency to disseminated and extrapulmonary disease). Give inactivated vaccines (influenza, pneumococcal, COVID) *before* immunosuppression; avoid live vaccines during it.

### ⑦ Eye

- **Uveitis** — part of the spondyloarthropathy family, associated with **undifferentiated spondyloarthropathy, IBD and psoriasis**; refer any red painful photophobic eye urgently.
- **Keratoconjunctivitis sicca is the most common ocular finding in psoriatic arthritis.**

## Part 5. Psychosocial Comorbidity — Do Not Treat This as a Footnote

Psoriasis patients are prone to **depression and anxiety**, which frequently progress to **alcohol and substance misuse**.

Understand the full cycle:

```
Visible, scaling, "contagious-looking" disease
        ↓
Stigma, shame, avoidance of swimming/gym/intimacy
        ↓
Social isolation + itch-driven insomnia + fatigue
        ↓
Depression and anxiety  ←── reinforced by IDO/tryptophan diversion
        ↓
Alcohol, smoking, comfort eating, poor adherence
        ↓
More severe psoriasis + metabolic syndrome
        ↓
        └────────── back to the top ──────────┘
```

Studies consistently show psoriasis impairs quality of life as much as or more than diabetes, ischaemic heart disease and depression itself. **Practical action:** use the **DLQI** at every review; a DLQI > 10 signals significant impairment and, in most guidelines, helps justify systemic therapy. Ask directly about mood and suicidal thoughts. And note the pleasing corollary — **clearing the skin measurably improves depression scores**, so effective dermatological treatment *is* mental‑health treatment.

Special caution: **brodalumab (anti‑IL‑17RA)** carries a warning regarding suicidal ideation and behaviour; take a careful psychiatric history before prescribing.

## Part 6. Management: Where Comorbidity Changes Your Prescription

Psoriasis patients consume significantly more medication than matched controls — polypharmacy is the norm. Two directions of trouble follow.

### (a) Drugs for comorbidities that worsen psoriasis

Think of the classic list — and remember the mnemonic **"BALI"** (Beta‑blockers, Antimalarials, Lithium, Interferons/Imiquimod), with ACE inhibitors and NSAIDs added:

| Drug | Effect | Mechanism / note |
|---|---|---|
| **Beta‑blockers** | Trigger or worsen psoriasis | Reduce intra‑epidermal cAMP → increased keratinocyte proliferation |
| **ACE inhibitors** | Worsen psoriasis | Kinin/ACE polymorphism effects |
| **Lithium** | Notorious aggravator; often refractory | Inositol depletion → neutrophil and keratinocyte activation |
| **Antimalarials** (chloroquine/hydroxychloroquine) | Flare, sometimes erythrodermic | Inhibit transglutaminase |
| **Systemic corticosteroids** | Rebound flare, often **pustular**, on withdrawal | The reason systemic steroids are avoided in chronic plaque psoriasis |
| **NSAIDs, terbinafine, interferons, TNF inhibitors (paradoxically)** | Occasional flares, including palmoplantar pustulosis | — |

**Practical point:** in a hypertensive psoriasis patient, prefer a **calcium channel blocker, thiazide or ARB** over a beta‑blocker or ACE inhibitor where clinically reasonable — but never stop a cardioprotective beta‑blocker after MI purely for the skin. Weigh the risks explicitly.

### (b) Antipsoriatic drugs that worsen comorbidities

| Drug | Worsens | Therefore… |
|---|---|---|
| **Acitretin** | **Dyslipidaemia** (raises triglycerides and LDL), hepatotoxicity; teratogenic for 3 years | Avoid in uncontrolled hyperlipidaemia; monitor fasting lipids; absolute contraindication in women of childbearing potential without strict contraception |
| **Ciclosporin** | **Hypertension and raised lipids**, nephrotoxicity, malignancy | Avoid in uncontrolled hypertension or renal impairment; monitor BP and creatinine; limit duration |
| **Methotrexate** | Hepatic fibrosis (amplified by NAFLD, obesity, alcohol), myelosuppression, pneumonitis | Avoid/monitor closely in liver disease; folic acid; fibrosis surveillance |
| **Systemic steroids** | Diabetes, hypertension, osteoporosis; pustular rebound | Avoid in plaque psoriasis |
| **PUVA** | Skin cancer, cataract | Eye protection, genital shielding, cumulative dose log |

### (c) Drugs that treat both disease and comorbidity — the elegant solutions

- **TNF‑α inhibitors** (adalimumab, etanercept, infliximab, certolizumab): treat skin **and** psoriatic arthritis (including axial disease), **and** Crohn's disease/UC, **and** uveitis; observational data suggest reduced cardiovascular events. Certolizumab is the preferred option in pregnancy (minimal placental transfer).
- **IL‑12/23 inhibitor (ustekinumab):** skin + joints + Crohn's disease.
- **IL‑23 inhibitors** (guselkumab, risankizumab, tildrakizumab): excellent skin efficacy, effective in PsA, favourable in IBD.
- **IL‑17 inhibitors:** superb for skin and axial arthritis — but **relatively contraindicated in IBD**, and associated with *Candida* infection (IL‑17 is essential for mucosal antifungal defence).
- **Methotrexate:** skin + peripheral joints, and is used in rheumatoid arthritis — but a poor choice with NAFLD.
- **Apremilast (PDE4 inhibitor):** modest efficacy, but no lab monitoring required and causes weight loss — attractive in the multi‑morbid, polypharmacy patient.

**The unifying principle:** *choose the drug that treats the most diseases and aggravates the fewest.* Comorbidity mapping is not an academic exercise — it is how you select therapy.

## Part 7. Screening — The Dermatologist as Physician

The guiding rule:

- **Begin cardiovascular risk screening at age 20**, and repeat **every 2 years** thereafter — and **annually (or at least every 2 years) from age 40** onward, with more frequent assessment in severe disease.

A practical screening bundle at diagnosis and at review:

| Domain | What to do |
|---|---|
| Joints | Symptom enquiry + PEST/PASE questionnaire at **every** visit |
| Metabolic | BMI, waist circumference, blood pressure, fasting glucose/HbA1c, fasting lipids |
| Hepatic | LFTs, alcohol history (AUDIT‑C), ultrasound/elastography if NAFLD suspected |
| Cardiovascular | Formal risk score, with upward adjustment for severe disease |
| Mental health | DLQI, direct enquiry about mood and suicidal ideation |
| Ocular | Ask about dry eye; urgent referral for red painful eye |
| GI | Enquire about chronic diarrhoea/rectal bleeding before starting an IL‑17 inhibitor |
| Pre‑biologic | TB (IGRA/Mantoux + chest X‑ray), HBV, HCV, HIV; vaccination review |
| Lifestyle | Smoking cessation, alcohol reduction, weight loss, exercise — counselled at every opportunity |

**Lifestyle modification is advised for all** — and it is not a soft recommendation. Weight loss, smoking cessation, Mediterranean‑style diet and exercise reduce psoriasis severity, improve drug response, and reduce cardiovascular death. It is the only intervention that acts simultaneously on the skin, the arteries, the liver and the mind.

## Part 8. Common Misunderstandings — Let's Clear These Up

1. **"Comorbidities only matter in severe psoriasis."** Severity *increases* risk but does not gate it. Even mild psoriasis shows excess metabolic and depressive burden, and psoriatic arthritis can occur with trivial skin disease.

2. **"The joints follow the skin, so if the skin is clear the joints are safe."** False. Skin and joint activity often dissociate completely. A patient with clear skin may be quietly eroding their joints.

3. **"Cardiovascular risk is just because these patients are fat and smoke."** Confounders contribute, but the association **persists after adjustment** for BMI, smoking, diabetes, lipids and hypertension. Inflammation is an *independent* risk factor.

4. **"If psoriasis and Crohn's share a pathway, any biologic will help both."** Dangerously wrong — IL‑17 inhibition helps skin but can *worsen* the gut. Shared pathways ≠ identical roles in every organ.

5. **"HLA‑B27 is the psoriasis gene."** No. **HLA‑Cw6** is the principal psoriasis (skin) association; **HLA‑B27** is associated with **axial psoriatic arthritis** and the wider spondyloarthritis/uveitis cluster.

6. **"Depression is simply a consequence of looking bad."** Partly — but there is a genuine inflammatory neurobiology (IDO/kynurenine). And the relationship is bidirectional: stress triggers flares.

7. **"Infections shouldn't happen — the immune system is overactive."** The immune system is *misdirected*, not globally strengthened; add barrier failure and immunosuppressive therapy, and infection risk rises.

8. **"Myocardial infection."** A frequent transcription slip. The term is **myocardial infarction** — *infarct* from the Latin *infarcire*, "to stuff in," describing an obstructed, dead segment of muscle.

9. **"Fatty liver is incidental."** It is the commonest liver disease in Indian psoriatic patients and it directly changes whether you can safely give methotrexate.

## Part 9. The Summary Anchor — The Core Idea in One Picture

If you remember nothing else, remember this single diagram:

```
                    SHARED GENETICS
        (HLA-Cw6, HLA-B27, TNF, IL-12/23, PSORS 2/3/4)
                            │
                            ▼
              Th1 + Th17 ACTIVATION
     (TNF-α, IFN-γ, IL-17, IL-21, IL-23 — in skin AND serum)
                            │
        ┌───────────┬───────┼────────┬──────────┬─────────┐
        ▼           ▼       ▼        ▼          ▼         ▼
      SKIN       JOINTS    GUT     LIVER       EYE      BRAIN
    plaques      PsA     IBD      NAFLD     uveitis   depression
    infection   (30%)                       dry eye    anxiety
        │
        └──► INSULIN RESISTANCE → METABOLIC SYNDROME
                            │
                            ▼
                 ENDOTHELIAL DYSFUNCTION
                            │
                            ▼
                     ATHEROSCLEROSIS
                            │
                            ▼
         MI · STROKE · PVD · AF · VTE  →  EARLY DEATH
                    ("the psoriatic march")

   Amplifiers: obesity · smoking · alcohol · OSA · stress · poor sleep
   Iatrogenic: PUVA→cancer · ciclosporin→HTN · acitretin→lipids ·
               MTX+NAFLD→hepatotoxicity · steroids→rebound
   Also: cancer (NMSC, lymphoma) · gout · AA amyloidosis ·
         asthma/COPD/OSA · preterm birth & low birth weight
```

**In one sentence:** *Psoriasis is a systemic Th1/Th17 inflammatory disease in which shared genes and circulating cytokines carry inflammation from the skin to the joints, gut, liver, eye, arteries and mind — so the dermatologist's job is not merely to clear plaques, but to screen for, prevent and choose therapy around a body‑wide disease that shortens life.*

**And in three clinical commands:**
1. **Ask about joints every single visit** — damage is irreversible.
2. **Measure the waist, the pressure, the sugar, the lipids and the mood** — you may be the only doctor who does.
3. **Map the comorbidities before you choose the drug** — because the right drug treats several diseases at once, and the wrong one trades a rash for a heart attack, a hepatic fibrosis, or a flare of colitis.
