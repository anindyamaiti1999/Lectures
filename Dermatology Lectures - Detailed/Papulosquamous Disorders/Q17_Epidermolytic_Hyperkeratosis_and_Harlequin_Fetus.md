# Disorders of Keratinization — A Mini-Lecture

## Epidermolytic Hyperkeratosis & The Harlequin Fetus

---

## 0. Setting the Stage: Why This Pair of Diseases Sits Together in One Question

Before we touch either disease, let's zoom out.

The epidermis has exactly one job that matters for survival: **it must be a wall**. A wall that keeps water in, microbes out, and heat regulated. Nature builds this wall using two completely different engineering strategies, stacked on top of each other:

1. **The internal skeleton of each cell** — bundles of **keratin intermediate filaments** that criss-cross the keratinocyte's interior and anchor into desmosomes. Think of this as the **steel rebar** inside each brick. It gives the cell tensile strength so it can be squeezed, stretched, and sheared without bursting.

2. **The extracellular mortar** — sheets of **lipid lamellae** (ceramides, cholesterol, free fatty acids) secreted between the dead corneocytes of the stratum corneum. This is the **waterproof grout** between bricks.

Now the elegance of today's question becomes obvious:

| Strategy that fails | Disease | What you see |
|---|---|---|
| **Rebar fails** (keratin cytoskeleton) | **Epidermolytic ichthyosis (EHK/BCIE)** | Cells *burst* → **blisters** + reactive hyperkeratosis |
| **Mortar fails** (lipid barrier) | **Harlequin ichthyosis** (ABCA12) | No waterproofing → catastrophic **hyperkeratosis**, armour-like skin |

So this is not a random pairing. It is a two-part question on **"structural protein failure vs. lipid transport failure"** — the two axes of keratinization disease. In the classification schemes (e.g., the 2009 Sorèze consensus), EHK belongs to the **keratinopathic ichthyoses**, and harlequin ichthyosis is the **most severe end of autosomal recessive congenital ichthyosis (ARCI)**.

Hold that framework in your mind. Everything below hangs on it.

# PART I — EPIDERMOLYTIC HYPERKERATOSIS (Epidermolytic Ichthyosis)

## 1. Names: Why One Disease Has Four of Them

This disease has accumulated names the way an old house accumulates layers of paint. You must know all of them, because different examiners and textbooks use different ones.

- **Epidermolytic hyperkeratosis (EHK)** — the *histopathological* name. Describes what the pathologist sees down the microscope.
- **Bullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma (BCIE)** — the *clinical* name, describing the triad: blisters + ichthyosis + redness.
- **Bullous ichthyosis** — the shorthand clinical name.
- **Brocq's disease / "described by Brocq"** — the eponym. **Louis-Anne-Jean Brocq** (1856–1928), French dermatologist, delineated the entity. *(A typographic note: the eponym should be written **Brocq**, with initial capital only — not "BROCQ" in full caps, which is simply a printing artifact.)*
- **Epidermolytic ichthyosis (EI)** — the **currently preferred term**, adopted at the **2009 consensus conference on ichthyosis (Sorèze)**.

> **Why the rename in 2009?** Because "bullous" is misleading — blistering dominates only in the neonate and then *fades*. And "congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma" was hopelessly confusable with **CIE (non-bullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma)**, a completely different, recessive disease. The new name ties the disease to its unambiguous defining feature: **epidermolysis** (cell lysis within the epidermis).
>
> **Common misunderstanding #1:** "BCIE" and "CIE" are *not* variants of one another. BCIE = autosomal **dominant**, keratin genes, blistering. CIE = autosomal **recessive** ARCI, lipid/enzyme genes, no blistering. One letter, entirely different disease.

---

## 2. Epidemiology and Inheritance

- **Autosomal dominant** with **complete penetrance**. Complete penetrance means: if you carry the mutation, you *will* manifest disease. There are no silent carriers. This is critically important for counselling — an apparently unaffected parent is, in the classic case, genuinely non-carrier, which points toward a **de novo mutation** in the child.
- **Rare autosomal recessive** forms exist (usually KRT10 with loss-of-function alleles). These are the exception, and they matter because they break the counselling rule above: here, both parents *are* silent carriers, and the recurrence risk is 25%, not 50%.
- Mosaic forms exist (discussed below), where the mutation occurred **after** fertilisation.

---

## 3. Pathogenesis: How a Single Amino Acid Wrecks a Wall

### 3.1 The players

Keratins work in **obligate heterodimer pairs** — one acidic type I keratin plus one basic type II keratin. The pairing is layer-specific:

| Layer | Keratin pair | Disease if mutated |
|---|---|---|
| **Basal** keratinocytes | **K5 / K14** | **Epidermolysis bullosa simplex** |
| **Suprabasal** (spinous/granular) | **K1 / K10** | **Epidermolytic ichthyosis** |
| Palms & soles (suprabasal) | **K1 / K9** | K9 → epidermolytic PPK (Vörner) |

This table is one of the highest-yield things in all of dermatological genetics, because it explains **where the blister forms**:

- Mutate **K5/K14** (basal) → the *bottom* row of cells fails → blister at the **basal layer**, right above the basement membrane → **EB simplex** (fragility only, no hyperkeratosis).
- Mutate **K1/K10** (suprabasal) → the *middle* rows fail → blister is **suprabasal/intraepidermal**, and because the differentiating compartment is chronically injured, the epidermis responds with **hyperkeratosis** → **epidermolytic ichthyosis**.

> **Analogy:** If the ground-floor pillars of a building crack, the whole building shears off its foundation (EB simplex). If the third-floor pillars crack, the upper floors collapse into rubble that piles up — and the building keeps frantically adding more concrete (hyperkeratosis) to compensate.

### 3.2 Where in the gene, and why that location matters

Keratin monomers have a **central α-helical rod domain** flanked by head and tail domains. The rod domain has four sub-helices, and at the very beginning and very end of it sit two short, exquisitely conserved sequences:

- the **helix initiation peptide (HIP)** — at the start of helix 1A
- the **helix termination peptide (HTP)** — at the end of helix 2B

These are the **plug-and-socket connectors** of the keratin world. They are where one heterodimer docks onto the next during **head-to-tail oligomerisation** and lateral assembly into a 10-nm filament.

**Mutations in EHK cluster in the HIP and HTP of the α-helical rod region of KRT1 and KRT10.** Most are **missense mutations affecting a conserved arginine** — and they act by a **dominant-negative mechanism**: the abnormal monomer is still made, still incorporated, and then **poisons the whole filament**. One bad Lego brick in the middle of a chain breaks the chain. This is precisely why the disease is **dominant** — you only need one bad allele to sabotage the assembly line.

### 3.3 The cascade

```
Missense mutation in HIP/HTP of KRT1 or KRT10
            ↓
Defective keratin alignment, oligomerisation, filament assembly
            ↓
Keratin filaments collapse into CLUMPS instead of forming a network
            ↓
Loss of cytoskeletal tensile strength; desmosomal anchorage weakened
            ↓
Keratinocytes cannot withstand mechanical shear → CYTOLYSIS
            ↓
(a) Cell rupture → intraepidermal BLISTERS
(b) Chronic injury + abnormal terminal differentiation → HYPERKERATOSIS
```

> Note the beautiful internal logic: **one lesion, two opposite-looking clinical signs.** Blisters (too little cohesion) and thick scale (too much keratin) are not contradictory — the hyperkeratosis is the epidermis's *reaction* to the fragility.

## 4. Genotype–Phenotype Correlation — The Palm-and-Sole Rule

This is the single most frequently examined point in the topic.

| Gene | Protein | Expression in palmoplantar skin | Clinical consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***KRT1*** (chr 12q13.13) | Keratin 1 | **Expressed** in suprabasal palm/sole epidermis (partnered with K9) | **Severe palmoplantar keratoderma (PPK)** |
| ***KRT10*** (chr 17q21.2) | Keratin 10 | **Not expressed** in palms and soles | **Palms and soles are SPARED** |

**The reasoning is purely anatomical, and that's why it's easy to remember:** you can only get disease in a tissue where the mutated protein is actually made. Palmoplantar epidermis substitutes **K9** for K10 as K1's partner. So:

- Break **K1** → you break the partnership in *both* general skin *and* palms/soles → **PPK present**.
- Break **K10** → palms/soles never used K10 anyway; they're running on the K1/K9 pair → **palms/soles spared**.

> **Typographic warning:** the gene is ***KRT1*** (numeral one), not "KRTI" (letter I). This is a very common OCR/printing error in Indian and photocopied texts. Similarly the protein is **K1**, and **KRT9** mutations give **epidermolytic PPK of Vörner** — localised only to palms and soles, again for exactly the same anatomical logic.

**Clinical use:** A child with epidermolytic ichthyosis and thick, painful, contracture-producing palms and soles → suspect **KRT1**. A child with widespread ridged hyperkeratosis but perfectly smooth palms → suspect **KRT10**. You can genotype-guess at the bedside.

## 5. Clinical Features: A Disease That Changes Its Costume with Age

The **biphasic, age-dependent evolution** is the clinical hallmark. Learn it as a timeline, not as a list.

### Phase 1 — The Neonate: "The Blistering Phase"

- Presents **at birth** with **erythroderma**
- **Widespread denuded skin**, **erosions**, **peeling**
- Marked **skin fragility** — blisters appear at sites of handling, friction, and trauma (nurses' fingers, monitor leads, nappy edges)
- This is the **dangerous** phase: transepidermal water loss, thermal instability, and an open door for bacteria

> **Common misunderstanding #2:** In this phase the baby looks exactly like **epidermolysis bullosa** or **staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome (SSSS)**. Many EHK babies are initially misdiagnosed. The give-away over the following weeks is the *appearance of hyperkeratosis* — EB and SSSS never become ichthyotic.

### Phase 2 — Infancy onward: "The Hyperkeratotic Phase"

As blistering **improves with age** (a genuinely reassuring fact for parents), hyperkeratosis progressively takes over:

- **Corrugated / ridged hyperkeratosis** — scale arranged in parallel ridges running **along skin lines**, most marked in the **flexures** (neck, axillae, groins, antecubital and popliteal fossae). The ridging is a beautiful physical clue: scale accumulates preferentially where the skin is repeatedly creased and sheared.
- On **extensor surfaces**, the hyperkeratosis forms a **cobblestone pattern** — discrete, closely set, verrucous, hyperkeratotic papules, especially over knees, elbows, dorsa of hands and feet.
- **Periodic shedding of large plates of superficial epidermis**, leaving behind a **tender, erythematous base**. Patients describe cyclical "peeling episodes." (Compare with ***cyclic ichthyosis*** — a recognised variant where these episodes are strikingly rhythmic.)
- If PPK is severe (i.e. KRT1) → **digital contractures**, flexion deformities, impaired hand function.
- **Angular cheilitis** — the perioral skin is thickened, inelastic, and constantly macerated by saliva.
- **Hair shaft encasement** — hair emerges through a plug of compacted keratin; follicular ostia become entombed, contributing to **alopecia**.
- **Pungent body odour.** Do not dismiss this as a trivial detail — it is one of the most socially disabling features of the disease. Mechanism: the thick, macerated, warm keratin in flexures is a perfect culture medium; **bacterial (and yeast) overgrowth, particularly *Staphylococcus aureus* and corynebacteria**, metabolise sebum and keratin into **volatile short-chain fatty acids and thioalcohols**. Hence antiseptic washes are genuinely therapeutic, not cosmetic.

## 6. Clinical Patterns and Variants

The source lists four, and each teaches something different:

1. **Epidermolytic epidermal naevus** — a **mosaic**, localised form (see §7).
2. **Variants with or without PPK** — i.e., the KRT1 vs KRT10 dichotomy expressed clinically.
3. **Cyclic ichthyosis with epidermolytic hyperkeratosis** — dramatic, periodic waves of generalised blistering/peeling alternating with relative quiescence; classically associated with certain KRT1 mutations.
4. **Annular epidermolytic ichthyosis** — annular and polycyclic erythematous, scaly, **migratory** plaques on the trunk and limbs, often with PPK, in a patient who had neonatal blistering. Clinically it can be mistaken for tinea or annular erythema.

> **A nuance worth flagging:** many compressed texts (including the source here) merge "annular epidermolytic ichthyosis" with the **mosaic** forms. Strictly, **annular EI is a generalised autosomal dominant variant** with annular morphology, whereas the **epidermolytic epidermal naevus** is the **mosaic** entity. Know both; don't let the merge confuse you.

## 7. Mosaicism — The Most Conceptually Beautiful Part of This Disease

This deserves its own section because it is a favourite of examiners and it teaches a principle that applies far beyond ichthyosis.

### The mechanism

A **postzygotic** (after fertilisation) mutation in **KRT1 or KRT10** occurs in a single embryonic keratinocyte precursor. Its descendants migrate along **Blaschko's lines**. Result: **unilateral or bilateral linear streaks** of hyperkeratosis on an otherwise normal skin background. When the hyperkeratosis is spiky and protruding, it is described as **porcupine-like spines** → the descriptive label **"ichthyosis hystrix"** (*hystrix* = porcupine).

### Type 1 vs Type 2 segmental mosaicism — and why it matters in the clinic

- **Type 1 segmental mosaicism:** postzygotic mutation in a **genetically normal** embryo. The patient has *only* the linear/segmental lesion; the rest of the skin is normal. This is the epidermolytic epidermal naevus.
- **Type 2 segmental mosaicism:** the patient is **already heterozygous** for the dominant mutation (generalised disease) and additionally loses the normal allele in a clone → a **segment of unusually severe disease superimposed on generalised disease**.

**The counselling bombshell:** If the postzygotic mutation occurred early enough to involve the **gonadal cells** (gonosomal mosaicism), the patient with a mere linear naevus can transmit the mutation **in the germline** — and the offspring, heterozygous in *every* cell, will have **full-blown generalised autosomal dominant epidermolytic ichthyosis**.

> **Practical lesson:** A patient with what looks like a trivial "linear wart-like naevus" on the leg, if it shows epidermolytic hyperkeratosis histologically, **must receive genetic counselling about the risk of a severely affected child.** This transforms a cosmetic curiosity into a reproductive-genetics consultation. This is one of the great "so what" moments in dermatology.

## 8. Complications

Each one follows logically from the two core defects (barrier failure + fragility):

| Complication | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Sepsis** | Eroded skin + colonised hyperkeratosis = portal of entry; greatest risk in the neonate |
| **Secondary skin infections** | *S. aureus*, streptococci, dermatophytes, *Candida* in macerated flexures |
| **Fluid and electrolyte imbalance** | Massive transepidermal water loss through denuded and fissured skin; hypernatraemic dehydration in neonates |
| **Posture and gait abnormalities** | Severe PPK + digital/plantar contractures → pain on weight-bearing, altered gait, secondary musculoskeletal strain |
| **Alopecias** | Follicular ostia plugged and destroyed by compacted keratin (hair shaft encasement), plus scarring from repeated erosion |
| *(add:)* Failure to thrive, heat intolerance, and major **psychosocial morbidity** (odour, appearance) |

## 9. Investigations

### 9.1 Histopathology (HPE) — the diagnostic gold standard in practice

The classic pattern, in order from surface inward:

1. **Dense orthokeratotic hyperkeratosis** — thick, compact stratum corneum that *retains no nuclei* ("ortho-" = normal maturation, just far too much of it).
2. **Prominent suprabasal acanthosis** — the spinous layer is thickened (a proliferative response).
3. **Hypergranulosis** with **coarse, clumped keratohyalin granules** — the granular layer is thickened and its granules are abnormally large and irregular. (These granules are profilaggrin aggregates; their clumping reflects disturbed keratin–filaggrin interaction.)
4. **Cytolysis (vacuolar/ballooning degeneration) of the suprabasal and granular layers** — cells appear swollen, pale, with perinuclear clearing and indistinct borders.
5. **Small intraepidermal blisters** formed where enough adjacent cells have lysed and coalesced.

Together, this constellation *is* the histological entity **"epidermolytic hyperkeratosis."** The disease is literally named after its biopsy.

> **Common misunderstanding #3 — and an important one:** **"Epidermolytic hyperkeratosis" is a histological *pattern*, not a disease-specific finding.** It can appear as an **incidental focal finding** in:
> - **solar (actinic) keratoses**
> - **seborrhoeic keratoses**
> - **epidermolytic acanthoma** (a solitary benign papule)
> - **epidermolytic epidermal naevi**
> - incidentally in otherwise normal skin, and in some **leukoplakia**
>
> So a report saying "epidermolytic hyperkeratosis" does **not** by itself diagnose epidermolytic ichthyosis. You must correlate with the clinical picture — generalised disease from birth with blistering.

### 9.2 Electron microscopy — the ultrastructural correlate

EM shows the *cause* of what light microscopy shows as cytolysis:

- **Clumped, fragmented keratin intermediate filaments** — "**tonofilament clumping/aggregation**." Instead of a delicate meshwork spanning the cytoplasm, the filaments have collapsed into dense, coarse aggregates.
- Abnormal **keratin intermediate filaments (KIF)** in the **lower epidermis**.
- **Perinuclear KIF shells** — dense rings of aggregated keratin hugging the nucleus — in the **upper epidermis / granular layer**.

> **Important correction to a common textbook typo:** these perinuclear shells are in the **upper *epidermis***, **not** the "upper dermis." The dermis contains no keratinocytes and therefore no keratin intermediate filaments at all. This is not a pedantic point — it is a conceptual error that reveals misunderstanding of where the disease lives.

> **A related distinction worth knowing:** **Ichthyosis with confetti** and **Ichthyosis hystrix of Curth–Macklin** are also KRT1/KRT10 disorders, and Curth–Macklin classically shows **concentric perinuclear shells of keratin filaments *without* cytolysis**. So: *shells + cytolysis* = epidermolytic ichthyosis; *shells without cytolysis* = Curth–Macklin. The presence or absence of cell lysis is the discriminator.

### 9.3 Molecular testing

- **Mutation screening and complete sequencing of KRT1 and KRT10** — confirms the diagnosis, establishes the genotype–phenotype correlation, and enables the next step.
- **Prenatal diagnosis** is possible **once the parental/index mutation is known** — via chorionic villus sampling or amniocentesis (DNA-based). Historically, fetal skin biopsy was used; molecular testing has replaced it. Preimplantation genetic diagnosis is also feasible.

---

## 10. Differential Diagnosis

Organise this by **phase**, because the differentials differ completely:

**In the neonatal blistering phase:**
- **Epidermolysis bullosa** (all types) — no hyperkeratosis develops
- **Staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome (SSSS)** — acute onset, unwell, perioral crusting, positive cultures, rapid response to antibiotics
- **Bullous impetigo**, neonatal **herpes simplex**
- **Incontinentia pigmenti** (vesicular stage) — Blaschkoid, eosinophilia, female
- Other **vesiculobullous and erosive disorders** of the newborn

**In the established hyperkeratotic phase:**
- Other **ichthyosiform disorders**: **lamellar ichthyosis**, **CIE (non-bullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma)**, **Netherton syndrome**
- Other **keratinopathic ichthyoses**: **superficial epidermolytic ichthyosis (ichthyosis bullosa of Siemens, KRT2)** — milder, "mauserung" (moulting) phenomenon of superficial peeling, flexural predominance, no erythroderma
- **Ichthyosis hystrix Curth–Macklin**
- **Erythrokeratodermas**

---

## 11. Management (brief but examinable)

- **Neonate:** humidified incubator, barrier nursing, meticulous fluid/electrolyte monitoring, non-adherent dressings, avoid adhesive tapes (they strip skin), culture and treat infection.
- **Emollients** — the backbone, lifelong.
- **Keratolytics** — urea, lactic acid, low-strength salicylic acid. **Caution:** avoid salicylic acid over large body surface areas, especially in infants → **systemic salicylism**.
- **Topical retinoids**, **topical calcipotriol**.
- **Antiseptic washes** (chlorhexidine, dilute bleach baths) — for odour and colonisation.
- **Systemic retinoids (acitretin)** — reduce hyperkeratosis, but **paradoxically may increase skin fragility and blistering in epidermolytic ichthyosis**. Hence: **start low, titrate slowly**, and monitor. Contraception is mandatory in women (teratogenicity; acitretin requires 3 years post-treatment).
- **Genetic counselling** — essential, including the mosaicism issue discussed above.

# PART II — HARLEQUIN FETUS (Harlequin Ichthyosis)

## 1. Definition and Nomenclature

- Synonyms: **harlequin baby**, **harlequin ichthyosis**, **ichthyosis congenita gravior** (Latin: "the graver congenital ichthyosis"), **keratosis diffusa fetalis**
- It is the **most severe form of autosomal recessive congenital ichthyosis (ARCI)**. ARCI is a spectrum: **harlequin ichthyosis (most severe) → lamellar ichthyosis → non-bullous CIE (mildest)**.
- **Why "harlequin"?** Because the thick keratin cracks into **large, diamond- (rhomboid-) shaped plates** separated by deep red fissures — a geometric pattern that resembles the **diamond-patterned costume of *Arlecchino* (Harlequin)**, the stock character of Italian **commedia dell'arte**.

> *Typographic note:* the phrase sometimes printed as "harlequin conic" in photocopied texts is simply a garbled rendering of **"harlequin" pattern**. There is no such thing as a "conic" scale.

- **Why "fetus" rather than "baby"?** Because historically these infants were born **prematurely** and died within hours to days — they were, in the grim clinical language of the time, barely more than fetuses. The name preserves a prognosis that modern neonatology has, thankfully, begun to overturn.

---

## 2. Epidemiology

- **Autosomal recessive** — parents are asymptomatic heterozygous carriers; recurrence risk **25%**; consanguinity increases incidence.
- **Very rare** (roughly 1 in 300,000 births).
- **Historically near-universally fatal.** Survival has **significantly improved** with modern intensive care — humidified incubators, aggressive fluid and electrolyte management, early systemic retinoids, and broad-spectrum antibiotics. **Survival into adolescence and adulthood is now well documented** in multiple case series. Survivors, however, are left with a severe lifelong **exfoliative ichthyosiform erythroderma** requiring continuous retinoid therapy.

---

## 3. Pathogenesis: The Failed Delivery Truck

### The gene and protein

**Loss-of-function (typically nonsense/frameshift, i.e. null) mutations in *ABCA12*** (chromosome 2q35).

**ABCA12** encodes an **ATP-binding cassette (ABC) transporter** — a membrane pump that uses ATP hydrolysis to push substrate across a lipid bilayer. Its substrate is **lipid: ceramides (especially glucosylceramides) and other barrier lipids.** Its job is to **load these lipids into lamellar bodies** inside granular-layer keratinocytes.

### The normal physiology (essential background)

```
Granular layer keratinocyte
   → synthesises barrier lipids (ceramide, cholesterol, fatty acids)
   → ABCA12 pumps these lipids INTO lamellar bodies (secretory organelles)
   → lamellar bodies fuse with the apical cell membrane at the
     granular/corneal interface
   → lipids extruded into the extracellular space
   → lipids reorganise into stacked LAMELLAR BILAYERS = the "mortar"
   → WATERPROOF BARRIER
```

> **Analogy:** Lamellar bodies are **delivery trucks**. ABCA12 is the **loading crane** that puts the cargo (lipids) into the trucks. In harlequin ichthyosis the crane is broken, so the trucks leave the warehouse **empty**. The mortar never arrives at the wall. And because the wall doesn't work, the epidermis panics and produces bricks at a furious rate — hence massive hyperkeratosis.

### The cascade

```
Loss-of-function mutation in ABCA12
            ↓
Ceramides/lipids cannot be loaded into lamellar bodies
            ↓
Lamellar bodies abnormal or absent; no lipid extrusion
            ↓
PREVENTION OF FORMATION OF LIPID BILAYERS in the stratum corneum
            ↓
Complete epidermal barrier breakdown
     +
Failure of normal corneocyte desquamation (lipids also needed for
enzyme delivery and corneodesmosome degradation)
            ↓
MASSIVE, COMPACT HYPERKERATOSIS — armour-like stratum corneum
```

### The allelic series — a lovely genotype–severity rule

The **degree of residual ABCA12 function determines the phenotype**:

| ABCA12 function | Phenotype |
|---|---|
| **Null / complete loss** | **Harlequin ichthyosis** |
| **Partial loss (hypomorphic, e.g. missense)** | **Lamellar ichthyosis / CIE** |

This is a superb illustration of a general genetic principle: **one gene, a spectrum of disease, calibrated by residual protein activity.** Never think of these ARCI subtypes as unrelated diseases; think of them as points on a dimmer switch.

---

## 4. Clinical Features — And the Physics Behind Each One

Every feature of harlequin ichthyosis can be derived from one idea: **the newborn is encased in a thick, rigid, inelastic, non-expandable shell.** Once you accept that, you can *predict* the clinical signs rather than memorise them.

### At birth

- **Premature delivery**, average gestational age **~35 weeks** — abnormal fetal skin appears to trigger preterm labour. Prematurity compounds every other problem (surfactant deficiency, immature immunity, poor thermoregulation).
- **Hard, armour-like thickened stratum corneum** encasing the whole body.

### Derived consequences

| Sign | Mechanism (the physics) |
|---|---|
| **Severe immobilisation** | Rigid casing across every joint; the baby cannot flex or extend |
| **Restricted ventilation → respiratory insufficiency** | The chest wall cannot expand against the armour; a *restrictive* ventilatory defect. Plus prematurity. A leading cause of death. |
| **Large yellow adherent plates separated by deep red fissures; diamond-shaped scales** | As the body grows, a rigid inelastic shell must crack. Cracking along lines of maximal tension yields the geometric rhomboid (harlequin) pattern. Fissures expose bleeding dermis. |
| **Dehydration, hypernatraemia, electrolyte imbalance** | Absent lipid barrier + fissures → enormous transepidermal water loss; loss can be several-fold normal |
| **Temperature instability** | Evaporative heat loss from water loss + inability to sweat through occluded ducts |
| **Ectropion** (eversion of eyelids) | The taut, inelastic peri-orbital stratum corneum contracts and **pulls the thin eyelid skin outward**, everting the lids. Result: exposed conjunctiva → keratitis, corneal ulceration, blindness risk. Think **shrink-wrap pulling on a soft flap**. |
| **Eclabium** (eversion of lips) | Identical mechanism around the mouth → fish-mouth appearance, inability to form a seal → **feeding difficulty** |
| **Rudimentary/flattened ear and nasal cartilage** | Cartilage is soft and moulded by prolonged external pressure from the thick casing; ears appear absent/adherent, nose flattened with pinched nares |
| **Distorted facial appearance** | Sum of ectropion + eclabium + flattened ears/nose |
| **Swollen, oedematous hands and feet in "mitten-like" encasement** | Distal parts sheathed in keratin; lymphatic/venous return impaired → oedema |
| **Constricting skin bands → autoamputation of digits** | Circumferential rings of rigid hyperkeratosis tighten around growing digits → ischaemia → **digital necrosis and spontaneous amputation**. Conceptually this is a **pseudoainhum**. |
| **Hair loss** — scalp, eyebrows, eyelashes | Follicular ostia obliterated by compacted keratin |
| **Sepsis** | Fissured, non-barrier skin + prematurity + invasive lines |

> **Correction of a frequent typo:** the bands are ***constricting*** (tightening), not "constructing." The distinction matters clinically because it dictates therapy — **early recognition and release/softening of constricting bands** (intensive emollients, keratolytics, occasionally surgical release) can save digits.
>
> **Common misunderstanding #4:** these constricting bands are **not** amniotic bands. In **amniotic band syndrome** the constriction is by **extrinsic fibrous strands** of amnion. In harlequin ichthyosis the constrictor is **the patient's own hyperkeratotic skin**. Same outcome (autoamputation), different mechanism — and the harlequin variety is potentially medically reversible.

> **Common misunderstanding #5 — harlequin vs collodion baby.** Both are neonatal encasement phenotypes, but:
> - **Collodion baby:** a **shiny, taut, translucent, cellophane-like membrane** that **sheds over 2–4 weeks**, revealing the underlying ichthyosis. It is a **clinical presentation**, not a diagnosis — most evolve into lamellar ichthyosis or CIE; some become **self-healing collodion baby**; it is also associated with syndromic ichthyoses (Netherton, trichothiodystrophy, **Gaucher disease type II**, Conradi–Hünermann).
> - **Harlequin:** a **thick, opaque, hard, plate-like armour** that does **not** simply shed; the child does not become normal.
>
> *(Note: it is **Gaucher disease type II** — Roman numeral two, the acute neuronopathic infantile form with severe CNS disease and death by ~2 years. "Type 11" as sometimes printed is an OCR artifact; there is no type 11.)*

## 5. Complications in Survivors

Once the neonatal crisis is survived, the phenotype evolves into a **severe, generalised exfoliative ichthyosiform erythroderma**, with:

- **Palmoplantar keratoderma (PPK)**
- **Dystrophic nails**
- **Recurrent skin infections** (barrier never normalises)
- **Joint contractures** — from persistent taut skin across flexures
- **Delayed motor skill development** — a direct consequence of contractures, pain, and immobility, plus lost developmental opportunity during prolonged hospitalisation
- **Heat intolerance** — occluded sweat ducts → hypohidrosis → inability to thermoregulate; risk of hyperthermia with exercise or hot weather
- **Chronic constipation** — multifactorial: reduced mobility, retinoid therapy, poor fluid intake relative to enormous cutaneous losses, restricted abdominal wall compliance
- **Short stature / growth retardation** — the metabolic cost of a hyperproliferative epidermis and massive transepidermal water loss is enormous; caloric requirements are markedly increased
- **Alopecia, ocular complications from ectropion, and major psychosocial burden**

---

## 6. Investigations

### 6.1 Histopathology

- **Extraordinarily compact and thickened orthokeratosis** — the stratum corneum may be many times normal thickness and is densely compacted (not the loose basket-weave of normal skin).
- **Occasional parakeratosis** (retained nuclei in the corneum — evidence of disordered, hurried maturation).
- **Concentric accumulation of keratotic material around hair shafts** — keratin laid down in onion-like rings around follicles; the ultrastructural basis of the alopecia and of the "spiky" follicular appearance.

### 6.2 Electron microscopy — the *decisive* ultrastructural test, and the direct correlate of the ABCA12 defect

- **Abnormal or missing lamellar bodies** in the granular layer → *because ABCA12 cannot load them, they never mature.*
- **Absent extracellular lipid lamellae** in the stratum corneum interstices → *no mortar.*
- **Lipid inclusions / remnant organelles retained within corneocytes and the stratum corneum** → the lipid was made but never correctly packaged or extruded; it sits stranded inside the cells as droplets and vesicular remnants.

> **Contrast for the exam:** **Lamellar ichthyosis** EM shows **cholesterol clefts** and **partially formed** lamellae — consistent with a *partial* barrier defect. **Harlequin** EM shows **absent** lamellae. Again, the dimmer-switch principle made visible at 50,000×.

### 6.3 Molecular and prenatal diagnosis

- **ABCA12 mutation analysis** — confirms diagnosis definitively.
- **Prenatal testing** in subsequent pregnancies by **chorionic villus sampling (CVS, ~11–13 weeks)** or **amniocentesis (~15–18 weeks)** for ABCA12 mutations. Also **preimplantation genetic diagnosis** where available.
- Prenatal **ultrasound** may show suggestive signs (fixed flexed limbs, persistently open mouth, ectropion, polyhydramnios, floating echogenic particles in amniotic fluid) but is **not** diagnostic — molecular testing is required.
- Because the disease is AR with 25% recurrence, **genetic counselling is mandatory** for every family.

---

## 7. Treatment

Organise by **time of life** — this is how the source presents it, and it maps onto the changing threats.

### 7.1 General measures (all ages)

- **Topical and systemic antibiotics** — for established infection
- **Antibacterial soaps**, **chlorhexidine baths** — reduce colonisation and odour
- **Soft clothing** to decrease friction
- **Oral retinoids** — the cornerstone of systemic therapy

### 7.2 The neonate — the critical window

- ***ICU isolation***, ideally a **humidified incubator** (high ambient humidity dramatically reduces transepidermal water loss)
- **Prevent and treat**:
 - **Dehydration** — aggressive fluid replacement; requirements may be 2–3× normal
 - **Electrolyte imbalance** — especially **hypernatraemia**; monitor serially
 - **Cutaneous superinfection and sepsis** — **broad-spectrum antibiotics**, surveillance cultures
 - **Temperature instability** — servo-controlled incubator
 - **Respiratory insufficiency** — respiratory support as needed
 - **Nutrition** — high-calorie feeding, often nasogastric because of eclabium; the metabolic demand is enormous
 - **Ocular care** — lubricants, ophthalmology review for ectropion/exposure keratopathy
- **Careful handling** — every touch can shear skin
- **Protective padding**
- **Lubricants / bland emollients** — applied frequently, in thick layers
- **Early systemic retinoid** — see below
- **Monitor and release constricting digital bands**

### 7.3 Children and adults — the maintenance phase

Goal: **decrease hyperkeratosis and soften the skin.**

- **Keratolytic creams and lotions**: **urea**, **salicylic acid**, and **alpha-hydroxy acids** (lactic, glycolic)
 - *Caution:* **salicylic acid over large surface areas in children risks systemic salicylate toxicity**; urea and lactic acid are generally safer for extensive use.
- **Topical tretinoin** and **topical vitamin D preparations** (calcipotriol) — both normalise keratinocyte differentiation
- **Gentle scrub baths** — mechanical debridement of thick scale; long soaks followed by gentle abrasion
- **Frequent, generous use of emollients** — the single most important lifelong measure; applied immediately after bathing to trap water
- **Early systemic retinoids** — **acitretin 1 mg/kg/day**

### 7.4 A word of caution on retinoids

The recommendation of **acitretin 1 mg/kg/day** is consistent with guidelines for severe congenital ichthyosis, and **early initiation in the neonate improves survival** by accelerating shedding of the armour, relieving chest restriction, and reducing constricting bands. But retinoids in infants demand respect:

- **Skeletal toxicity** — **premature epiphyseal closure**, bone pain, extraspinal calcification with long-term use
- **Hepatotoxicity** — monitor LFTs
- **Hypertriglyceridemia / dyslipidaemia** — monitor lipid panel
- **Mucocutaneous dryness**, cheilitis, paradoxical increased skin fragility
- **Pseudotumour cerebri** (especially with concomitant tetracyclines)
- **Teratogenicity** — acitretin requires contraception for **3 years** after cessation in females of childbearing potential (because of conversion to etretinate, which has a long half-life)

Hence many centres start at a **lower dose (0.5 mg/kg/day)** and titrate to clinical response, with **monitoring of growth velocity, LFTs, lipids, and skeletal surveys** on long-term therapy.

# THE CORE IDEA IN ONE PICTURE

Picture the epidermis as a **brick wall** whose job is to be waterproof.

```
                    THE EPIDERMAL WALL
        ┌───────────────────────────────────────────┐
        │  BRICKS (corneocytes)                     │
        │    ↑ internal REBAR = keratin K1/K10      │
        │  MORTAR (extracellular lipid lamellae)    │
        │    ↑ delivered by lamellar bodies,        │
        │      loaded by ABCA12                     │
        └───────────────────────────────────────────┘

   BREAK THE REBAR                    BREAK THE MORTAR SUPPLY
   (KRT1 / KRT10, AD)                 (ABCA12 null, AR)
          │                                    │
   Cells burst under shear           No waterproofing at all
          │                                    │
   BLISTERS + reactive               MASSIVE compact
   ridged hyperkeratosis             hyperkeratosis (armour)
          │                                    │
   EPIDERMOLYTIC ICHTHYOSIS           HARLEQUIN ICHTHYOSIS
   (survivable; improves with age)    (neonatal emergency;
   KRT1 → PPK; KRT10 → palms spared   survivors = severe
                                       exfoliative erythroderma)
```

**Three sentences to carry away:**

1. **Epidermolytic ichthyosis** is a **dominant, dominant-negative keratin disease**: missense mutations in the **helix initiation/termination peptides** of the **KRT1/KRT10 rod domain** clump the keratin cytoskeleton → **suprabasal cytolysis** (neonatal blisters) → **ridged/cobblestone hyperkeratosis** (later life); **KRT1 gives PPK because K1 is expressed in palms and soles, KRT10 spares them because K10 is not**; histology shows hyperkeratosis + hypergranulosis with clumped keratohyalin + suprabasal cytolysis; and a **mosaic epidermolytic naevus can transmit generalised disease if gonadal cells are involved**.

2. **Harlequin ichthyosis** is a **recessive lipid-transport disease**: **null ABCA12** cannot load ceramides into lamellar bodies → **no extracellular lipid lamellae** → total barrier failure and **armour-like hyperkeratosis**, producing ectropion, eclabium, mitten-hands with constricting bands, chest restriction, and hypernatraemic dehydration; EM shows **absent lamellar bodies and lamellae**; treatment is **humidified NICU care + fluids + antibiotics + early acitretin**.

3. Across both, the recurring principle is this: **the phenotype is a readout of how much functional protein remains** — complete keratin poisoning gives blistering ichthyosis, complete ABCA12 loss gives harlequin, and partial ABCA12 loss gives the milder lamellar ichthyosis/CIE end of the same spectrum.
