# Q2. Describe the etiopathogenesis and cytostatic management of psoriasis.

**Chapter**: 10 — Disorders of Keratinization | **Source Pages**: 155–157 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Introduction

Psoriasis is an **immune-mediated polygenic skin disorder** that may be elicited by several environmental triggers in predisposing individuals.
- It is characterized by **sharply demarcated, red scaly plaques** of variable extent and severity.
- Affects approximately **2% of the world population**.

---

### Epidemiology

- **Age**: Bimodal distribution — 2 peaks:
  - **Type 1**: 16–22 years (early onset)
  - **Type 2**: 57–62 years (late onset)
- **Sex**: Male = Female
- **HLA associations**:
  - ***Strong* HLA-C**: **HLA-Cw0602** (previously written as HLA-C: 0602)
    - Associated with **Type II psoriasis** and **Guttate psoriasis**
  - ***HLA-Cw6***: Associated with **early age of onset (Type 1)**
- **Genes**: PSORS1–9, ERAP1

> **Margin Note (Handwritten):** "found on PSORS1 Gene" — annotated next to HLA-Cw6, indicating that HLA-Cw6 is located within the **PSORS1** susceptibility locus on chromosome 6p21.3.

---

### Triggers

- **Streptococcal infection** (especially β-haemolytic streptococci → guttate psoriasis)
- **COVID-19** and other respiratory infections
- **HIV** (paradoxical worsening despite immunosuppression)
- **Medications**: Lithium, systemic antimalarials, TNF-alpha inhibitors (paradoxical psoriasis), Interferon-alpha, Beta-blockers, CCBs, ACE inhibitors, etc.
- **Alcohol**
- **Smoking**
- **Stress**
- **Sunlight** (can trigger or exacerbate in some patients; photosensitive psoriasis)
- **Physical trauma** — **Koebner phenomenon** (isomorphic response)
- **Hypocalcemia**

---

### Immunopathogenesis

#### Role of T Cells and Dendritic Cells

- Psoriasis is linked with specific genes (**HLA-Cw6** and **ERAP1**) which affect the processing of antigens by the immune system.
- Specific **T-cell subsets** including **CD4+** and **CD8+** T cells are present in lesional skin.
- **Dendritic cells (DCs)** are present in involved and uninvolved skin with increased numbers and enhanced ability to activate T cells in lesional skin.
- DCs differentiate into proinflammatory forms producing cytokines such as **TNF-alpha** and **iNOS**.
- ***pDCs*** (plasmacytoid dendritic cells) play a significant role by producing **IFN-alpha** in response to self-DNA or RNA complexes with the antimicrobial peptide **LL37** which **triggers IFN-alpha**.
- **Neutrophils** are a prominent feature in **acute/pustular psoriasis**.
- Increased expression of **VEGF** in psoriatic plaques → **angiogenesis**.

#### Cytokines and Chemokines

Psoriasis is marked by a dysregulated cytokine network with overactivation of the **Th1**, **Th17**, and **Th22** pathways.

- **TNF-alpha** — a central cytokine in the pathogenesis of psoriasis, produced primarily by activated T cells and APCs like DCs.
- **TNF-alpha + IFN-γ** → promotes endothelial cell activation and immune cell infiltration into the skin.
- **IL-23** secreted by DCs and APCs is a major driver of the **Th17 pathway**, leading to the production of **IL-17**, which plays a central role in inflammation:
  - Release of proinflammatory cytokines
  - Antimicrobial peptides
  - Chemokines by keratinocytes
  - All above amplify the inflammatory response
- IL-17 also **synergizes with TNF-alpha** to enhance keratinocyte proliferation and upregulation of psoriasis-related genes.

##### Chemokine Network

- Chemokines guide immune cells into the skin, contributing to immune cell infiltration; expression of chemokines like **CXCL9**, **CXCL10**, **CCL2**, and **CCL20** is regulated by **IL-17** and **IFN-γ**.
- Chemokines help recruit **Th17 cells**, **neutrophils**, and other immune cells to psoriatic lesions.
- **CCL20** attracts **CCR6-expressing Th17 cells** and T cells, contributing to the persistence of inflammation.
- Cytokine and chemokine **feedback loops** — cytokines and chemokines operate within a **self-amplifying loop**.

#### IL-23/Th17 Axis (Flowchart)

```
Dendritic cells → IL-23 → Drives Th17 differentiation
                                  ↓
                            IL-17 → Activates keratinocytes to produce CCL20
                                  ↓
                            CCL20 → Recruits more Th17 cells
                                  ↓
                            Chronic inflammation
```

#### Signaling Pathways

| Pathway | Effect |
|---|---|
| **NF-κB pathway** | Upregulated → **TNF-alpha** production |
| **JAK-STAT pathway** | Activated → **IL-6, IL-23**, and **IFN-γ** |
| **MAP K pathway** | Rapid cell turnover |

#### Triggers → Pathogenesis Flow

```
Triggers → Stimulate cytokines and chemokines via pattern recognition
           receptors and toll-like receptors
              ↓
         NF-κB / JAK-STAT / MAP K pathways activated
              ↓
         Chronic inflammatory cascade
```

### Innate Immunity and Keratinocytes

- Keratinocytes express **antimicrobial proteins** such as:
  - **Beta-defensin 1** (BLD-1)
  - **Secretory leucocyte protease inhibitor** (SLP-1)
  - **LBD2** and **LL37**
- Keratinocytes also express **TLRs** (Toll-like receptors) and secrete signaling molecules including **IL-6**, **IL-8**, and **TNF-alpha**, which contribute to inflammation.
- Increased proliferation leads to expression of **STAT3**, which regulates genes involved in psoriasis.

---

### Criteria for Stable Psoriasis

- Individuals with **no active lesions**
- Individuals with no new lesions while others remained the same size even if the affected area is extensive
- **Scaling predominates**, light red (as opposed to bright red/angry-looking plaques of active disease)

---

### Cytostatics in Therapy

**Definition**: Cytostatic agents are substances that **slow or stop the growth of cells without killing them**.

*In psoriasis the agent's target:*
- **Keratinocyte hyperproliferation**
- **Inhibit inflammatory cells**
- **Modulate immune response**

---

### Methotrexate

**Folic acid antagonist** that has an excellent therapeutic control of psoriasis.

#### Mechanism of Action (MOA)

```
Methotrexate
    ├── Irreversible inhibition:
    │     Folic acid → Dihydrofolate → Tetrahydrofolate
    │     (inhibits Dihydrofolate Reductase — DHFR)
    │     → Prevents formation of tetrahydrofolate
    │       which is crucial for DNA synthesis
    │
    └── Partially irreversible inhibition:
          Thymidylate synthetase
```

- **Leads to reduced nucleotide production** and inhibition of cell division, especially in the **S phase**.
- **Partially inhibits thymidylate synthetase** also, which disrupts DNA synthesis.
- **Immunosuppressive effects** due to suppression of proliferating cells and their migration into tissues.
- **Anti-inflammatory effect**: By increasing adenosine through a mechanism involving **5-aminoimidazole-4-carboxamide ribonucleotide (AICAR) transformylase** and ecto 5′ nucleotide.

#### Indications (for Methotrexate in Psoriasis)

- Severe psoriasis:
  - Pustular
  - Erythroderma
  - PSA (Psoriatic arthritis)
  - Nail involvement
- Psoriasis not responding to topical treatments, phototherapy, and/or systemic retinoids
- Chronic plaque (BSA > 10–15%)

---

### Cyclosporine

- A **cyclic undecapeptide** isolated from the fungus ***Tolypocladium inflatum***.
- It is a **calcineurin inhibitor**.

#### Mechanism of Action (MOA)

```
CSA + Cyclophilin → Complex
    │
    ├── Binds → Reduces activity of transcription factor NFAT1
    │            (Crucial for IL-2 production)
    │            → Fewer activated T cells (CD4+ and CD8+)
    │            → No T-cell proliferation
    │
    ├── Reduces IFN-gamma production
    │
    └── Reduces ICAM-1
         → Reduces inflammation and lymphocyte infiltration

Also directly influences:
  • Mast cells
  • Langerhans cells
  • Keratinocytes
```

#### Indications (for Cyclosporine in Psoriasis)

- **Severe psoriasis** (due to rapid action)
- Conventional therapies ineffective

---

### Other Cytostatic / Immunosuppressive Agents

- Azathioprine
- Hydroxyurea
- 6-Thioguanine
- Mercaptopurine
- Sulfasalazine
- Leflunomide

## Corrections & Verification Notes

- **"HLA-C: 0602"** — The source lists this as "Strong HLA C: 0602." The standard allele designation is **HLA-Cw0602** (or HLA-C*06:02 in modern nomenclature). Per **Rook's (10th ed.)** and **Bolognia (4th ed.)**, HLA-Cw0602 is the single strongest genetic determinant of psoriasis susceptibility, particularly for Type I (early-onset) and guttate psoriasis. The source text associates it with "Type II psoriasis and Guttate Psoriasis" — this warrants clarification:
  - **HLA-Cw0602** is most strongly linked to **Type I (early-onset) psoriasis** and guttate psoriasis (Ref: Rook's, Bolognia).
  - The text on the page says "Type II psoriasis" under HLA-C: 0602 — this appears to be a **typographical/conceptual error** in the source. Type II (late-onset) psoriasis has weaker HLA-Cw0602 association. However, it is possible the source uses a different numbering scheme. The handwritten note clarifies "HLA Cw6: Early age of onset (type 1)" separately, which is consistent with standard references.

- **"BLD-1" (Beta-defensin 1)** → The standard abbreviation is **hBD-1** (human Beta-defensin 1). "BLD-1" appears to be a typographical error or non-standard abbreviation in the source. (Ref: Bolognia, Chapter on innate immunity)

- **"SLP-1" (Secretory leucocyte protease inhibitor)** → The standard abbreviation is **SLPI** (Secretory Leukocyte Protease Inhibitor). "SLP-1" in the source is a non-standard abbreviation. (Ref: Rook's)

- **"LBD2"** → Should likely be **hBD-2** (human Beta-defensin 2). "LBD2" appears to be a typographical error in the source text. (Ref: Bolognia)

- **"STAT-3"** — Listed in context of keratinocyte proliferation. Confirmed: **STAT3** (Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 3) is indeed overexpressed in psoriatic keratinocytes and plays a role in hyperproliferation and impaired apoptosis. (Ref: Rook's, Bolognia)

- **"6-Thioqnanine"** → **6-Thioguanine** — Typographical/OCR error in the source. The correct spelling is 6-Thioguanine (a purine analogue). (Ref: Andrews')

- **"Mercaptopurine"** — Correctly listed. Also known as 6-Mercaptopurine (6-MP). Note: 6-Thioguanine is a metabolite of Azathioprine and is related to the Mercaptopurine pathway.

- **"CSA" (Cyclosporine)** — Standard abbreviations include **CsA** (Cyclosporine A) or **CyA**. The source uses "CSA" in the MOA flowchart, which is acceptable but less precise.

- **"Tolypocladium inflatum"** — Confirmed correct. Per **Rook's** and pharmacology references, Cyclosporine is indeed isolated from *Tolypocladium inflatum* (formerly classified as *Trichoderma polysporum*).

- **"NFAT1"** — The source mentions NFAT1 as the transcription factor inhibited by Cyclosporine. More precisely, Cyclosporine inhibits **calcineurin**, which prevents dephosphorylation of **NFAT** (Nuclear Factor of Activated T cells) family members, thereby blocking NFAT translocation to the nucleus and subsequent **IL-2 transcription**. NFAT1 (NFATc2) is one member; the broader family (NFATc1–c4) is affected. (Ref: Bolognia)

- **"Myocardial infection"** (Page 157, under Cardiovascular disease comorbidities of Q3) → This is likely **"Myocardial infarction"** — an OCR/autocorrect error. While this is technically in Q3 territory, it was observed during page scanning for Q2 and is noted here for completeness.
