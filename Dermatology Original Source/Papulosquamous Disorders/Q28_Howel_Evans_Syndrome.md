# Q28. Write a short note on Howel–Evans syndrome.

**Chapter**: Disorders of Keratinization | **Source Pages**: 184 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Definition

- **Howel–Evans syndrome** is also known as **tylosis with esophageal cancer (TOC)**.
- It is an **autosomal-dominant inherited**, **focal type** of **palmoplantar keratoderma** associated with later development of **esophageal cancer** (squamous cell carcinoma).

> **High-Yield Point — The Tylosis–Esophageal Cancer Link**: Howel–Evans syndrome is the **only hereditary PPK with a well-established, near-obligate association with internal malignancy** (esophageal SCC). The penetrance for esophageal carcinoma exceeds **95% by age 65** in affected families, making it one of the strongest genotype–cancer associations in dermatology. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

---

### Pathophysiology

- Caused by a **missense mutation** in the ***RHBDF2*** gene (also known as ***iRhom2***).
- **RHBDF2** normally produces the **RHBDF2 protease** (iRhom2 protein) that regulates the **maturation of TACE (TNF-α–converting enzyme / ADAM17)** in the skin.
- **Pathogenetic cascade**:

```
RHBDF2 mutation
       ↓
Dysregulated TACE (ADAM17) activity
       ↓
Excessive cell growth and improper wound healing
       ↓
Development of precancerous lesions → Esophageal SCC
```

> **Molecular Note — RHBDF2/iRhom2 and TACE/ADAM17**: iRhom2 is an inactive member of the rhomboid family of intramembrane proteases. It functions as a **chaperone** for ADAM17/TACE in the endoplasmic reticulum, facilitating its trafficking to the cell surface. Gain-of-function mutations in *RHBDF2* lead to **increased ADAM17 activity**, causing excessive cleavage of EGFR ligands (amphiregulin, epiregulin) → sustained EGFR signaling → enhanced proliferation and impaired differentiation in esophageal and palmoplantar epithelium. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

---

### Clinical Features

#### Palmoplantar Keratoderma
- **PPK predominantly over pressure points** (weight-bearing areas) of the soles — i.e., **focal, non-transgradient** pattern.
- Onset typically in **late childhood to adolescence**.

#### Oral Involvement
- **Oral leukokeratosis** (whitish mucosal plaques).

#### Follicular Changes
- **Follicular accentuation** (keratotic follicular papules).

#### Esophageal Malignancy
- **Esophageal carcinoma** can develop, typically in the **5th decade** of life.
- **>95% lifetime risk** of developing **esophageal squamous cell carcinoma** in affected individuals.
- **Tylotic lesions can progress to squamous cell carcinoma** (the PPK itself may undergo malignant transformation).

> **Clinical Pearl — Surveillance Protocol**: Given the near-obligate cancer risk, affected individuals and at-risk family members require **regular upper GI endoscopy with biopsy**, typically starting from **age 30** and repeated at **1–3-year intervals**. Early detection of esophageal dysplasia dramatically improves survival. Genetic testing for *RHBDF2* mutations enables presymptomatic identification of at-risk family members. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

---

### Differential Diagnosis (D/D)

- **Pachyonychia congenita** (nail involvement distinguishes PC from Howel–Evans)
- **Clouston syndrome** (hidrotic ectodermal dysplasia — nail dystrophy, alopecia, PPK)

---

### Treatment

- **Oral retinoids** for oral disease and **prevention of autoamputation**.
- **Regular endoscopic surveillance** for esophageal SCC — lifelong screening recommended.
- **Keratolytics** (urea, salicylic acid) for symptomatic PPK management.
- **Genetic counseling** — autosomal dominant inheritance means 50% risk for offspring.

## Corrections & Verification Notes

- **"Howel-Evans" (p. 184)** → **"Howel–Evans"** — The source uses a hyphen ("Howel-Evans"). The standard eponymous form uses an **en-dash** (Howel–Evans) as it connects two surnames (Howel and Evans), per medical typography conventions. The syndrome was first described by **Howel-Evans, McConnell, Clarke, and Sheppard in 1958** in two Liverpool families. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"mis-sense mutation" (p. 184)** → **"missense mutation"** — The source hyphenates "mis-sense." The standard molecular biology term is **"missense"** (one word, no hyphen), referring to a point mutation that results in a different amino acid in the protein product. (Ref: Standard molecular biology nomenclature)

- **"RHBDF2 protease" (p. 184)** → **Clarification:** The source states that RHBDF2 "produces RHBDF2 protease." This is slightly misleading — *RHBDF2* is the **gene**, and it encodes the **iRhom2 protein**. iRhom2 is actually an **inactive (catalytically dead) rhomboid pseudoprotease** that functions as a **chaperone/regulator** of ADAM17 (TACE) rather than as a protease itself. The "protease" label in the source is a simplification. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Tylosis with esophageal cancer" nomenclature** → **Verification:** The source correctly identifies Howel–Evans as being "also known as Tylosis with esophageal cancer." The term **tylosis** refers to focal, non-epidermolytic PPK of the plantar surface. The gene locus was mapped to chromosome **17q25** (the **TOC locus**), and the causative *RHBDF2* mutations were identified in 2012 (Blaydon et al., *Am J Hum Genet*, 2012). (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Esophageal carcinoma can develop in the 5th decade" (p. 184)** → **Verification confirmed.** The source states esophageal carcinoma develops in the 5th decade. Literature confirms that the mean age of onset of esophageal SCC in tylosis families is approximately **45–65 years**, with cumulative risk approaching **>95% by age 65**. This is specifically **squamous cell carcinoma**, not adenocarcinoma. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia; Ellis et al., *NEJM*, 1994)

- **"Tylotic lesions can progress to squamous cell carcinoma" (p. 184)** → **Clarification:** This statement in the source is slightly ambiguous. The esophageal SCC arises from the **esophageal mucosa** (not from the palmoplantar keratoderma directly). However, the shared RHBDF2-driven proliferative pathway affects both palmoplantar and esophageal epithelium. The PPK itself does not "transform" into SCC; rather, both are manifestations of the same genetic defect affecting different epithelial sites. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Rx: (same as previous)" (p. 184)** → **Source abbreviation decoded:** The source text states "Rx: (same as previous)" referring back to the Q27 (PLS) treatment section. This indicates **oral retinoids for disease management and prevention of autoamputation**. In clinical practice, however, Howel–Evans treatment additionally emphasizes **lifelong endoscopic surveillance** for esophageal malignancy, which is not a feature of PLS management. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)
