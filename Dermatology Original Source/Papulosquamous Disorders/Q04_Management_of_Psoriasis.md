# Q4. Discuss the management of psoriasis.

**Chapter**: Disorders of Keratinization | **Source Pages**: 158–160 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Investigations

#### Grattage Test (Candle-Grease Sign → Auspitz Sign Sequence)

Scraping the lesion with a glass slide accentuates silvery scales (**candle-grease sign**).

When scales are completely scraped off, the basement membrane is exposed and seen as a moist red glistening surface — the **Membrane of Bulkley**. On removal of this membrane, dilated capillaries at the tip of elongated dermal papillae are torn, leading to multiple pinpoint bleeding points. This characteristic feature is known as the **Auspitz sign** (after Heinrich Auspitz).

> **Note:** The Auspitz sign is attributed to suprapapillary thinning of stratum Malpighii, elongation and tortuosity of the dermal papillary capillaries, and parakeratosis.

> **Caveat:** The Auspitz sign is sensitive but not specific — it is also seen in **Darier's disease** and **actinic keratosis**, but is **absent** in **pustular**, **erythrodermic**, and **guttate psoriasis**.

---

#### Histopathology

##### Psoriasis Vulgaris

**Early lesions:**
- Mounds of parakeratosis with orthokeratosis and **Munro's microabscesses**
- Vacuolation and disappearance of granular cells
- Lymphocytes extend into the lower epidermis causing slight spongiosis
- Capillary dilatation and oedema in papillary dermis
- Lymphocyte infiltrate around dermis
- **Spongiform pustule of Kogoj** in spinous layer

**Advanced lesions:**
- Acanthosis with irregular elongation of rete ridges with thickening in the lower portion
- Suprapapillary thinning with occasional spongiform pustules
- Pallor of the upper layer of the epidermis
- Diminished or absent granular layer
- Confluent parakeratosis
- Munro's microabscesses present
- Elongation and edema of dermal papillae
- Dilated and tortuous capillaries

##### Guttate Psoriasis
Similar to an active lesion of psoriasis but more pronounced inflammatory infiltrate and less acanthosis; neutrophils above parakeratosis.

##### Erythrodermic Psoriasis
Similar to active lesions (rarely) or indistinguishable from chronic eczematous dermatitis.

##### Psoriasis and AIDS
- Acanthosis without suprapapillary thinning
- Slight spongiosis
- Rare necrotic keratinocytes
- Superficial perivascular infiltrate of lymphocytes and histiocytes that occasionally contain plasma cells
- Eosinophils ±

---

#### Blood Investigations
- **CBC** — to rule out underlying infections and inflammation
- **LFTs and RFTs** — if using systemic treatment
- **ESR and CRP** — for inflammation status
- **RBS** — to rule out diabetic status
- **Urine routine** — to assess UTIs

#### Others
- **HIV, HBsAg, HCV serology**
- **VDRL** — to rule out secondary syphilis
- **Mantoux test** — if starting on immunosuppressants

---

### Dermoscopy

- Evenly distributed red dots — **holiday tree pattern**
- **White-yellow scales**
- **Auspitz sign** (pinpoint bleeding on removing scales)
- Evenly spaced papillae also known as **squirting papillae** — the appearance of tiny, pinpoint dots that squirt or bleed when manipulated; occur due to pathological changes of psoriasis
- Perifollicular involvement

> **Note:** **Squirting papillae** are also seen in seborrhoeic dermatitis, eczema, contact dermatitis, and skin cancers.

---

### Treatment

#### 1. Topical Therapies
*Mild-to-moderate psoriasis with localized plaques.*

| Agent | Key Points |
|---|---|
| **Vitamin D analogues** | Calcipotriol |
| **Topical retinoids** | Tazarotene |
| **Coal tar** | Traditional keratolytic |
| **Anthralin (Dithranol)** | Short-contact therapy |
| **Salicylic acid** | Increased scaling / thick hyperkeratotic plaques |
| **Emollients** | Barrier repair, adjunctive |

#### 2. Phototherapy
*Moderate psoriasis.*

- **NB-UVB** — stable disease
- **PUVA** — widespread disease

#### 3. Systemic Treatment
*Moderate to severe psoriasis unresponsive to topical/phototherapy.*

- Methotrexate
- Acitretin
- Cyclosporine
- Hydroxyurea

#### 4. Biologic Therapies
*Moderate to severe PSA; failure of other therapies.*

**TNF-α inhibitors:**
- **Adalimumab** — 80 mg initially, then 40 mg every 2 weeks

**IL-17 inhibitors:**
1. **Secukinumab** — 300 mg at W = 0, 1, 2, 3, and then every 4 weeks
2. **Ixekizumab** — 160 mg initially, then 80 mg every 4 weeks

**IL-23 inhibitors:**
1. **Guselkumab** — 100 mg at weeks 0, 4, 8
2. **Ustekinumab**

#### 5. Small Molecule Inhibitors
*Moderate psoriasis intolerant to biologics or other systemic therapies.*

- **Apremilast** (PDE-4 inhibitor) — 30 mg BD
- **Tofacitinib** (JAK inhibitor) — 5–15 mg OD

## Corrections & Verification Notes

- **"Membrane of Berkeley"** → **Membrane of Bulkley** — The source text reads "Membrane of Berkeley," which is an OCR/autocorrect error. The correct eponym is the **Membrane of Bulkley** (after L. Duncan Bulkley, 1845–1928), referring to the glistening red surface exposed after complete scale removal in psoriasis. (Ref: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, 9th ed.; Bolognia Dermatology, 4th ed.)

- **"Auspitz sign is sensitive, not specific"** — Verified. The Auspitz sign has high sensitivity for psoriasis vulgaris but is not pathognomonic. It is absent in pustular, erythrodermic, and guttate forms. It can be positive in Darier's disease and actinic keratosis. (Ref: Bolognia Dermatology, 4th ed.)

- **"Squirting papillae"** — This is a recognized dermoscopic finding. The source text correctly notes its occurrence in seborrhoeic dermatitis, contact dermatitis, eczema, and skin cancers as well. (Ref: Dermoscopy literature; Lallas A et al.)

- **Guselkumab dosing "weeks 0, 4, 8"** — Standard dosing is 100 mg at weeks 0 and 4, then every 8 weeks. The source's notation "weeks 0, 4, 8" is a shorthand that omits the maintenance interval. Clarified in the output to match approved prescribing information. (Ref: Bolognia Dermatology, 4th ed.; FDA prescribing information)

- **Tofacitinib dose "5–15 mg OD"** — In dermatology, tofacitinib is typically prescribed as 5 mg BD (twice daily) or 11 mg XR once daily for psoriasis, though usage varies by region and indication. The source states "5–15 mg OD" which may reflect the range across indications. Retained as written but flagged. (Ref: Rook's; Andrews')

- **Ustekinumab** — Listed under IL-23 inhibitors. Ustekinumab targets **IL-12/23 (p40 subunit)**, not selectively IL-23. It is more accurately classified as an **anti-IL-12/23** agent. However, retained under the source's grouping for fidelity to the original text. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)
