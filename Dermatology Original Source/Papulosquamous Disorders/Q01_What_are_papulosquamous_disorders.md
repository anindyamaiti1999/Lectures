# Q1. What are papulosquamous disorders? Enumerate them.

**Chapter**: 10 — Disorders of Keratinization | **Source Page**: 155 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Definition

- Disorders wherein the primary lesion is a relatively **thin or flat-topped papule or plaque with scale**.
- **Crusting or lichenification is usually absent**.
- Individual papules or plaques are typically **well-demarcated** and normal skin is visible between each discrete papule or plaque.

> **Key Distinction (Rook's/Bolognia):** Papulosquamous disorders are characterized by **well-defined, scaly papules and plaques** — in contrast to eczematous (spongiotic) dermatoses, which typically have **poorly defined borders**, may show vesiculation, oozing, and crusting, and demonstrate spongiosis histologically. Chronic eczema can mimic papulosquamous morphology (scaling, lichenification), making clinical distinction challenging at times.

---

### Classification & Enumeration

#### 1. Psoriasis Group of Diseases
- Psoriasis vulgaris (and its clinical variants)
- Sebopsoriasis

#### 2. Parapsoriasis Group
- **Small plaque parapsoriasis** *(benign course)*
- **Large plaque parapsoriasis** *(⚠️ precancerous — risk of progression to Mycosis Fungoides / CTCL)*

> **Margin Note (Handwritten):** "Classic" is annotated next to the Psoriasis group; "Precancerous" is highlighted next to large plaque parapsoriasis — emphasizing the malignant transformation potential to cutaneous T-cell lymphoma (Mycosis Fungoides).

#### 3. Pityriasis Group
- **Pityriasis rubra pilaris** (PRP)
- **Pityriasis rosea**
- **Pityriasis lichenoides et varioliformis acuta** (PLEVA) and **Pityriasis lichenoides chronica** (PLC)
- **Pityriasis rotunda**

> **Margin Note (Handwritten):** "Pityriasis [fine scales]" — annotated as a group label, denoting that these conditions share the feature of fine, bran-like scaling.

#### 4. Lichenoid Disorders
- **Lichen planus**
- **Lichenoid dermatitis** (Lichen planus-like eruptions)
- *Lichen nitidus* *(per Bolognia classification)*
- *Lichen striatus* *(per Bolognia classification)*

#### 5. Dermatitis / Chronic Eczema Spectrum
- Atopic dermatitis
- Allergic contact dermatitis (ACD)
- Irritant contact dermatitis (ICD)
- **ABCD** — Airborne Contact Dermatitis
- Photodermatoses
- Eczemas (chronic lichenified eczema may clinically overlap with papulosquamous morphology)

> **Margin Note (Handwritten):** "Dermatitis / Chronic Eczema Spectrum" is bracketed and labeled — indicating these are not classic papulosquamous disorders but are grouped here due to morphological overlap when chronic.

#### 6. Miscellaneous
- **Granular parakeratosis**
- **Erythroderma** (exfoliative dermatitis — may represent end-stage of multiple papulosquamous conditions)

> **Margin Note (Handwritten):** "Miscellaneous" is bracketed next to Granular parakeratosis and Erythroderma.

## Corrections & Verification Notes

- **"Lichen planus and lichenoid dermatitis"** — The source groups these together. Per **Bolognia (4th ed., Ch. 11)**, the broader papulosquamous classification also includes **Lichen nitidus** and **Lichen striatus** as distinct lichenoid papulosquamous entities. These have been added to the classification above for completeness.
- **"ABCD"** — Confirmed as **Airborne Contact Dermatitis** (per *Rook's* and *Contact Dermatitis* literature). This is a pattern of contact dermatitis caused by airborne allergens (e.g., *Parthenium hysterophorus* pollen, cement dust, epoxy resins) affecting exposed sites. Not to be confused with Atopic dermatitis (AD), which is listed separately.
- **Large plaque parapsoriasis → Mycosis Fungoides** — Per **Rook's (10th ed.)** and **Bolognia**: Large plaque parapsoriasis (plaques >5 cm) has a ~10–30% risk of progression to Mycosis Fungoides (CTCL). This is correctly flagged as "precancerous" in the handwritten annotation. Small plaque parapsoriasis (<5 cm) is considered benign with negligible transformation risk.
- **Pityriasis rotunda** — A rare condition included in the pityriasis group. Per **Bolognia**, it presents as perfectly circular, scaly, hyperpigmented or hypopigmented patches. More commonly reported in Japanese, South African, and West Indian populations.
