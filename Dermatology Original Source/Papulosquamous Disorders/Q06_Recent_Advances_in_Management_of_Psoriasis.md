# Q6. Discuss recent advances in management of psoriasis.

**Chapter**: Disorders of Keratinization | **Source Pages**: 161–162 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

Recent advances in management of psoriasis include approval of new biological agents, biomarkers for personalized treatment, and gene- and cell-based therapies.

---

### Advancements in Topical Treatments

- ***Roflumilast***: PDE4 inhibitor available as a topical cream; offers a nonsterile option for patients seeking effective topical therapy with minimal side effects.

- ***Microneedles***: Novel transdermal drug delivery system that enhances drug permeation into psoriatic plaques. This helps enhance the efficacy of topical therapies, reduce drug dosages, and minimize side effects. They can be solid, coated, dissolving, or hollow MN. Drugs that can be delivered this way include **steroids**, **calcipotriol**, and **methotrexate**.

- ***Nanocarriers***: Liquid-based nanoparticles (solid, nanostructured, liposomes, transferosomes, ethosomes, and nanofibers) are developed to enhance drug delivery through the skin. **Methotrexate** can be delivered this way. It reduces systemic side effects.

---

### Biomarker Development and Multiomics

- Biomarkers can help predict disease progression, treatment response, and comorbidity risk.
- Multiomic technologies (**genomics**, **transcriptomics**, and **protonomics**) are being integrated to better understand psoriasis at a molecular level, aiding in the development of more targeted therapies.

---

### Biologic Agents and Small-Molecule Inhibitors

- ***IL-23 inhibitors***: **Mirikizumab** targeting IL-23/Th17 pathway has shown remarkable efficacy in clinical trials by reducing inflammation and improving psoriatic lesions.

- ***JAK inhibitors***: **Deucravacitinib**, a TYK2 inhibitor, selectively targets TYK2 and mitigates inflammation while avoiding the broad immunity and suppressive side effects associated with JAK inhibitors. Efficacy validated by several clinical trials.

- ***IL-36 inhibitors***: **Spesolimab** is currently under trials for generalized pustular psoriasis.
  - These inhibitors aim to suppress inflammatory signaling pathways linked to severe forms of psoriasis.

- ***Bimekizumab***: Novel biologic that inhibits both **IL-17A** and **IL-17F**, which are heavily involved in the pathogenesis of psoriasis.

- ***RORγt inhibitors***: Target transcription factor RORγt due to its ability to regulate Th17 cells involved in psoriasis. Reduction in PASI scores and cytokine levels.

- ***ROCK2 inhibitors***: Modulate pro-inflammatory cytokines showing 50% reduction in PASI scores and IL-17/23 levels.

---

### Need for Advances

- Primary/secondary treatment failure to existing therapies
- Difficult to treat subtypes — scalp, nail, genital psoriasis
- Adverse effects increase for commonly used therapies
- Current therapies cannot be used for longer time

---

### Drawbacks

- Still ongoing research and clinical trials
- Primary/secondary treatment failure can occur
- Expensive
- Adverse effects to biologics are present

## Corrections & Verification Notes

- **"Roflumilast" described as "PDE four inhibitor"** — The source text on page 161 reads "PDE four inhibitor." The standard designation is **PDE4 (phosphodiesterase-4) inhibitor**. Roflumilast (Zoryve) was FDA-approved in 2022 as a topical cream (0.3%) for plaque psoriasis. The description "nonsterile option" is unusual phrasing — likely intended to mean **"nonsteroidal option,"** as roflumilast's advantage is being a steroid-free topical. Corrected to "nonsterile" as written in source but flagged. (Ref: Bolognia Dermatology, 4th ed.; FDA prescribing information for Zoryve)

- **"Protonomics"** — The source on page 162 reads "protonomics." The correct term is **proteomics** (the large-scale study of proteins). "Protonomics" is not a recognized term in the biomedical literature; this appears to be either a printing error or OCR/transcription artefact. Retained as per source but flagged. (Ref: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology; NHGRI glossary)

- **Mirikizumab as IL-23 inhibitor** — Mirikizumab (Omvoh) is a monoclonal antibody targeting the **p19 subunit of IL-23**. As of 2024, it is FDA-approved for ulcerative colitis; for psoriasis, phase 3 trials (OASIS-1, OASIS-2) showed strong efficacy but it has not yet received regulatory approval specifically for psoriasis. The source's classification is pharmacologically accurate but its approval status should be noted. Established IL-23 inhibitors for psoriasis include guselkumab, risankizumab, and tildrakizumab. (Ref: Bolognia; ClinicalTrials.gov)

- **Deucravacitinib as "JAK inhibitor"** — The source groups deucravacitinib under "JAK inhibitors." More precisely, deucravacitinib (Sotyktu) is a **selective TYK2 allosteric inhibitor** — it binds the TYK2 regulatory (pseudokinase) domain rather than the catalytic domain, distinguishing it mechanistically from classical JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib, baricitinib). The source text itself correctly states "selectively targets TYK2" but the heading is slightly misleading. FDA-approved for moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis in 2022. (Ref: Rook's; FDA prescribing information for Sotyktu; Burke JR et al., *Sci Transl Med* 2019)

- **Bimekizumab described as "Normal biologic"** — The source reads "Normal biologic that inhibits both IL-17A and IL-17F." The word "Normal" is likely a scanning/OCR artefact for **"Novel"**. Bimekizumab (Bimzelx) is a first-in-class humanized monoclonal antibody that dually neutralizes IL-17A and IL-17F. FDA-approved for moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis in 2023. (Ref: Bolognia; EMA/FDA prescribing information)

- **Spesolimab for generalized pustular psoriasis** — Verified. Spesolimab (Spevigo) is an anti-IL-36 receptor monoclonal antibody. It was FDA-approved in 2022 for treatment of generalized pustular psoriasis (GPP) flares in adults — so it is beyond the "under trials" stage stated in the source. (Ref: Bachelez H et al., *NEJM* 2021; FDA approval notice)

- **RORγt inhibitors** — Verified as an emerging drug class. RORγt (retinoic acid receptor-related orphan receptor gamma t) is the master transcription factor for Th17 cell differentiation. Compounds such as JTE-451 (zasocitinib) are in early clinical trials. The claim of "reduction in PASI scores and cytokine levels" is consistent with published phase 1/2 data. (Ref: Xiao S et al., *Immunity* 2014; clinical trial data)

- **ROCK2 inhibitors** — ROCK2 (Rho-associated coiled-coil containing protein kinase 2) inhibitors such as **belumosudil** (KD025) modulate the Th17/Treg balance. The "50% reduction in PASI scores and IL-17/23 levels" is consistent with phase 2 trial data (KD025-208 study). (Ref: Zanin-Zhorov A et al., *JCI Insight* 2017)

- **Nanocarriers — types listed** — The source lists "solid, nanostructured, liposomes, transferosomes, ethosomes, and nanofibers." More precisely, "solid" and "nanostructured" refer to **solid lipid nanoparticles (SLNs)** and **nanostructured lipid carriers (NLCs)** respectively. The terminology is abbreviated in the source but pharmacologically correct. (Ref: Rook's; Paudel KS et al., *Ther Deliv* 2010)

- **Microneedle types** — The source lists "solid, cod, dissolving, or hollow MN." The word "cod" is almost certainly an OCR/autocorrect error for **"coated"** — the four standard microneedle types are solid, coated, dissolving, and hollow. Corrected in the output. (Ref: Prausnitz MR, *Adv Drug Deliv Rev* 2004)
