# Q9. Describe isomorphic response.

**Chapter**: Disorders of Keratinization | **Source Pages**: 164–165 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Definition

The **Koebner's (isomorphic) phenomenon** or **isomorphic response** is one of the most well-known phenomena named after **Dr. Heinrich Koebner**, who first described it in psoriasis, where he observed that patients developed new lesions at sites of excoriations, horse bites, or tattoos (trauma).

It refers to the development of **isomorphic pathological lesions in traumatized uninvolved skin** of patients, who have cutaneous disease, and these lesions are **clinically and histopathologically identical** to those in diseased skin.

---

### Classification

#### 1. True Isomorphic Phenomenon / True Koebner's

Diseases that **reproducibly** demonstrate the isomorphic response at trauma sites:

- Psoriasis
- Lichen planus
- Vitiligo

#### 2. Pseudo-Isomorphic Phenomenon (Infectious Diseases)

Diseases where the infectious agent is **inoculated** at the trauma site, producing a lesion by direct inoculation rather than a true immunological Koebner response:

- Warts
- Molluscum contagiosum
- Behçet's disease
- Pyoderma gangrenosum

> **Correction Note:** The source lists Behçet's disease and pyoderma gangrenosum under the "pseudo-isomorphic phenomenon (infectious diseases)" category. However, Behçet's and PG demonstrate **pathergy** (an exaggerated skin-injury response), which is distinct from the infectious pseudo-Koebner (warts, molluscum). Standard references (Rook's, Bolognia) classify Behçet's and PG under **pathergy** rather than pseudo-Koebner. They are listed here as per the source, with this clarification.

#### 3. Occasionally Occurring Isomorphic Phenomenon

Diseases that **occasionally** localize to the site of trauma (not reproducible in all patients):

- Darier's disease
- Erythema multiforme
- Hailey–Hailey disease
- Kyrle's disease
- Pellagra
- Perforating folliculitis

#### 4. Questionable Isomorphic Phenomenon

Diseases where the relationship to trauma is **debatable or inconsistent**:

- Anaphylactoid purpura
- Bullous pemphigoid (BP)
- Dermatitis herpetiformis (DH)
- Discoid lupus erythematosus (DLE)
- Eczema
- Lichen amyloidosis / lichen nitidus
- Pityriasis rubra pilaris
- Urticaria pigmentosa
- Morphea

---

### Causes of Koebner's Phenomenon

1. **Mechanical / thermal trauma** — excoriations, burns, surgical wounds, friction
2. **Dermatoses** — carbuncles, furuncles, dermatophytosis, scabies
3. **Allergic / irritant reactions** — BCG, hair spray, hair tints, tattoos, etc.

---

### Pathogenesis

Growing evidence suggests **immunological factors** are responsible following trauma.

**In psoriasis:**
- Response varies between **11–75%** of patients
- More severe injury leads to more extensive lesions

#### Molecular Pathogenesis Flowchart

```
Skin injury
      ↓
AMP (LL-37) produced by keratinocytes / infiltrating neutrophils
      ↓
Form complexes with nucleic acids of dying cells
      ↓
Complexes transported into intracellular compartments
      ↓
Activation of plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs) via TLR7 and TLR9
      ↓
Release of IFN-α and IFN-β
      ↓
Trigger dendritic cells
      ↓
Stimulate autoreactive T cells
      ↓
Produce Th-17 cells
      ↓
IL-17, IL-22
```

#### Positive Feedback Loop

- Induces expression of **AMPs** in keratinocytes
- **Inhibit terminal differentiation** of keratinocytes
- Induce **hyperproliferation** of keratinocytes

> This creates a **self-sustaining inflammatory loop** — IL-17/IL-22 upregulate AMPs, which restart the LL-37 → pDC → Th17 cascade, explaining why Koebnerized lesions persist and expand.

---

### The "Zone of Local Immunity"

- The **involute central portion** of a psoriatic plaque is **refractory** to experimental induction of psoriasis
- This central zone of the plaque cannot be Koebnerized — it behaves as an immunologically "spent" area

---

### All-or-None Phenomenon

- If a patient reacts to **one** experimental stimulus, he will react to **all** stimuli applied
- Conversely, lack of a positive response to a known Koebner-inducing stimulus **credits failure to other stimuli** in the series
- This means the Koebner response reflects the **systemic immunological state** of the patient at that time, not a property of the specific stimulus

---

### Reverse Koebner

- An area of **psoriasis clears following injury** at that site
- Also described in vitiligo: **spontaneous repigmentation** of vitiligo patches distant from autologous skin graft sites

---

### Clinical Significance

1. **Clinical diagnosis** — presence of Koebnerization supports diagnosis of psoriasis, LP, and vitiligo
2. **Activity of disease** — active Koebnerization indicates an **active, unstable phase** of the disease
3. **Guides clinician** to:
   - **Avoid physical/chemical irritants** in active disease (e.g., avoid surgical procedures, aggressive topical therapies, tattoos)
   - **Treat underlying concurrent disease** that may be triggering skin trauma

## Corrections & Verification Notes

- **"Farm complexes with nucleic acids of dying cells"** → **"Form complexes with nucleic acids of dying cells"** — OCR/scan error; "Farm" is a misread of "Form." LL-37 (cathelicidin) binds self-DNA and self-RNA released from dying cells to form complexes that activate innate immunity. (Ref: Lande R et al., *Nature* 2007; Rook's)

- **"TLR_x 7 and"** → **"TLR7 and TLR9"** — The source text reads "TLRₓ 7 and" with the second TLR number cut off or garbled. The correct pathway is activation of **TLR7** (by LL-37–RNA complexes) and **TLR9** (by LL-37–DNA complexes) on plasmacytoid dendritic cells. (Ref: Ganguly D et al., *J Exp Med* 2009; Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Farm complexes" garble** — The source flowchart reads "Farm complexes with nucleic acids of dying cells." Corrected to **"Form complexes"** — LL-37 acts as a vehicle to transport self-nucleic acids into endosomal compartments of pDCs. (Ref: Lande R et al., *Nature* 2007)

- **"auto reactive T cells"** → **"autoreactive T cells"** — Compound adjective, should be written as one word per standard medical convention. (Ref: Grammar/formatting)

- **"intra cellular compartments"** → **"intracellular compartments"** — Same compound-word correction. (Ref: Grammar/formatting)

- **"hyper proliferation"** → **"hyperproliferation"** — Standard compound term. (Ref: Grammar/formatting)

- **Behçet's disease and pyoderma gangrenosum classified under "pseudo-isomorphic phenomenon (infectious diseases)"** — This classification is **partially misleading**. Behçet's disease and PG are classically associated with **pathergy** (an exaggerated inflammatory response to minor skin injury, such as a needle prick), which is immunologically distinct from the pseudo-Koebner phenomenon seen in infectious diseases (warts, molluscum) where the pathogen is directly inoculated. Rook's and Bolognia discuss pathergy as a separate entity. However, Boyd and Nelder (1990) in their original classification did group these conditions under pseudo-Koebner; the source follows this older classification. **Retained as per source with clarification.** (Ref: Boyd AS, Nelder KH. *J Am Acad Dermatol* 1990; Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Dr Heinrich Koebner's"** → **"Dr. Heinrich Koebner"** — Minor punctuation. Heinrich Koebner (1838–1904) was a German dermatologist who first described the phenomenon in 1876 in patients with psoriasis, lichen planus, and lichen nitidus after trauma from horse bites, tattoos, and excoriations. (Ref: Bolognia; Andrews')

- **"Produce Th-17" in the flowchart** — The source uses "Th-17." Standard nomenclature is **Th17** (without hyphen) as per IUIS nomenclature. Corrected in output. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Koebner inducing stimulus credits failure to other stimuli"** — The source phrasing is awkward. The intended meaning is: if a patient fails to Koebnerize to a known stimulus, this **predicts** failure to Koebnerize to other stimuli in the same series of experiments — i.e., the all-or-none nature is tied to the patient's disease state, not the stimulus type. **Clarified in output.** (Ref: Eyre RW, Krueger GG. *Br J Dermatol* 1982)

- **Classification of Koebner phenomenon into 4 categories** — Verified. This four-tiered classification (true, pseudo, occasional, questionable) was proposed by **Boyd and Nelder (1990)** and remains the most widely cited framework. (Ref: Boyd AS, Nelder KH. *J Am Acad Dermatol* 1990; Rook's 9th ed.)

- **"Response varies between 11–75%"** — Verified. The wide range reflects variability across studies and depends on the phase of disease activity. The Koebner phenomenon is more likely during active, unstable phases of psoriasis. Estimates range from 24–51% in most clinical series (Weiss G et al., 2002), with experimental studies reporting up to 75%. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **Reverse Koebner** — Verified. Also termed **Renbök phenomenon** (Koebner spelled backwards). In psoriasis, it refers to clearing of psoriatic plaques at sites of injury (e.g., surgical scars). In vitiligo, it refers to spontaneous repigmentation at distant sites following autologous grafting. Both phenomena are well-documented. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

## Integrated Margin Notes

- **Page 164 (bottom right, Q9 introduction):** The question "Describe isomorphic response" begins at the bottom of page 164, with only the definition visible. The main body of the answer continues onto page 165. The definition explicitly names Dr. Heinrich Koebner and links the phenomenon to trauma (excoriations, horse bites, tattoos) — these are the three classic triggers cited in Koebner's original 1876 report and are high-yield for viva examinations.

- **Page 165 (left column, Classification):** The four-tier classification (true → pseudo → occasional → questionable) is a frequently examined classification scheme. The three diseases under "True Koebner's" — **psoriasis, lichen planus, and vitiligo** — form the classic triad and are the most important to memorize. For exam purposes, students should note that **vitiligo** is the only non-inflammatory disease in the true Koebner group, underscoring that the phenomenon is not exclusively inflammatory.

- **Page 165 (left column, "Pseudo isomorphic phenomenon — infectious diseases"):** The grouping of warts and molluscum here makes pathogenic sense (direct viral inoculation at wound sites). The inclusion of Behçet's and PG is a source-specific classification choice — in examinations, if asked about **pathergy**, candidates should mention Behçet's and PG; if asked about **pseudo-Koebner**, they should mention warts and molluscum, adding Behçet's/PG only if referencing the Boyd–Nelder classification.

- **Page 165 (left column, "Occasionally occurring" list):** This list includes Darier's disease, Hailey–Hailey disease, and Kyrle's disease — all of which are covered later in this chapter (Q36, Q37, Q35 respectively). The appearance of these diseases under "occasional Koebner" is a useful cross-linking point for comprehensive chapter revision.

- **Page 165 (left column, "Questionable" list):** Morphea at the bottom of this list is noteworthy — it is increasingly recognized that morphea can genuinely Koebnerize (e.g., post-radiation morphea, post-surgical morphea), and some modern references upgrade it from "questionable" to "occasional." This may be a point of controversy in examinations.

- **Page 165 (left column, "Cause of Koebner's phenomenon"):** The three categories (mechanical/thermal, dermatoses, allergic/irritant) provide a practical framework for clinical advice. The inclusion of **BCG** under allergic/irritant triggers is clinically relevant — BCG vaccination sites can trigger psoriasis in predisposed individuals, which is particularly important in countries with universal BCG vaccination programs.

- **Page 165 (right column, molecular pathogenesis flowchart):** This flowchart is a high-yield exam diagram. The key pathway is: **LL-37 → pDC activation (TLR7/9) → IFN-α/β → DC maturation → Th17 → IL-17/IL-22 → keratinocyte hyperproliferation + AMP production (positive feedback)**. This is identical to the innate immune activation pathway in psoriasis pathogenesis (covered in Q2), reinforcing that Koebnerization is essentially a **localized re-initiation of the psoriatic inflammatory cascade** at the trauma site.

- **Page 165 (right column, "Zone of local immunity"):** This concept — that the central involute portion of a plaque is refractory to Koebnerization — explains why experimental Koebner induction studies use **uninvolved skin** and not active plaques. It suggests that the plaque center has an immunosuppressive microenvironment, possibly related to regulatory T cells or resolution mediators.

- **Page 165 (right column, "All or none phenomenon"):** This is a critical concept for examinations. It means that Koebner positivity or negativity reflects the patient's **systemic disease activity** at the time of trauma, not the nature of the trauma itself. Clinically, this translates to: a patient who Koebnerizes from one insult will Koebnerize from all insults during that active phase.

- **Page 165 (right column, "Reverse Koebner"):** The two examples given — psoriasis clearing at injury sites and vitiligo repigmenting distant from graft sites — illustrate the spectrum of the phenomenon. The reverse Koebner in vitiligo is therapeutically exploited in **autologous melanocyte transplantation** techniques.

- **Page 165 (right column, "Clinical significance"):** The three points (diagnosis, disease activity, avoidance of irritants) summarize the practical applications. Point 3 is particularly important for surgical dermatology: elective procedures should be deferred during active, Koebner-positive phases of psoriasis to avoid surgical-site flares.
