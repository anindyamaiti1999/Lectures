# Q16. Classify ichthyoses and discuss syndromes associated with ichthyosis.

**Chapter**: Disorders of Keratinization | **Source Pages**: 173–174 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Definition

- **Ichthys** — fish; referring to similarity to **fish scales** in this group of disorders
- The ichthyoses are a heterogeneous group of disorders of cornification characterized by **generalized scaling of the skin**
- Classification is based on the **2009 First Ichthyosis Consensus Conference** (Oji et al., 2010)

---

### Classification Overview

The ichthyoses are broadly classified into:

1. **Acquired ichthyosis**
2. **Inherited ichthyosis**
   - **Nonsyndromic** forms
   - **Syndromic** forms

---

### Acquired Ichthyosis

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Endocrine / Metabolic disorders** | Malnutrition, malabsorptive disorders, sarcoidosis, renal failure, hyperparathyroidism, diabetes |
| **Lymphoproliferative and solid malignancies** | Hodgkin lymphoma (most classic), non-Hodgkin lymphoma, solid tumors |
| **Infections** | Leprosy, HIV |
| **Drugs** | Retinoids, clofazimine, HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors (statins), hydroxyurea |
| **Connective tissue diseases** | CTD (SLE, dermatomyositis) |

---

### Inherited Ichthyoses: Nonsyndromic

#### 1. Common Ichthyosis

- **Ichthyosis vulgaris** (IV) — the most common ichthyosis; AD; *FLG* (filaggrin) mutations
- **Recessive X-linked ichthyosis** (XLI) — STS (steroid sulfatase) deficiency; Xp22.31

#### 2. Autosomal Recessive Congenital Ichthyosis (ARCI)

- **Harlequin ichthyosis** (HI) — ABCA12; most severe form
- **Congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma** (CIE) — TGM1, ALOX12B, ALOXE3, and others
- **Lamellar ichthyosis** (LI) — TGM1 (~90%); dark plate-like scales
- **Self-improving collodion ichthyosis** — presents as collodion baby, resolves spontaneously
- **Acral self-healing collodion baby** — limited acral distribution with spontaneous resolution
- **Bathing suit ichthyosis** — scaling confined to trunk ("bathing suit" distribution), spares extremities; TGM1 temperature-sensitive mutations

#### 3. Keratinopathic Ichthyosis

- **Epidermolytic ichthyosis** (EI) — KRT1 / KRT10
- **Superficial epidermolytic ichthyosis** — KRT2
- **Annular epidermolytic ichthyosis** — KRT1 / KRT10 (migratory annular polycyclic plaques)
- **Congenital reticular ichthyosiform erythroderma** — KRT10
- **Ichthyosis Curth–Macklin** — KRT1 (distinctive perinuclear shell of keratin on EM)
- **Autosomal recessive epidermolytic ichthyosis** — KRT10
- **Epidermolytic nevi** — postzygotic mosaic KRT1 / KRT10 mutations

#### 4. Others

- **Loricrin keratoderma** — *LOR* mutations; transgradient PPK with ichthyosis
- **Erythrokeratoderma variabilis** — GJB3 / GJB4 / GJA1 (connexin mutations)
- **Inflammatory peeling skin disease** — CDSN (corneodesmosin) mutations
- **Keratosis linearis with ichthyosis congenita and keratoderma (KLICK)** — POMP mutations

---

### Inherited Ichthyoses: Syndromic Forms

#### 1. X-Linked Ichthyosis Syndromes

- **Recessive X-linked ichthyosis** (XLI) — STS deficiency; contiguous gene syndromes (Kallmann, chondrodysplasia punctata)
- **Ichthyosis follicularis / atrichia / photophobia syndromes (IFAP)** — MBTPS2 mutations; follicular ichthyosis, universal atrichia, photophobia
- **Conradi–Hünermann–Happle syndrome** — EBP (emopamil-binding protein); X-linked dominant chondrodysplasia punctata with ichthyosis
- **Congenital hemidysplasia with ichthyosiform nevus and limb defects (CHILD) syndrome** — NSDHL mutations; strict lateralization

#### 2. Autosomal Syndromes

##### A. With Prominent Hair Abnormalities

- ***Netherton syndrome*** — SPINK5 / LEKTI; ichthyosis linearis circumflexa (ILC), trichorrhexis invaginata (bamboo hair), atopy / hyper-IgE
- **Congenital and noncongenital trichothiodystrophy** — ERCC2 (XPD), ERCC3 (XPB); tiger-tail pattern on polarized microscopy; photosensitive and non-photosensitive forms
- **Ichthyosis / hypotrichosis syndromes** — various loci; ichthyosis with sparse hair
- **Ichthyosis / hypotrichosis / sclerosing cholangitis syndrome** — CLDN1 (claudin-1) mutations; neonatal ichthyosis with progressive liver disease

##### B. With Prominent Neurological Signs

- ***Sjögren–Larsson syndrome*** (SLS) — ALDH3A2 (fatty aldehyde dehydrogenase); triad of ichthyosis, spastic diplegia/quadriplegia, intellectual disability; Glistening white dots on macular OCT
- ***Refsum disease*** — PHYH (phytanoyl-CoA hydroxylase) or PEX7; phytanic acid accumulation; tetrad of ichthyosis, retinitis pigmentosa (RP), peripheral neuropathy, cerebellar ataxia
- **MR / enteropathy / deafness / neuropathy / ichthyosis / keratoderma syndrome (MEDNIK)** — AP1S1 mutations; multi-system syndromic ichthyosis

##### C. Fatal Course

- **Gaucher syndrome Type II** — GBA (glucocerebrosidase); collodion membrane at birth, hepatosplenomegaly, severe neurodegeneration, fatal in infancy
- **Multiple sulfatase deficiency** — SUMF1 (sulfatase-modifying factor 1); combined features of metachromatic leukodystrophy + mucopolysaccharidosis + ichthyosis
- **CEDNIK syndrome** — SNAP29; **CE**rebral **D**ysgenesis, **N**europathy, **I**chthyosis, palmoplantar **K**eratoderma (PPK)

##### D. Others

- **KID syndrome** — GJB2 (connexin 26); **K**eratitis, **I**chthyosis, **D**eafness (sensorineural hearing loss)
- **Neutral lipid storage disease with ichthyosis** — ABHD5 (Chanarin–Dorfman syndrome); lipid vacuoles in leukocytes (Jordans anomaly)
- **Ichthyosis prematurity syndrome** — SLC27A4 (FATP4); premature birth, thick caseous skin coating, resolves to mild ichthyosis

## Corrections & Verification Notes

- **"Recessive X-linked IS" (p. 173, Syndromic forms)** → **Correction:** The source lists "Recessive X-linked IS" under the syndromic X-linked ichthyosis syndromes. The abbreviation "IS" likely stands for "ichthyosis syndrome." The correct and complete name is **X-linked recessive ichthyosis** (XLI) caused by **STS (steroid sulfatase)** deficiency at **Xp22.31**. The syndromic aspects refer to the **contiguous gene deletion syndromes** that can accompany XLI when larger deletions at Xp22.3 involve adjacent genes — including *KAL1* (Kallmann syndrome with anosmia), *ARSE* (X-linked chondrodysplasia punctata), and *NLGN4X* (intellectual disability). The isolated form is nonsyndromic; the contiguous gene deletion form is syndromic. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Sjogren Larson" (p. 174, Neurological syndromes)** → **Correction:** The source spells this as "Sjogren Larson syndrome." The correct spelling is ***Sjögren–Larsson syndrome*** (SLS) — named after Swedish physicians Karl Gustaf Torsten Sjögren and Tage Konrad Leopold Larsson. The gene is **ALDH3A2** encoding **fatty aldehyde dehydrogenase (FALDH)**, located at 17p11.2. Deficiency leads to accumulation of fatty alcohols and aldehydes. The classic triad is: (1) **congenital ichthyosis** (generalized, pruritic), (2) **spastic diplegia or quadriplegia**, and (3) **intellectual disability**. Pathognomonic ophthalmologic finding: **glistening white dots** (crystalline macular dystrophy) on fundoscopy/OCT. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"CEDNIK syndrome cerebral dysgenesis/neuropathy ichthyosis/PPK" (p. 174, Fatal course)** → **Verification:** CEDNIK is correctly placed in the "fatal course" category. The acronym expands to **CE**rebral **D**ysgenesis, **N**europathy, **I**chthyosis, palmoplantar **K**eratoderma. The causative gene is **SNAP29** (synaptosomal-associated protein 29), which encodes a SNARE complex protein essential for lamellar body secretion and epidermal barrier formation, as well as synaptic vesicle fusion in neurons. Mutations result in defective lamellar body exocytosis → ichthyosis, and defective neuronal vesicle trafficking → cerebral dysgenesis and neuropathy. Most patients succumb in early childhood. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"HMG-COA" (p. 173, Acquired ichthyosis — Drugs)** → **Correction:** The source writes "HMG-COA" as a drug category. This refers to **HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors** (statins). The correct abbreviation is **HMG-CoA** (3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl coenzyme A). Statins (e.g., lovastatin, atorvastatin) can cause acquired ichthyosis by interfering with cholesterol synthesis in the epidermis, as cholesterol is a critical component of the intercellular lipid lamellae of the stratum corneum. This mechanism is directly relevant to the mevalonate pathway — the same pathway implicated in porokeratosis (Q34). (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"CTD" (p. 173, Acquired ichthyosis)** → **Clarification:** The source lists "CTD" under acquired ichthyosis causes without expansion. This abbreviation stands for **connective tissue diseases** — principally **SLE** (systemic lupus erythematosus) and **dermatomyositis**. Acquired ichthyosis in CTD is thought to be mediated by circulating autoantibodies or immune complexes affecting epidermal differentiation. It should be distinguished from the ichthyosis of hypothyroidism (dry, rough skin from decreased sebaceous and eccrine activity) and the "parchment-like" skin of scleroderma. (Ref: Rook's; Andrews')

- **"MR/enteropathy/deafness/neuropathy/ichthyosis/keratoderma syndrome (MEDNIK)" (p. 174, Neurological syndromes)** → **Verification:** The "MR" in MEDNIK stands for **M**ental **R**etardation (now preferably termed intellectual disability). The gene is **AP1S1** (adaptor protein complex 1, sigma-1 subunit), involved in vesicular trafficking. MEDNIK patients present with ichthyosis, erythroderma, sensorineural deafness, peripheral neuropathy, enteropathy (copper metabolism defect similar to Menkes disease), and intellectual disability. This is an extremely rare autosomal recessive condition. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Ichthyosis Curth Macklin" (p. 173, Keratinopathic ichthyosis list)** → **Correction:** The correct eponym uses an **en-dash**: ***Ichthyosis Curth–Macklin***. Named after Henrietta Curth and Madge Thurlow Macklin. The causative gene is **KRT1**. It is distinctive among keratinopathic ichthyoses because electron microscopy shows a unique **perinuclear shell of aggregated keratin filaments** (binucleate-like pattern), as opposed to the cytolysis and clumping seen in standard epidermolytic ichthyosis. Clinically presents with severe, spiny hyperkeratosis. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **Source lists "Congenital reticular ichthyosiform erythroderma" under keratinopathic ichthyosis (p. 173)** → **Verification:** This is also known as **ichthyosis with confetti** — caused by dominant-negative KRT10 mutations with revertant mosaicism. Patients are born with generalized erythroderma and ichthyosiform scaling, but progressively develop hundreds of small confetti-like spots of normal skin (revertant patches) due to mitotic recombination correcting the heterozygous KRT10 mutation in individual keratinocyte clones. The "confetti" spots increase with age and are pathognomonic. This is a high-yield exam entity. (Ref: Bolognia; Rook's)

## Integrated Margin Notes

- **Page 173 (bottom left, Q16 question and introduction):** Q16 begins at the bottom of page 173, immediately below the Q15 treatment section. The question text is presented in large bold font: "Classify ichthyoses and discuss syndromes associated with ichthyosis." The answer opens with the etymological definition: "Ichthys — fish, referring to similarity to fish scales in this group of disorders." This serves as a quick recall mnemonic. The classification immediately proceeds to the 2009 consensus framework, splitting ichthyoses into acquired and inherited (nonsyndromic + syndromic) — this is the current standard classification and supersedes older schemes (e.g., Frost & Van Scott 1966, Williams & Elias 2003).

- **Page 173 (right column, Acquired ichthyosis):** The acquired ichthyosis classification lists 5 etiological categories. The most classic and highest-yield association is **acquired ichthyosis with Hodgkin lymphoma** — this is one of the most frequently tested paraneoplastic dermatoses. The drug-induced category is clinically important: **nicotinic acid (niacin)**, **retinoids** (paradoxically, despite treating ichthyoses), **clofazimine**, **hydroxyurea**, and **statins** (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors) can all produce acquired ichthyotic scaling. The mechanism for statins involves cholesterol depletion in the epidermal lipid barrier.

- **Page 173 (right column, Nonsyndromic — Common ichthyosis):** The two "common" ichthyoses — **ichthyosis vulgaris (IV)** and **X-linked ichthyosis (XLI)** — are separated from the rarer forms. IV is the most common genetic ichthyosis (prevalence ~1:250) caused by loss-of-function mutations in *FLG* (filaggrin), resulting in reduced/absent keratohyalin granules. XLI (prevalence ~1:2,000–6,000 males) is caused by STS deficiency. Both are "common" but have fundamentally different genetics (AD semidominant vs X-linked recessive) and histopathology (decreased vs normal/increased granular layer).

- **Page 173 (right column, ARCI subtypes):** The ARCI group lists 6 entities, all autosomal recessive. The **self-improving collodion ichthyosis** and **acral self-healing collodion baby** are important because they represent the favorable end of the ARCI spectrum — unlike harlequin ichthyosis (most severe) and lamellar ichthyosis (lifelong severe scaling). **Bathing suit ichthyosis** is particularly notable: caused by temperature-sensitive TGM1 mutations that result in transglutaminase-1 enzyme inactivation at core body temperature (trunk) but retained function at cooler peripheral temperature (extremities) — hence the "bathing suit" distribution of scaling.

- **Page 173 (right column, Keratinopathic ichthyosis):** This group includes 7 entities, all caused by mutations in keratin genes (KRT1, KRT2, KRT10). The common thread is **epidermolytic hyperkeratosis** on histopathology — characterized by clumping of tonofilaments, cytolysis of suprabasal keratinocytes, and coarse keratohyalin granules. **Epidermolytic nevi** (postzygotic mosaic KRT1/KRT10 mutations) are listed separately because they are localized, but carry the important clinical implication that affected individuals can transmit generalized epidermolytic ichthyosis to offspring if gonadal mosaicism is present. This concept (type 2 segmental mosaicism / gonadal transmission risk) is a frequently tested genetics concept.

- **Page 173 (right column, Nonsyndromic — Others):** The "Others" category includes 4 entities that do not fit neatly into the common/ARCI/keratinopathic triad. **Erythrokeratoderma variabilis (EKV)** is detailed in Q21, and **KLICK** (**K**eratosis **L**inearis with **I**chthyosis **C**ongenita and **K**eratoderma) is a rare autosomal recessive condition caused by POMP mutations affecting proteasome maturation. **Inflammatory peeling skin disease** (CDSN mutations) presents with lifelong superficial skin peeling and is distinct from peeling skin syndrome type B (TGM5 mutations, acral distribution).

- **Page 173–174 (transition, Syndromic forms — X-linked):** The syndromic ichthyosis classification begins at the bottom of page 173 and continues through page 174. The X-linked syndromic group includes 4 entities. The **CHILD syndrome** (NSDHL) and **Conradi–Hünermann–Happle** (EBP) are both X-linked dominant conditions affecting cholesterol biosynthesis — they are typically lethal in males and present with strict unilateral (CHILD) or patterned (Blaschkoid) distribution in females. Both are part of the broader group of **X-linked dominant cholesterol biosynthesis disorders** involving the post-squalene pathway.

- **Page 174 (left column, Autosomal syndromes with hair abnormalities):** The hair-associated syndromic ichthyoses highlight the intimate relationship between keratinization and hair follicle biology. **Netherton syndrome** (Q23) is the most important of this group — the SPINK5/LEKTI deficiency leads to unchecked kallikrein (KLK5/7) activity, degrading desmoglein-1 (DSG-1) and corneodesmosin. **Trichothiodystrophy (TTD)** is notable for its overlap with xeroderma pigmentosum group D (XPD/ERCC2) — both are nucleotide excision repair (NER) disorders, but TTD patients do NOT have increased skin cancer risk (unlike XP), a classic exam distinction.

- **Page 174 (left column, Fatal course syndromes):** This subgroup contains 3 entities where ichthyosis is a presenting feature of a rapidly fatal metabolic or neurodegenerative disease. **Gaucher Type II** (acute neuronopathic) presents in the neonatal period with collodion membrane, hepatosplenomegaly, and brainstem dysfunction — death typically occurs by age 2. **Multiple sulfatase deficiency (Austin disease)** combines features of multiple lysosomal storage diseases because the SUMF1 gene is required to activate ALL sulfatase enzymes. **CEDNIK** (SNAP29) causes defective vesicular trafficking in both epidermis (lamellar body secretion) and neurons — this shared pathogenic mechanism explains the combined skin and neurological phenotype.

- **Page 174 (left column, Others — KID syndrome):** **KID syndrome** (keratitis, ichthyosis, deafness) is caused by **GJB2** (connexin 26) mutations — the same gene responsible for the most common form of nonsyndromic autosomal recessive sensorineural hearing loss (DFNB1). However, KID syndrome is caused by **gain-of-function** (dominant-negative) mutations in GJB2, as opposed to the **loss-of-function** mutations causing isolated deafness. Patients have a significantly elevated risk of **squamous cell carcinoma** (SCC), particularly of mucocutaneous junctions. This distinction (same gene, different mutation type, different disease) is a high-yield genetics concept.

- **Page 174 (left column, Overall Q16 structure):** Q16 provides the master classification framework for all ichthyoses — functioning as the "roadmap" question for Q17–Q24, which individually elaborate specific entities (EHK/harlequin, collodion baby, XLI, lamellar ichthyosis, EKV, CIE, Netherton, Refsum). This hierarchical question architecture (overview → detailed elaboration) mirrors the Q14→Q15–Q38 pattern. For exam preparation, this classification table should be the first item memorized, as it provides the organizational scaffold onto which all subsequent detail is attached.
