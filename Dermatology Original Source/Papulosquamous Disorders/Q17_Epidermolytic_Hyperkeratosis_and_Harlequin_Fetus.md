# Q17. Discuss epidermolytic hyperkeratosis and write a note on harlequin fetus.

**Chapter**: Disorders of Keratinization | **Source Pages**: 174–175 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

## Answer

### Part I: Epidermolytic Hyperkeratosis (EHK)

#### Synonyms

- Also known as **bullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma (BCIE)** or **bullous ichthyosis**
- Eponymously: *Brocq* (described the entity)
- Current preferred terminology (2009 consensus): **Epidermolytic ichthyosis (EI)**

#### Epidemiology

- Inheritance: **Autosomal dominant (AD)** with **complete penetrance**
- Rarely autosomal recessive (AR)

#### Pathogenesis

- Pathogenesis of epidermolytic ichthyosis involves **mutations in genes KRT1 and KRT10**
- These mutations impact **helical initiation and termination** in the **alpha-helix rod region** — perturbing keratin alignment, oligomerization, and filament assembly
- Weakens the **cytoskeleton** and compromises **mechanical strength and cellular integrity** of the epidermis — leading to **cytolysis** and **blistering**

#### Genotype–Phenotype Correlation

| Gene | Keratin | Pattern | Clinical Feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***KRT1*** | Keratin 1 | Expressed in palms and soles (suprabasal) | Associated with **severe palmoplantar keratoderma (PPK)** |
| ***KRT10*** | Keratin 10 | Not expressed in palms and soles | **Sparing of palms and soles** |

> **High-Yield Distinction**: KRT1 mutations → PPK present; KRT10 mutations → palms and soles spared. This genotype–phenotype correlation is a frequently tested point.

#### Clinical Features

- **Neonatal presentation**: Presents at birth with **erythroderma** characterized by widespread **denuded skin** with **erosions and peeling of skin**
- **Skin fragility and blistering** — improves with age
- **Hyperkeratosis in infancy**: As blistering subsides, progressive corrugated/ridged hyperkeratosis develops
- Patients periodically shed **large plates of superficial epidermis** revealing a tender **erythematous** base
- Ridges along skin lines — common in **flexures**
- Hyperkeratosis on **extensors** — forms a **cobblestone pattern**
- If severe PPK — **digital contractures**
- **Angular cheilitis**, **hair shaft encasement**
- **Pungent body odor** (secondary bacterial colonization of macerated hyperkeratotic skin)

#### Clinical Patterns

1. **Epidermolytic epidermal nevus** variant
2. Variants **with or without PPK**
3. **Cyclic ichthyoses** with epidermolytic hyperkeratosis
4. ***Annular epidermolytic ichthyosis***:
   - Epidermolytic epidermal nevi (mosaic epidermolytic ichthyosis)
   - Mosaic form of epidermolytic ichthyosis characterized by **unilateral or bilateral streaks** of hyperkeratosis leading to protruding **porcupine-like spines** — "**ichthyosis hystrix**"
   - Caused by **postzygotic mutation** of KRT1 and KRT10
   - If **gonadal cells are involved** — inherited as **severe generalized disease** (type 2 segmental mosaicism)

#### Complications

- **Sepsis**
- **Secondary skin infections**
- **Fluid and electrolyte imbalance**
- **Posture and gait abnormalities** (due to severe PPK and contractures)
- **Alopecias**

#### Investigations

1. **HPE (Histopathology)**:
   - Dense **orthokeratotic hyperkeratosis**, prominent **suprabasal acanthosis**
   - **Hypergranulosis** — coarse, clumped **keratohyalin granules**
   - **Cytolysis** of suprabasal and granular layers leading to small **intraepidermal blisters**
   - This histologic pattern — "**epidermolytic hyperkeratosis**" — is the defining feature

2. **Electron microscopy**:
   - **Fragmented, clumped keratin epidermis** (tonofilament clumping)
   - Abnormal **intermediate filaments (KIF)** in **lower epidermis**
   - **Perinuclear KIF shells** in upper dermis (perinuclear aggregation of keratin filaments)

3. **Mutation screening and complete sequencing** of KRT1 & KRT10

4. **Prenatal diagnosis** if underlying mutation in parents is known

#### Differential Diagnosis

1. Other **ichthyosiform disorders** (lamellar ichthyosis, CIE)
2. **Epidermolysis bullosa** (EB) — especially in the neonatal blistering phase
3. **SSSS** (staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome)
4. Other **vesiculobullous and erosive disorders**

### Part II: Harlequin Fetus (Harlequin Ichthyosis)

#### Definition & Synonyms

- Also known as **harlequin baby**, **ichthyosis congenita gravior**
- **Most severe form** of autosomal recessive congenital ichthyosis (ARCI)
- Called "harlequin fetus" because of the **diamond-shaped plates** resembling the costume pattern of the *harlequin* character in commedia dell'arte

#### Epidemiology

- Inherited as **autosomal recessive (AR)**
- **Rare disorder** — previously near-universally fatal; survival improving with modern NICU care
- Called harlequin fetus because of **premature delivery** and **fatal outcome** of these patients (historically)

#### Pathogenesis

- Caused by **loss-of-function mutations** in the **ABCA12 gene**
- ABCA12 encodes an **ATP-binding cassette (ABC) lipid transporter** that transports **ceramides and other lipids** into **lamellar bodies**
- Loss of function of ABCA12 gene →
  - **Prevention of formation of lipid bilayers** in the **stratum corneum**
  - Results in **massive hyperkeratosis** and complete **barrier breakdown**

#### Clinical Features

- Most babies born **prematurely** with average gestational age of **35 weeks**; increased neonatal mortality due to **respiratory insufficiency** and **sepsis**
- Newborns encased in a **hard, armor-like thickened stratum corneum** causing **severe immobilization** and **restricted ventilation**
- Cracks forming **large, yellow, adherent plates** separated by **deep red fissures** and **diamond-shaped scales** (harlequin pattern)
- **Dehydration** and **electrolyte imbalance**
- **Temperature instability**
- **Ectropion** (eversion of eyelids), **eclabium** (eversion of lips), and **rudimentary ear and nasal cartilage** giving face a **distorted appearance** and **feeding difficulties**
- Swollen, **edematous hands and feet** often encased in a **mitten-like covering**; **constricting skin bands** causing **autoamputation of digits**
- **Hair loss** — eyebrows, eyelashes, scalp

#### Complications

- **PPK** (palmoplantar keratoderma)
- **Exfoliative ichthyosiform erythroderma** (in survivors)
- **Dystrophic nails**
- **Recurrent skin infections**
- **Joint contractures**
- **Delayed motor skill development**
- **Heat intolerance**
- **Chronic constipation**

#### Investigations

1. **HPE**: Extraordinarily **compact and thickened orthokeratosis**, occasional **parakeratosis**, concentric **accumulation of keratotic material around hair shafts**

2. **Electron microscopy**: **Abnormal or missing lamellar bodies** in granular layer, **absent extracellular lipid lamellae**, presence of **lipid inclusions or remnant organelles** in stratum corneum

3. **Molecular diagnosis** and **prenatal testing** (amniocentesis and **CVS sampling**) — ABCA12 mutation analysis

#### Treatment

##### General:
- **Topical and systemic antibiotics**
- **Antibacterial soaps**
- **Chlorhexidine baths**
- **Soft clothing** to decrease friction
- **Oral retinoids**

##### Neonate:
- ***ICU isolation***: Prevent and treat **dehydration**, **electrolyte imbalance**, **cutaneous superinfection**, **sepsis** — should be treated with **broad-spectrum antibiotics**
- **Careful handling**
- **Protective padding**
- **Lubricants**

##### Children and adults:
- Therapy to **decrease hyperkeratosis** and **soften skin**
- **Keratolytic creams and lotions** (urea, salicylic acid, and alpha-hydroxy acids)
- **Topical tretinoin** and **vitamin D preparations**; gentle **scrub baths**
- **Frequent use of emollients**
- Early **systemic retinoids** like **acitretin at 1 mg/kg/day**

## Corrections & Verification Notes

- **"BROCQ" (p. 174, EHK synonyms)** → **Correction:** The source capitalizes the eponym as "BROCQ." The correct formatting is ***Brocq*** — referring to **Louis-Anne-Jean Brocq** (1856–1928), the French dermatologist who described bullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma. While the full capitalization is not strictly incorrect, standard medical typography uses initial capitalization for eponyms. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"KRTI" (p. 174, genotype–phenotype)** → **Correction:** The source text appears to print "KRTI" in the genotype–phenotype section. The correct gene symbol is ***KRT1*** (keratin 1 gene), located at chromosome 12q13.13. This is likely an OCR/typographic artifact where the numeral "1" was rendered as the letter "I." KRT1 encodes keratin 1, a type II intermediate filament keratin expressed in the suprabasal layers of stratified squamous epithelium **including palms and soles** — hence KRT1 mutations produce PPK. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Harlequin conic" (p. 175, clinical features)** → **Correction:** The source text reads "Harlequin conic" in the description of diamond-shaped scales. The correct term is **"harlequin"** pattern — referring to the diamond-shaped motif on the costume of the *Arlecchino* (harlequin) character in Italian commedia dell'arte. The word "conic" is likely an OCR misread or truncation of "comic" or simply garbled text. The clinical description should read: cracks forming large, yellow adherent plates separated by deep red fissures and **diamond-shaped scales** (the harlequin pattern). (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"constructing skin bands" (p. 175, clinical features)** → **Correction:** The source reads "constructing skin bands causing autoamputation of digits." The correct term is ***constricting* skin bands** — tight circumferential bands of thickened, cracked skin that encircle digits and can cause **autoamputation** (digital constriction and ischemic loss), analogous to the pseudoainhum seen in other disorders of keratinization. This is similar to the constricting bands seen in ainhum (dactylolysis spontanea) and amniotic band syndrome, but the mechanism here is extreme hyperkeratosis rather than fibrous tissue. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia; Andrews')

- **"Electron microscopy: Fragmented, clumped keratin epidermis" (p. 174, EHK investigations)** → **Clarification:** The source text description is somewhat compressed. On electron microscopy, epidermolytic ichthyosis shows **clumping and collapse of keratin intermediate filaments (tonofilament aggregation)** in the suprabasal layers, with **perinuclear shells of dense keratin filaments** in the upper epidermis/granular layer. The filament aggregation explains the cytolysis seen on light microscopy — the disrupted cytoskeleton cannot maintain cell-cell and cell-substrate adhesion. This is distinct from the **perinuclear shell** pattern seen in **Ichthyosis Curth–Macklin** (KRT1), which shows concentric rings of keratin around the nucleus without cytolysis. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Perinuclear KIF shells in upper dermis" (p. 174, EM findings)** → **Correction:** The source states "perinuclear KIF shells in upper dermis." This should read **"upper epidermis"** — the keratin intermediate filament aggregates are found in the suprabasal and granular layers of the **epidermis**, not the dermis. The dermis does not contain keratinocytes or keratin intermediate filaments. This is a critical anatomical distinction. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Gaucher syndrome Type 11" (p. 175, Q18 collodion baby associations)** → **Correction:** On page 175 in the beginning of Q18 (collodion baby), the source lists "Gaucher syndrome Type 11." This should be **Gaucher syndrome Type II** (type 2, using Roman numerals) — the acute neuronopathic form characterized by severe CNS involvement with onset in infancy and death by age 2. Type 11 (eleven) does not exist. This is an OCR artifact where Roman numeral "II" was misread as the Arabic numeral "11." (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **Harlequin ichthyosis survival (p. 175, general)** → **Verification:** Historically, harlequin ichthyosis was uniformly fatal within hours to days of birth. With modern NICU care — including **humidified incubators**, aggressive **fluid/electrolyte management**, **systemic retinoids** (oral acitretin at 1 mg/kg/day started as early as possible), and **broad-spectrum antibiotics** — survival has significantly improved. Multiple case reports document survival into adolescence and adulthood. However, survivors develop severe chronic ichthyosis requiring lifelong retinoid therapy and suffer from ectropion, eclabium, growth retardation, and recurrent infections. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **Acitretin dosing in neonates (p. 175, treatment)** → **Verification:** The source recommends "acitretin at 1 mg/kg/day." This is consistent with current guidelines for systemic retinoid therapy in severe congenital ichthyoses. However, retinoids in neonates must be used with extreme caution given the risks of **skeletal toxicity** (premature epiphyseal closure), **hepatotoxicity**, **mucocutaneous dryness**, and **hypertriglyceridemia**. Close monitoring of growth, liver function, and lipid panels is mandatory. Some centers use lower starting doses (0.5 mg/kg/day) and titrate based on clinical response. (Ref: Bolognia; Andrews')

This biphasic course (blistering → hyperkeratosis) is the clinical hallmark and a key exam point. The blistering phase is frequently mistaken for epidermolysis bullosa or SSSS in the neonatal period. The "pungent body odor" is clinically significant — secondary bacterial colonization of macerated, hyperkeratotic skin folds produces volatile short-chain fatty acids, a source of significant psychosocial morbidity.

- **Page 174 (right column, annular epidermolytic ichthyosis):** The text describes annular epidermolytic ichthyosis as a mosaic variant with "protruding porcupine-like spines" — termed **ichthyosis hystrix**. This represents **type 1 segmental mosaicism** (postzygotic mutation in one allele of KRT1/KRT10 in a previously normal embryo). The critical clinical implication: if the mosaic mutation is present in **gonadal cells** (gonosomal mosaicism), the affected individual can transmit the mutation to offspring as a **generalized autosomal dominant** disease — **type 2 segmental mosaicism** in the offspring. Genetic counseling regarding this gonadal transmission risk is essential for patients with epidermolytic nevi.

- **Page 174 (right column, EHK investigations):** The HPE description captures the classic triad of epidermolytic hyperkeratosis: (1) dense orthokeratotic hyperkeratosis with hypergranulosis and coarse/clumped keratohyalin granules, (2) vacuolar degeneration/cytolysis of the suprabasal and granular layers (the "epidermolytic" change), and (3) small intraepidermal blisters (subcorneal/intra-spinous). This histologic pattern is so distinctive that it serves as the naming convention for the disease. Note that "epidermolytic hyperkeratosis" as a histopathologic pattern can also be seen as an incidental finding in solar keratosis, seborrheic keratosis, and epidermolytic epidermal nevi — it is not exclusively diagnostic of EI.

- **Page 175 (left column, harlequin fetus — pathogenesis flowchart):** The source presents a vertical flowchart for harlequin fetus pathogenesis: ABCA12 transports ceramides and other lipids into lamellar bodies → loss of function mutation of ABCA12 gene → prevention of formation of lipid bilayers in stratum corneum → massive hyperkeratosis and barrier breakdown. This concise cascade illustrates why ABCA12 mutations produce the most severe ichthyosis phenotype — complete failure of lamellar body lipid loading means total absence of the extracellular lipid lamellae that normally form the waterproof barrier of the stratum corneum. The severity is directly proportional to the degree of ABCA12 functional loss: null mutations → harlequin ichthyosis; partial loss-of-function (hypomorphic) mutations → lamellar ichthyosis or CIE.

- **Page 175 (left column, harlequin clinical features):** The clinical description is vivid and comprehensive. Key neonatal features include: (1) armor-like encasement, (2) diamond-shaped scale plates with deep red fissures, (3) ectropion/eclabium/rudimentary ears, (4) mitten-like hand/foot encasement with constricting bands causing autoamputation, and (5) respiratory compromise from chest restriction. The "mitten-like covering" of hands/feet with potential autoamputation parallels the pseudoainhum concept and is clinically important — early release of constricting bands may be necessary. The gestational age of 35 weeks reflects premature labor induced by the abnormal fetal skin.

- **Page 175 (right column, harlequin investigations):** The EM findings of harlequin ichthyosis are the direct ultrastructural correlate of the ABCA12 defect: **abnormal or missing lamellar bodies** in the granular layer (because ABCA12 cannot load lipids into them), **absent extracellular lipid lamellae** (no lipid bilayer formation), and **remnant lipid inclusions** in the stratum corneum (lipid accumulation without proper bilayer organization). These findings are distinct from lamellar ichthyosis EM, which shows cholesterol clefts but partially formed lamellae. Prenatal diagnosis via CVS/amniocentesis for ABCA12 mutations is available and is important for genetic counseling in families with a previously affected child.

The distinguishing feature is the emphasis on **early systemic retinoids** (acitretin 1 mg/kg/day) — retinoids normalize keratinocyte differentiation and reduce the massive hyperkeratosis. For children and adults, the treatment transitions to a maintenance regimen of keratolytic emollients (urea, salicylic acid, alpha-hydroxy acids), topical retinoids, and continued systemic retinoids as needed. The inclusion of "gentle scrub baths" reflects the need for mechanical debridement of thick scale.
