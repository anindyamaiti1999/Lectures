# Q31. Describe the clinical features and management of confluent and reticulate papillomatosis.

**Chapter**: Disorders of Keratinization | **Source Pages**: 186–187 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Definition

It is an uncommon disorder of epithelial keratinization characterized by the development of hyperkeratotic papules which coalesce into confluent and in places reticulated plaques on truncal skin.

### Synonyms

- **Papillomatose pigmentée innominée**
- **Papillomatose pigmentée confluente et réticulée**
- **Gougerot-Carteaud syndrome**
- It is considered a specific form of **cutaneous papillomatosis**

### Epidemiology

- Young adults, peak onset ~19 years
- M = F

### Pathophysiology

- **Abnormal host reaction to *Malassezia* organisms** — *Malassezia furfur* (and other species) found on affected skin; however, antifungals alone are often insufficient, suggesting the organism is not the sole cause
- **Actinomycetes bacteria may play a role** — specifically ***Dietzia papillomatosis*** (formerly *Rhodococcus*), a named actinomycete isolated from CRP lesions
- **Metabolic abnormalities** such as **thyroid dysfunction** and **Cushing disease** lead to abnormal keratinocyte differentiation and increased epidermal turnover
- **Response to antibiotics** due to their **anti-inflammatory properties** (not solely antimicrobial) — supports an inflammatory/host-response etiology rather than simple infection

### Clinical Features

- **Location**: Plaques located mainly on the **trunk** — **presternal, interscapular, and epigastric** areas
- **Morphology**: 1–2 mm diameter, **pigmented hyperkeratotic papules**
- **Pattern**: Coalesce to form **greyish-blue confluent plaques** centrally, which become **reticular towards the periphery** (abdomen, face, limbs, pubic areas)
- **Distribution**: Central confluence + peripheral reticulation is the hallmark pattern

### Histopathology (HPE)

- Hyperkeratosis and **papillomatosis**
- Decrease in thickness or disappearance of the **granular cell layer**
- Increase in melanin in the **basal cell layer** and stratum
- Dermis shows at most a mild, non-specific inflammatory infiltrate

### Immunohistochemistry / Special Studies

- Increased expression of **involucrin**, **keratin 16**, and **Ki-67**
- Increased **number of Odland bodies (lamellar bodies)**
- **Fungal stains**: Show *Malassezia* yeasts on the surface of the epidermis

### Differential Diagnosis

1. Acanthosis nigricans
2. Prurigo pigmentosa
3. Macular amyloid
4. Darier disease
5. Epidermal nevus
6. Plane warts
7. Pityriasis versicolor
8. Dermatitis neglecta

### Course and Prognosis

- **Chronic disease** with remissions and exacerbations
- If responds to treatment, may **relapse if withdrawn**
- Persistent remission treated with **tetracyclines**

### Management

**Chronic disease — therefore only cosmetic treatment**
- Weight reduction (if applicable — metabolic cofactors)
- Spontaneous improvement has been reported

#### First Line
- **Oral antibiotics** — **Minocycline**, Azithromycin
- **Topical mupirocin ointment**

> **Key Point**: Minocycline is the first-line drug of choice. Its efficacy is attributed to its **anti-inflammatory properties** (suppression of neutrophil chemotaxis, TNF-α inhibition, MMP downregulation) rather than purely antimicrobial action.

#### Second Line
- Topical and systemic **antifungals**
- **Selenium sulfide shampoo**
- **Topical retinoids**
- **Vitamin D analogues**

#### Third Line
- **High and low dose isotretinoin**

## Corrections & Verification Notes

- **"Papillomatose pigmentee confluente et reticulee"** → **"Papillomatose pigmentée confluente et réticulée"** — Correct French diacritics added (Ref: Bolognia *Dermatology*, 4th ed.)
- ***Dietzia papillomatosis*** (the specific actinomycete implicated) — source text mentions "Actinomycetes bacteria" without naming the organism. *Dietzia papillomatosis* (formerly classified under *Rhodococcus*) was specifically isolated from CRP lesions by Natarajan et al. (Ref: Bolognia *Dermatology*; Andrews')
- **"Papillomatose pigmentee innominee"** → **"Papillomatose pigmentée innominée"** — French accents corrected
- **Minocycline mechanism** — Source states response is "due to anti-inflammatory properties." Verified: Minocycline inhibits neutrophil chemotaxis, suppresses MMP activity, and reduces TNF-α — these anti-inflammatory actions likely drive efficacy rather than direct antimicrobial killing of *Dietzia* (Ref: Rook's, Bolognia)
- **"1.2 mm diameter"** → **"1–2 mm diameter"** — The source text appears to read "1.2 mm" but contextually and per standard references, the papules are **1–2 mm** in diameter, not precisely 1.2 mm (Ref: Bolognia *Dermatology*)
- **Granular cell layer** — Source states "decrease in thickness or disappearance of granular cell layer." Verified correct — this feature helps distinguish CRP from acanthosis nigricans histologically, where the granular layer may be preserved (Ref: Rook's)
