# Q20. Write short notes on lamellar ichthyosis.

**Chapter**: Disorders of Keratinization | **Source Pages**: 177–178 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Definition & Synonyms

- Also known as **nonerythrodermic autosomal recessive lamellar ichthyosis**, **ichthyosis congenita type 2**, **ichthyosis lamellaris**
- A rare genetic disorder within the **autosomal recessive congenital ichthyosis (ARCI)** spectrum
- Characterized by **large, dark, plate-like scales** with **minimal or absent erythroderma** — the classic "lamellar" phenotype

### Inheritance & Genetics

1. Inherited as an **autosomal recessive (AR)** pattern
2. **Rarely autosomal dominant (AD)** transmission has been reported
3. **Deleterious mutations in both copies of the TGM1 gene** (transglutaminase-1) — the most common cause, accounting for **~90%** of lamellar ichthyosis cases
   - **TGM-1 enzyme deficiency** → catalyzes **calcium-dependent cross-linking** between proteins and **hydroxyceramides** → **formation of the cornified cell envelope**
   - Loss of TGM-1 function disrupts the cornified envelope assembly, resulting in defective barrier function and abnormal desquamation

#### Other ARCI Gene Mutations (Non-TGM1):

| Gene | Protein/Function |
|---|---|
| **ABCA12** | ATP-binding cassette transporter — lipid transport |
| **SULT2B1** | Sulfotransferase 2B1 — cholesterol sulfate metabolism |
| **CYP4F22** | Cytochrome P450 — acylceramide synthesis |
| **NIPAL4** | NIPA-like domain-containing 4 — magnesium transporter |
| **ALOX12B** | Arachidonate 12-lipoxygenase — lipid barrier |
| **ALOXE3** | Epidermis-type lipoxygenase 3 — lipid barrier |

> **High-Yield Point**: While TGM1 mutations account for the majority (~90%) of LI cases, the ARCI gene panel encompasses at least 9 genes. The same genes can also cause congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma (CIE), and the phenotypic distinction between LI and CIE lies along a spectrum: **LI = dark plates, minimal erythema, orthokeratosis**; **CIE = bright erythroderma, fine white scale, parakeratosis**.

### Clinical Features

#### Neonatal Presentation:
- **Severe disorder** that **presents at birth** and **persists throughout life**; milder variants (±) exist
- **Collodion membrane** may be present at birth — gradually replaced by scales

#### Scale Morphology:
- **Large, brown, plate-like scales** forming a **mosaic or bark-like pattern** with **minimal or no erythroderma**
- Scales are **centrally attached** and have **raised borders**, leading to **fissures**
- **"Bathing suit ichthyosis"** — a distinctive pattern variant where thick scaling is confined to the trunk (bathing-suit distribution) while limbs and face are relatively spared; caused by specific *TGM1* mutations

#### Dermatologic Complications:
- **Severe tautness of skin** can result in:
  - **Eclabium** (eversion of the lips)
  - **Ectropion** (eversion of the eyelids)
  - **Hypoplasia of nasal and auricular cartilage**
  - **Scarring alopecia**

#### Ocular Features:
- **Madarosis** (loss of eyelashes and/or eyebrows)
- **Keratitis**, **conjunctivitis**

#### Palms & Soles:
- **Palmoplantar keratoderma (PPK)** — thickened palms and soles

#### Nail Changes:
- **Secondary nail dystrophy**, **thickened nail plates**, and **ridging**

#### Eccrine/Thermoregulatory Abnormality:
- **Intraepidermal constriction of sweat ducts** results in **severe heat intolerance**
  - This anhidrosis can lead to hyperthermia and is a significant functional morbidity

#### External Ear:
- **Accumulation of scales in external ear** — occlusion, bacterial colonization, and **recurrent infections**

### Pathology (Histopathology)

- **Nondiagnostic** (histology alone is not pathognomonic)
- **Massive orthokeratotic hyperkeratosis** — a key histologic distinguishing feature from CIE (which shows parakeratosis)
- **Acanthosis**
- **Psoriasiform hyperplasia** — elongated rete ridges

### Electron Microscopy

- **Elongated cholesterol clefts** — a distinctive ultrastructural finding
- **Variable numbers of lipid droplets** in the stratum corneum
- **Thin or absent cornified cell envelope** — reflecting defective TGM-1 cross-linking

### Other Investigations

- **Transglutaminase-1 (TG-1) activity** in cultured keratinocytes — absent or markedly reduced
- **Genetic testing** — sequencing of TGM1 and other ARCI genes
- **Molecular prenatal diagnosis** — **CVS** (chorionic villous sampling) or **amniocentesis** for at-risk families

### Differential Diagnosis

- **Congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma (CIE)** — the principal differential; CIE shows **bright erythroderma with fine white scales** and **parakeratosis** on histology vs. LI's dark plates and orthokeratosis
- **Self-improving (self-healing) collodion baby** — ~10–20% of collodion babies shed the membrane to reveal near-normal skin

### Treatment

#### *Neonatal Management:*
- **ICU admission** due to risks of:
  - Thermostability failure
  - Hypernatremia (transepidermal water loss)
  - Skin infections and sepsis
- **Humidified incubator** — gradual desquamation of the basement membrane
- **Wet compresses**
- **Light emollients**
- **No manual removal of membrane**; **no topical keratolytics** — due to increased risk of:
  - **Infections** (disrupted barrier)
  - **Percutaneous absorption** (systemic toxicity from salicylic acid, urea, etc.)
- **Minimal handling**
- **Systemic antibiotics** (for infection prophylaxis/treatment)
- **Soft clothing** — decreases friction
- **Antiseptic baths** with antibiotic soaps

#### *Early Childhood & Long-Term:*
- Initiate **oral retinoids — Acitretin** (first-line systemic agent)
- **Topical vitamin D analogues** (calcipotriol)
- **Tazarotene** (topical retinoid)
- **Lactic acid** (keratolytic)
- **Propylene glycol** (humectant/keratolytic)
- **Ophthalmic care** and **surgical correction of ectropion** — essential for visual protection and prevention of exposure keratopathy

## Corrections & Verification Notes

- **"SULTZBI" (p. 177, right column, Other mutations list)** → **Correction:** The source text lists "SULTZBI" as one of the non-TGM1 ARCI genes. This is an **OCR/typographic error** for **SULT2B1** (sulfotransferase family 2B member 1), a gene encoding a cholesterol sulfotransferase involved in epidermal lipid metabolism. SULT2B1 mutations are a rare cause of ARCI. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"NIPAL 4" with space (p. 177)** → **Correction:** The source prints "NIPAL 4" with a space before the numeral. The correct gene symbol is **NIPAL4** (no space) — NIPA-like domain-containing protein 4, also known as ichthyin. Mutations in NIPAL4 disrupt magnesium-dependent processes in epidermal differentiation. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"TGM-1 enzyme deficiency → catalyzes calcium-dependent cross-linking" (p. 177, pathogenesis)** → **Verification:** The source correctly describes the function of transglutaminase-1: it catalyzes calcium-dependent cross-linking of structural proteins (involucrin, loricrin, small proline-rich proteins) and hydroxyceramides to form the cornified cell envelope. In LI, loss-of-function mutations abolish this cross-linking, producing a thin or absent cornified envelope (confirmed by electron microscopy on p. 178). The TGM1 gene is located on chromosome 14q12. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"~90% TGM1" (p. 177, genetics)** → **Verification:** The source's claim that TGM1 mutations account for ~90% of lamellar ichthyosis cases is **consistent with major references**, though the exact proportion varies by population (70–90% across studies). TGM1 is by far the most commonly mutated gene in classic LI. Other ARCI genes (ALOX12B, ALOXE3, CYP4F22, ABCA12, NIPAL4, SULT2B1) account for the remainder, and some of these overlap with the CIE phenotype. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Orthokeratotic hyperkeratosis" vs "Parakeratosis" — LI vs CIE histologic distinction (p. 177–178)** → **Verification:** The source correctly states LI shows **massive orthokeratotic hyperkeratosis**, which is a critical distinguishing feature from CIE. In CIE, the hyperkeratosis is **parakeratotic** (retained nuclei in the stratum corneum), reflecting the high epidermal turnover rate. In LI, the turnover is slower (more normal), so keratinocytes fully mature and lose their nuclei → orthokeratosis. This orthokeratosis vs. parakeratosis distinction is one of the most commonly tested differences between LI and CIE. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Cholesterol clefts" on electron microscopy (p. 178)** → **Verification:** The source correctly identifies **elongated cholesterol clefts** as a distinctive EM finding in LI. These cholesterol clefts (needle-shaped birefringent crystals in histologic sections, linear clefts on EM) result from abnormal lipid processing in the stratum corneum and are more characteristic of LI than CIE. They indicate accumulation of unesterified cholesterol due to disrupted lipid envelope formation. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Bathing suit ichthyosis" (p. 177, clinical features)** → **Verification:** The source correctly mentions "bathing suit ichthyosis." This distinctive phenotype is caused by specific temperature-sensitive *TGM1* mutations (e.g., p.R315C) where the mutant enzyme retains partial activity at cooler skin temperatures (extremities) but loses activity at warmer core temperatures (trunk). The result is a "bathing suit" distribution of thick scaling on the trunk with relative sparing of cooler extremities. This is a well-characterized genotype-phenotype correlation. (Ref: Bolognia; Rook's)

- **"Nondiagnostic" histopathology (p. 177)** → **Verification:** The source correctly states that the histopathology of LI is **nondiagnostic** — meaning the findings (orthokeratotic hyperkeratosis, acanthosis, psoriasiform hyperplasia) are suggestive but not pathognomonic. Definitive diagnosis requires **TGM-1 enzymatic assay** (absent activity in keratinocyte culture) or **molecular genetic testing** (TGM1 sequencing). This contrasts with epidermolytic hyperkeratosis (Q17), where the histology (epidermolytic changes with keratin clumping) IS diagnostic. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Gradual desquamation of basement membrane" (p. 178, neonatal treatment)** → **Likely Intended Meaning:** The source states "Humidified incubator — gradual desquamation of basement membrane." The term "basement membrane" here is a **misnomer/imprecision**; the intended meaning is the **collodion membrane** (the tight, shiny, parchment-like encasement of the neonate), not the histologic basement membrane zone (BMZ). The collodion membrane is composed of keratinized stratum corneum, and it gradually desquamates (sheds) over days to weeks in a humidified environment. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Madarosis, keratitis, conjunctivitis" (p. 177)** → **Verification:** These ocular complications are correctly attributed to the severe ectropion and chronic exposure of the ocular surface. Ectropion → exposure keratopathy → secondary keratitis and conjunctivitis. Madarosis (eyelash/eyebrow loss) results from chronic cicatricial changes at the lid margin. Lubricating eye drops, tarsorrhaphy, and ultimately surgical ectropion repair are essential components of care. (Ref: Rook's; Andrews')
