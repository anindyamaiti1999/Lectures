# Q35. Enumerate transepidermal elimination disorders.

**Chapter**: Disorders of Keratinization | **Source Pages**: 190–191 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Definition

Transepidermal elimination is a purposeful, pathologic, dermoepidermal reactive phenomenon incited by exogenous substances or altered dermal constituents (of inflammatory, metabolic, or neoplastic origin), characterized by **pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia** of the epidermis and/or follicular epithelium and formation of multiple transepithelial perforating channels, facilitating extrusion of altered dermal material or foreign substances to the exterior.

---

### Classification

#### Classical (Mehregan)

| Type | Disorder | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | ***Elastosis perforans serpiginosa*** (EPS) | Isolated; associated with **Penicillamine** therapy, Osteogenesis imperfecta, **Marfan's syndrome**, **Ehlers-Danlos syndrome**, Acrogeria, **Down syndrome** |
| **B** | **Reactive perforating collagenosis** (RPC) | Inherited |
| **C** | **Acquired perforating dermatoses** — **Kyrle's disease** | Acquired perforating dermatosis |
| **D** | **Acquired adult form of reactive perforating collagenosis** (APD) | Secondary to **DM** and **CKD** |
| **E** | **Perforating folliculitis** (PF) | Follicular-based perforation |

#### Other / Unspecified

- **Chondrodermatitis nodularis helicis chronica**
- ***Noninfective granulomatous disorders***: Perforating granuloma annulare, necrobiosis lipoidica diabeticorum, rheumatoid nodule, sarcoidosis
- ***Dermatosis with calcification***: Pseudoxanthoma elasticum, pilomatricoma, calcinosis cutis, and osteoma cutis
- ***Infectious diseases***: Cutaneous tuberculosis, botryomycosis, schistosomiasis, leishmaniasis, rhinosporidiosis, lobomycosis, chromoblastomycosis, and histoid leprosy
- ***Exogenous foreign material***: Silica, wood splinters, tattoo pigment
- ***Others***: Lichen nitidus, papular mucinosis, amyloidosis, melanoma, nevocellular nevus, eruptive vellus hair, vitiligo, gout crystals, Paget's disease, mycosis fungoides, vitiligo, and porokeratosis of Mibelli

### Pathophysiology — Mehregan's Types of Epidermal Reaction to Foreign Materials in the Dermis

- ***Type I***: **Trapping and upward epidermal migration** — desquamation of particles that are not capable of eliciting sufficient dermal reaction. For example: amyloid, hemosiderin, and erythrocytes.

- ***Type II***: **Migration of microorganisms** and motile cells — such as *Treponema pallidum* and leukocytes into epidermal spaces, to be carried upward by physiological desquamation process.

- ***Type III***: **Active and unidirectional DEJ interaction** — whereby dermal altered material and foreign components are actively extruded through the epidermis.
  - → **Fibronectin** implicated
  - → ↑ Expression of **TGF-β3**, **MMP-1**, and **TIMP-1** in lesional skin

#### Pathophysiology Flowchart (as depicted in source)

```
Binding of altered dermal constituents
            ↓
    Incites dermal reaction
            ↓
      Chemical mediators
            ↓
Epidermal hyperplasia and formation of 
  transepidermal perforating channels
            ↓
Foreign substances are surrounded and 
phagocytosed by epidermal cells of these 
        perforating channels
            ↓
 Subsequently moved upward to the surface
```

### Kyrle's Disease

**Eponym**: Also known as **hyperkeratosis follicularis et parafollicularis in cutem penetrans**

- **Perforating disorder of uremia**
- Rare chronic disorder characterized by **hyperkeratotic, follicular or parafollicular papules** with **cone-shaped plugs**, removal of which leaves **pit-like depressions**
- **Associations**: Diabetes mellitus (DM), chronic renal failure, liver disease

#### Clinical Features

- Multiple, asymptomatic, discrete or confluent, **brownish-black hyperkeratotic, follicular or parafollicular papules** with **cone-shaped plugs**
- Removal of plugs → **pit-like depressions**
- Usually **lower extremities**
- **Palms, soles, and mucosa spared**
- **Koebner's phenomenon** positive
- Coalesce to form **circinate lesions**
- Keratotic lesions of **conjunctiva and cornea**

#### Histopathology

- **Heavily keratotic, partly parakeratotic plug** containing an invagination of epidermis
- Plug may be **acanthotic or basophilic**
- Epidermis around the plug: dyskeratotic cells thinned
- **Granular layer absent** in the area below parakeratosis
- **Vacuolated dyskeratotic cells** can be seen extending to the basal cell layer
- Pronounced **granulomatous reaction** composed of inflammatory cells and **FBGC** (foreign body giant cells) where they plug in contact with dermis
- Surrounding mixed inflammatory infiltrate comprised of **neutrophils, lymphocytes, and epithelioid histiocytes**

#### Treatment

- **Topical keratolytics** (salicylic acid) — gives temporary improvement
- **Topical 0.1% tretinoin cream**
- **Oral Vitamin A** (100,000 IU/day) ± **Vitamin E** (400 IU/day)
- **Oral isotretinoin** 40 mg BD × 1 month
- **Etretinate**
- **UV light**

*For recalcitrant lesions:*
- Cryotherapy
- Electrodesiccation
- Surgical excision

## Corrections & Verification Notes

- **"pseudoepitheliomatōus"** → **pseudoepitheliomatous** — diacritical artifact on the "o" (Ref: Bolognia)
- **"helices"** (in chondrodermatitis nodularis helicis) → **helicis** — the source text appears correct; the implementation plan note "helices → helicis" refers to ensuring proper Latin genitive singular is used; the scanned text reads "helicis" which is correct (Ref: Rook's)
- **"TGF-B3, MMP-1 and TIMP-I"** → **TGF-β3, MMP-1, and TIMP-1** — Greek letter β and Roman numeral formatting corrected (Ref: Bolognia)
- **"imagination"** (in HPE section: "plug containing an imagination of epidermis") → **invagination** — critical OCR/autocorrect error; the plug lies within an invagination (downward folding) of the epidermis, not an "imagination" (Ref: Rook's, Bolognia)
- **"incited"** in source text is acceptable medical English (= provoked/triggered), retained as-is
- **Kyrle's disease nosology**: Modern dermatopathology increasingly subsumes Kyrle's disease under the umbrella of **acquired perforating dermatosis (APD)** of CKD/DM, but the textbook maintains the classical Mehregan classification separating them. Both approaches noted. (Ref: Bolognia Ch. 97; Rook's 9th Ed.)
- **"foliaceous"** (noted in implementation plan for Q30) — not applicable to Q35; confirmed no instance here
- **Vitamin A dosage 100,000 IU/day**: This is the high-dose retinol regimen historically used; modern practice favors synthetic retinoids (isotretinoin/acitretin) due to hepatotoxicity risk at this dose (Ref: Andrews')
