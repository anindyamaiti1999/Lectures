# Q27. Describe the clinical features of Papillon–Lefèvre syndrome.

**Chapter**: Disorders of Keratinization | **Source Pages**: 183–184 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Definition

- **Papillon–Lefèvre syndrome (PLS)** is also known as **keratoderma periodontitis**.
- It is an **autosomal recessive** trait characterized by:
  - **Palmoplantar keratoderma** (transgradient)
  - **Periodontitis** (aggressive, leading to premature tooth loss)
  - **Frequent pyodermas** (recurrent pyogenic skin and internal organ infections)

---

### Etiopathogenesis

- Caused by a **homozygous mutation** in the ***CTSC*** gene encoding **cathepsin C** (also known as **dipeptidyl peptidase I**).
- **Cathepsin C** is expressed in:
  - **Immune cells** (neutrophils, macrophages, mast cells, cytotoxic T lymphocytes)
  - **Lungs**
  - **Kidneys**
  - **Epithelial tissues**
- Functions in **protein degradation** and **proenzyme activation** (activates serine proteases — e.g., neutrophil elastase, cathepsin G, proteinase 3, granzymes A and B).

#### Consequences of CTSC Mutation

| System | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Immune** | Decreased activity of critical proteases; impaired neutrophil phagocytosis; high oxidative stress markers; mitochondrial dysfunction; impaired NK cell toxicity |
| **Dental** | Gingivitis; periodonitis; gram-negative *Actinobacillus actinomycetemcomitans* infection |
| **Cancer risks** | Melanoma; SCC |

> **High-Yield Point — Cathepsin C and Immune Dysfunction**: CTSC loss-of-function leads to failure of activation of downstream serine proteases in neutrophils and other immune cells. The resulting immune dysfunction particularly affects the periodontium, where gram-negative organisms like *Aggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans* (formerly *Actinobacillus actinomycetemcomitans*) colonize and cause aggressive periodontitis. The impaired neutrophil function also predisposes to recurrent pyogenic skin abscesses and visceral infections. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

---

### Clinical Features

#### Palmoplantar Keratoderma
- **Transgradient palmoplantar keratoderma** — extends beyond the palmoplantar margins onto the dorsal surfaces.
- **Punctiform accentuation** along palmoplantar creases.

#### Extra-Palmoplantar Skin Involvement
- **Psoriasiform plaques** on the **tendon Achilles, knees, and elbows**.
- **Pseudoainhum** (constricting fibrous bands) — **erythematous plaques** over knees, elbows, IPJ (interphalangeal joints), and dorsum of feet.

#### Hyperhidrosis
- **Hyperhidrosis**, particularly of the **soles**, may cause a **fetid odor**.

#### Dental Features
- **Periodontitis and gingivitis** — aggressive and early onset.
- **Loss of deciduous teeth by 5 years** of age, and then **loss of permanent teeth** (often edentulous by the teens).
- **Deep periodontal abscesses** from **gram-negative bacteria**.

#### Systemic Infections
- **Recurrent pyogenic infections** of the **skin and internal organs**.

---

### Haim-Munk Syndrome

- **Subtype of Papillon–Lefèvre syndrome** (allelic variant — also caused by *CTSC* mutations).
- **Identified in Jews of South India** (Cochin Jewish community).
- Characterized by:
  - **Onychogryphosis** (thickened, curved nails resembling ram's horns)
  - **Arachnodactyly** (abnormally long, spider-like fingers)
  - **Acro-osteolysis** (resorption of the distal phalanges)

> **Clinical Pearl — Haim-Munk vs PLS**: Both Haim-Munk syndrome and PLS are caused by *CTSC* mutations and share transgradient PPK and periodontitis. Haim-Munk is distinguished by the additional triad of **onychogryphosis, arachnodactyly, and acro-osteolysis**, and was originally described in the **Cochin Jewish community of South India**. The two conditions represent allelic variants with different mutation severity. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

---

### HPE (Histopathological Examination)

- **Hyperkeratosis**
- **Focal irregular parakeratosis**
- **Moderate perivascular infiltration** (inflammatory cells around dermal vessels)

---

### Differential Diagnosis (D/D)

- **Hypotrichosis–osteolysis** syndrome
- **Periodontitis–PPK syndrome (HOPP)**

---

### Electron Microscopy

- **Reduced tonofilaments**
- **Lipid-like vacuoles** in **corneocytes** and **granulocytes** (granular layer cells)
- **Irregular keratohyalin granules**

---

### Treatment

1. **Oral retinoids** for oral disease and **prevention of autoamputation** (of digits due to pseudoainhum/constricting bands).
2. **Systemic retinoids** (**acitretin** or **isotretinoin**) for PPK — if started **before permanent tooth eruption**, may preserve normal dentition.
3. **Elective extraction of affected teeth** to prevent excess **bone resorption** (alveolar bone destruction is progressive).
4. **Systemic antibiotics** for recurrent infections.

> **High-Yield Point — Early Retinoid Therapy**: Early administration of **acitretin** (before eruption of permanent teeth) can significantly **slow or prevent the aggressive periodontitis** that leads to loss of permanent dentition in PLS. This makes early diagnosis critical. The retinoid normalizes keratinization and may modulate the inflammatory response in the periodontium. Dental management should be coordinated with dermatological care. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia; Andrews')

## Corrections & Verification Notes

- **"Depp periodontal abscess" (p. 184)** → **"Deep periodontal abscess"** — The source text reads "Depp periodontal abscess." This is an OCR/typographical error; the correct word is **"deep,"** referring to deep-seated periodontal abscesses caused by gram-negative bacteria in PLS. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Pseudoainhum" (p. 184)** → **Verification:** The source lists "Pseudoainhum, erythematous plaques over knees, elbows, IPJ, dorsum of feet." While pseudoainhum (constricting fibrous bands causing auto-amputation of digits) has been reported in PLS, it is an **uncommon** feature and not part of the classical triad. It is more commonly associated with **vohwinkeliosis (Vohwinkel syndrome)**. The source listing is retained but the reader should note this is a minor/variable feature. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"*Actinobacillus actinomycetemcomitans*" (p. 184, etiopathogenesis flowchart)** → **Updated nomenclature: *Aggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans***. The organism was reclassified from the genus *Actinobacillus* to *Aggregatibacter* in 2006 (Nørskov-Lauritsen & Kilian, *Int J Syst Evol Microbiol*, 2006). The source uses the older nomenclature. Both names refer to the same gram-negative, facultatively anaerobic rod that is the key periodontal pathogen in PLS. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Periodonitis" (p. 184, etiopathogenesis box)** → **"Periodontitis"** — The source text in the dental box reads "Periodonitis," which is missing the second "t." The correct medical term is **periodontitis** (inflammation of the periodontium). (Ref: Standard medical terminology)

- **"Pseudoainhum" vs "Pseudoainhum" spelling (p. 184)** → **Verification:** The source text reads "Pseudoainhum" which is the accepted variant spelling. The term derives from the Nagô (Yoruba) word *ainhum* meaning "to saw/cut." Both "pseudoainhum" and "pseudo-ainhum" are acceptable in medical literature. (Ref: Rook's)

- **"acro-osteolysis" (p. 184, Haim-Munk)** → **Verification confirmed.** Acro-osteolysis (resorption of distal phalanges) is a recognized feature of Haim-Munk syndrome that distinguishes it from classical PLS. Other conditions with acro-osteolysis include progeria, vinyl chloride disease, hyperparathyroidism, pyknodysostosis, and scleroderma. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Jews of south India" (p. 184, Haim-Munk)** → **Clarification:** The Haim-Munk syndrome was originally described in the **Cochin Jewish community** of **Kerala, South India**, by Haim and Munk in 1965. This is a genetically isolated population with high rates of consanguinity, explaining the autosomal recessive inheritance pattern. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **Page 185 printer artifact** → The top of page 185 contains a **duplicate line**: "Systemic retinoids (Acitretin or isotretinoin) for PPK and normal dentition if started early." This is a **printer/pagination artifact** — the same line appears at the end of Q27's treatment section on page 184 and is repeated at the top of page 185. No additional Q27 content exists beyond this duplicate. (Ref: Source scan observation)

- **Cancer risks (p. 184, etiopathogenesis box)** → **Verification:** The source lists **melanoma** and **SCC** as cancer risks in PLS. While case reports exist linking PLS to increased malignancy risk (impaired immune surveillance due to cathepsin C deficiency), this is a **minor and debated association**. The primary clinical concern remains periodontitis and pyodermas. Pyogenic liver abscesses and brain abscesses have also been reported. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia; Andrews')
