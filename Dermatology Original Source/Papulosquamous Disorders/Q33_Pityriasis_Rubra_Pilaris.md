# Q33. Describe the classification, clinical features, and management of pityriasis rubra pilaris.

**Chapter**: Disorders of Keratinization | **Source Pages**: 187–189 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Definition

Pityriasis rubra pilaris (PRP) is a group of **rare, inflammatory, papulosquamous** common skin disorders of **unknown etiopathology** sharing clinical and histopathological features.

- **"Pityriasis"** — bran-like "scaling"
- **"Rubra"** — redness
- **"Pilaris"** — involvement of hair follicles

### Eponyms

Also known as **Lichen Ruber Acuminatus** and ***Devergie's*** disease.

### Epidemiology

- **Rare** dermatosis
- **Bimodal age distribution** — juvenile and **5th–6th decade**
- Rare **familial form** begins in early childhood
- **M = F** (equal sex distribution)

### Associations

- **Hypothyroidism**
- **HIV** (Type VI)  and other infections
- **Autoimmune diseases**
- **Malignancies**

### Pathophysiology

- **IL-23/Th17 pathway** suspected due to clinical similarities to psoriasis
- **IL-17** may be involved indicating **keratinocyte-derived cytokines**
- *Sporadic but familial cases*: **Gain-of-function (GOF) mutations** in the **CARD14** gene and sustained **NF-κB activation**

> **Key Point**: The CARD14 GOF mutation → NF-κB → IL-23/Th17 axis parallels the pathogenesis of psoriasis (also CARD14-associated), but PRP has distinct histopathology (checkerboard pattern, no Munro microabscesses) and clinical features (islands of sparing, orange hue) that differentiate it.

### Triggers

- **Photo-aggravation (UV)**
- **Trauma** (koebnerization)
- **Drugs**
- **Infections** (bacterial and viral)

---

### Classification — *Griffiths* Classification

Based on **age of onset**, **clinical presentation**, and **prognosis** (6 types):

#### Type I: Classic Adult Type
- **Most common type; ~80%** of cases
- Age: **40–60 years**
- **Pink-red, slightly scaly macules** over face, scalp, or neck
- Classic **keratotic follicular papules** — discrete, **cephalocaudal spread**, coalesce to form characteristic **orange-red plaques** with **bran-like scaling** and **islands of sparing**
- **Yellowish-orange, waxy palmoplantar keratoderma** ("PRP sandals")
- **Thickened nail plate**, yellowish-brown discoloration and **subungual hyperkeratosis**
- **Erythroderma** with facial redness and ectropion
- **Best prognosis** — clinical resolution within **3 years**

#### Type II: Atypical Adult
- **~5%** of cases
- Age: **~20 years**; **no clinical resolution after 3 years** — chronic
- Predilection for **lower extremities**
- **Eczematous lesions** with **ichthyosiform scaling**
- **Coarser PPK** with **lamellar scaling**
- **Sparseness of body hair**
- **Cephalocaudal progression absent**

#### Type III: Classic Juvenile Onset
- Age: **5–10 years**
- **~10%** of cases
- Resolution within **1–2 years**; rarely longer
- Rarely evolves to **Type IV**
- **Recurrence** in later adult life

#### Type IV: Circumscribed Juvenile
- **~25%** of cases
- **Prepubertal children \<12 years**
- **Well-demarcated red plaques** of grouped hyperkeratotic follicular papules over **elbows** and **knees** and **bony prominences**
- **PPK** present
- Prognosis uncertain with **spontaneous remissions and exacerbations**

#### Type V: Atypical Juvenile
- **~5%** of cases
- **Early onset (at birth)**, chronic course
- **Follicular hyperkeratosis** and **ichthyosiform scales**
- **Scleroderma-like changes** on palms and soles

#### Type VI: HIV-Related
- Similar to **Type I** in morphology
- **Red follicular papules** with **follicular plugging** and **spicules**
- Associated with **hidradenitis suppurativa (HS)** and **acne conglobata**
- **Poor prognosis**, resistance to **ART**

> **High-Yield Mnemonic**: Types I, III = classic (adult / juvenile) — best prognosis. Types II, V = atypical (adult / juvenile) — chronic, poor prognosis. Type IV = circumscribed juvenile. Type VI = HIV-related.

---

### Complications

- **Erythroderma** → high-output cardiac failure
- **Ectropion** → dry eyes or **peripheral ulcerative keratitis**

### Investigations

#### Histopathological Examination (HPE)

- **Alternating perpendicular and horizontal orthokeratosis and parakeratosis** — **"checkerboard" pattern** (pathognomonic)
- **Hyperkeratosis** with **follicular plugging** and **parakeratosis on either side** of **shoulder parakeratosis**
- **Focal or confluent hypergranulosis**
- **Irregular psoriasiform acanthosis**
- **Acantholysis** may be present
- Sparse, superficial, **dermal perivascular lymphohistiocytic infiltrate** — **lacking neutrophils**
- Erythroderma — **thinned-out epidermis** with a **diminished granular layer**

> **Key Differentiator from Psoriasis**: PRP shows a **checkerboard pattern** of alternating ortho- and parakeratosis with **shoulder parakeratosis** and **no Munro microabscesses** or **spongiform pustules of Kogoj** — in contrast to psoriasis which has regular psoriasiform hyperplasia, suprapapillary thinning, Munro microabscesses, and dilated tortuous capillaries.

---

### Treatment

#### Topical
- **Emollients** (with urea, salicylic acid, α-hydroxy acids)
- **Topical corticosteroids**
- **Topical retinoids**
- **Topical vitamin D analogs**
- **Topical calcineurin inhibitors**

#### Systemic
- ***First line***: **Systemic retinoids** — **isotretinoin**, **acitretin**, **alitretinoin**
- ***Second line***: **Methotrexate**, **ustekinumab**, **cyclosporine**, **fumaric acid esters**
- ***Third line***: **Anti-TNF agents**, **anti-IL-17**, **IV immunoglobulin (IVIG)**, **azathioprine**

#### Others (from margin notes, page 189)
- **UVB phototherapy**
- **Acitretin + UVA1 phototherapy** — for Type VI
- **Extracorporeal photochemotherapy** (photopheresis)

> **Clinical Pearl**: Unlike psoriasis, PRP may **worsen with phototherapy** in some patients (photo-aggravation is a known trigger). Use phototherapy with caution and monitor for flares. Combination of acitretin + UVA1 is specifically noted for HIV-related Type VI.

## Corrections & Verification Notes

- **"Griffith's" → Griffiths** — Source text uses the possessive form "Griffith's classification." The correct citation is **Griffiths classification** (after H.A. Griffiths, who described the six types in 1980). No possessive apostrophe is used in standard dermatology references. (Ref: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, 9th ed.)

- **"Lichen Ruber Acuminatus and Devergie's disease"** — Verified correct. Marie-Guillaume-Alphonse Devergie described PRP in 1857. The eponym "Lichen Ruber Acuminatus" was used by Kaposi. Both eponyms are valid historical terms. (Ref: Bolognia *Dermatology*, 4th ed.)

- **CARD14 gene** — Verified. **CARD14** (caspase recruitment domain family member 14) gain-of-function mutations are found in familial PRP (particularly Type V) and activate **NF-κB signaling**. The same gene is associated with familial psoriasis (PSORS2 locus, 17q25.3), reinforcing the pathogenetic overlap. (Ref: Bolognia *Dermatology*; Rook's)

- **IL-23/Th17 pathway** — Source correctly identifies IL-23/Th17 involvement. This parallels psoriasis pathogenesis and explains the therapeutic response to **ustekinumab** (anti-IL-12/23), **secukinumab/ixekizumab** (anti-IL-17), and **guselkumab** (anti-IL-23). (Ref: Rook's)

- **Type I resolution "within 3 years"** — Verified. Classic adult PRP (Type I) spontaneously resolves in approximately **80% of patients within 3 years**. This is the key prognostic differentiator from Type II (atypical adult), which has a chronic course exceeding 3 years. (Ref: Bolognia *Dermatology*)

- **Type IV "25%"** — Source states 25%. Standard references quote Type IV as approximately **25%** of cases. Verified correct. (Ref: Rook's)

- **Type VI HIV-related** — Source correctly identifies association with HS and acne conglobata, poor prognosis, and ART resistance. Verified: Type VI was added by Miralles et al. in 1995 specifically for HIV-associated PRP. These patients may have concurrent **follicular occlusion triad** (HS, acne conglobata, dissecting cellulitis). (Ref: Andrews' *Diseases of the Skin*)

- **"Checkerboard pattern"** — Verified. The alternating orthokeratosis and parakeratosis in both vertical and horizontal planes is the **hallmark histopathological feature** of PRP and is the single most important histological distinction from psoriasis. (Ref: Bolognia *Dermatology*; Rook's)

- **"Shoulder parakeratosis"** — Verified. Parakeratosis at the shoulder (edges) of follicular ostia is characteristic of PRP. In psoriasis, parakeratosis is confluent and diffuse. (Ref: Rook's)

- **"Lacking neutrophils"** — Verified and clinically significant. The **absence of neutrophils** in the dermal infiltrate of PRP contrasts sharply with psoriasis (which has Munro microabscesses composed of neutrophils in the stratum corneum and spongiform pustules of Kogoj in the spinous layer). (Ref: Bolognia *Dermatology*)

- **Systemic retinoids as first line** — Source lists isotretinoin, acitretin, and alitretinoin. Verified: **Isotretinoin** (0.5–1 mg/kg/day) and **acitretin** (0.5–0.75 mg/kg/day) are considered first-line systemic therapy. **Alitretinoin** (a pan-RAR/RXR agonist) has emerging evidence but is not universally accepted as first-line. (Ref: Rook's; Andrews')

- **Ustekinumab listed as second line** — Verified. **Ustekinumab** (anti-IL-12/23 p40) has shown efficacy in case series and is appropriately placed as second-line. Anti-IL-17 agents (secukinumab, ixekizumab) are listed under third line in the source, though recent evidence supports their efficacy as well. (Ref: Bolognia *Dermatology*)

- **"Fumaric acid esters" listed with second line** — Source text on page 188 lists "fumaric acid esters" (written as "fumaric" in abbreviated form at the end of the second-line listing). This is dimethyl fumarate, used more commonly in European practice for psoriasis and PRP. (Ref: Rook's)

- **Phototherapy caution** — Source margin notes (p. 189) list "UVB phototherapy" and "acitretin + UVA1 phototherapy for Type 6." This is important because PRP is known to be **photo-aggravated** in some patients. Phototherapy should be used cautiously and is more relevant for HIV-related Type VI where combination with acitretin is specifically recommended. (Ref: Andrews' *Diseases of the Skin*)
