# Q38. Describe the clinical features and management of Grover's disease.

**Chapter**: Disorders of Keratinization | **Source Pages**: 193–194 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Introduction

Also known as **transient acantholytic dermatosis (TAD)** and **papular acantholytic dermatosis**.

A relatively common, **transient or persistent**, monomorphous, **papulovesicular eruption** mainly affecting the **trunk**, which may be **pruritic or asymptomatic**.

---

### Epidemiology

- **Age**: Older adults, typically **>60 years**
- **Sex**: ***M > F*** — ratio approximately **4:1**
- **Ethnicity**: More common in **Caucasians**

---

### Associations

- **Psoriasis**
- **Asteatotic eczema**
- **Contact allergic dermatitis**
- **Irritant contact dermatitis**
- **Malignancies** of skin and blood

---

### Triggers

- **UV light**
- **Heat**
- **Sweating**
- **BRAF inhibitor therapy** (e.g., vemurafenib, dabrafenib — used in melanoma)
- **SARS-CoV-2 infection**
- **Bedridden patients** (prolonged immobilization, heat retention)

---

### Pathophysiology

- **Autoantibodies** against proteins involved in **keratinocyte development, activation, growth, adhesion, and motility**
- **Eccrine sweat** may be involved (sweat duct occlusion/irritation hypothesis)

---

### Clinical Features

- **Sudden onset**, itchy **papulovesicular** lesions on the **trunk** that crust and develop **keratotic erosions**
- May involve **proximal limbs**
- Usually **2–4 weeks** duration; **persistent in few** patients

---

### Differential Diagnoses

- **Scabies**
- **Insect bite reaction (IBR)**
- **Herpes zoster**
- **Galli–Galli disease** (reticulate pigmented variant of Dowling–Degos disease with acantholysis)
- **Dowling–Degos disease**

---

### Complications

- **Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC)**

---

### Investigations

#### Hematological Workup
- To **rule out malignancies** (given association with hematological and cutaneous malignancies)

#### HPE (Histopathology)

- Presence of **small foci of acantholysis** with **dyskeratosis**, **intraepidermal clefting**, and sometimes **vesicle formation**
- **Five patterns of acantholysis** are described (hallmark — multiple patterns may coexist in a single biopsy):
  1. **Darier-like** (dyskeratotic acantholysis with corps ronds and grains)
  2. **Hailey–Hailey-like** (partial "dilapidated brick wall" acantholysis)
  3. **Pemphigus vulgaris (PV)-like** (suprabasal acantholysis)
  4. **Spongiotic** (eczematous pattern with mild acantholysis)
  5. **Porokeratosis-like** (cornoid lamella-like columns of parakeratosis)
- Within the dermis: **sparse lymphohistiocytic infiltrate**

> **Key diagnostic point**: The presence of **multiple acantholytic patterns within a single biopsy** is the histopathological hallmark of Grover's disease and distinguishes it from other acantholytic disorders (Ref: Bolognia *Dermatology*, 4th ed.).

---

### Treatment

#### General Measures
- **Avoid sun exposure**, strenuous exercise, and heat
- *First line*: Most cases resolve spontaneously

#### First Line
- **Potent TCS** (topical corticosteroids)
- **Topical vitamin D analogues**
- **Calcineurin inhibitors** (tacrolimus, pimecrolimus)
- **Antihistamines** (for pruritus)

#### Second Line
- Short course of **systemic corticosteroids**
- **Systemic retinoids** (acitretin/isotretinoin)
- **Phototherapy** (NB-UVB/PUVA)
- **Methotrexate**

#### Third Line
- **Etanercept** (anti-TNF biologic)
- **Photodynamic therapy (PDT)**

## Corrections & Verification Notes

- **"Bed ridden patients"** (p. 194, Triggers) → **Bedridden patients** — "Bedridden" is a single compound word, not two separate words. The source incorrectly separates it.
- **"PK like"** (p. 194, HPE section) → **Porokeratosis-like** — The abbreviation "PK" is ambiguous; the full histologic pattern name is "porokeratosis-like" (cornoid lamella-like parakeratotic columns). Clarified for exam accuracy.
- **"PV like, Spongiotic"** (p. 194, HPE patterns) → **Pemphigus vulgaris-like, Spongiotic** — Source uses abbreviation "PV like" which requires expansion. The spongiotic pattern is the fifth pattern and is sometimes called the "eczematous" pattern.
- **Five histologic patterns**: The source lists "Darier like, Hailey-Hailey like, PV like, Spongiotic and PK like." Cross-referenced with Bolognia (4th ed., Ch. 56) and Rook's (9th ed.) — verified as the five recognized acantholytic patterns. Some references additionally mention a "pemphigus foliaceus-like" pattern, but the five listed in the source are the standard classification.
- **Autoantibodies in pathophysiology**: The source states "autoantibodies against proteins involved in keratinocyte development, activation, growth, adhesion and motility." Per Rook's and Bolognia, the exact pathogenesis of Grover's disease remains **unclear/debated**; the autoimmune hypothesis is one proposed mechanism. Heat-induced sweat duct occlusion and direct acantholytic damage are alternative theories. The source text is preserved as written.
- **SCC complication**: Verified. Rare reports of SCC arising within lesions of persistent Grover's disease exist in the literature, though this is uncommon (Ref: Andrews' *Diseases of the Skin*, 13th ed.).
- **Galli–Galli disease** as DD: Verified. Galli–Galli disease is the acantholytic (reticulate) variant of Dowling–Degos disease and is an important histologic differential for Grover's disease (Ref: Bolognia, 4th ed.).
- **Etanercept as third-line**: Case reports support anti-TNF therapy (etanercept) for recalcitrant Grover's disease (Ref: Bolognia, 4th ed.).
