# Q22. Describe the clinical features and management of nonbullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma.

**Chapter**: Disorders of Keratinization | **Source Pages**: 179–180 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Definition & Synonyms

- **Nonbullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma (CIE)**
- Also known as **congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma**
- An **autosomal recessive lamellar ichthyosis** — classified under **autosomal recessive congenital ichthyosis (ARCI)**
- Previously referred to as **ichthyosis congenita type 1**
- It is a **clinically and genetically heterogeneous** ARCI trait, inherited in an **autosomal recessive (AR)** manner
- **More common than lamellar ichthyosis**
- **Rarely autosomal dominant (AD)**
- **Incidence**: approximately **1 in 100,000–200,000** people

### Pathogenesis

#### 9 Gene Mutations Implicated in CIE (ARCI Genes):

| # | Gene | Notable Associations |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **TGM1** | Collodion membrane ++ |
| 2 | **ALOX12B** | Collodion membrane ++ |
| 3 | **ALOXE3** | Collodion membrane ++ |
| 4 | **NIPAL4** | Abnormal lamellar bodies |
| 5 | **PNPLA1** | Scalp involvement, collodion membrane |
| 6 | **CERS3** | Scalp involvement, collodion membrane |
| 7 | **LIPN** | Childhood-onset ichthyosis, generalized white scales and minimal erythema |
| 8 | **ABCA12** | Missense mutation |
| 9 | **CYP4F22** | Rare erythroderma during infancy |

> **High-Yield Point**: **TGM1** (transglutaminase 1) mutations are the most commonly implicated gene in both CIE and lamellar ichthyosis (Q20). In CIE, **TGM1** mutations are associated with collodion membrane presentation at birth. The nine ARCI genes represent the shared genetic substrate across CIE, lamellar ichthyosis, and other ARCI subtypes — the phenotypic expression depends on the specific mutation and its functional impact on the cornified cell envelope and lipid barrier.

> **Key Distinction — CIE vs Lamellar Ichthyosis (LI)**:
> - **CIE**: High epidermal cell turnover → **parakeratosis** on HPE
> - **LI**: Low epidermal cell turnover → **orthokeratosis** on HPE
> - This turnover difference is the fundamental histopathological distinction between the two ARCI phenotypes.

### Clinical Features

#### Onset:
- **Present at birth** — typically born as a **collodion baby** (encased in a tight, shiny collodion membrane)
- Collodion membrane **progresses to generalized erythroderma and scaling** that **remains throughout life**

#### A. Skin:

- **Milder than lamellar ichthyosis** but with **variability** in the intensity of erythema and the size and shape of scale
- ***Severe cases***: Intense **bright red erythroderma** with **generalized white scales** of a **powdery consistency**
- **Ectropion** (eversion of the eyelids)
- **Scarring alopecia**
- **Scales may become dark, plate-like** especially on the **extensor surface of lower extremities**

#### B. Palms and Soles — Severely Affected:

- **Diffuse fissuring keratoderma**
- **Palmoplantar hyperlinearity**

#### C. Additional Cutaneous Features:

- **Hypohidrosis and heat intolerance** due to **obstruction of sweat ducts**
- **Secondary nail dystrophy**
- **Onychomycosis**
- **Digital flexion contractures**

#### D. Severe Exfoliative Erythroderma in Children:

- **Metabolic stress**
- **Growth failure**
- **Growth retardation**
- **Malnutrition**

#### E. Adults:

- Risk of **basal cell carcinoma** and **squamous cell carcinoma**

### Investigations

#### 1. Histopathological Examination (HPE):

- **Nondiagnostic** (the histologic findings are suggestive but not pathognomonic)
- **Focal or extensive parakeratosis** — this is the **key HPE distinguishing feature from LI** (which shows orthokeratosis)
- **Acanthosis**
- **Hypergranulosis**
- **Epidermal cell turnover rate increased** — reflected by the parakeratosis

> **High-Yield Comparison**:
> | Feature | CIE | Lamellar Ichthyosis (LI) |
> |---|---|---|
> | Epidermal turnover | **Increased** (high) | **Normal/Decreased** (low) |
> | Keratinization pattern | **Parakeratosis** | **Orthokeratosis** |
> | Erythroderma | **Prominent** (bright red) | **Minimal** |
> | Scale type | **Fine, white, powdery** | **Dark, plate-like, mosaic** |

#### 2. Electron Microscopy (EM):

- **Increased lamellar bodies**
- **Accumulation of lipid droplets in stratum corneum**
- **Disorganized intracellular lipid lamellae**

#### 3. Transglutaminase 1 (TG-1) Activity Assessment:

- **Immunostaining** or **assessing activity of transglutaminase 1 deficiency** in cultured keratinocytes or skin biopsy
- Useful when TGM1 mutation is suspected

#### 4. Genetic Testing:

- **Genetic testing of culprit genes** — sequencing the 9 ARCI genes for definitive molecular diagnosis

#### 5. Prenatal Molecular Diagnosis:

- **When disease-causing mutations are identified in family members**, prenatal diagnosis is possible via:
  - **Chorionic villous sampling (CVS)**
  - **Amniocentesis**

### Differential Diagnoses

- **Lamellar ichthyosis** — the principal differential; distinguished by dark plate-like scales, minimal erythema, and orthokeratosis on HPE
- **Self-improving collodion ichthyosis** — presents as collodion baby at birth but undergoes spontaneous improvement (10–20% of collodion babies)
- **Netherton syndrome** — ichthyosis linearis circumflexa with bamboo hair (trichorrhexis invaginata) and elevated IgE
- **Epidermolytic hyperkeratosis (EHK)** — presents with neonatal blistering that transitions to corrugated hyperkeratosis; blistering distinguishes from CIE

### Treatment

#### *A. In Case of Collodion Membrane (Neonatal):*

- **ICU management** — essential for thermoregulation, fluid balance, and infection prevention
- **Humidified incubator** — prevents transepidermal water loss (TEWL) and hypothermia
- **Wet compresses**
- **Light emollients**
- **Avoid topical keratolytics/manual removal** due to **increased risk of infections and toxicity** from **increased percutaneous absorption** through the impaired skin barrier
- **Minimal handling**
- **Systemic antibiotics** — for secondary bacterial infections
- **Soft clothing decreasing friction**
- **Antiseptic baths and soaps**

#### *B. Erythroderma (Long-Term Management):*

- **Emollients** — mainstay of lifelong management
- **Increased intake of fluids, calories, iron, and protein** to balance loss from chronic erythroderma/exfoliation
- **Oral retinoids** — can reduce scaling but **less beneficial in erythroderma** (may exacerbate erythema and irritation)
- **Eye abnormalities → surgical correction** (for ectropion)
- **Flexion contractures → surgical correction**

#### *C. Topical Keratolytics for PPK:*

- **Urea**
- **Salicylic acid**

#### *D. For Onychomycosis:*

- **Topical and systemic antifungals**

## Corrections & Verification Notes

- **"Ichthyosis congenita type 1" (p. 179, Q22 answer introduction)** → **Verification:** The source uses the older designation "ichthyosis congenita type 1" for CIE. In the current ARCI nomenclature (2009 Ichthyosis Consensus Conference), this terminology has been largely replaced. CIE and lamellar ichthyosis (LI) are both classified under the umbrella of **autosomal recessive congenital ichthyosis (ARCI)**, with phenotypic subtypes distinguished clinically and histologically rather than by "type" numbering. However, the "type 1" designation is still encountered in some Indian dermatology textbooks and is acceptable for exam purposes. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"1 in 1,00,000–2,00,000 people" (p. 179, epidemiology)** → **Clarification:** The source uses the Indian numbering system (lakh notation): 1,00,000 = 100,000 and 2,00,000 = 200,000. The incidence of CIE is approximately **1 in 100,000 to 200,000** in the international notation. This figure is broadly consistent with reported incidence for ARCI subtypes, though precise epidemiologic data is limited due to the rarity of these conditions. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"More common than lamellar ichthyosis" (p. 179, introduction)** → **Verification:** The source states CIE is more common than LI. This is debated in the literature — some references suggest LI and CIE have roughly similar prevalence within ARCI, while others report CIE as slightly more common. In populations where TGM1 mutations predominate, LI may be more prevalent. The statement is acceptable as a general approximation but should not be taken as definitively established. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Rarely AD" (p. 179, inheritance)** → **Verification:** The source mentions rare autosomal dominant inheritance for CIE. While ARCI is overwhelmingly autosomal recessive, rare reports of autosomal dominant transmission exist in the literature, typically involving atypical or partial phenotypes. This is a minor point but accurately reflects the literature's acknowledgment of exceptional inheritance patterns. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **9 ARCI genes listed (p. 179, pathogenesis)** → **Verification:** The source lists 9 genes: TGM1, ALOX12B, ALOXE3, NIPAL4, PNPLA1, CERS3, LIPN, ABCA12, and CYP4F22. This list is correct and comprehensive per current understanding. All nine genes encode proteins involved in the formation of the cornified cell envelope or the lipid barrier of the epidermis. **TGM1** (transglutaminase 1) is the most commonly mutated gene. **ALOX12B** and **ALOXE3** encode lipoxygenases involved in lipid processing. **NIPAL4** (ichthyin) is involved in lamellar body secretion. **ABCA12** mutations cause severe phenotypes (homozygous null = harlequin ichthyosis; missense = CIE/LI). **CYP4F22** encodes a fatty acid hydroxylase. **PNPLA1**, **CERS3**, and **LIPN** are more recently identified genes. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Nondiagnostic" HPE (p. 179, investigations)** → **Verification:** The source correctly states that HPE in CIE is nondiagnostic. The histologic findings (parakeratosis, acanthosis, hypergranulosis) are suggestive but not pathognomonic, as similar patterns can be seen in other ichthyoses and inflammatory dermatoses. The critical role of HPE is to demonstrate parakeratosis (indicating high cell turnover), which helps distinguish CIE from LI (orthokeratosis/low turnover) — but definitive diagnosis requires molecular/genetic testing. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Hypergranulosis" in CIE HPE (p. 179, investigations)** → **Clarification:** The source lists hypergranulosis as an HPE finding. This is somewhat nuanced — in CIE, the granular layer is generally **present and may be thickened** (hypergranulosis), in contrast to the **absent or reduced** granular layer seen in ichthyosis vulgaris. However, in areas of prominent parakeratosis, the granular layer may actually be **reduced**. The source's inclusion of hypergranulosis is consistent with some references describing a focally prominent granular layer in CIE. Students should note that the granular layer status varies by region of biopsy and specific mutation. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia; Andrews')

- **"Increased lamellar bodies" on EM (p. 179, investigations)** → **Verification:** The source correctly notes increased lamellar bodies on electron microscopy. In CIE, the high epidermal turnover leads to increased production of lamellar bodies, but their lipid content may be disorganized — resulting in defective lipid lamellae in the stratum corneum and impaired barrier function. This contrasts with LI, where lamellar body abnormalities may include cholesterol clefts (as noted in Q20). (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Avoid topical keratolytics/manual removal" in neonatal treatment (p. 180)** → **Verification:** The source correctly warns against topical keratolytics and manual removal of the collodion membrane in neonates. The impaired skin barrier in CIE/collodion babies leads to **increased percutaneous absorption**, creating a risk of **systemic toxicity** (e.g., salicylism from salicylic acid, metabolic acidosis from lactic acid). This is a critically important clinical pearl also emphasized in Q18 (Collodion Baby). (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia; Andrews')

- **"Oral retinoids can reduce scaling but less beneficial in erythroderma" (p. 180, treatment)** → **Verification:** The source correctly notes that oral retinoids (acitretin) can reduce scaling but are less beneficial for the erythroderma component. In clinical practice, acitretin is useful for reducing the hyperkeratotic/scaling burden in CIE but may paradoxically worsen erythema and cause mucocutaneous dryness. The dose must be carefully titrated. This is an important distinction from LI (Q20), where acitretin is often more straightforwardly beneficial because LI has less erythroderma. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **Risk of BCC and SCC in adults (p. 179)** → **Verification:** The source mentions the risk of basal cell carcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma in adult CIE patients. Chronic scaling and barrier dysfunction may predispose to UV-related damage and keratotic lesions, but the malignancy risk in CIE is considered **low** in the literature and not a defining feature. The association is more robustly established for other ichthyoses, particularly those with severe barrier impairment or immunologic abnormalities (e.g., Netherton syndrome). (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia; Andrews')
