# Q25. Discuss palmoplantar keratodermas (KD). Classify, and enumerate syndromes associated.

**Chapter**: Disorders of Keratinization | **Source Pages**: 182 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Definition

- **Palmoplantar keratodermas (PPKs)** are a group of cutaneous disorders characterized by **hyperkeratosis of the palms and soles**.
- They may be **acquired** or **inherited**, and are further subclassified by the pattern and distribution of hyperkeratosis as well as associated systemic features.

### Classification

```
                        PPK
                         │
              ┌──────────┴──────────┐
          Acquired              Inherited
                                     │
                    ┌────────────────┼────────────────┐
                 Diffuse          Focal           Punctate
```

---

### I. Acquired PPKs

- **Keratoderma climactericum**
- **KD associated with malignancy** (paraneoplastic keratoderma)
- **KD due to inflammatory disorders, circulatory disorders, infectious causes, drugs**
- **Aquagenic keratoderma**

---

### II. Inherited PPKs

#### A. Diffuse PPK

Diffuse PPK involves **uniform, confluent hyperkeratosis** of the entire palmoplantar surface. It is further subclassified based on whether the keratoderma extends beyond the palmoplantar margins:

- **May extend beyond palms and soles** — **transgradient keratoderma**
- **May involve dorsal and flexor aspects of wrist, elbows, knees, and tendo Achilles**

##### Transgradient (extends beyond palmoplantar margins):

| Syndrome | Gene/Protein |
|---|---|
| **Camissa syndrome** | Loricrin |
| **Mal de Meleda syndrome** | SLURP-1 |
| **ARS gene → SLURP-1** | ARS (SLURP-1 associated) |
| **Papillon-Lefèvre syndrome** | Cathepsin C (*CTSC*) |
| **Vohwinkel's syndrome** | Connexin 26 (*GJB2*) |
| **Olmsted syndrome** | K1/K10, TRPV3 |
| **Huriez syndrome** | SLURP-1 |
| **Clouston syndrome** | Connexin 30 (*GJB6*) |
| **Nagashima-type PPK** | SERPINB7 |
| **Bart-Pumphrey syndrome** | Connexin 26 (*GJB2*) |

##### Non-transgradient (confined to palmoplantar surface):

| Syndrome | Gene/Protein |
|---|---|
| **Vörner's syndrome** | K1/K9 |
| **Unna-Thost syndrome** | K1/K2 |
| **Naxos disease** | Plakoglobin |

> **High-Yield Point — Vörner vs Unna-Thost**: Historically these were considered separate entities, but molecular studies have shown significant overlap. **Vörner's keratoderma** (epidermolytic PPK) is caused by mutations in *KRT9* (palmoplantar-specific keratin) or *KRT1*, and shows **epidermolytic hyperkeratosis** on histopathology. **Unna-Thost** (non-epidermolytic PPK) was traditionally attributed to *KRT1* mutations without epidermolysis, though the distinction is now debated. Both are **autosomal dominant, diffuse, non-transgradient** PPKs. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

> **Clinical Pearl — Naxos Disease**: Naxos disease is caused by mutations in the **plakoglobin (JUP)** gene and presents with the triad of **(1) diffuse non-transgradient PPK**, **(2) woolly hair**, and **(3) arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy (ARVC)**. It is the **autosomal recessive** form of ARVC with cutaneous features — a critical exam association linking dermatology to cardiology. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

---

#### B. Focal PPK

Focal PPK involves **localized, non-confluent areas** of hyperkeratosis, typically over pressure points or friction-bearing sites.

| With associated systemic features | Without associated systemic features |
|---|---|
| **Howel-Evans syndrome** | **Buschke-Fischer disease** |
| **Focal keratoderma with oral leukokeratosis KRT16** | **Punctate keratoderma of palmar creases** |
| **Pachyonychia congenita type 1 and 2** | **Marginal papular acrokeratoderma** |
| **Carvajal syndrome** | |
| **Richner-Hanhart disease** (*TAT*c gene) | |

> **High-Yield Point — Howel-Evans Syndrome**: Caused by mutations in *RHBDF2* (encoding iRhom2), this syndrome presents with **focal PPK** (weight-bearing areas) and carries a **>95% lifetime risk of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (SCC)**. Endoscopic surveillance is mandatory. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

> **Clinical Pearl — Carvajal Syndrome**: Caused by mutations in **desmoplakin (*DSP*)**, Carvajal syndrome presents with **striate PPK** (along the lines of digital flexion), **woolly hair**, and **dilated cardiomyopathy** (left ventricular predominant). It is the autosomal recessive counterpart to Naxos disease but with **left-sided cardiac involvement** (dilated cardiomyopathy) rather than right-sided (ARVC). (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

> **Clinical Pearl — Richner-Hanhart Disease**: Also known as **tyrosinemia type II**, caused by mutations in the *TAT* gene (tyrosine aminotransferase). Presents with **painful, pseudo-dendritic corneal erosions**, **focal PPK**, and **intellectual disability**. Elevated plasma tyrosine is diagnostic. Treatment is dietary restriction of tyrosine and phenylalanine. (Ref: Bolognia)

---

#### C. Punctate PPK

Punctate PPK features **tiny, discrete keratotic papules** scattered across the palmoplantar surface, giving a "raindrop" or "music box" pattern.

- **Schopf-Schulz-Passarge syndrome** — *WNT10A*
- **Punctate PPK with ankylosing spondylitis**
- **Punctate PPK with facial sebaceous hyperplasia**
- **Punctate PPK with spastic paralysis**

> **High-Yield Point — WNT10A**: Schopf-Schulz-Passarge syndrome (SSPS) is caused by mutations in *WNT10A* and features **punctate PPK**, **eyelid cysts** (hidrocystomas), **hypodontia**, **hypotrichosis**, and **nail dystrophy**. *WNT10A* mutations are now recognized as the most common genetic cause of selective tooth agenesis (hypodontia/oligodontia) worldwide — an important cross-specialty association. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

## Corrections & Verification Notes

- **"Marginal popular acrokeratoderma" (p. 182, Focal PPK table — right column)** → **"Marginal papular acrokeratoderma"** — The source text reads "Marginal popular acrokeratoderma," which is a typographical/OCR error. The correct term is **marginal papular acrokeratoderma** (also known as acrokeratoderma of Costa), a benign condition characterized by small papular keratoses along the lateral margins of the palms and fingers. "Popular" is a contextual substitution error for "papular." (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Camissa syndrome" (p. 182, Transgradient diffuse PPK table)** → **Verification/Clarification:** The source lists "Camissa syndrome (loricrin)" as a transgradient diffuse PPK. In the dermatology literature, **Camisa's disease** is a variant of **loricrin keratoderma** — a transgradient, mutilating diffuse PPK caused by mutations in the *LOR* gene (loricrin). The loricrin keratoderma variant of Vohwinkel syndrome (sometimes called the Camisa variant) features transgradient PPK with honeycomb-pattern hyperkeratosis and pseudoainhum (digital constriction bands), but **without deafness** (unlike classic Vohwinkel caused by *GJB2*/connexin 26 mutations, which includes sensorineural hearing loss). The spelling in the source ("Camissa") may be an alternate transliteration. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"ARS gene → SLURP-1" (p. 182, Transgradient table — listed as separate entry from Mal de Meleda)** → **Clarification:** The *ARS* (also designated *SLURP1*) gene on chromosome 8q24 encodes **secreted Ly-6/uPAR-related protein 1 (SLURP-1)**. Mal de Meleda syndrome (also called keratosis palmoplantaris transgradiens of Siemens) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutations in this same *SLURP1*/*ARS* gene. The source appears to list "ARS gene → SLURP-1" as a separate entry from "Mal de Meleda syndrome" — these refer to the **same disorder**. The listing may be intended to emphasize the gene-protein relationship rather than denoting a distinct syndrome. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Olmsted syndrome (K1/K10, TRPV3)" (p. 182, Transgradient table)** → **Correction/Clarification:** The source lists Olmsted syndrome with "K1/K10, TRPV3." The primary molecular cause of Olmsted syndrome is **gain-of-function mutations in *TRPV3*** (transient receptor potential vanilloid 3), not KRT1/KRT10. KRT1 and KRT10 mutations cause **epidermolytic hyperkeratosis (Vörner-type PPK/bullous CIE)**, not Olmsted syndrome. Olmsted syndrome is characterized by **bilateral mutilating transgradient PPK**, **periorificial keratotic plaques**, alopecia, and nail dystrophy. Some cases of Olmsted syndrome have also been linked to *MBTPS2* (X-linked recessive form). The K1/K10 association in the source is likely an error or refers to a different entity grouped in the same row. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"TATc gene" (p. 182, Focal PPK table — Richner-Hanhart disease)** → **"*TAT* gene"** — The gene for Richner-Hanhart disease (tyrosinemia type II) is ***TAT*** (tyrosine aminotransferase), located on chromosome 16q22.2. The source prints "TATc gene" — the lowercase "c" is either a typographical artifact or an OCR error. The correct gene symbol is ***TAT***. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia; OMIM)

- **"Punctate keratoderma of palmar creases" (p. 182, Focal PPK table — right column)** → **Clarification:** The source lists "Punctate keratoderma of palmar creases" under the focal PPK category (no systemic features). This is listed among focal keratodermas, although traditionally punctate keratoderma (keratosis punctata palmaris et plantaris / Buschke-Fischer-Brauer) is classified under the **punctate** rather than focal category. Its placement here may reflect the source's intent to classify palmar-crease-specific keratoses as focal rather than punctate. Both classifications exist in the literature. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **Nagashima-type PPK gene (p. 182, Transgradient diffuse PPK table)** → **Verification/Supplement:** The source does not list a specific gene for Nagashima-type PPK. This condition, also known as **Nagashima-type palmoplantar keratosis** (NPPK), is caused by mutations in *SERPINB7* (serpin peptidase inhibitor, clade B, member 7). It is an **autosomal recessive** condition particularly prevalent in **East Asian** populations, characterized by mild transgradient PPK with a characteristic **spongiotic** histopathology rather than hyperkeratotic histology. (Ref: Bolognia)
