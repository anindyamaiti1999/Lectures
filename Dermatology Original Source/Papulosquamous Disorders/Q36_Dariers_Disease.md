# Q36. Discuss the classification, clinical features, and management of Darier's disease.

**Chapter**: Disorders of Keratinization | **Source Pages**: 191–192 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Introduction

- Also known as **Darier–White Disease** / **Keratosis Follicularis**
- It is an **autosomal dominant genodermatosis** characterized by a chronic eruption of **keratotic papules** which on histology shows **acantholysis and dyskeratosis**
- First described by **Darier** and **White**

---

### Epidemiology

- **M = F** (equal sex incidence)
- Initially appears in **early teenage**: **6–20 years**
- **Autosomal dominant** inheritance

---

### Pathophysiology

- Mutations in the **ATP2A2** gene at chromosome locus **12q24.1**
- Encodes **ER Ca²⁺ ATPase type 2 (SERCA2)**
- Maintains a low cytoplasmic **Ca²⁺** level by actively transporting Ca²⁺ ions from the **cytosol to the ER**
- **Nonsense/insertion/deletion mutations** in ATP2A2
- Severe **disruption of calcium homeostasis**

  ↓

- **Abnormal expression** of cytokeratin **10** and **14**
- **Transport of desmosomal proteins** — **desmoplakin 1 and 11** (I and II), **plakoglobin**, and **desmoglein** — is impaired
- Impaired **stress response** may also predispose to **apoptosis** leading to **dyskeratotic cells**

---

### Clinical Features

#### Aggravating Factors
- **Friction**, **heat**, **sweating**, **sunlight**, and **UVB**

#### Cutaneous Lesions
- **Discrete or confluent**, rough, greasy, **yellowish-brown**, dirty, **warty papules** on **seborrheic areas**
- **Trunk, face, scalp margins, temples, ears, neck**, and **flexures** (perineum, axillae, and groin)
- Begins as a **small group of keratotic papules** on **sun-exposed sites** such as the neck
- May coalesce forming **fissured plaques**, **vegetating** and **malodorous**
- Isolated **nipple hyperkeratosis**
- Scattered lesions present over **lower limbs**
- **Focal palmoplantar pits** and keratoses
- **Filiform keratoses**

#### Acrokeratosis Verruciformis
- **Plane-wart-like lesions** best seen by **transverse illumination**
- **Dorsum of hands**

#### Nail Changes
- **Fragility**
- **Painful longitudinal splits**
- Distinctive **red and white longitudinal bands**
- **V-shaped nicks** (V-notch)
- May become **severely dystrophic**

#### Mucosal Involvement
- **Oral, esophageal, cervical, and rectal mucosa** involved
- **Palate-white umbilicate cobblestone papules** coalesce to form **leucoplakia**
- **Salivary duct occlusion** and **recurrent sialadenitis**

#### Ocular
- **Hyperkeratotic plaques** and seborrheic debris at **eyelid margin**
- **Chronic blepharitis**
- **Dry eye syndrome**
- **Corneal erosions**
- **Endophthalmitis**

---

### Clinical Variants

1. **Erosive or vesiculobullous disease**
2. **Eroded or hyperkeratotic type**
3. **Comedonal or nodulocystic**
4. **Palmoplantar hemorrhagic macules**
5. **Pigmented papular lesions**
6. **Guttate leukoderma**
7. **Nevoid**

---

### Severity Grading

| Grade | Description |
|---|---|
| ***Grade 0*** | Subclinical; typical rash **absent or subtle** and **asymptomatic** |
| ***Grade I*** | Localized keratotic papules (**<10% BSA**) with **mild pruritus or irritation** |
| ***Grade II*** | Moderate (**10–30%** BSA) and symptomatic |
| ***Grade III*** | Widespread (**>30%** BSA); **confluent/eroded/hyperkeratotic** |

---

### Complications

- **Impetiginization**
- **Eczematization**
- Increases the susceptibility of infection with **herpes simplex**, **herpes zoster**, or **cowpox** (Kaposi varicelliform eruption / eczema herpeticum)
- **Squamous cell carcinoma**
- **Mammary Paget's disease**
- ***Neuropsychiatric function***: **Depression**, **bipolar disorder**, **suicidal tendency**, and **epilepsy**

---

### Course and Prognosis

- **Chronic relapsing** course with **unpredictable severity**

---

### Investigations

#### 1. HPE (Histopathological Examination)

- **Hyperkeratosis** with **orthokeratosis** and **focal parakeratosis**
- **Suprabasal acantholysis**
- Mild **papillomatosis**
- Projections of papillary dermis covered by a **single layer of basal cells** extend into **lacunae**
- **Dyskeratotic cells**:
  - **Corps ronds**: Found in the **upper malpighian layer** and **stratum corneum**. Round cells with a **pyknotic nucleus**, a clear **perinuclear halo**, and **eosinophilic cytoplasm**
  - **Grains**: Small cells with **elongated nuclei** and scant cytoplasm located in **upper epidermal layers**

#### 2. Gene Sequencing

- Commercial **ATP2A2 gene sequencing** available

---

### Treatment

#### First Line
- **Emollients** and **topical antiseptics**; **avoid excess heat or UVB**

#### Second Line
- **Moderately potent topical combination corticosteroids** with **antifungals** and **antibiotics**
- **Topical retinoids**

#### For Exacerbations
- **Oral antibiotics**
- **Oral antiviral**
- **Oral antifungals**

#### Chronic Disease
- **Oral acitretin 0.25–0.5 mg/kg** or **oral isotretinoin 0.5 mg/kg/day**

#### Third Line
- **Tacrolimus**
- **5-Fluorouracil cream**
- **Tacalcitol**
- **Diclofenac gel** (3%)
- **Oral alitretinoin**
- **Low-dose naltrexone**
- **Photodynamic therapy**
- **IV immunoglobulin**
- **Surgical or physical therapies**

#### Disease-Specific Adjuncts
- ***Inflammatory disease***: **Cyclosporine**
- ***Flexural disease***: **BOTOX** (botulinum toxin — reduces sweating as trigger), **breast reduction**

## Corrections & Verification Notes

- **"lacune"** (p. 192, HPE section: "extend into lacune") → **lacunae** — correct Latin plural of *lacuna*; the scanned text uses the singular/non-standard "lacune" (Ref: Bolognia, Rook's)

- **"Diclofenac gel 17"** (p. 192, third-line treatment) → **Diclofenac gel 3%** — the "17" is an OCR/scan artifact. Diclofenac sodium gel is available as **1% gel** (Voltaren) for musculoskeletal use and **3% gel** (Solaraze) for actinic keratoses/dermatologic use. There is no "17%" formulation. The dermatologic formulation used in Darier's is **3%** (Ref: Bolognia; Andrews')

- **Grade I BSA threshold**: The scanned text reads *"10% BSA"* for Grade I. Per the severity grading convention and the Grade II lower boundary (10–30%), Grade I should be **<10% BSA**. The source text appears to state "10% BSA" without the "<" sign, likely a typographic omission. Corrected to **<10% BSA** for internal consistency with the grading scale (Ref: Bolognia)

- **"Desmoplakin 1 and 11"**: The scanned text shows "desmoplakin 1 and 11" — this likely represents **desmoplakin I and II** (Roman numerals), as desmoplakin exists in two isoforms (I and II). The Arabic "11" is an OCR artifact from "II" (Ref: Rook's)

- **"Desmoglein"** in source → retained as generic term; the specific desmosomal cadherin affected in Darier's is primarily **desmoglein-3 (Dsg-3)** and desmosomal transport in general (Ref: Bolognia)

- **Cowpox** listed as a complication: The textbook lists herpes simplex, herpes zoster, and cowpox. While historically accurate, modern literature more commonly references **eczema vaccinatum** (vaccinia) and **eczema herpeticum** (HSV) as the viral complications. Cowpox is exceptionally rare but technically correct as a poxvirus complication. Retained as per source. (Ref: Rook's)

- **Mammary Paget's disease** association: This is listed in the source as a complication. The association is uncommon but reported — both Darier's and Paget's share features of aberrant keratinocyte differentiation. Retained as per source. (Ref: Andrews')

- **Acrokeratosis verruciformis (Hopf)**: This is an allelic variant of Darier's disease caused by mutations in the same **ATP2A2** gene. Some authorities consider it a distinct entity (autosomal dominant), while others classify it as a clinical variant of Darier's. The textbook treats it within Darier's clinical features. (Ref: Bolognia; Rook's)

- **SERCA2**: The source correctly identifies the protein product. SERCA2 is the **sarco/endoplasmic reticulum Ca²⁺ ATPase type 2**, and the isoform relevant to keratinocytes is specifically **SERCA2b** (Ref: Bolognia)
