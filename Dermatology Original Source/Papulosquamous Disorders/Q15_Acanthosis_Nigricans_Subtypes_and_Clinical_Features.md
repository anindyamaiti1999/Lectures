# Q15. Describe the sub-types and clinical features of acanthosis nigricans.

**Chapter**: Disorders of Keratinization | **Source Pages**: 172–173 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Definition

- **Acanthosis nigricans (AN)** is an acquired disorder of keratinization characterized by **asymptomatic and symmetrical darkening** affecting skin of **intertriginous areas**
- The skin is thickened with a **velvety texture** and may be studded with **acrochordons** (skin tags)

---

### Epidemiology

- **Benign >> malignant** (malignant AN is rare, <5% of all cases)
- Can occur at **any age**
- **M = F** (equal sex distribution)
- Most common in **darker skin tones**

---

### Types — Benign vs. Malignant

| Feature | **Benign** | **Malignant** |
|---|---|---|
| **Age** | Most common in adults and younger population | Most common in older population |
| **Course** | Chronic and primarily cosmetic concern | Paraneoplastic syndrome, often with underlying malignancy |
| **Prognosis** | Good prognosis | Poor prognosis with an average survival of 2 years |
| **Genetics** | Genetically transmitted | No genetic component |

---

### Pathophysiology

- **IGF-1 receptors** are overexpressed in patients with **hyperinsulinemia** (insulin resistance and obesity)
- **EGF receptors** and **FGF receptors** are also implicated
- **FGFR3 mutations** are also identified in familial AN, *Beare-Stevenson syndrome*, and *SADDAN syndrome*
- In **malignant AN**, **TGF-α** (transforming growth factor-alpha) plays a key role — tumor-secreted TGF-α binds to EGF receptors on keratinocytes, driving epidermal proliferation

---

### Predisposing Factors

1. **Insulin resistance**
2. **Obesity**
3. **Friction**
4. **Perspiration**

---

### Associations

- Acromegaly
- Gigantism
- Diabetes mellitus (DM)
- Obesity, PCOS
- Dermatomyositis, SLE
- Phenylketonuria
- *Prader–Willi syndrome*
- *Laurence–Moon–Bardet syndrome*
- *Bloom syndrome*
- *Rud syndrome*
- *Wilson syndrome*
- *Hashimoto thyroiditis*
- ***HAIR-AN syndrome***: **H**yperandrogenism, **I**nsulin **R**esistance, **A**canthosis **N**igricans, PCOS
- *Werner syndrome*
- *Costello syndrome*
- Addison disease
- Pellagra
- Hemochromatosis

#### Malignancies Associated with AN

- Bladder adenocarcinoma
- Ovarian adenocarcinoma
- Breast adenocarcinoma
- Renal cell carcinoma
- **Gastric adenocarcinoma** (most common malignancy associated with malignant AN)
- Thyroid adenocarcinoma

---

### Clinical Features

- Predominantly involves **knuckles, neck, axillae, groins**
- Can become widespread involving **mucosal surfaces, eyelids, conjunctivae**, and associated **nail changes**
- **Velvety dark patches**

---

### Clinical Variants

- **HAIR-AN syndrome** — Hyperandrogenism, Insulin Resistance, Acanthosis Nigricans
- **Familial AN** — autosomal dominant, presents in childhood, FGFR3 mutations
- **Drug-induced AN** — hormones, insulin, systemic corticosteroids, and oral contraceptives
- **Acral AN** — affecting dorsum of hands and feet
- **Unilateral nevoid AN** — rare, appears as a fragmented plaque along a line of Blaschko with histopathological features of AN
- **Malignant AN** — characterized by rugose hypertrophy and warty thickening of mucosal surfaces; **tripe palms** pattern

---

### Investigations

#### Histopathological Examination (HPE)

- **Hyperkeratosis** with minimal acanthosis and **papillomatosis** (finger-like upward projections of dermal papillae)
- **Pigmentation is due to hyperkeratosis** — the pigment is "optical"
- **Melanin number or production is not increased** (melanocyte count is normal)
- Screen patients for underlying **endocrinopathies** and **malignancies**

#### Homeostatic Model Assessment for Insulin Resistance (HOMA-IR)

- **HOMA-IR** = [Glucose (mg/dL) × Insulin (μIU/mL)] / 405
- **N = 0.7–2.0**
- **If >2** = Insulin resistance

---

### Treatment

1. **Lifestyle modifications** (weight loss, dietary changes)
2. **First line**: Topical retinoids — **tretinoin 0.1%**, **adapalene**
3. **Second line**: Topical **vitamin D analogues** — **calcipotriene**
4. **Third line**: **Alpha hydroxy acids** and **isotretinoin**
5. **Others**:
   - Sun protection
   - Chemical peels with **trichloroacetic acid**, **salicylic acid**
   - Oral agents like **metformin**, **rosiglitazone**
   - **Acitretin**
   - Urea-based creams (**ammonium lactate 12%**)
   - **Podophyllin 20%**
   - Retinol-based serums (up to 5%), creams, peels (10%)
   - Long-pulsed **alexandrite laser** (4–8 weeks)
   - **Fish oil** and **omega-3 fatty acids** decrease hyperkeratosis

## Corrections & Verification Notes

- **"Benign > malignant (rare) (20%)" (p. 172, Epidemiology)** → **Correction:** The source states "Benign > malignant (rare) (20%)" which implies malignant AN constitutes 20% of cases. This is **incorrect**. Malignant AN accounts for **<5%** of all AN cases in most large series. The 20% figure has no basis in standard references. Gastric adenocarcinoma is the most commonly associated malignancy (~50–60% of malignant AN cases). The vast majority (>90%) of AN cases are benign and associated with insulin resistance, obesity, or medications. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia; Andrews')

- **"Insulin MIU/405" (p. 173, HOMA-IR formula)** → **Correction:** The source writes "Insulin MIU/405." The correct unit is **μIU/mL** (micro-international units per milliliter), not "MIU." The standard HOMA-IR formula is: **[Fasting glucose (mg/dL) × Fasting insulin (μIU/mL)] / 405**. The value 405 is a mathematical constant derived from the product of the normal fasting glucose (90 mg/dL) and normal fasting insulin (4.5 μIU/mL). When using SI units (glucose in mmol/L), the formula becomes: **[Glucose (mmol/L) × Insulin (μIU/mL)] / 22.5**. (Ref: Matthews et al., *Diabetologia* 1985; Bolognia)

- **"Pigmentation is due to hyperkeratosis" (p. 173, HPE section)** → **Verification:** This is an important **exam-tested concept**. The dark color of AN is an **optical effect** caused by the thick, undulating hyperkeratotic epidermis (papillomatosis) — this thickened keratin layer refracts light differently, creating the appearance of hyperpigmentation. **Melanocyte number is NOT increased** and melanin production is not significantly elevated. This is similar to the "dirty" appearance in *terra firma-forme dermatosis*. Some sources note mild basal hyperpigmentation can coexist, but the dominant darkening is optical/structural. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"TGF plays a key role" (p. 172, Pathophysiology)** → **Clarification:** The source states "TGF plays a key role" in malignant AN. This specifically refers to **TGF-α (transforming growth factor-alpha)**, which is structurally homologous to EGF and binds to the **EGFR (EGF receptor)**. In malignant AN, tumor cells secrete excessive TGF-α, which acts on keratinocyte EGF receptors to stimulate proliferation and papillomatosis. This should NOT be confused with **TGF-β** (transforming growth factor-beta), which has distinct, often inhibitory, effects on epithelial proliferation. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"FGFR3 mutations also identified in Familial AN, Beare-Stevenson syndrome, SADDAN syndrome" (p. 172, Pathophysiology)** → **Verification:** **FGFR3** (fibroblast growth factor receptor 3) gain-of-function mutations cause constitutive receptor activation. **Beare-Stevenson cutis gyrata syndrome** (FGFR2, primarily) features cutis gyrata, AN, craniosynostosis, and digital anomalies. **SADDAN** = **S**evere **A**chondroplasia with **D**evelopmental **D**elay and **A**canthosis **N**igricans (FGFR3 p.Lys650Met). Familial benign AN is linked to **FGFR3** activating mutations transmitted in an **autosomal dominant** pattern. Note: Beare-Stevenson is primarily an FGFR2 disorder, though some FGFR3-related phenotypes overlap. (Ref: Bolognia; Rook's)

- **"Gastric adenocarcinoma" (p. 172, Malignancies list)** → **Verification:** Gastric adenocarcinoma is the **most commonly associated malignancy** with malignant AN, accounting for approximately **50–60%** of paraneoplastic AN cases. The **Leser–Trélat sign** (sudden eruption of multiple seborrheic keratoses) may co-occur with malignant AN. **Tripe palms** (acanthosis palmaris / pachydermatoglyphia) — exaggerated ridging of palmar dermatoglyphics — is found in ~75% of patients with malignant AN and is strongly associated with pulmonary and gastric malignancies. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia; Andrews')

- **"DDs" (p. 173, mentioned in Implementation Plan)** → **Note:** The differential diagnoses section is not explicitly labeled on the source page but is implied in the transition to Q16 (ichthyoses). Standard differentials for AN include: *terra firma-forme dermatosis* (responds to isopropyl alcohol wiping), *confluent and reticulate papillomatosis of Gougerot-Carteaud* (treated with minocycline), *dowling-degos disease* (reticulate pigmentation in flexures), and *macular amyloidosis*. (Ref: Bolognia; Andrews')

- **Page 172 (left column, transition from Q14 to Q15):** Q15 begins in the lower half of page 172, immediately after the Q14 classification list ends. - **Page 172 (left column, benign vs. malignant table):** The comparison table is a high-yield exam format. The key distinction: benign AN is **genetically transmitted** (familial, autosomal dominant) with a **chronic, cosmetic** course in younger patients, whereas malignant AN has **no genetic component**, is **paraneoplastic**, occurs in **older** patients, and carries a **poor prognosis** (average survival ~2 years). Malignant AN characteristically shows **rapid onset**, **widespread distribution** (including mucosal surfaces), and often **precedes, coincides with, or follows** the diagnosis of an internal malignancy.

- **Page 172 (right column, Associations list):** The extensive list of associations emphasizes that AN is a **cutaneous marker of systemic disease** — particularly of **insulin resistance** and **endocrinopathy**. The HAIR-AN syndrome mnemonic is frequently tested: **H**yperandrogenism, **I**nsulin **R**esistance, **A**canthosis **N**igricans. The inclusion of seemingly unrelated syndromes (Bloom, Werner, Costello, Prader–Willi) reflects their shared involvement of the **IGF-1/insulin receptor signaling axis** or associated metabolic derangements. Students should also note the margin annotations listing **Addison disease**, **pellagra**, and **hemochromatosis** — these represent conditions where generalized hyperpigmentation (as seen in Addison disease and hemochromatosis) or skin thickening (pellagra — *Casal necklace*) may clinically mimic AN.

- **Page 172 (right column, Malignancies list):** The malignancy associations highlight **adenocarcinomas** as the predominant histological type. **Gastric adenocarcinoma** is the classic and most frequently tested association. The list also includes bladder, ovarian, breast, renal, and thyroid malignancies — all adenocarcinomas. This pattern is important: **malignant AN = adenocarcinoma until proven otherwise**. The sign of **Leser–Trélat** (explosive seborrheic keratoses) frequently co-occurs with malignant AN and shares the same TGF-α/EGFR pathogenesis.

- **Page 173 (left column, HPE and optical pigmentation):** The margin notes emphasize the key histopathological teaching point: **"Pigmentation is due to hyperkeratosis. Melanin number or production is not increased."** This is the **most commonly tested fact** about AN histopathology. The darkening is an **optical illusion** created by the thick, folded keratin layer — not by increased melanocyte activity. The annotation also instructs to **"Screen patients for underlying endocrinopathies and malignancies"** — this is a clinical pearl reinforcing that AN should always trigger investigation for metabolic or neoplastic causes, particularly in older patients with acute-onset, widespread, or mucosal involvement.

- **Page 173 (left column, HOMA-IR formula and treatment ladder):** The HOMA-IR formula is a high-yield clinical tool for dermatology examinations. The normal range (0.7–2.0) and the insulin resistance cutoff (>2.0) should be memorized. The treatment ladder proceeds logically: **lifestyle modifications** → **topical retinoids** (first line) → **vitamin D analogues** (second line) → **alpha hydroxy acids/isotretinoin** (third line). The "Others" section lists several adjunctive treatments including **metformin** (addressing the root insulin resistance), **chemical peels** (cosmetic improvement), and **alexandrite laser** (for resistant cases). The inclusion of **fish oil and omega-3 fatty acids** reflects emerging evidence for their keratolytic and anti-inflammatory properties.

- **Page 173 (overall Q15 structure and exam relevance):** Q15 is a **comprehensive question** that covers definition, epidemiology, pathophysiology, classification, clinical features, variants, investigations, and treatment — making it a self-contained, exam-ready answer. The question's placement immediately after Q14 (classification of keratinization disorders) contextualizes AN as the first detailed disorder discussed in the "acquired keratinization disorders" block. AN is one of the most frequently tested dermatology topics in clinical examinations because of its dual importance as (a) a dermatological marker of **metabolic syndrome/insulin resistance** and (b) a potential **paraneoplastic marker** of internal malignancy.
