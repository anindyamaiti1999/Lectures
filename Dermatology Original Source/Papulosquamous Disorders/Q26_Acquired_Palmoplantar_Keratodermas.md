# Q26. Classify and discuss acquired palmoplantar keratodermas.

**Chapter**: Disorders of Keratinization | **Source Pages**: 182–183 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Definition

- **Acquired palmoplantar keratodermas (PPKs)** are **non-hereditary, non-frictional hyperkeratoses** of the palms and soles that involve **≥50% of the surface** and that may or may not be associated with clinical and histological inflammation.

### Classification

1. **Keratoderma climactericum**
2. **Chemicals** (toxic/occupational)
   - Arsenic
   - Chloracnegens
3. **Idiopathic**
4. **Malnutrition**
5. **Drugs**
6. **Malignancy** (paraneoplastic)
7. **Hypothyroidism**
8. **Dermatoses**
9. **Infections**
10. **Aquagenic PPK**

---

### 1. Keratoderma Climactericum

- Also known as ***Haxthausen's disease***.
- Appears commonly in **women of menopausal age**.
- Strong association with **obesity** and **hypertension**.

#### Pathophysiology
- **Hormonal dysregulation** is proposed as the underlying mechanism.

#### Clinical Features
- **Round or oval keratotic lesions** of varying sizes, appearing first on **plantar pressure points**.
- Lesions develop **erythema, hyperkeratosis, and fissuring**, causing **painful walking**.
- Hyperkeratosis appears on **palms**.
- **Pruritus** may or may not be present (pruritus ±).
- Associated **menopausal symptoms**: hot flushes, genital atrophy, osteoporosis, loss of libido.

#### HPE (Histopathological Examination)
- **Compact orthokeratotic hyperkeratosis**.
- **Hypergranulosis of the epidermis**.

#### Management
- Administration of **conjugated estrogens**.

| **Quinacrine** |

---

### 3. Malignancy (Paraneoplastic PPK)

- **Acrokeratosis paraneoplastica** (*Bazex syndrome*)
- **Tripe palms**
- Associated malignancies: **Lung, breast, bladder, stomach carcinoma**
- **Mycosis fungoides** (cutaneous T-cell lymphoma)

---

### 4. Hypothyroidism

- Acquired PPK can occur as a manifestation of **hypothyroidism** due to thyroid-related mucinous and keratinization derangements.

---

### 5. Dermatoses

Various inflammatory dermatoses can produce secondary PPK:

- **Psoriasis**
- **Pityriasis rubra pilaris**
- **Atopic dermatitis**
- **Chronic hand dermatitis**
- **Lichen planus**
- **Lichen nitidus**
- **Lupus erythematosus**
- **Aquagenic** (keratotic reaction to water exposure — listed both under dermatoses and as a distinct entity)

---

### 6. Infections

| Infection |
|---|

| **Syphilis** (secondary — palmoplantar papulosquamous lesions) |
| **Norwegian (crusted) scabies** |
| **Leprosy** |
| **Trichophytosis** (dermatophyte infection) |
| **Tuberculosis** |

### 7. Aquagenic PPK

- Also known as **transient reactive papulotranslucent acrokeratoderma**.
- Subtypes:
  - **Aquagenic syringeal acrokeratoderma**
  - **Aquagenic wrinkling of palms and soles**
- **Inheritance/association**: AR/AD
- **Associated with use of cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) inhibitors** and **cystic fibrosis** (CFTR mutations).

#### Clinical Features
- **Thickening and white translucent, "pebbly" changes** on palms shortly after immersion in water.
- Associated **edema and burning pain**.
- **Plantar involvement is rare**.
- **Females are significantly more prone** than males.

#### HPE
- **Normal skin** or dilated eccrine ostia with a **mildly hyperkeratotic stratum corneum**.

#### Treatment
- **20% aluminum chloride hexahydrate lotion** with urea cream.
- **Botulinum toxin (Botox)** injections.

---

### 8. Keratoderma and Cancer

- ***Filiform PPK***: Associated with **breast, colon, and kidney cancer**.
- ***Tripe palms***: Velvety, thickened palmar skin with exaggerated edges; associated with **lung, stomach, or genitourinary tract** malignancies. Can progress to **SCC**.

> **High-Yield Point — Tripe Palms**: Tripe palms (acanthosis palmaris) presents with a velvety, rugose, ridged thickening of the palms resembling the lining of bovine foregut (tripe). It is a paraneoplastic sign associated most commonly with **lung carcinoma** (when tripe palms occurs alone) and **gastric carcinoma** (when tripe palms co-occurs with acanthosis nigricans). Up to **90% of cases** are associated with an underlying malignancy. The combination of tripe palms + acanthosis nigricans strongly suggests gastric carcinoma. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

> **Clinical Pearl — Bazex Syndrome (Acrokeratosis Paraneoplastica)**: Bazex syndrome presents with violaceous, psoriasiform keratoderma affecting the acral areas (ears, nose, fingers, toes, and nails) in the context of an underlying **upper aerodigestive tract SCC** (pharynx, larynx, esophagus, lung). It is distinct from Bazex-Dupré-Christol syndrome (a genodermatosis). Response to treatment of the underlying malignancy is the hallmark. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

---

### 9. Arsenical Keratosis

- **Small, corn-like areas of hyperkeratosis** on palms and soles.
- The lesions **enlarge, thicken, and increase in number**, spreading to the **dorsal surface of hands and feet**.
- **SCC can develop** and lead to ulceration.

> **High-Yield Point — Arsenical Keratoses**: Chronic arsenic exposure (via contaminated groundwater, occupational exposure, or Fowler's solution) produces punctate keratoses on palms and soles, diffuse pigmentary changes ("rain-drop" hypopigmentation on a hyperpigmented background), and Mees' lines on nails. Arsenical keratoses are **premalignant** — they can transform into **SCC** (and less commonly **BCC**). The latency between exposure and malignancy can be **10–20 years or more**. Concurrent development of internal malignancies (bladder, lung, liver) should also be screened for. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia; Andrews')

## Corrections & Verification Notes

- **"HOV" (p. 183, Infections list)** → **"HPV"** — The source text lists "HOV" under infections causing acquired PPK. This is an OCR/typographical error; the correct term is **HPV (human papillomavirus)**, which causes verrucous keratotic lesions on palms and soles. "HOV" is not a recognized pathogen and is a character-substitution artifact (P → O). (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Glucan" (p. 183, Drugs list)** → **Verification/Possible error:** The source lists "Glucan" as a drug causing acquired PPK. In standard references, **glucan** is not commonly listed as a cause of drug-induced PPK. The agent could not definitively identify a clear alternative (though "5-FU" / fluorouracil is listed separately, and some beta-glucan supplements have been rarely reported). This is retained as printed but flagged for reader verification. The more commonly cited drug-induced PPK triggers include **tegafur** (a 5-FU prodrug), **bleomycin**, **practolol**, **MEK inhibitors**, and **multikinase inhibitors** (sorafenib, sunitinib). (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Chloracnegens" (p. 183, Chemicals list)** → **Verification:** The term "chloracnegens" is used in the source to refer to chemicals that cause chloracne (e.g., dioxins, PCBs, polychlorinated dibenzofurans). While chloracne itself is characterized by comedones and cysts rather than palmoplantar hyperkeratosis, some chloracnegens can produce generalized keratinization abnormalities including palmoplantar changes. The term is uncommon but technically valid. (Ref: Rook's; Andrews')

- **"Aquagenic" listed both under "Dermatoses" and as a separate entity (pp. 182–183)** → **Clarification:** On page 182 (Q25 classification), aquagenic keratoderma is listed as a separate category of acquired PPK. On page 183 (Q26), it appears at the bottom of the dermatoses list and then again as a standalone entity with full description. This double listing likely reflects the overlapping classification — aquagenic PPK is best classified as a **distinct entity** rather than a dermatosis per se, as it represents an **exaggerated physiological reaction** of eccrine ducts to water immersion, often with a genetic basis (CFTR heterozygosity). (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Cyclooxygenase two inhibitors" (p. 183, Aquagenic PPK section)** → **Clarification:** The source states aquagenic PPK is "associated with use of cyclooxygenase two inhibitors and cystic fibrosis." While COX-2 inhibitors (celecoxib, rofecoxib) have been reported in isolated case reports as triggering aquagenic wrinkling, the **principal and most important association** of aquagenic PPK is with **cystic fibrosis (CFTR mutations)** — aquagenic wrinkling of palms is used as a clinical screening test for CF carriers. The COX-2 inhibitor association is a minor, case-reportlevel finding. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)

- **"Norwegian scabies" (p. 183, Infections list)** → **Updated terminology:** The preferred modern term is **crusted scabies** (rather than "Norwegian scabies"), as the historical eponym is considered geographically misleading. The condition is caused by massive *Sarcoptes scabiei* infestation, typically in immunocompromised patients, and can produce hyperkeratotic crusted plaques on the palms and soles mimicking PPK. (Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)
