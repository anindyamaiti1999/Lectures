# Q34. Describe the subtypes and management of porokeratosis.

**Chapter**: Disorders of Keratinization | **Source Pages**: 189–190 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Definition

Porokeratosis is a **heterogeneous group of keratinization disorders** characterized by the presence of the **coronoid lamella** — a histopathological hallmark consisting of a **column of parakeratotic cells** at the marginate keratotic border of the lesion.

- Clinically, the disorders are characterized by **marginate keratotic lesions** with a raised, fine, keratotic, elevated border and a central groove.

---

### Pathophysiology

- **DSAP**: **Loss of heterozygosity** at **12q**
- **Porokeratosis of Mibelli (POM)**: **(HPV 66, 14)** heterozygous **germline mutations** in the **mevalonate pathway** leading to **impaired cytoskeleton signaling** and **intracellular signaling**
- **Linear porokeratosis**: **Second-hit somatic mutations**
- **p53 overexpressed** in the coronoid lamella
- **Cytogenetic anomalies** in fibroblasts

> **Mevalonate Pathway Connection**: The genes implicated in porokeratosis — **MVK** (mevalonate kinase), **PMVK** (phosphomevalonate kinase), **MVD** (mevalonate diphosphate decarboxylase), and **FDPS** (farnesyl diphosphate synthase) — all encode enzymes in the **cholesterol biosynthesis (mevalonate) pathway**. This explains the rationale for **topical cholesterol and lovastatin** as pathway-directed therapy, replenishing the deficient end-product while inhibiting upstream toxic metabolite accumulation.

---

### Clinical Features — Subtypes (8 Variants)

#### 1. Disseminated Superficial Actinic Porokeratosis (DSAP)
- **Most common** variant
- Multiple lesions **up to 10 mm**
- Predominantly found in **sun-exposed areas**
- **Middle-aged**; in women — **extremities**
- **Papules surrounded by a keratotic ridge**
- Lesions **provoked by photochemotherapy and radiotherapy**
- **D/D**: Actinic keratosis

#### 2. Disseminated Superficial Porokeratosis of Immunosuppression
- Reported after **transplant** and in **AIDS**
- History of **sun exposure ±**

#### 3. Disseminated Superficial Porokeratosis of Childhood
- **AD / sporadic**
- **Widely disseminated flat lesions**
- Begin in childhood **(5–10 years)**
- **Palmoplantar lesions** — porokeratosis palmaris et plantaris disseminata
- **Craniosynostosis**
- **Congenital malformations**

#### 4. Porokeratosis of Mibelli
- **AD (childhood)**; sporadic **(later onset)**
- **Single, scanty, large lesions**
- **Annular dry plaques** surrounded by a raised, fine, keratotic, elevated border with a **central groove**
- Predilection for **limbs**
- **Centrifugal spread**
- Centre is usually **atrophic** but may be **hyperkeratotic**
- **Face, scalp, genitalia, oral mucosa**, and **cornea** may be affected

#### 5. Giant Porokeratosis
- Lesion size **≥20 cm** with a surrounding elevated edge of **1 cm**
- **Rare**
- Predilection for **foot**
- **Malignant transformation** risk

#### 6. Palmoplantar Porokeratosis of Mantoux
- **Annular lesions** of palms and soles
- **Punctate keratodermas** with **absence of marginate** keratotic border

#### 7. Linear Porokeratosis
- Follows **lines of Blaschko**
- **Highest risk of malignant transformation** among all subtypes

#### 8. *Porokeratosis ptychotropica*
- Confined to **skin folds**
- **Brownish to reddish macules or plaques** usually develop symmetrically on **perianal region / scrotum**
- **Highly pruritic**
- **Coexists with DSAP and LP** (linear porokeratosis)

> **High-Yield Note**: **Linear porokeratosis** carries the **highest risk** of malignant transformation (squamous cell carcinoma) among all porokeratosis variants. Giant porokeratosis also has significant SCC risk. DSAP has the lowest malignant potential despite being the most common.

---

### Investigations

#### Histopathological Examination (HPE)

- Changes best seen on **edge of lesion** when **cut at right angles**
- **Stratum corneum hyperkeratotic**
- At the raised border — a **column of poorly staining parakeratotic stratum corneum cells** — the **coronoid lamella** — is seen running through surrounding normally staining cells
- **Granular layer absent** beneath the parakeratotic column
- The **underlying keratinocytes are large, vacuolated**, with some **dyskeratotic and pleomorphic** cells
- **Dense lichenoid lymphocytic infiltrate** with **colloid bodies** and **amyloid material**
- Involvement of **acrosyringia** (eccrine duct orifice)
- The central area is usually **atrophic** but can show **hyperkeratosis**
- **Papillary dermis fibrotic** and contains **melanophages**

> **Key Histopathological Feature**: The **coronoid lamella** — a **thin column of closely stacked parakeratotic cells** arising from an invagination of the epidermis at the lesion margin — is the **sine qua non** of porokeratosis. It corresponds clinically to the characteristic raised, threadlike peripheral ridge. The **absence of the granular layer** directly beneath the coronoid lamella is another diagnostic criterion.

---

### Treatment

#### Topical
- **Photoprotection** (essential — DSAP is UV-provoked)
- **Keratolytics** — salicylic acid
- **Tacalcitol** (vitamin D analog)
- **Retinoids** — topical tretinoin
- **5-Fluorouracil** (topical)
- **Imiquimod**

#### Systemic
- **Oral etretinate** / acitretin (systemic retinoid)

#### Procedural
- **Cryotherapy**
- **CO₂ laser**
- **Pulsed dye laser**
- **Deep dermabrasion**

#### Pathway-Directed Therapy
- **Topical application of cholesterol and lovastatin** — directly addresses the mevalonate pathway defect by replenishing cholesterol and blocking accumulation of toxic pathway metabolites

> **Clinical Pearl**: The therapeutic rationale for **cholesterol + lovastatin** is unique to porokeratosis among keratinization disorders. Since the causative mutations (MVK, PMVK, MVD, FDPS) impair the mevalonate-to-cholesterol biosynthetic pathway, topical cholesterol **replenishes the depleted end product** while lovastatin (an HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor) paradoxically helps by **reducing accumulation of toxic mevalonate pathway intermediates** proximal to the enzymatic block.

> **Exam Tip**: Accumulation of mevalonate pathway toxic metabolites → impaired keratinocyte differentiation → coronoid lamella formation. This links pathogenesis directly to the characteristic histopathology.

## Corrections & Verification Notes

- **"POM: (HPV 66, 14)" — Clarification needed** — The source lists "POM-(HPV 66,14)" adjacent to the mevalonate pathway mutations. This notation is ambiguous. HPV (human papillomavirus) types 66 and 14 are **not** established causative agents of porokeratosis of Mibelli. Standard references identify **mevalonate pathway gene mutations** (MVK, PMVK, MVD, FDPS) as the genetic basis of POM. The "HPV 66,14" likely refers to the **chromosome loci** or is an OCR/scanning artifact. Per Bolognia *Dermatology* (4th ed.), POM is caused by heterozygous germline mutations with somatic second-hit loss in the mevalonate pathway. HPV has been reported in isolated porokeratosis lesions as a coincidental finding but is not etiologic. The text has been preserved as written but should be interpreted as chromosomal locus notation rather than HPV type attribution. (Ref: Bolognia *Dermatology*; Rook's)

- **"Loss of heterozygosity at 12q" for DSAP** — Verified. DSAP maps to **chromosome 12q21.2–24.1** (DSAP1 locus) and **15q25.1–26.1** (DSAP2 locus). Loss of heterozygosity at 12q is the most well-characterized genetic mechanism. Additional loci at **1p31.3–36.3**, **18p11.3**, and **16q24.1–24.3** have also been identified. (Ref: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, 9th ed.)

- **Mevalonate pathway genes (MVK, PMVK, MVD, FDPS)** — Verified. These four genes encoding sequential enzymes in the mevalonate pathway have been identified through whole-exome sequencing studies as causative genes in multiple porokeratosis variants. The source correctly identifies the mevalonate pathway as central to pathogenesis. (Ref: Bolognia *Dermatology*, 4th ed.; Zhang et al., *Nature Genetics* 2012)

- **"p53 overexpressed in coronoid lamella"** — Verified. Immunohistochemical studies demonstrate **p53 overexpression** in keratinocytes of the coronoid lamella, suggesting that porokeratosis represents a **pre-malignant clonal keratinocyte disorder**. This finding supports the recognized risk of **SCC transformation**, particularly in linear and giant porokeratosis. (Ref: Rook's)

- **"Palmoplantar porokeratosis of Mantoux"** — The source uses "Mantoux." The correct eponym is **Mantoux** (porokeratose palmaire et plantaire disséminée de Mantoux). This is a valid, though uncommonly used, eponymous term. It should not be confused with the Mantoux tuberculin skin test. (Ref: Bolognia *Dermatology*)

- **"Porokeratosis ptychotropica"** — Verified. *Ptychotropica* (from Greek *ptyche* = fold, *tropos* = turning toward) — describes the predilection for skin folds. First described by Lucker et al. in 1995. The symmetric perianal/scrotal distribution and coexistence with DSAP and linear porokeratosis are correctly noted. (Ref: Rook's)

- **Linear porokeratosis — highest malignant transformation** — Verified. Linear porokeratosis has the **highest rate of malignant transformation** among all porokeratosis variants, estimated at **7–11%** in some series. The SCC risk is attributed to the larger burden of mutant keratinocyte clones. Giant porokeratosis also carries significant SCC risk (~**7.5%**). DSAP has the lowest risk (<1%). (Ref: Bolognia *Dermatology*; Andrews')

- **"Acrosyringia"** — Source states "involvement of acrosyringia." Verified: the coronoid lamella characteristically arises from the **intraepidermal eccrine duct (acrosyringium)**, which is the histologic basis for the original name "porokeratosis" (from Greek *poros* = pore, referring to the sweat pore). Despite the name, the disorder is not limited to sweat ducts. (Ref: Bolognia *Dermatology*)

- **"Colloid bodies and amyloid material"** — Source mentions these in the dermal infiltrate description. Verified: colloid (Civatte) bodies result from apoptotic keratinocytes and can be seen in the lichenoid infiltrate zone. **Amyloid** deposition is reported in some lesions of porokeratosis and is thought to be secondary (derived from apoptotic keratinocyte filaments, similar to macular amyloidosis). (Ref: Rook's)

- **"Oral etretinate"** — The source lists etretinate as the systemic retinoid. **Etretinate** has been **withdrawn** from most markets and replaced by its active metabolite **acitretin**. The source text likely reflects older prescribing practice. Current standard of care is **acitretin** (0.5 mg/kg/day). (Ref: Andrews' *Diseases of the Skin*, 13th ed.)

- **"Tacalcitol"** — Verified. **Tacalcitol** (1α,24-dihydroxyvitamin D₃) is a topical vitamin D₃ analog used in porokeratosis, particularly DSAP. It modulates keratinocyte proliferation and differentiation. Alternative vitamin D analogs include calcipotriol. (Ref: Rook's)
