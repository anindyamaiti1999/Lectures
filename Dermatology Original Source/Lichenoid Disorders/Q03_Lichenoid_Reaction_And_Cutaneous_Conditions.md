# Q3. Define lichenoid reaction and cutaneous conditions showing this phenomenon.

**Chapter**: Lichen Planus (Book Chapter 45 / Chapter 53) | **Source Pages**: 791–792 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Definition

**"Lichenoid"** refers to resemblance to **"lichen planus."**

A **lichenoid tissue reaction (LTR)** or **interface dermatitis** refers to a histopathological finding of an inflammatory infiltrate that **obscures the DEJ** (dermoepidermal junction).

---

### Classification

Histologically, lichenoid lesions are classified based on predominant inflammatory cells involved:

| Classification Basis | Types |
|---|---|
| **Cell density** | Cell-rich vs. Cell-poor |
| **Predominant cell type** | Neutrophilic, Lymphocytic, Lymphohistiocytic |

---

### Pathogenesis

- **Cell-mediated cytotoxicity** plays a central role: **CD8+ cytotoxic T cells, granzymes (perforin)**
- Pathogenic mechanisms at the basal layer:
  - **Liquefactive/vacuolar degeneration** of basal cells
  - **Formation of civatte bodies** (colloid bodies — apoptotic keratinocytes)
  - **Basal cell damage** leading to:
    - **Apoptosis**
    - **Necrosis**
  - **Melanin incontinence** — frequent due to release of melanin into dermis → **pigment changes**

---

### Conditions Showing Lichenoid Tissue Reaction (LTR)

*(Refer Q1 for full enumeration)*

#### 1. Lichen Planus (LP) — *(See Q2)*

- The **prototype** lichenoid dermatosis

#### 2. Lupus Erythematosus

- **Damage more focal** than LP
- **SLE**: Prominent vacuolar change and minimal cell death
- **DLE**: More cell death, superficial and deep infiltrate, mucin
- **Both**: Follicular plugging, BM thickening, and civatte bodies
  - *(Note: Source states "BM thinning" — see Corrections below)*

#### 3. Lichenoid Drug Reactions

- **Causative agents**: ACE inhibitors, amlodipine, beta blockers, NSAIDs, carbamazepine, gold, etc.
- **Histological features**: Mimic LP but with additional distinguishing features:
  - **Eosinophils** and **parakeratosis** (absent in classic LP)
  - **Higher melanin incontinence**
  - **Dermal infiltrate less dense** than LP

#### 4. GVHD (Graft-versus-Host Disease)

- **In chronic phase** — LP-like features
- **Dense inflammatory infiltrate** (prominent pigment incontinence and columnar epidermal necrosis)

#### 5. AIDS-Related Photoeruptions

- **Lichenoid photoeruptions** in immunodeficient patients

#### 6. PLEVA (Pityriasis Lichenoides et Varioliformis Acuta)

- **Lichenoid-shaped dermal infiltrate** with epidermal necrosis
- **Wedge-shaped** pattern
- Pigment changes

#### 7. Late Secondary Syphilis

- **Lichenoid pattern** where the infiltrate is composed primarily of **plasma cells**
- Found on **palms and soles**

#### 8. Paraneoplastic Pemphigus

- **Chronic stages**: Features include **EM-like changes** with **suprabasal acantholysis** and **clefting**
- Sometimes **subepidermal clefting**

## Corrections & Verification Notes

### 🚨 Factual & Medical Errors

- **"BM thinning" under DLE** (Page 791) → **BM thickening**
  DLE (Discoid Lupus Erythematosus) is characterized by **prominent basement membrane thickening** (demonstrable with PAS staining), NOT thinning. Basement membrane thickening is in fact one of the key histopathological differentiators between DLE and LP (where the basement membrane is obscured/disrupted but not thickened). (Ref: Rook's — Lupus Erythematosus; Bolognia — DLE Histopathology)

- **"BM thinning" listed under Both (SLE and DLE)** — The source appears to use "BM thinning" as a shared feature of SLE and DLE. This is inaccurate for DLE. In SLE, the basement membrane zone changes are more subtle (focal vacuolar interface), while in DLE, the BMZ is characteristically **thickened** on PAS staining. Neither shows "BM thinning" as a defining feature. (Ref: Bolognia — Comparative histopathology of LE subtypes)

### 🔍 Typographical & OCR Errors

- **"EM-like changed with supra basal acantholysis"** (Page 792, Paraneoplastic Pemphigus) → **"EM-like changes with suprabasal acantholysis"**
  "changed" is a typographical error for "changes." "Supra basal" should be written as the single compound word **"suprabasal."** (Ref: Standard dermatopathology terminology)

- **"sub epidermal clefting"** (Page 792) → **"subepidermal clefting"**
  Standard medical terminology uses the compound form without a space. (Ref: Bolognia, Rook's)
