# Q6. Write a brief note on lichen planus pigmentosus and its differentials.

**Chapter**: Lichen Planus (Book Chapter 45 / Chapter 53) | **Source Pages**: 794 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Definition & Overview

**Lichen planus pigmentosus (LPPig)** is a rare, chronic, acquired **macular pigmentary variant** of lichen planus characterized by chronic dark brown to slate-gray **macular pigmentation**.

- Sometimes considered an **abortive variant** of LP — representing a forme fruste in which the inflammatory phase is subtle or subclinical, and the predominant finding is **pigmentary incontinence**
- **Acquired dermal macular hyperpigmentation**
- **Demographics**: More common in **darker skin phototypes** (Fitzpatrick III–VI) — particularly in **Asians, Middle Easterners, Hispanics**, and **Genetic Asians**
- **Sex predilection**: **Females > Males**

---

### Etiology: Suspected Agents & Triggers

| Category | Agents |
|---|---|
| **Cosmetics** | Fragrances, henna, hair dyes |
| **Mustard oil** | Contains photosensitizer **allyl isothiocyanate** *(source erroneously reads "allyl thiocyanate")* |
| **Amla oil** | |
| **Sunlight** | UV exposure — particularly in sun-exposed areas |
| **Elemental contact** | Ni and Ni-containing foods |
| **Hormonal** | Perimenopausal women |
| **Racial/genetic** | Darker skin types (Fitzpatrick III–VI); Genetic Asians |

---

### Pathogenesis

- Immunopathogenesis of LPPig characterized by **altered immune responses mediated by CD8+ T lymphocytes**
- These lymphocytes recognize and attack **epidermal basal melanocytes/keratinocytes** → causing significant **pigmentary incontinence**
- **Abortive variant** — i.e., rapidly inflammatory infiltrate → followed by regression, leaving **dermal melanin** for months to years
- **Mechanism**:
  - Cell-mediated cytotoxic attack on **basal melanocytes** → rapid basal vacuolization
  - Heavy **dermal pigmentary incontinence** — melanin released into the dermis is phagocytosed by **dermal melanophages**
  - Melanophages persist in the upper dermis for **months to years**, producing the characteristic persistent slate-gray to brown discoloration

---

### Clinical Features

- **Morphology**: Small, ill-defined, round to oval, **slate-gray to brownish-black macules** which can coalesce into larger areas of hyperpigmentation
- **Typically asymptomatic**; mild pruritus may occur
- **Distribution**:
  - **Commonly affected areas**: Face, mask (temporal, preauricular), temples, and forehead
  - **Flexural areas**: Axillae, inguinal folds
  - Others: Trunk and extremities
  - **Sun-exposed areas**: Face, neck
- **Condition progresses over time**

---

### Variants

| Variant | Description |
|---|---|
| **LPP Inversus** | Affects **flexural regions**, sparing sun-exposed areas; friction-related trigger |
| **Blaschkoid / Zosteriform** | Follows lines of Blaschko; represents **somatic mosaicism patterns**; Men > Women |
| **Generalized** | Widespread involvement |
| **Reticular** | Net-like or reticulate pattern of pigmentation |

> **Note**: Associated with **genetic mosaicism** in Blaschkoid/zosteriform patterns.

---

### Associations

- **Lichen planopilaris / Oral LP / FFA** (Frontal Fibrosing Alopecia) *(source erroneously reads "FFP" → corrected to FFA)*
- **Vitiligo**
- **Hypothyroidism**
- **Lupus**

---

### Differential Diagnoses

| Differential | Key Distinguishing Features |
|---|---|
| **Ashy dermatosis (Erythema dyschromicum perstans — EDP)** | Blue-gray macules with characteristic **elevated erythematous border** in active phase; typically spares face and flexures (unlike LPPig which favors these areas); histology shows basal vacuolation with melanophages similar to LPPig |
| **Riehl's melanosis** | Pigmented contact dermatitis; slate-brown reticulate pigmentation on face/neck; history of cosmetic/fragrance contact allergy; follicular accentuation |
| **Ochronosis (Exogenous)** | Blue-black pigmentation from prolonged hydroquinone or phenol use; banana-shaped ochre deposits on histology |
| **FDE (Fixed Drug Eruption)** | Recurrent round, violaceous-to-brown patch at **identical site** with each drug re-exposure; well-demarcated; temporal relationship to drug ingestion |
| **Idiopathic eruptive macular pigmentation (IEMP)** | Brown macules on trunk in children/young adults; NO preceding inflammation; histology shows only basal hyperpigmentation without interface dermatitis |
| **Acrokeratosis of Bazex** | Paraneoplastic acrokeratosis; violaceous, psoriasiform plaques on acral sites (ears, nose, fingers, toes); associated with upper aerodigestive tract malignancy |

---

### Histopathology

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| **Epidermis** | **Epidermal atrophy** — thinning of the epidermis |
| **Basal layer** | **Vacuolar degeneration** of the basal cell layer |
| **Dermal infiltrate** | Band-like or **lichenoid perivascular lymphocytic infiltrate** in the papillary dermis |
| **Melanophages** | **Prominent dermal melanophages** — pigmentary incontinence and melanin deposition in the papillary dermis (hallmark finding) |
| **Immunofluorescence** | **Epidermal atrophy and hyperkeratosis** |
| **Globular IgM** | Globular deposition of **IgM** in papillary dermis and DEJ |

> **Histological Pearl**: The combination of **basal vacuolation**, **band-like lymphocytic infiltrate**, and **prominent melanophages** in the upper dermis — with minimal epidermal changes — is the histopathological hallmark that distinguishes LPPig from other pigmentary dermatoses.

---

### Prognosis

- **Course**: Chronic and progressive with **remissions and exacerbations**
- Treatment aimed to **halt inflammatory process** and **decrease aesthetic appearance concerns**
- Pigmentation may persist for months to years even after inflammation resolves due to persistent dermal melanophages

---

### Management

#### Trigger Avoidance
- **Avoidance of triggers**: Mustard oil, amla oil, cosmetic fragrances, hair dyes, excessive sun exposure

#### Topical Therapy
- **Topical corticosteroids**: Medium-to-high potency CS *(Note: Source erroneously groups tacrolimus, retinoids, and sunscreens under "CS" — these are distinct therapeutic categories)*
- **Topical calcineurin inhibitors**: Tacrolimus 0.1% — preferred for facial involvement and to avoid CS-related atrophy
- **Topical retinoids**: Tretinoin — promotes epidermal turnover and pigment reduction
- **Sunscreens**: Strict broad-spectrum photoprotection (SPF 50+) — essential adjunctive measure

#### Systemic Therapy
- **CS-Pulsed/Continuous**: Oral mini-pulse dexamethasone or low-dose prednisolone — for progressive or widespread disease
- **Dapsone**: Anti-inflammatory; useful for widespread disease
- **Isotretinoin**: Low-dose; normalizes keratinization and reduces pigmentation

## Corrections & Verification Notes

### 🚨 Factual & Medical Errors

- **Classification Error**: Source text states *"Topically: Medium-to-high potency CS (Tacrolimus, retinoids, and sunscreens)"* — this erroneously categorizes **tacrolimus** (a calcineurin inhibitor), **retinoids**, and **sunscreens** under "corticosteroids (CS)". These are **distinct therapeutic classes**:
  - **CS** = Corticosteroids (e.g., clobetasol, betamethasone)
  - **Tacrolimus** = Calcineurin inhibitor (macrolide immunosuppressant)
  - **Retinoids** = Vitamin A derivatives
  - **Sunscreens** = Photoprotective agents
  Restructured into separate categories with correct drug class labeling. (Ref: Bolognia — Management of Pigmentary Disorders; Rook's — Lichen Planus Pigmentosus Treatment)

- **Acronym OCR Error**: Source reads **"Lichen planopilaris/oral LP/FFP"** → **"FFP"** is an OCR error for **FFA (Frontal Fibrosing Alopecia)**. FFP does not exist as a dermatological association. LPPig is known to be associated with FFA, classical LPP, and oral LP. (Ref: Rook's — Lichen Planus Pigmentosus Associations)

### 🔍 Typographical & OCR Errors

- **Chemical name error**: Source reads **"Allyl thiocyanate"** → correct chemical name is **allyl isothiocyanate** (CH₂=CHCH₂NCS). Allyl isothiocyanate is the primary irritant and photosensitizing compound in mustard oil (*Brassica* seed oils). The prefix "iso-" is critical — allyl thiocyanate (CH₂=CHCH₂SCN) is a different compound with different biological activity. (Ref: Bolognia — Contact Dermatitis and Photosensitizers)

### 📝 Grammar & Formatting

- **Treatment section restructured**: Source lists all topical agents as a continuous sentence under "CS". Reformatted into distinct therapeutic categories (CS, calcineurin inhibitors, retinoids, photoprotection, systemic) for clinical accuracy and exam readability.

- **Differential diagnosis table**: Source lists differentials as simple bullets. Reformatted as a comparative table with key distinguishing features to facilitate exam-oriented pattern recognition.

- **EDP vs LPPig distinction added**: The critical clinical differentiator between Erythema dyschromicum perstans (Ashy dermatosis) and LPPig — EDP has a characteristic **elevated erythematous border** in the active phase and typically **spares face and flexures**, while LPPig **lacks borders** and **favors flexures and sun-exposed head/neck** — has been integrated into the differential diagnosis table as a high-yield exam point. (Ref: Bolognia — Disorders of Hyperpigmentation; Andrews' — Ashy Dermatosis)
