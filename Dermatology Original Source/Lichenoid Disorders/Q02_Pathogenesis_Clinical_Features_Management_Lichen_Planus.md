# Q2. Briefly discuss pathogenesis, clinical features, and management of lichen planus.

**Chapter**: Lichen Planus (Book Chapter 45 / Chapter 53) | **Source Pages**: 789–791 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Etymology & Definition

- **"Lichen"** means tree moss; **"planus"** means flat
- Lichen planus (LP) is an inflammatory dermatosis affecting ectodermal-derived tissue, characterized by **flat-topped, polygonal, purple papules** over the body
- A **T cell-mediated autoimmune disease** possibly targeting basal keratinocytes due to various triggers

---

### Pathophysiology

- **Etiology**: Unknown
- **Pathogenesis**: Cell-mediated autoimmune attack on basal keratinocytes
  - **CD8+ cytotoxic T cells** and **γδ T cells** present in lymphocytic infiltrate
  - Cell-mediated cytotoxicity plays a central role: **CD8+ cytotoxic T cells, granzymes (perforin)**
- **HLA associations**: HLA-A3, HLA-B5, and HLA-5
  - *(Note: "HLA-5" likely represents HLA-DR5; see Corrections below)*

---

### Triggers

- **Causative organisms**: HCV, HBV, HHV-6, and HHV-7, VZV
- **Vaccines**: Hepatitis B
- **Drugs**:
  - H2 blockers
  - NSAIDs
  - Antibiotics
  - Antihypertensives
  - Anticonvulsants
  - Diuretics
  - Metals (met-als — line break across source text)
  - Imatinib
  - IVIg
  - Adalimumab
  - Etanercept
  - Antidepressants
- **Dental amalgam**
- **Betel nut**
- **Lichen planus-like contact dermatitis**
- **Radiotherapy**
- **Anxiety, stress, and depression**

---

### Clinical Features

#### Morphology — The **6 Ps**

> **P**ruritic, **P**olygonal, **P**lanar (flat-topped), **P**urple, **P**apules, **P**laques

- **Primary**: Firm, shiny, polygonal, red to violaceous papules, 1–3 mm with thin white lines in a lacy network on surface — **Wickham's striae**
- **Shape/Configuration**:
  - **Grouped**
  - **Annular** (most common: penis)
  - **Linear**

#### Mucosal Involvement

- **Wickham's striae**: White streaks forming lacy network (burning discomfort associated with hot and spicy foods)
- **Tongue**: Irregular fixed white plaques
- **Pruritus**: Rubbing

#### Pigmentary Changes

- **Resolve**: Greyish-brown pigmentation due to deposition of melanin in superficial dermis
- Around hair follicles: **Spring** – KP: Lichen planopilaris
  - *(Note: "Spring – KP" is an error; see Corrections below)*
- **Flexors**

#### Complications

- **Hair**: LP pilaris, alopecia due to follicular destruction
- **Nails**: 20-nail dystrophy, pits, longitudinal melanonychia, squamous cell carcinoma, cicatricial conjunctivities, lacrimal canalicular obstruction
  - *(Note: "conjunctivities" is a typo for "conjunctivitis")*
- **Mucosa**: SUHK, longitudinal nails, and pterygium
  - *(Note: "SUHK" — abbreviation unclear from source; see Corrections below)*
  - *(Note: "longitudinal nails" is an error; see Corrections below)*

#### Associations

- Ulcerative colitis
- Vitiligo
- Dermatomyositis
- Morphea
- Lichen sclerosus
- Paraneoplastic pemphigus
- DM (diabetes mellitus)
- AA (alopecia areata)

### Histopathology

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| **Stratum corneum** | Hyperkeratosis with ortho/parakeratosis *(see Corrections)* |
| **Granular layer** | Wedge-shaped hypergranulosis (resulting in Wickham's striae) |
| **Rete ridges** | Saw-toothed elongated rete ridges |
| **Epidermis** | Acanthosis |
| **Dermis** | Melanophages in upper dermis with pigment incontinence due to damage to basal cells |
| **Dermal infiltrate** | Band-like dermal lymphocytic infiltrate |
| **Colloid/Civatte bodies** | Necrotic keratinocytes in lower epidermis and papillary dermis — apoptotic, dyskeratotic; **colloid**, hyaline, or **PAS-positive** (disease-resistant) |
| **Homogeneous and eosinophilic** | Lying in clusters |
| **Aggregation** | Results in perforation of epidermis with subsequent transepidermal elimination |
| **Clefts** | Artificial separation between epidermis and dermis — **Max Joseph spaces** |
| **DIF** | Globular deposits of IgG, IgM around DEJ and occasionally C3 *(see Corrections)* |

### Investigations

- **HPE** (histopathological examination)
- **DIF**: Globular deposits of IgG, IgM and occasionally C3 around DEJ
- **Dermoscopy**
- **Blood investigations**: CBC, URM, LFT, ESR, and CRP

---

### Management

#### First-Line

- **Limited cutaneous LP** (resolves 1–2 years): Very potent topical corticosteroids (TCS)-like **clobetasol propionate 0.05%**
- **Widespread cutaneous LP**: **Prednisolone 0.5–1 mg/kg/day**

#### Second-Line

- **Methotrexate** 15–20 mg once a week for 12 weeks; reevaluate
- **Acitretin** 30 mg/day for 12 weeks; reevaluate
- **PUVA/UVB** 2–5 times/week alone/+ retinoids
- **JAK inhibitors** (tofacitinib and baricitinib) for 8 weeks

#### Third-Line

- **Antimalarial**
- **Cyclosporine**
- **Azathioprine**
- **MMF** (mycophenolate mofetil)

## Corrections & Verification Notes

### 🚨 Medical & Factual Errors

- **"Hyperkeratosis with ortho/parakeratosis"** → **Hyperkeratosis with orthokeratosis (without parakeratosis)**
  Classic lichen planus exhibits dense orthokeratosis. **Parakeratosis is NOT a feature of classic LP**; its presence suggests a lichenoid drug reaction, LP-lupus overlap, or other lichenoid dermatosis. (Ref: Rook's Chapter on LP histopathology; Bolognia — Lichenoid Dermatoses)

- **DIF description: "Globular deposits of IgG, IgM around DEJ"** → **Shaggy fibrinogen band along the BMZ** is the hallmark DIF finding of LP. Globular/cytoid body fluorescence represents IgM (and less commonly IgA) deposition within colloid bodies (apoptotic keratinocytes), NOT IgG. The source conflates these two patterns. (Ref: Bolognia — Direct Immunofluorescence in LP; Rook's)

- **"HLA-5"** → **HLA-DR5** or **HLA-B5**
  "HLA-5" is a non-standard designation. The recognized HLA associations of lichen planus include **HLA-A3, HLA-B5, HLA-B7, HLA-Bw15, HLA-DR1, and HLA-DR5**. The source text should be standardized to the correct nomenclature. (Ref: Rook's — Immunogenetics of LP)

### 🔍 Typographical & OCR Errors

- **"Spring – KP: Lichen planopilaris"** (Page 790) → This appears to be a garbled OCR/layout artifact. In context, the source is describing **spiny follicular keratotic papules** (KP-like) around hair follicles in LP. "Spring" is not a recognized dermatological term here. The intended meaning is: **"Spiny keratotic follicular papules (KP-like): Lichen planopilaris."** (Ref: Clinical context)

- **"longitudinal nails"** (Page 790) → **longitudinal ridging** (of nails)
  Nails do not come in "longitudinal" form. The correct term is **longitudinal ridging** or **longitudinal splitting** — a recognized nail finding in LP. (Ref: Andrews' — Nail changes in LP)

- **"conjunctivities"** → **conjunctivitis**
  Simple spelling error; "conjunctivities" is not a medical term.

- **"SUHK"** — This abbreviation appears on page 790 in the context of mucosal complications. The meaning of this abbreviation is **unclear from the source text**; it may be a garbled OCR rendering. It is listed alongside "longitudinal nails, and pterygium" under Mucosa complications. *Interpretation withheld to avoid hallucination; requires user verification against original text.*
