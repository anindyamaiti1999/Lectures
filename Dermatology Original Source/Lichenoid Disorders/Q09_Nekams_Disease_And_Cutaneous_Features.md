# Q9. Describe Nekam's disease and its cutaneous features.

**Chapter**: Lichen Planus (Book Chapter 45 / Chapter 53) | **Source Pages**: 796 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Definition & Historical Context

**Nekam's disease**, formally known as **Keratosis Lichenoides Chronica (KLC)**, is a **rare, chronic, progressive dermatosis** characterized by violaceous keratotic papules and nodules arranged in distinctive **linear, reticular, or striped patterns**. It was first described by the Hungarian dermatologist **Lajos Nékám** in **1938** and later further characterized by **Margolis** and colleagues in **1972**, who proposed the current nomenclature "keratosis lichenoides chronica."

---

### Synonyms & Eponyms

KLC is known by several names reflecting its morphological and historical identity:

| Synonym | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| **Keratosis Lichenoides Chronica (KLC)** | Preferred current term |
| **Nekam's disease** | Eponymous designation (Lajos Nékám, 1938) |
| **Keratosis striata lichenoides** | Emphasizes the characteristic linear/striated pattern |
| **Lichen ruber moniliformis** | "Moniliform" = bead-like/necklace-like arrangement of papules |
| **Lichen verrucosus et reticularis** | Describes the verrucous and reticular morphology |

---

### Demographics

- **Age**: Primarily affects **young adults** (20–40 years)
- **Sex**: Slight **male predominance**
- **Rarity**: Extremely rare; fewer than 100 cases reported in the literature

---

### Variant of LP

KLC is classified as a **variant of lichen planus** — it shares histopathological features of lichenoid interface dermatitis with LP but has a distinct clinical morphology, a more chronic/progressive course, and a characteristic reticular pattern that distinguishes it from classic LP.

- **Occasionally affects children** (rare)

---

### Clinical Features

#### Cutaneous Features

- **Morphology**: **Violaceous, keratotic papules** arranged in **linear, reticular, or plaque forms**
- **Distribution**: Lesions typically found on the **trunk, buttocks, and extremities**
- **Surface**: **Reddish verrucous papules** with **hyperkeratotic plugs** which are difficult to remove, revealing **irregular indentations** upon removal (leaving "bleeding craters")
- **Pattern**: Striking **linear, cord-like, or net-like (reticular)** arrangement following cleavage lines
- **Extent**: **Symmetrical** distribution; commonly involves **antecubital fossae, lumbosacral region, thighs, popliteal fossae, and extensors of forearms**

> **Exam Pearl**: The **linear, reticular, or moniliform (bead-like) arrangement** of keratotic papules along skin cleavage lines is the hallmark clinical feature of KLC that distinguishes it from classic LP, which typically presents as scattered polygonal papules.

#### Facial Features

- **Characteristic rosacea-like or seborrheic dermatitis-like midfacial erythema** with scaling and telangiectasia
- This facial involvement is a distinctive feature that can mimic rosacea or seborrheic dermatitis

#### Mucosal Involvement

- **Oral mucosa**: Recurrent **aphthae-like ulcers**, chronic erosions, erythrokeratotic papules
- **Genital lesions** may also occur
- **Chronic ulceration** of the oral cavity

#### Other Features

- **Erythrokeratotic papules** — widespread
- **Palmoplantar keratoderma** — hyperkeratosis of palms and soles
- **Nail changes**: Longitudinal ridging, subungual hyperkeratosis, yellow discoloration

---

### Histopathology

The histopathological features of KLC show **lichenoid interface dermatitis** with several characteristic findings:

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| **Infiltrate pattern** | **Band-like (lichenoid) and periappendageal** lymphocytic infiltrate |
| **Interface dermatitis** | **Lichenoid interface dermatitis** with basal vacuolar degeneration |
| **Stratum corneum** | Alternating **parakeratosis and orthokeratosis** (basket-weave and compact alternation) |
| **Basal layer** | **Liquefactive degeneration** of basal keratinocytes |
| **Civatte bodies** | **Colloid/apoptotic bodies** (dyskeratotic keratinocytes) present |
| **Dermal changes** | **Perivascular and periappendageal** chronic inflammatory infiltrate |
| **Vasculature** | **Dermal telangiectasia** — prominent dilated vessels in the papillary dermis |

> **Histology Pearl**: The combination of **lichenoid interface dermatitis** with **alternating ortho- and parakeratosis** and prominent **periappendageal infiltrate** distinguishes KLC histologically from classic LP (which shows pure orthokeratosis and a strictly band-like superficial infiltrate without periappendageal extension).

---

### Associations

KLC has been associated with several systemic conditions:

- **Glomerulonephritis** — renal involvement (immune complex-mediated)
- **Lymphoproliferative disorders** — increased risk of lymphoma
- **Hepatitis** — viral hepatitis (B and C)

> **Clinical Significance**: Patients with KLC should be screened for **renal disease** (urinalysis, serum creatinine), **hepatitis serology** (HBV/HCV), and periodically monitored for **lymphoproliferative disease** given the documented associations.

---

### Differential Diagnoses

| Differential | Key Distinguishing Feature |
|---|---|
| **Lichen planus** | Polygonal violaceous papules with Wickham's striae; does not form linear/reticular patterns along cleavage lines; DIF shows shaggy fibrinogen |
| **Darier's disease** | Greasy, crusted keratotic papules in seborrheic areas; pathognomonic corps ronds and grains on histology; autosomal dominant (*ATP2A2* mutation) |
| **Lichen amyloidosis** | Rippled, brownish papules on shins; Congo red-positive amyloid deposits in papillary dermis; intensely pruritic |
| **Lupus erythematosus** | Photosensitive distribution; prominent follicular plugging; thick PAS-positive BMZ; positive lupus band test |
| **Pityriasis rubra pilaris (PRP)** | Orange-red plaques with islands of sparing; nutmeg-grater follicular papules; waxy palmoplantar keratoderma |

---

### Treatment

KLC is notoriously **treatment-resistant**, and no single consistently effective therapy exists. Management typically requires **combination approaches**:

#### Mainstay Therapy
- **PUVA (psoralen + UVA)** and **systemic retinoids** (acitretin) — **combination of acitretin + phototherapy** is considered the most effective approach
- **NBUVB phototherapy** — alternative to PUVA

#### Other Reported Therapies
- **Photodynamic therapy (PDT)** — emerging option with some reported success
- **Topical therapies**: Calcipotriol (vitamin D analogue), tacrolimus (calcineurin inhibitor) — adjunctive role
- **Systemic retinoids** alone (acitretin, isotretinoin) — partial response in some cases

> **Treatment Pearl**: The combination of **acitretin + PUVA phototherapy** is the best-documented effective regimen for KLC in the literature. Monotherapy with either agent alone tends to produce incomplete or temporary responses.
