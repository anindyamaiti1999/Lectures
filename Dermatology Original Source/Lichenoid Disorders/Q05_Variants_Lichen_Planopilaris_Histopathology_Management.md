# Q5. Discuss variants of lichen planopilaris, along with histopathology and management.

**Chapter**: Lichen Planus (Book Chapter 45 / Chapter 53) | **Source Pages**: 793–794 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Definition & Epidemiology

**Lichen planopilaris (LPP)** is a variant of lichen planus that primarily affects **hair follicles**, leading to **scarring alopecia**. Also known as **"Follicular lichen planus"**, it is a rare cause of **progressive cicatricial alopecia** with inflammatory infiltrates targeting hair follicles.

- **Demographics**: Commonly seen in **females 40–60 years** of age
- **Classification**: Primary **lymphocytic cicatricial alopecia** (NAHRS classification)
- **Sites**: Lesions on **scalp, trunk, and medial aspects of proximal extremities**
- **Scalp involvement**: **40%** with scalp involvement develop **cicatricial alopecia**

---

### Clinical Variants

#### 1. Classical Lichen Planopilaris (LPP)

- **Early scalp lesions**: Violaceous papules, erythema, and desquamation
- **Late/active phases**: Follicular keratoses, and scarring → **scarring alopecia**
- Follicular papules at margins of existing alopecic patches

#### 2. Frontal Fibrosing Alopecia (FFA)

- **Postmenopausal women** (most common demographic)
- **Frontotemporal hairline recession** — progressive recession of the frontal and temporal hairline
- **Shiny, tight skin on forehead** — loss of follicular ostia, skin appears smooth and pale
- **Eyebrow involvement** (++) — loss of lateral eyebrows is often the first sign
- **"Lonely hair sign"** — isolated hairs at the leading edge of hairline recession
- Rare: **Body hair loss** (limbs, axillae, pubic area)
- **Men**: Can also be affected (frontal/temporal recession with eyebrow loss)

#### 3. Graham-Little-Piccardi-Lassueur Syndrome (GLPLS)

- **Classic triad**:
  1. **Cicatricial (scarring) alopecia** of the scalp — patchy, multifocal
  2. **Non-cicatricial hair loss** of axillae and pubis
  3. **Follicular spinous (keratotic) papules** on the trunk and extremities — spiny, keratotic follicular papules predominantly on scalp, trunk, and extremities
- **Linear distribution** may also occur

#### 4. Follicular LP Tumidus (Lichen Planopilaris Tumidus)

- **Rare**
- **Plaques studded with follicular papules**
- Often in **retroauricular areas**

---

### Pathogenesis

- **T cells target follicular antigen** in the **bulge area** of the outer root sheath (ORS) — the niche of hair follicle stem cells (**CD34+, K15+ keratinocyte stem cells**)
- **Immune-mediated destruction** of hair follicle stem cells (SC) → **irreversible follicular destruction**
- Loss of **immune privilege** of the hair follicle bulge region
- **Inhibition of hair follicle regeneration** → permanent scarring
- CD8+ cytotoxic T lymphocytes and γδ T cells mediate basal keratinocyte apoptosis in the follicular epithelium

> **Key Concept**: The destruction of the **bulge stem cell compartment** is what makes LPP a *permanent* (cicatricial) alopecia — once the stem cell reservoir is lost, hair cannot regenerate even after inflammation resolves.

---

### Differential Diagnoses

| Differential | Key Distinguishing Features |
|---|---|
| **Keratosis pilaris** | Non-scarring; follicular papules on extensor arms/thighs; no inflammation |
| **Darier's disease** | Keratotic papules in seborrheic distribution; ATP2A2 mutation; dyskeratotic corps ronds and grains on histology |
| **Follicular mucinosis** | Alopecia mucinosa; mucin deposits in follicular epithelium; may be associated with mycosis fungoides |
| **Lichen scrofulosorum** | Lichenoid papules in tuberculosis; positive tuberculin test; caseating granulomas |
| **LE of scalp** (DLE) | Scarring alopecia with follicular plugging, dyspigmentation, atrophy; lupus band test positive; deep and superficial infiltrate |

---

### Histopathology

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| **Follicular plugging** | Hyperkeratosis and keratotic plugging of follicular infundibulum |
| **Liquefactive degeneration** | Vacuolar degeneration of basal cell layer of ORS (outer root sheath) |
| **Perifollicular infiltrate** | Dense **band-like lymphocytic infiltrate** concentrated around upper third of hair follicle (infundibulum and isthmus) |
| **Concentric lamellar fibroplasia** | Perifollicular fibrosis in cases with established scarring alopecia — concentric rings of fibrosis surrounding the follicle |
| **Loss of hair follicles** | Destroyed follicles **replaced by linear fibrous tracts** (vertical fibrous tracts) |
| **Interfollicular epidermis** | **Normal** in non-scalp areas — the pathology is exclusively folliculocentric |

> **Histological Pearl**: The hallmark triad of LPP is: (1) **perifollicular lymphocytic infiltrate** at the isthmus/infundibulum, (2) **vacuolar interface change** at the follicular basal layer, and (3) **concentric lamellar fibroplasia** with follicular destruction.

### Management

#### Treatment Aims

1. **Reduce inflammation** (halt active disease progression)
2. **Alleviate symptoms** (pruritus, pain, trichodynia)
3. **Prevent progression** of scarring alopecia

#### First-Line — Topical Corticosteroids

- **Topical corticosteroids**: Ultra-potent (clobetasol 0.05%) to affected scalp areas
- **Intralesional corticosteroids**: Triamcinolone acetonide **10 mg/mL** — inject into active inflammatory margins **every 4–6 weeks**
  - *(Source correction: Source reads "for 4–6 weeks" → should be "every 4–6 weeks" — referring to the injection interval, not course duration)*

#### Second-Line — Systemic Therapy (if CS fail for 6–12 months)

- **Hydroxychloroquine** — 200 mg BID; first-line systemic agent for LPP
  - 6-month trial recommended before assessing response
- **Extensive/Systemic corticosteroids**: Prednisolone 0.5–1 mg/kg/day — short course for acute flares

#### Third-Line — Immunomodulators

| Agent | Dosage | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Cyclosporine** | 3–5 mg/kg/day | Short-term use; nephrotoxicity monitoring |
| **MMF (Mycophenolate mofetil)** | 500 mg BD for 5–6 months | Steroid-sparing; good safety profile |
| **Thalidomide** | 50 mg | Anti-inflammatory and anti-TNF properties; teratogenicity counseling |
| **PPAR-γ agonists: Pioglitazone** | 15–30 mg OD | Decreases inflammation; emerging evidence in LPP/FFA |
| **Tetracyclines: Doxycycline** | 100 mg | Anti-inflammatory (not antimicrobial) dose |
| **Systemic retinoids: Isotretinoin** | 0.5–1 mg/kg/day | Normalizes follicular keratinization |
| **Acitretin** | 0.2–0.6 mg/kg/day | Particularly for hyperkeratotic variants |
| **Minoxidil** | 5% topical | Supportive therapy to stimulate residual follicles; does not reverse scarring |

#### Surgical Management

- **Hair transplant**: Only after **≥2 years of complete disease stability** (quiescence)
  - Must confirm no active inflammation clinically or histologically before transplantation
  - Risk of graft failure if disease is still active

#### Specific FFA Management

- **5-alpha reductase inhibitors** (finasteride 2.5–5 mg/day or dutasteride 0.5 mg/day) — current standard-of-care for FFA per Rook's and Bolognia, often used in combination with hydroxychloroquine

## Corrections & Verification Notes

### 🚨 Factual & Medical Errors

- **"Follicular LP follicularis tumidus"** (Page 793) → **"Follicular LP tumidus"** / **"Lichen planopilaris tumidus"**
  Tautological duplication: "LP follicularis" already means follicular LP; adding "follicularis" again is redundant. The correct term is **Lichen planopilaris tumidus** or **Follicular LP tumidus**. (Ref: Rook's — Variants of LPP)

- **"Destruction of hair follicles SC"** (Page 793) → **"Destruction of hair follicle stem cells"**
  The abbreviation "SC" is unexpanded in the source. In context, this refers to **stem cells** of the hair follicle bulge area (CD34+, K15+ keratinocyte stem cells). (Ref: Bolognia — Cicatricial Alopecias; Rook's — Pathogenesis of LPP)

- **Treatment frequency**: Source states *"Triamcinolone acetonide 10 mg/mL for 4–6 weeks"* → should read **"every 4–6 weeks"**
  This refers to the **injection interval** for intralesional steroids in LPP, not the total treatment duration. Standard ILK protocol for cicatricial alopecia is triamcinolone acetonide 5–10 mg/mL administered **every 4–6 weeks** for a minimum of 6–12 months. (Ref: Bolognia — Management of Cicatricial Alopecias)

- **5-alpha reductase inhibitors omitted** — The source does not mention **finasteride** or **dutasteride** as therapies for FFA, which are now considered **standard first/second-line agents** for FFA.
