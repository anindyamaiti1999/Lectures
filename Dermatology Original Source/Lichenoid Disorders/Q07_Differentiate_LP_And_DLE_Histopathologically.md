# Q7. Differentiate between LP and DLE histopathologically.

**Chapter**: Lichen Planus (Book Chapter 45 / Chapter 53) | **Source Pages**: 794–795 | **References**: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, Bolognia Dermatology, Andrews' Diseases of the Skin

---

## Answer

### Introduction

**Discoid lupus erythematosus (DLE)** and **Lichen planus (LP)** are two distinct lesions in terms of clinical, histopathological, and immunopathological features. However, there is some **clinico-histopathological overlap** — both demonstrate **interface dermatitis** with basal layer damage, making histopathological differentiation a high-yield examination topic.

> **Key Concept**: Both LP and DLE are **lichenoid interface dermatoses** — they share basal cell damage and a lymphocytic infiltrate at the dermoepidermal junction. The distinguishing features lie in the *pattern, depth, and character* of these changes, along with ancillary findings such as mucin, fibrosis, and immunofluorescence patterns.

---

### Comprehensive Histopathological Comparison: LP vs. DLE

| Feature | **Lichen Planus (LP)** | **Discoid Lupus Erythematosus (DLE)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Stratum corneum** | **Dense orthokeratotic hyperkeratosis** *(without parakeratosis)* | **Hyperkeratosis with superficial ortho/parakeratosis**; prominent **follicular plugging** (dilated follicular ostia filled with keratinous material) |
| **Granular layer** | **Wedge-shaped hypergranulosis** (focal thickening of the granular layer, pathognomonic) | **Normal or attenuated** (–); granular layer may be thinned or absent in areas of atrophy |
| **Basal layer** | **Liquefactive / hydropic degeneration** of basal cells (cytotoxic CD8+ T-cell mediated) | **Hydropic degeneration of basal cells** — diffuse; often more widespread and extensive than LP |
| **DEJ / Clefts** | **Clefts between epidermis and dermis** (**Max Joseph spaces**) — subepidermal clefting due to extensive basal cell destruction | **No clefting; rare** — instead, prominent **basement membrane zone (BMZ) thickening** (PAS-positive, diastase-resistant thickened BMZ) |
| **Epidermal thickness / Rete ridges** | **Saw-toothing of rete ridges** — irregular acanthosis with pointed (V-shaped) elongation of rete ridge tips | **Significant atrophy of epidermis**; flattening and effacement of rete ridges *(Note: Source lists "pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia" for DLE — this is characteristic of hypertrophic/verrucous LE, NOT standard DLE)* |
| **Dermal infiltrate (pattern)** | **Lichenoid, band-like** infiltrate directly **hugging the DEJ** (superficial); tightly apposed to the epidermis | **Deep perivascular and periadnexal** infiltrate — both superficial AND deep dermis involvement (perieccrine, perifollicular) |
| **Infiltrate composition** | **Mainly lymphohistiocytes** (CD8+ cytotoxic T cells predominate) | **Lymphohistiocytes with plasma cells** — plasma cells are an important distinguishing feature of lupus |
| **Civatte bodies (colloid/apoptotic bodies)** | **Abundant** — numerous eosinophilic, PAS-positive apoptotic keratinocytes (Civatte bodies) scattered at the DEJ and in the superficial dermis | **Less abundant** — present but far fewer in number; apoptotic keratinocytes are scattered |
| **Dermal mucin** | **Absent** | **Present** — abundant **dermal mucin and edema** in the reticular dermis (Alcian blue/colloidal iron positive); a hallmark of lupus |
| **Dermal fibrosis / sclerosis** | **Absent** | **Present** — dermal **fibrosis** and sclerosis in late/chronic lesions; replacement of adnexal structures |
| **Pigmentary incontinence** | **Present** — melanophages in superficial dermis due to basal cell damage | **Present** — but may be less conspicuous |
| **Vasculature** | No significant vascular changes | **Rare Ig deposition along dermal blood vessels**; perivascular inflammatory cuffing |

### Direct Immunofluorescence (DIF) Comparison

| DIF Feature | **Lichen Planus (LP)** | **Discoid Lupus Erythematosus (DLE)** |
|---|---|---|
| **BMZ pattern** | **Shaggy/irregular fibrinogen** band at the BMZ | **Granular deposition** of **IgG, IgM, and C3** along the BMZ (**Lupus Band Test** — positive in lesional skin) |
| **Colloid body staining** | **Globular deposits of IgM** (and occasionally IgA) in and around **Civatte/colloid bodies** at the DEJ | Not a prominent feature |
| **Ig deposition along blood vessels** | Absent | **Rare Ig deposition along dermal blood vessel walls** |
| **Sensitivity** | **Higher sensitivity** with **fibrin deposition** at the BMZ | **Higher sensitivity** with **Ig (IgG/IgM/C3) deposition** at the BMZ |
| **Basement membrane** | Fibrinogen deposited in a **shaggy** pattern along BMZ | **Continuous granular** immunoglobulin deposition along BMZ; BMZ appears **thickened** on PAS staining |

> **Exam Pearl — DIF Distinction**:
> - **LP**: Shaggy **fibrinogen** at BMZ + globular **IgM** in colloid bodies
> - **DLE**: Granular **IgG/IgM/C3** at BMZ (Lupus Band Test positive in lesional skin)

### Additional Clinical Context

> **Why this differentiation matters**: Both LP and DLE can present as violaceous, scaly papules/plaques on sun-exposed skin and share the feature of interface dermatitis. On scalp biopsy, both cause **cicatricial (scarring) alopecia** — LP via lichen planopilaris and DLE via discoid lupus of the scalp. Histopathological distinction is therefore critical for:
> - Correct diagnosis and prognostication
> - Appropriate treatment (immunosuppressives vs. antimalarials)
> - Systemic workup (DLE may be part of systemic lupus erythematosus — requires ANA, anti-dsDNA, complement levels)

## Corrections & Verification Notes

### 🚨 Factual & Medical Errors

- **Parakeratosis in LP**: Source table on page 795 states **"Dense hyperkeratosis with ortho/parakeratosis"** for LP. Classic LP exhibits **hyperkeratosis *without* parakeratosis** (pure orthokeratosis). Parakeratosis in a lichenoid infiltrate suggests a **lichenoid drug eruption**, **LP-like keratosis**, or other overlap condition — NOT classic idiopathic LP. Corrected to **"Dense orthokeratotic hyperkeratosis"**. (Ref: Rook's Textbook of Dermatology — Histopathology of LP; Bolognia — Lichen Planus Chapter)

- **Pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia (PEH) under DLE**: Source table on page 795 states **"Pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia"** under DLE for rete ridges/epidermal changes. PEH is **NOT** a feature of typical/standard DLE, which characteristically shows **epidermal atrophy and effacement of rete ridges**. PEH is seen in:
  - **Hypertrophic / Verrucous LE** (a variant)
  - **Keratoacanthoma**
  - **Deep fungal infections**
  - **Halogenoderma**
  Corrected to **"Significant atrophy of epidermis"** with effacement of rete ridges. (Ref: Bolognia — Lupus Erythematosus Histopathology; Andrews' — DLE)
