# 🎓 Mini-Lecture: Nékám's Disease (Keratosis Lichenoides Chronica)

*"The lichen planus that forgot how to stop — and learned how to draw straight lines."*

---

## 1. Let's start with the big picture: why should you care about a disease with <100 cases?

Before we dissect any details, let me place this entity on your mental map.

You already know **lichen planus (LP)** — that T-cell–driven, itchy, violaceous, polygonal-papuled disease with Wickham's striae, which typically flares for a year or two and then burns itself out. LP is not one disease so much as a **reaction pattern** — a particular way the immune system attacks the basal layer of the epidermis. Because it's a *pattern*, it can express itself in many clinical costumes: hypertrophic LP, annular LP, linear LP, LP pigmentosus, actinic LP, bullous LP, lichen planopilaris… and, at the far end of the spectrum, **keratosis lichenoides chronica (KLC)**.

KLC — **Nékám's disease** — is the member of this family that is:

- **Chronic and progressive** rather than self-limiting
- **Architecturally distinctive** — it lays its papules down in **lines, cords, nets and bead-strings** rather than scattering them randomly
- **Multi-compartment** — skin *plus* face *plus* mucosae *plus* nails *plus* palms/soles
- **Systemically linked** — with reported associations to kidney, liver and lymphoid disease
- **Famously stubborn** to treat

So why do examiners love it? Because it is a **"named-pattern" question**. It tests whether you can recognise a rare morphological signature, place it correctly within the lichenoid family, and justify a systemic work-up. It is a high-yield, low-effort mark if you have the picture in your head — and a blank page if you don't.

## 2. The name, the history, and what each synonym is *telling* you

### Historical attribution

| Who | When | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| **Lajos Nékám** (Hungarian dermatologist) | **1938** | First described the entity — hence **"Nékám's disease"** |
| **Margolis and colleagues** | **1972** | Re-characterised the disease and coined the now-preferred term **"keratosis lichenoides chronica"** |

Notice how the modern name is a three-word diagnosis in itself:

- **Kerat-osis** → the lesions are **hyperkeratotic** (thick, horny, plugged) — not the flat-topped shiny papules of ordinary LP
- **Lichenoides** → the histology is **lichenoid** (band-like lymphocytes attacking the basal layer)
- **Chronica** → it is **chronic and progressive**, unlike self-remitting LP

If you remember nothing else, remember that the name *is* the answer.

### The synonyms — each is a clinical clue in disguise

| Synonym | What the words literally mean | What it teaches you |
|---|---|---|
| **Keratosis lichenoides chronica (KLC)** | Chronic lichenoid keratosis | **Preferred modern term** |
| **Nékám's disease** | Eponym | Historical / eponymous designation (1938) |
| **Keratosis striata lichenoides** | *Striata* = striped | The papules run in **stripes/lines** |
| **Lichen ruber moniliformis** | *Moniliform* = like a **necklace of beads** | Papules are strung together like **pearls on a string** |
| **Lichen verrucosus et reticularis** | *Verrucosus* = warty; *reticularis* = net-like | The papules are **warty** and arranged in a **net/lattice** |

> 🧠 **Teaching point:** Four of the five names describe *geometry* — striped, bead-like, net-like. This is the single most important clinical fact about KLC: **it is a disease defined by its pattern, not just its papule.** Classic LP is "papules scattered like confetti." KLC is "papules threaded like beadwork."

### ⚠️ Two textbook errors worth knowing (they appear in print and in question papers)

1. **"Orokeratosis striata lichenoides"** is an **OCR/typographical corruption** of **"keratosis striata lichenoides."** The prefix "oro-" (mouth) crept in erroneously. The synonym refers to the **striated keratotic pattern of the skin**, *not* to oral keratosis. (Oral disease does occur in KLC — but that's a separate clinical feature, not what this synonym encodes.)
2. **"Lichen verrucous et reticularis"** should be **"lichen verrucosus et reticularis."** Latin adjectives must agree with the noun: *lichen* is masculine nominative singular, so the adjective is *verrucosus*, not the anglicised "verrucous."

## 3. Demographics — who walks through your door

- **Age:** predominantly **young adults, 20–40 years**
- **Sex:** slight **male predominance**
- **Children:** **occasionally** affected — a genuinely rare paediatric variant exists, and reported paediatric cases tend to be widespread and persistent
- **Frequency:** **extremely rare** — fewer than **100 well-documented cases** in the world literature

> 🩺 **Why the epidemiology matters practically:** rarity means you will almost never diagnose KLC by pattern recognition alone the first time. You will diagnose it by **thinking "this LP is behaving wrongly"** — it's too linear, too keratotic, too chronic, and it involves the face in a rosacea-like way. That cognitive trigger, followed by a biopsy, is how KLC actually gets diagnosed in real life.

---

## 4. Where KLC sits in the nosology: a *variant* of lichen planus

KLC is classified as a **variant of lichen planus**. Let's be precise about what "variant" means here, because students often fudge this.

**What it shares with LP:**
- The same fundamental immunopathology — a **cytotoxic T-cell attack on basal keratinocytes** producing **lichenoid interface dermatitis**
- Violaceous colour, mucosal involvement, nail involvement

**What makes it its own entity:**

| Axis | Classic LP | KLC (Nékám's) |
|---|---|---|
| **Arrangement** | Scattered, grouped polygonal papules | **Linear, reticular, cord-like, moniliform** along cleavage lines |
| **Surface** | Flat-topped, shiny, Wickham's striae | **Verrucous, keratotic, plugged** |
| **Face** | Usually spared | **Rosacea-/seborrhoeic dermatitis-like midfacial erythema** |
| **Course** | Often self-limited (1–2 years) | **Chronic, progressive, non-remitting** |
| **Cornified layer histology** | Compact **orthokeratosis** | **Alternating ortho- and parakeratosis** |
| **Depth of infiltrate** | Strictly **band-like, superficial** | Band-like **plus periappendageal** extension |
| **Systemic links** | Hepatitis C (chiefly) | Glomerulonephritis, lymphoproliferative disease, hepatitis |
| **Response to therapy** | Often good | **Notoriously refractory** |

> **Analogy:** Think of LP as a **thunderstorm** — violent, dramatic, and then it passes. KLC is the **slow, grinding monsoon** — same physics, same clouds, but it settles in, follows the drainage channels of the land (the cleavage lines), and does not leave.

## 5. Mechanistic reasoning: *why* does KLC look the way it does?

Examiners rarely ask this, but understanding it makes every clinical feature memorable rather than a list to cram. Let's build the cause → effect chain.

**Step 1 — The immunological insult.**
Cytotoxic CD8⁺ T lymphocytes recognise an (unknown) antigen on basal keratinocytes and mount an attack. They accumulate in a **dense band along the dermo-epidermal junction**.

**Step 2 — Basal keratinocyte death.**
The attacked basal cells undergo **apoptosis** and **liquefactive (vacuolar) degeneration** — the cell contents liquefy, producing tiny vacuoles at the junction. Dead, condensed keratinocytes drop into the papillary dermis as **Civatte bodies** (also called colloid or hyaline bodies).

**Step 3 — Disordered epidermal maturation → the keratotic plug.**
Here's the KLC-specific twist. When the **basal layer** — the epidermis's *factory floor* — is chronically damaged, the keratinocytes above it mature abnormally and *unevenly*. Some columns of cells complete keratinisation properly (**orthokeratosis** — nuclei fully dissolved), while adjacent columns rush through it and retain their nuclei (**parakeratosis**). Histologically you see **alternating ortho- and parakeratosis**; clinically you see **rough, warty, plugged papules** rather than LP's smooth, flat-topped ones.

> **Analogy:** Imagine a production line where the workers at the start keep being shot. Some batches come off the line finished; others come off half-made. The output is a **lumpy, uneven, horny product** — exactly the verrucous, plugged surface you see in KLC.

**Step 4 — Adnexal targeting → follicular plugs and dense plugs that "bleed" on removal.**
Unlike classic LP, KLC's lymphocytes also cuff the **appendages (hair follicles, sweat glands)** — a **periappendageal infiltrate**. Damage to follicular ostia produces **hyperkeratotic plugs that are firmly anchored**. Try to pick one off and you don't get a clean scale; you get an **irregular, pitted indentation that may ooze blood** — the so-called **"bleeding craters."**

**Step 5 — Chronic inflammation → telangiectasia.**
Persistent inflammatory cytokines and vascular remodelling in the papillary dermis produce **dilated superficial vessels — dermal telangiectasia**. On the face, where the dermis is thin and the vasculature already prominent, this telangiectasia plus scaling produces the **rosacea-like / seborrhoeic dermatitis-like midfacial eruption** that is so characteristic. The facial picture is therefore not a coincidence — it is the *same pathology* read through the *different anatomy* of facial skin.

**Step 6 — Why lines?**
The linear/reticular arrangement follows **skin cleavage lines** (the natural tension/relaxation lines of the dermis) rather than Blaschko's lines. Why this happens is not fully understood, but the working idea is that mechanical stress and the orientation of dermal collagen and vasculature channel the inflammation along these routes — a phenomenon conceptually related to the **Koebner (isomorphic) response** of LP, in which trauma recruits disease to a site.

Now every clinical feature below becomes *predictable* rather than memorised.

## 6. Cutaneous features — the heart of the answer

I'll organise this the way I'd want you to write it in an exam: a **clinical triad** first, then the details.

### 🔺 The classic KLC triad

1. **Linear/reticular keratotic violaceous papules and nodules** on the trunk and extremities
2. **Seborrhoeic dermatitis-like or rosacea-like facial eruption** with scaling and telangiectasia
3. **Mucosal involvement** — oral (and genital, and sometimes ocular)

If you open your answer with this triad, you have already earned most of the marks. Now let's unpack each.

---

### 6a. The primary skin lesions

**Morphology**
- **Violaceous** (the lichenoid colour — reflecting dermal melanin and the dense lymphocytic infiltrate seen through the epidermis)
- **Keratotic papules and nodules** — *reddish, verrucous (warty)* rather than flat and shiny
- **Hyperkeratotic plugs** on the surface that are **difficult to remove**; when dislodged they reveal **irregular indentations** or **"bleeding craters"**
- May coalesce into **plaques**

**The signature arrangement (the examiner's key)**
- **Linear** and **cord-like** ("striata")
- **Reticular / net-like** ("reticularis")
- **Moniliform** — papules strung like **beads on a necklace** ("lichen ruber moniliformis")
- Follows **skin cleavage lines**

**Distribution**
- **Symmetrical** — a bilateral, mirror-image pattern
- **Trunk, buttocks, extremities**
- With a particular predilection for **flexural and extensor sites**:
  - **Antecubital fossae**
  - **Popliteal fossae**
  - **Extensor surfaces of the forearms**
  - **Thighs**
  - **Lumbosacral region**

> 🧩 **Exam Pearl:** *The linear, reticular, or moniliform (bead-like) arrangement of keratotic papules along skin cleavage lines is the hallmark clinical feature of KLC.* Classic LP presents as **scattered, discrete, flat-topped polygonal papules with Wickham's striae**. KLC presents as **plugged, verrucous papules threaded into lines and nets.** Pattern, pattern, pattern.

---

### 6b. The face — the feature that catches people out

- **Midfacial erythema** with **scaling** and **telangiectasia**
- Clinically mimics **rosacea** or **seborrhoeic dermatitis**

Why is this so important?

1. **It is nearly unique among lichenoid diseases.** Classic LP generally spares the face. So a patient with linear keratotic limb lesions *plus* a "seborrhoeic-looking" face should immediately raise KLC.
2. **It is a diagnostic trap.** If a patient presents with the facial eruption *first*, they may be treated for months as rosacea or seborrhoeic dermatitis, with the truncal linear lesions dismissed as incidental. Always examine the whole skin.

> **Analogy:** The face in KLC is the **"tell"** in a poker game. It's the small, seemingly unrelated sign that reveals the whole hand.

---

### 6c. Mucosal involvement

- **Oral mucosa:**
  - Recurrent **aphthae-like ulcers**
  - **Chronic erosions** and **chronic ulceration** of the oral cavity
  - **Erythrokeratotic papules** on the mucosa
- **Genital mucosa:** lesions may occur here too

> 🩺 **Clinical consequence:** Chronic oral ulceration matters functionally — pain, impaired eating, weight loss, and (as with any chronic lichenoid oral erosion) the need for long-term surveillance of persistent non-healing lesions.

> ⚠️ **Don't confuse:** The aphthae-like ulcers of KLC can trigger a Behçet's-type differential, especially if genital lesions co-exist. The distinguishing clue is the **keratotic, linear, reticular skin disease** and the **lichenoid histology** — Behçet's has neither.

---

### 6d. Other cutaneous and appendageal features

| Feature | Description | Why it happens / why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Erythrokeratotic papules** | Widespread red, scaly, keratotic papules | Reflects the generalised disorder of keratinisation driven by chronic basal damage |
| **Palmoplantar keratoderma** | Thickening/hyperkeratosis of palms and soles | Functionally disabling — fissuring, pain on walking and gripping |
| **Nail changes** | **Longitudinal ridging**, **subungual hyperkeratosis**, **yellow discolouration** | The nail matrix is a modified epidermis; lichenoid inflammation there disrupts orderly nail-plate production |

> 🧠 **Unifying idea:** KLC attacks **every keratinising epithelium the body owns** — interfollicular epidermis, follicles, palmoplantar skin, nail matrix, and (in erosive form) the mucosae. That's why the clinical picture is so multi-compartment. It is best conceived not as "a rash" but as a **generalised lichenoid disorder of keratinisation**.

---

### 6e. Course

**Chronic. Progressive. Non-remitting.** This is not a rash that waits out its two years like classic LP. Untreated, KLC tends to extend slowly over years to decades. This single fact — the "chronica" in the name — drives the entire management philosophy: you are aiming for **long-term control**, not cure.

## 7. Histopathology — reading the biopsy like a story

The biopsy is what converts "odd-looking LP" into a diagnosis. Here is what the pathologist sees, layer by layer, from the surface down.

| Level | Finding | Plain-English translation |
|---|---|---|
| **Stratum corneum** | **Alternating parakeratosis and orthokeratosis** | Adjacent columns of properly matured (nucleus-free) and prematurely matured (nucleus-retaining) horn — a "striped" cornified layer |
| **Basal layer** | **Liquefactive (vacuolar) degeneration** | Basal keratinocytes are dying and dissolving; vacuoles form at the junction |
| **DEJ / papillary dermis** | **Civatte bodies** (colloid / apoptotic / hyaline bodies) | Tombstones of dead keratinocytes that have fallen into the dermis |
| **Dermo-epidermal junction** | **Lichenoid interface dermatitis** with a **band-like lymphocytic infiltrate** | A dense ribbon of lymphocytes hugging and obscuring the junction |
| **Deeper dermis** | **Perivascular AND periappendageal** chronic inflammatory infiltrate | Inflammation also cuffs vessels and adnexa — it goes **deeper** than classic LP |
| **Vessels** | **Dermal telangiectasia** — dilated papillary dermal vessels | The histological basis of the facial erythema and telangiectasia |

> 🔬 **Histology Pearl:** *Lichenoid interface dermatitis + alternating ortho-/parakeratosis + prominent periappendageal infiltrate = KLC.* Classic LP shows **compact orthokeratosis** (no parakeratosis), a **strictly band-like superficial infiltrate**, and **no periappendageal extension**. Add to the LP list its other classic features — wedge-shaped hypergranulosis, saw-tooth rete ridges, Max-Joseph spaces — and you can see the two histologies are related cousins, not twins.

> 📌 **A note on textbook layout errors:** In some printed sources the histopathological line **"lichenoid band-like interface dermatitis"** is mistakenly typeset **under the "Differential diagnoses" heading.** It belongs under **Histopathology**. Similarly, the vague phrase *"non-specific and consistent with chronic dermatitis"* sometimes used in source texts undersells the biopsy — KLC has *specific* findings (alternating ortho-/parakeratosis, periappendageal infiltrate, Civatte bodies, telangiectasia). Don't let a layout error make you describe KLC histology as non-specific in an exam.

## 8. Systemic associations — and the logic of the work-up

KLC is not purely cutaneous. Reported associations include:

| Association | Proposed mechanism / significance |
|---|---|
| **Glomerulonephritis** | Immune-complex–mediated renal injury; suggests a shared systemic immune dysregulation |
| **Lymphoproliferative disorders (including lymphoma)** | A chronic, driven lymphocytic proliferation in the skin raises the question of clonal evolution elsewhere; requires long-term vigilance |
| **Hepatitis (B and C)** | Chronic viral infection is a well-recognised driver of lichenoid tissue reactions (as with LP and HCV); the virus may supply the antigenic trigger |

> 🩺 **Clinical Significance — what you actually order:**
> - **Renal screen:** urinalysis (look for proteinuria, haematuria, casts) + serum creatinine/eGFR
> - **Hepatitis serology:** HBsAg and anti-HCV
> - **Haematological surveillance:** history, full examination of lymph nodes/spleen, FBC, and **periodic re-assessment** over the years for lymphoproliferative disease
>
> The reasoning chain: *KLC is a chronic, T-cell–driven disease with documented visceral associations → the skin is a window → screen the organs the literature implicates, and keep the window open over time because the associations may appear later, not at presentation.*

## 9. Differential diagnosis — and how to win each argument

For each differential, I want you to hold **one discriminating fact** in mind. That's how clinicians actually think.

| Differential | Why it's considered | **The discriminator** |
|---|---|---|
| **Lichen planus** | Same lichenoid family, violaceous papules, mucosal + nail disease | LP has **polygonal, flat-topped papules with Wickham's striae**, **does not form linear/reticular patterns along cleavage lines**, usually **spares the face**, and is often **self-limiting**. DIF in LP shows **shaggy fibrinogen deposition at the BMZ** |
| **Darier's disease (keratosis follicularis)** | Keratotic papules, nail changes, palmar involvement | Darier's papules are **greasy, crusted, in seborrhoeic areas**; histology shows **pathognomonic corps ronds and grains** with suprabasal acantholysis; **autosomal dominant, *ATP2A2* mutation**; nails show **V-shaped nicks and red-white longitudinal bands** |
| **Lichen amyloidosis** | Grouped keratotic papules, chronic course | **Rippled, brownish papules classically on the shins**; **intensely pruritic**; **Congo red–positive amyloid in the papillary dermis** (apple-green birefringence) |
| **Lupus erythematosus (esp. DLE)** | Facial erythema with scale, interface dermatitis on biopsy | **Photosensitive distribution**; **prominent follicular plugging** with scarring/atrophy; **thickened PAS-positive basement membrane**; **positive lupus band test** on DIF; **deep periadnexal/perivascular infiltrate** with mucin |
| **Pityriasis rubra pilaris (PRP)** | Widespread erythrokeratotic papules + palmoplantar keratoderma | **Orange-red plaques with sharply demarcated "islands of sparing"**; **nutmeg-grater follicular papules**; **waxy, orange palmoplantar keratoderma**; histology shows **alternating ortho-/parakeratosis in a checkerboard pattern** — note this overlaps with KLC, so the *clinical* picture decides |

> 💡 **Note the honest overlap:** PRP and KLC *both* show alternating ortho- and parakeratosis. This is a good lesson in dermatopathology: a single histological feature rarely makes a diagnosis. You triangulate — **clinical pattern + histology + associations**. In KLC the tiebreakers are the **linear/reticular geometry**, the **lichenoid interface with Civatte bodies**, and the **rosacea-like face**.

## 10. Treatment — why this disease is humbling, and what actually works

Let's set expectations honestly: **KLC is notoriously treatment-resistant.** No single therapy is consistently effective, and most reported responses are partial or temporary. Management is about **combination therapy** and **long-term control**.

### 🥇 Mainstay: combination therapy

**Systemic retinoid (acitretin) + phototherapy (PUVA)** is the **best-documented effective regimen.**

*Why this combination makes biological sense:*
- **Acitretin** is a retinoid — it **normalises keratinocyte proliferation and differentiation**. Since KLC's clinical burden is largely the *hyperkeratosis* (the plugs, the verrucous surface, the palmoplantar keratoderma), a drug that re-regulates keratinisation attacks the visible disease directly.
- **PUVA** (psoralen + UVA) is **immunosuppressive and lymphocyte-depleting in the skin** — it induces apoptosis in the very T cells driving the lichenoid attack, and dampens antigen presentation. Since KLC's *engine* is the T-cell interface attack, PUVA attacks the cause.
- Together: **retinoid treats the output, phototherapy treats the input.** That's why the pair works better than either alone. (There is also a practical bonus: retinoids thin the hyperkeratotic scale, allowing UV to penetrate more effectively.)

> 💊 **Treatment Pearl:** *Acitretin + PUVA is the best-documented effective regimen for KLC. Monotherapy with either agent alone tends to produce incomplete or temporary responses.*

### Alternatives and adjuncts

| Therapy | Role |
|---|---|
| **NBUVB phototherapy** | Alternative to PUVA (avoids oral psoralen and its ocular/GI issues); reasonable where PUVA is unavailable |
| **Systemic retinoids alone** (acitretin, isotretinoin) | Partial response in some cases; useful when phototherapy isn't feasible |
| **Photodynamic therapy (PDT)** | Emerging option with some reported success — useful for localised, resistant plaques |
| **Topical calcipotriol** (vitamin D₃ analogue) | Adjunctive — modulates keratinocyte differentiation; helps the keratotic component |
| **Topical tacrolimus** (calcineurin inhibitor) | Adjunctive — blocks T-cell activation locally; particularly rational for **facial** and **mucosal** disease where steroids risk atrophy |

> ⚠️ **Practical cautions:** Acitretin is a **potent teratogen** — contraception is mandatory for **3 years** after stopping in women of child-bearing potential; monitor **lipids and liver function**. Do not combine systemic retinoids with **tetracyclines** (risk of pseudotumour cerebri). PUVA requires **eye protection** and carries cumulative **photocarcinogenic risk** — relevant given KLC's long treatment horizon.

## 11. Common misunderstandings — let's clear these up now

**❌ "KLC is just hypertrophic lichen planus."**
No. Hypertrophic LP produces **thick, warty plaques on the shins**, is **intensely pruritic**, and lacks the linear/reticular geometry, the facial eruption, and the systemic associations. KLC is a distinct, chronic, multi-compartment variant.

**❌ "The lesions follow Blaschko's lines."**
They follow **skin cleavage lines**, not Blaschko's lines. This matters: Blaschko's lines indicate **cutaneous mosaicism** (e.g. linear LP in a mosaic distribution, incontinentia pigmenti). KLC's linearity is an **inflammatory/mechanical** phenomenon, not a genetic-mosaic one.

**❌ "The synonym 'orokeratosis striata' means oral involvement."**
It's a **typo** for **keratosis striata lichenoides**, referring to the **striped skin pattern**. Oral involvement in KLC is real — but it's a separate clinical feature, not what that synonym denotes.

**❌ "KLC histology is non-specific."**
It has specific features: **lichenoid interface dermatitis + alternating ortho-/parakeratosis + periappendageal infiltrate + Civatte bodies + dermal telangiectasia.** Some source texts carelessly call it non-specific — don't repeat that.

**❌ "Like LP, it'll settle in a year or two."**
No — **"chronica"** is in the name for a reason. Counsel patients for **years of management**, and set realistic goals.

**❌ "The facial redness is an unrelated rosacea."**
The **rosacea-like/seborrhoeic-like midfacial erythema with scaling and telangiectasia** is a **defining feature of KLC**. Treating it as a separate diagnosis is one of the classic ways KLC gets missed.

## 12. 🎯 Summary Anchor — the core idea in one picture

> **Picture a young adult man.**
>
> **His face** looks like rosacea or seborrhoeic dermatitis — red, scaly, telangiectatic across the mid-face.
>
> **His trunk, buttocks and limbs** — antecubital and popliteal fossae, extensor forearms, thighs, lumbosacral back, symmetrically — carry **violaceous, warty, keratin-plugged papules strung into lines, cords and nets** along the skin's cleavage lines, like **beads threaded on a necklace**. Pick off a plug and you get a **small bleeding crater**, not a clean scale.
>
> **His mouth** has recurrent aphthae-like ulcers and chronic erosions; his **genitals** may be involved too. His **palms and soles** are thickened; his **nails** are ridged with yellow, subungual hyperkeratosis.
>
> **His biopsy** shows a **band-like lichenoid infiltrate** destroying the basal layer (vacuolar degeneration, Civatte bodies), with the infiltrate also **cuffing the appendages**, a cornified layer of **alternating ortho- and parakeratosis**, and **dilated papillary dermal vessels**.
>
> **And it never goes away.** So you screen his **kidneys, liver and lymphoid system**, and you settle in for the long haul with **acitretin plus PUVA**.

**One sentence to carry away:**
> **Nékám's disease (keratosis lichenoides chronica) is a rare, chronic, progressive variant of lichen planus in which violaceous, keratin-plugged papules are laid down in linear, reticular and moniliform patterns along skin cleavage lines, accompanied by a rosacea-like facial eruption, oral/genital erosions, palmoplantar keratoderma and nail dystrophy — underpinned by a lichenoid interface dermatitis with periappendageal extension and alternating ortho-/parakeratosis, associated with glomerulonephritis, hepatitis and lymphoproliferative disease, and best managed with acitretin plus PUVA.**

## 📝 Appendix: A 6-point skeleton for writing this answer under exam pressure

1. **Define + eponym:** Rare chronic progressive lichenoid dermatosis = KLC; Nékám 1938; Margolis 1972; a **variant of LP**.
2. **Synonyms:** keratosis striata lichenoides, lichen ruber moniliformis, lichen verrucosus et reticularis (note all describe the *pattern*).
3. **Demographics:** young adults 20–40, slight male predominance, rarely children, <100 cases.
4. **Cutaneous features — lead with the triad:** ① linear/reticular/moniliform violaceous keratotic papules with adherent plugs and bleeding craters on trunk, buttocks, extremities (antecubital, popliteal, extensor forearms, thighs, lumbosacral), symmetrical; ② rosacea-/seborrhoeic-like midfacial erythema with scale and telangiectasia; ③ oral aphthae-like ulcers/erosions ± genital lesions. **Plus** palmoplantar keratoderma, erythrokeratotic papules, nail ridging/subungual hyperkeratosis/yellow discolouration. **Chronic, progressive.**
5. **Histology (one line each):** lichenoid band-like + periappendageal infiltrate; basal vacuolar degeneration; Civatte bodies; alternating ortho-/parakeratosis; dermal telangiectasia.
6. **Associations, DDx, Treatment:** glomerulonephritis / lymphoproliferative disease / hepatitis B & C → screen. DDx: LP, Darier's, lichen amyloidosis, LE, PRP. Rx: **acitretin + PUVA** (mainstay), NBUVB, PDT, topical calcipotriol/tacrolimus — **treatment-resistant**.
