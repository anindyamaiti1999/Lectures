# 🎓 Mini-Lecture: Telling Lichen Planus from Discoid Lupus Under the Microscope

*Chapter 53 — Lichen Planus | Pages 794–795 | Rook's · Bolognia · Andrews'*

---

## 1. The Big Picture — Why This Question Even Exists

Let me start by telling you *why* this is such a beloved examiner's question, because once you understand the "why," the "what" becomes almost self-evident.

There is a family of skin diseases we call the **interface dermatoses**. "Interface" refers to the **dermoepidermal junction (DEJ)** — the boundary where the epidermis sits on the dermis. In this family of diseases, the immune system attacks the cells sitting *right at that boundary*: the **basal keratinocytes**. The result is a stereotyped picture — damaged, vacuolated basal cells plus a band of lymphocytes crowding the junction — which we call **lichenoid interface dermatitis** ("lichenoid" because clinically such lesions look like lichen growing on a rock).

**Lichen planus (LP)** and **discoid lupus erythematosus (DLE)** are the two heavyweight champions of this family. Both attack the same address (the DEJ), so their **core lesion is genuinely shared**:

> Basal cell damage (liquefactive/hydropic degeneration) + apoptotic keratinocytes + a lymphocytic infiltrate at the junction + pigment incontinence.

If you only looked at the basal layer, you could not reliably tell them apart. **This is the honest truth that most students never internalize.** The differentiation does *not* come from the presence of interface change; it comes from **the company that interface change keeps** — the *pattern*, the *depth*, the *cast of inflammatory cells*, what the epidermis does in response, and what the dermal matrix does around it.

And here is the unifying idea I want you to carry for the rest of this lecture:

> **LP is an acute, sharply targeted, *superficial* blitz against the basal layer, and the epidermis fights back by thickening.**
> **DLE is a chronic, smouldering, *deep* campaign that travels along vessels and adnexa, and the epidermis gives up and thins out.**

Everything else — every single row of the comparison table — is a logical consequence of those two sentences. Let's earn that claim.

## 2. Two Different Engines: The Immunopathology That Drives the Histology

You cannot memorize the histology safely without knowing the mechanism, because the mechanism *predicts* the histology.

### Lichen Planus — a cytotoxic T-cell assassination

* Some antigen (possibly a modified self-peptide on basal keratinocytes; HCV, drugs, and metals are known triggers) becomes visible on **basal keratinocytes**.
* **CD8⁺ cytotoxic T lymphocytes** are recruited, become tightly packed at the junction, and kill basal keratinocytes by perforin/granzyme and Fas–FasL apoptosis.
* Because the target antigen lives **in the basal layer of the interfollicular epidermis** (and in follicular epithelium in lichen planopilaris), the attack is **spatially confined to the DEJ**. The T cells have no business deeper in the dermis — so the infiltrate forms a **tight, flat, band-like ribbon hugging the epidermis** and stops abruptly.

**Analogy:** think of a **SWAT team lined up shoulder-to-shoulder against one specific wall of a building.** They are dense, they are at one level, and they do not wander into the basement.

* The epidermis is not passive. Losing basal cells triggers **compensatory hyperproliferation and accelerated, but *complete*, terminal differentiation**. Complete differentiation means keratinocytes have time to dissolve their nuclei → **orthokeratosis** (anuclear compact horn), a **thickened granular layer (hypergranulosis)**, and **irregular acanthosis**.

### Discoid Lupus Erythematosus — a chronic interferon-driven, antibody-flavoured campaign

* UV light causes keratinocyte apoptosis and externalises nuclear antigens (Ro/SSA, La, dsDNA, nucleosomes).
* **Plasmacytoid dendritic cells** sense nucleic-acid–immune complexes and pour out **type I interferon (IFN-α)**. The IFN signature (detectable as MxA expression) recruits **CD4⁺ T cells, CD8⁺ T cells, B cells and plasma cells**, and upregulates chemokines (CXCL9/10/11) that drag lymphocytes in **wherever the chemokine gradient goes** — and that gradient follows **blood vessels and adnexal structures**, which are rich in perivascular/periadnexal chemokine-producing cells and are where lymphocytes physically enter and traffic.
* Therefore the DLE infiltrate is **superficial *and* deep**, arranged **perivascularly and periadnexally** (around hair follicles and eccrine coils) — not a flat band, but **cuffs and clusters at multiple depths**.
* Because this process is **chronic and relapsing over months to years**, the epidermis cannot sustain compensatory proliferation. It becomes **atrophic**; the BMZ is damaged and repaired repeatedly until it becomes **thick and duplicated**; fibroblasts, stimulated by chronic cytokines, secrete **glycosaminoglycans (mucin)** and later **collagen (fibrosis/scarring)**; and the chronic B-cell–rich milieu leaves behind **plasma cells**.

**Analogy:** a **long guerrilla insurgency infiltrating a country along its highways and railway lines** (vessels and adnexa), at every depth, for years. The infrastructure it passes through gets worn down (atrophy), repeatedly patched (thick BMZ), waterlogged (mucin), and finally paved over with scar tissue (fibrosis).

Hold those two images. Now we read the slide from top to bottom.

## 3. The Layer-by-Layer Walkthrough

### 3.1 Stratum corneum — the roof

| | LP | DLE |
|---|---|---|
| Cornified layer | **Dense orthokeratotic hyperkeratosis** (compact, *no* parakeratosis) | **Hyperkeratosis with superficial ortho-/parakeratosis**, plus **prominent follicular plugging** |

**What it is:** Hyperkeratosis = a thickened horny layer. *Ortho*keratosis = mature, anuclear corneocytes. *Para*keratosis = retained nuclei in the horny layer, the signature of **hurried, incomplete maturation**.

**Why LP is orthokeratotic:** the LP epidermis is proliferating faster, but each keratinocyte still completes its full differentiation programme — it makes plenty of keratohyalin (hence hypergranulosis) and properly degrades its nucleus. A **compact, glassy, nucleus-free horn** results.

**Why DLE can be parakeratotic, and why there is follicular plugging:** in DLE, differentiation is *disordered* rather than merely accelerated, and the **follicular infundibula** are a special problem. The peri-infundibular inflammation obstructs and alters the follicular epithelium, so keratin cannot be discharged normally. Keratin plus scale accumulates in dilated follicular ostia → **follicular plugging**.

**Clinical payoff — this is your "aha" moment:** follicular plugging is exactly what generates the classic **"carpet-tack sign"** (*signe de la languette*) — peel an adherent scale off a DLE plaque and the keratin plugs project from its underside like the tacks on the back of a carpet. *Histology explains a bedside sign.*

**🚨 Correction you must know (source error):** The source table on p. 795 lists **"ortho/parakeratosis" for LP.** This is wrong for classic LP. **Classic idiopathic LP shows hyperkeratosis *without* parakeratosis.** If you see a lichenoid infiltrate **with parakeratosis**, your differential should shift toward:
* **Lichenoid drug eruption** (also: eosinophils, plasma cells, deeper perivascular extension, focal parakeratosis)
* **Lichen planus–like keratosis (benign lichenoid keratosis)**
* Lichenoid actinic/other overlap reactions

So "parakeratosis in a lichenoid biopsy" is actually a *clue against* idiopathic LP — a beautiful little exam trap.

---

### 3.2 Granular layer — the single most discriminating epidermal clue

| | LP | DLE |
|---|---|---|
| Granular layer | **Wedge-shaped (V-shaped) hypergranulosis** — focal, triangular thickening | **Normal or attenuated/absent**, especially over atrophic areas |

**What it is:** the stratum granulosum contains keratohyalin granules. In LP it thickens **focally**, typically in a **triangle with its base at the surface and its apex pointing down toward an acrosyringium or into the epidermis** — hence "wedge-shaped."

**Why it matters — and the most satisfying clinicopathological correlation in dermatology:** those focal wedges of thickened granular layer **scatter and reflect light differently** from the surrounding epidermis. Viewed on the surface of a violaceous papule, they appear as a **white, lacy network — Wickham's striae.** When you point out Wickham's striae on a wrist papule, you are literally pointing at wedge hypergranulosis.

In DLE, no such compensatory thickening occurs; the chronically suppressed, atrophic epidermis has a **thin or absent granular layer**, which is one reason DLE plaques lack Wickham's striae.

**Exam framing:** wedge-shaped hypergranulosis is described as **pathognomonic/highly characteristic of LP** and **absent in DLE**. It is one of the three "LP-only" epidermal features (with saw-toothing and Max Joseph spaces).

---

### 3.3 Basal layer — the shared battlefield

| | LP | DLE |
|---|---|---|
| Basal cells | **Liquefactive (hydropic) degeneration**, CD8⁺-mediated, intense but **focally band-limited** | **Hydropic degeneration**, typically **more diffuse and widespread**, following the infiltrate wherever it goes (including follicular epithelium) |

**What "liquefactive/hydropic degeneration" actually is:** the injured basal keratinocyte swells, its cytoplasm becomes watery and clear (vacuolated), and it may detach. Under low power the basal layer looks **blurred, "moth-eaten," or smudged** instead of a crisp palisade of cuboidal cells.

**The crucial teaching point:** *both* diseases have this. **Do not use basal vacuolation to differentiate them.** What differs is **extent and distribution** — in LP it is confined to the zone beneath the lymphocytic band; in DLE it is patchier but broader, and critically **it also involves adnexal (follicular) epithelium**, because the infiltrate is there too.

---

### 3.4 The DEJ itself — clefts versus a thickened membrane

| | LP | DLE |
|---|---|---|
| Junction | **Max Joseph spaces** — subepidermal clefts/vesicles | **Clefting rare/absent**; instead **BMZ thickening**, PAS-positive and diastase-resistant |

**Max Joseph spaces — what and why:** when basal cell destruction in LP is so *rapid and confluent* that a stretch of epidermis loses its anchoring basal cells altogether, the epidermis separates from the dermis, creating a **small subepidermal cleft**. If extensive, this produces **bullous LP** clinically.

**Analogy:** the basal layer is the **glue strip holding wallpaper to a wall.** Strip the glue away in a continuous run, and the wallpaper lifts off in a blister-like pocket. That pocket is a Max Joseph space.

**BMZ thickening in DLE — what and why:** DLE's damage is slower but *relentless*. Each cycle of injury and repair lays down more basement-membrane material; the lamina densa becomes **duplicated and reduplicated**, so the BMZ becomes a visibly **thick, homogeneous, magenta ribbon on PAS stain**. It resists diastase digestion (proving it is basement membrane glycoprotein, not glycogen).

**Analogy:** a wall **repainted and re-plastered every year for a decade** — the paint layer becomes conspicuously thick. LP is a one-off demolition; DLE is a decade of patching.

So: **LP separates at the junction; DLE thickens the junction.** Mechanistically opposite outcomes of acute versus chronic injury at the same site.

---

### 3.5 Epidermal architecture and rete ridges — thick versus thin

| | LP | DLE |
|---|---|---|
| Epidermis / rete ridges | **Irregular acanthosis with "saw-toothing"** — pointed, V-shaped elongation of rete ridge tips | **Marked epidermal atrophy** with **flattening/effacement of rete ridges** |

**Saw-toothing:** the alternation of focal hypergranulosis, irregular acanthosis, and focal basal destruction distorts the rete ridges into sharp, angulated, **downward-pointing spikes** — the profile of a **saw blade** or a row of **shark teeth**. It is a direct architectural readout of "the epidermis is proliferating irregularly while being chewed at the bottom."

**DLE atrophy:** chronic interferon-mediated suppression plus ongoing basal cell loss means the epidermis becomes **thin, sometimes only two or three cell layers**, and the rete ridges are **smoothed away**, so the DEJ runs as a flat line.

**Clinical payoff:** LP's acanthosis + hyperkeratosis = a **raised, flat-topped, shiny, firm papule**. DLE's atrophy + fibrosis = a **depressed, atrophic, scarred plaque** with telangiectasia and dyspigmentation. The biopsy and the bedside agree.

**🚨 Second correction you must know (source error):** The source table lists **"pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia" (PEH) under DLE.** This is **not** a feature of ordinary DLE — ordinary DLE is **atrophic**. PEH (irregular, deep, downward epithelial proliferation mimicking squamous cell carcinoma) is characteristic of:
* **Hypertrophic / verrucous lupus erythematosus** (an uncommon LE variant)
* **Keratoacanthoma / SCC**
* **Deep fungal infections** (chromoblastomycosis, etc.)
* **Halogenoderma** (bromoderma/iododerma)
* Chronic ulcers, granuloma inguinale, some mycobacterial infections

So the corrected answer is: **DLE = significant epidermal atrophy with effacement of rete ridges.** Mention PEH only if the question specifies **hypertrophic LE**.

### 3.6 The dermal infiltrate — pattern and depth (your low-power money shot)

| | LP | DLE |
|---|---|---|
| Pattern | **Lichenoid, band-like**, tightly apposed to and *hugging* the DEJ; **superficial only** | **Perivascular AND periadnexal** — superficial **and deep** dermis; perifollicular, perieccrine |

This is, practically speaking, **the most reliable single discriminator on a 4× objective**, and it costs you two seconds to assess.

* **LP:** a **flat, dense, sharply demarcated ribbon** sitting right under the epidermis, with an abrupt lower border and **clean dermis beneath it**. Because it obscures the junction, it is sometimes described as "**obscuring the DEJ**."
* **DLE:** **patchy cuffs and nodules of lymphocytes** clustered around superficial *and* deep vessels and around the mid/deep follicles and sweat coils — often described as a "**top and bottom heavy**" or "**superficial and deep perivascular and periadnexal**" pattern.

**Analogy:** LP is **a picket fence line drawn along the property boundary.** DLE is **inflammation scattered along the driveways, the plumbing, and the wiring throughout the house.**

**Clinical payoff (scalp):** *both* diseases produce **cicatricial (scarring) alopecia** — LP as **lichen planopilaris (LPP)** and DLE as **discoid lupus of the scalp** — and this is precisely where the differentiation becomes clinically urgent. Even here the pattern rule holds:
* **LPP:** inflammation is **confined to the upper follicle (infundibulum and isthmus)** with perifollicular lamellar fibrosis; the interfollicular epidermis and deep dermis are relatively spared.
* **DLE of scalp:** inflammation involves the **whole follicle including the deep/bulbar region**, plus interfollicular interface change, mucin, BMZ thickening, plasma cells, and follicular plugging.

### 3.7 Composition of the infiltrate — who is in the crowd

| | LP | DLE |
|---|---|---|
| Cells | **Lymphohistiocytic**, predominantly **CD8⁺ cytotoxic T cells** (with CD4⁺ helper cells and increased Langerhans cells); **plasma cells characteristically absent** | **Lymphohistiocytic *plus* plasma cells**; CD4⁺ cells prominent, **CD123⁺ plasmacytoid dendritic cells**, occasional B-cell aggregates |

**Why plasma cells matter so much:** plasma cells are terminally differentiated antibody factories — they appear where **chronic, antibody-driven, B-cell–engaged** inflammation lives. LP is a cytotoxic T-cell event with essentially no humoral arm, so **plasma cells are a red flag against pure LP.**

**Practical rule:** *lichenoid infiltrate **+ plasma cells** → think **lupus** (or lichenoid drug eruption, syphilis, or a mucosal/genital site where plasma cells are normally present).* Note the site caveat: on oral and genital mucosa, plasma cells are physiologically common, so this rule is weaker there.

**How to recognise a plasma cell:** eccentric nucleus, "clock-face"/cartwheel chromatin, abundant amphophilic cytoplasm, perinuclear pale hof.

---

### 3.8 Civatte bodies — the tombstones of dead keratinocytes

| | LP | DLE |
|---|---|---|
| Colloid/Civatte bodies | **Abundant** — numerous eosinophilic, PAS-positive apoptotic keratinocytes at the DEJ and in the papillary dermis | **Present but far fewer**, scattered |

**What they are:** a **Civatte body** (= colloid body = hyaline body = cytoid body) is the **mummified remnant of an apoptotic keratinocyte** — a round, glassy, brightly eosinophilic, PAS-positive globule, often found just below or within the lower epidermis.

**Why LP has so many:** LP's killing is **rapid and synchronous** — many basal keratinocytes die at once, and their corpses accumulate faster than macrophages can clear them. DLE's killing is **slow and piecemeal**, so dead cells are cleared as they appear and never pile up.

**Analogy:** LP is a **mass-casualty event** — bodies accumulate visibly. DLE is a **chronic attrition war** — losses are steady but cleared continuously.

**Why they matter twice over:** they are also the structures that **trap IgM in direct immunofluorescence** (see §4), giving LP its characteristic globular DIF pattern.

---

### 3.9 Dermal mucin — the DLE hallmark (and the margin note your book circled)

| | LP | DLE |
|---|---|---|
| Mucin | **Absent** | **Present** — abundant interstitial mucin and oedema in the **reticular dermis**; **Alcian blue / colloidal iron positive** |

**What it is:** "dermal mucin" = **glycosaminoglycans, chiefly hyaluronic acid**, secreted by fibroblasts. On H&E it appears as **faint, stringy, bluish-grey wisps separating collagen bundles**, or as apparent "empty spaces" between splayed collagen — easy to miss unless you look for it.

**Why DLE makes mucin:** chronic cytokine stimulation (IFN, TGF-β, IL-6) drives fibroblasts to overproduce hyaluronic acid. LP's brief, epithelium-focused attack never engages reticular dermal fibroblasts, so **no mucin**.

**Analogy:** the DLE dermis is **waterlogged, like soil after a long rain** — the collagen "grass blades" get pushed apart by jelly. The LP dermis is dry and normal below the inflammatory band.

**How to confirm:** **Alcian blue (pH 2.5)** or **colloidal iron** stains mucin blue; add **hyaluronidase digestion** to prove it is hyaluronic acid.

**Your margin note ("Mucin", circled) is exactly right:** in a two-feature exam answer, *mucin present = DLE, mucin absent = LP* is among the highest-yield discriminators, because it is easy to state, easy to demonstrate with a special stain, and almost never overlaps.

---

### 3.10 Dermal fibrosis and scarring

| | LP | DLE |
|---|---|---|
| Fibrosis | **Absent** (no scarring in cutaneous LP; LPP scars only *perifollicularly*) | **Present in late/chronic lesions** — dermal fibrosis/sclerosis with **destruction and replacement of adnexal structures** |

**Mechanism:** persistent inflammation → TGF-β → fibroblast activation → collagen deposition. Once **hair follicle stem cells in the bulge and the sebaceous glands are destroyed and replaced by fibrous tracts**, hair regrowth is impossible — hence **permanent scarring alopecia** and the atrophic, "cigarette-paper" central scar of a burnt-out DLE plaque.

**Why LP normally does not scar:** the attack stops at the epidermis and spares the deep dermis and adnexa, so once inflammation subsides the epidermis simply regenerates. **The exception is lichen planopilaris**, where the target *is* follicular epithelium, and the outcome *is* permanent perifollicular scarring.

---

### 3.11 Pigmentary incontinence — shared, but differently conspicuous

| | LP | DLE |
|---|---|---|
| Melanophages | **Present** — melanin-laden macrophages in the papillary dermis | **Present**, often less conspicuous |

**What it is:** basal keratinocytes are the **cells that receive and store melanin** from melanocytes. Destroy them and their melanin **spills ("becomes incontinent") into the papillary dermis**, where macrophages engulf it → **melanophages**.

**Clinical payoff:** this is the histological basis of the **long-lasting post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation** of LP (especially marked in darker skin types, and the entire substance of **lichen planus pigmentosus**), and of the **dyspigmentation — central hypopigmentation with a hyperpigmented rim — of DLE plaques.**

---

### 3.12 Vessels

| | LP | DLE |
|---|---|---|
| Vascular findings | No significant vascular change | **Perivascular lymphocytic cuffing** (superficial and deep); **rare immunoglobulin deposition along dermal vessel walls** on DIF |

This follows directly from mechanism: DLE is partly an **immune-complex–associated** disease, so immune reactants can be found on vessel walls; LP is not, so they cannot.

## 4. Direct Immunofluorescence — Two Different Fingerprints

DIF is where the differentiation becomes near-definitive, and again the patterns are *derivable* from mechanism.

| DIF feature | **LP** | **DLE** |
|---|---|---|
| **BMZ** | **Shaggy, irregular FIBRINOGEN** band | **Granular IgG, IgM and C3** — the **lupus band** |
| **Colloid/Civatte bodies** | **Globular IgM** (± IgA, C3) clusters at the DEJ and papillary dermis | Not a prominent feature |
| **Vessel walls** | Absent | Rare Ig deposition |
| **Best yield** | **Fibrin at BMZ** | **Ig (IgG/IgM/C3) at BMZ** |

### Why LP shows *shaggy fibrinogen*

Fibrinogen is a **plasma protein**. In LP, the BMZ is physically shredded by cytotoxic attack, so **plasma leaks out of the papillary dermal vessels and fibrinogen gets deposited irregularly along the disrupted junction** — giving a **ragged, frayed, "shaggy" or "fluffy" linear band** rather than a crisp line. It is a marker of **damage and leakage**, not of a specific autoantibody. (This is why it is not disease-specific, but in the right context it is highly characteristic.)

### Why LP shows *globular IgM in colloid bodies*

Apoptotic keratinocyte remnants are **sticky**: their exposed intracellular and nuclear material **nonspecifically binds circulating immunoglobulin, especially IgM**. On DIF these light up as **clusters of round, bright globules** at and below the DEJ — often described as looking like **a cluster of grapes or a shower of bright beads**. Since LP has abundant Civatte bodies (§3.8), it has abundant globular IgM. Elegant internal consistency.

### Why DLE shows a *granular lupus band*

In LE, **circulating immune complexes and antibodies to BMZ-adjacent antigens deposit at the BMZ**, and complement is fixed there. Because deposition is in discrete clumps of immune complexes rather than along a single continuous structural protein, the pattern is **granular** (contrast: **linear** in bullous pemphigoid, where a genuine structural antigen is targeted).

**Crucial clinical nuance about the Lupus Band Test (LBT):**
* **Lesional skin of DLE:** LBT is **positive in the majority (~70–90%)** of established lesions (ideally lesions >6 weeks old; new lesions may be negative).
* **Non-lesional, sun-protected skin (e.g., inner upper arm):** a positive band here is **much more suggestive of systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE)** than of localised DLE, and correlates with renal involvement. This is the test's real diagnostic power.
* **Caveat:** chronically sun-exposed normal skin can show a weak band in healthy people — so **site selection matters.**

> ### ⭐ Exam Pearl — the DIF one-liner
> **LP = shaggy FIBRINOGEN at the BMZ + globular IgM in colloid bodies.**
> **DLE = granular IgG/IgM/C3 at the BMZ (positive lupus band).**
>
> Mnemonic: **L**ichen planus → **F**ibrinogen (**"Lichen Fibrin"**). **L**upus → **I**mmunoglobulin (**"Lupus Ig"**).

## 5. How to Read the Slide in Thirty Seconds — A Practical Algorithm

This is how a dermatopathologist actually does it, and it is worth practising as a sequence.

**Step 1 — Low power (4×). Ask two questions only:**

| | Epidermis | Infiltrate depth |
|---|---|---|
| **LP** | **THICK** (acanthotic, hyperkeratotic, saw-toothed) | **SUPERFICIAL band only**, clean dermis below |
| **DLE** | **THIN** (atrophic, flat rete) | **SUPERFICIAL + DEEP**, perivascular & periadnexal |

Already, in most cases, you have your answer. **Thick + superficial = LP. Thin + deep = DLE.**

**Step 2 — Medium power. Look for the "extras":**
* **Follicular plugging?** → DLE
* **Wedge hypergranulosis?** → LP
* **Max Joseph clefts?** → LP
* **Mucin splaying the reticular collagen?** → DLE
* **Fibrosis / lost adnexa?** → DLE (late)

**Step 3 — High power. Count the cells and check the membrane:**
* **Plasma cells present?** → DLE (or drug/mucosal site)
* **Eosinophils present?** → suspect **lichenoid drug eruption**
* **Many Civatte bodies?** → LP
* **Thick magenta BMZ on PAS?** → DLE

**Step 4 — Special studies if needed:** **PAS** (BMZ thickening), **Alcian blue/colloidal iron** (mucin), **DIF** (fibrinogen vs Ig band), **CD123** (plasmacytoid DCs, ↑ in LE), and clinical correlation + serology (**ANA, anti-dsDNA, anti-Ro/La, C3/C4, CBC, urinalysis**).

## 6. Common Misunderstandings — Let Me Head These Off

**❌ "Interface dermatitis means lichen planus."**
No. Interface change is a *pattern*, shared by LP, all forms of LE, dermatomyositis, lichenoid drug eruptions, erythema multiforme/SJS-TEN, fixed drug eruption, GVHD, lichen sclerosus, lichen nitidus, pityriasis lichenoides, secondary syphilis, paraneoplastic pemphigus and more. **Interface change opens the differential; it does not close it.**

**❌ "Basal cell vacuolation favours one over the other."**
It favours *neither*. Both have it. **Depth and company, not the junction itself, are decisive.**

**❌ "Parakeratosis is fine in LP."**
No — and the source table's claim of "ortho/parakeratosis" in LP is an error. **Parakeratosis in a lichenoid biopsy should make you think drug eruption or lichenoid keratosis.**

**❌ "DLE can show pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia."**
Only **hypertrophic/verrucous LE** does. **Standard DLE is atrophic.** This is the second source error, and it matters because PEH in a lupus-like biopsy raises the serious alternative of **squamous cell carcinoma** — which, incidentally, can genuinely arise in long-standing DLE scars, so this is not a purely academic worry.

**❌ "Absence of a lupus band excludes DLE."**
No. DIF is **supportive, not obligatory**. Early lesions, treated lesions, and inadequately selected biopsy sites can be negative.

**❌ "They can always be separated."**
They cannot, always. Recognise three genuinely hard scenarios:
1. **LE/LP overlap syndrome** — real, rare, with mixed clinical, histological and immunofluorescence features; often on acral/palmoplantar skin, often chronic and treatment-resistant.
2. **Mucosal disease** — oral LP and oral DLE overlap considerably; DLE tends to show more mucin and a deeper, more periadnexal/perivascular infiltrate with plasma cells, but the site's baseline plasma cells reduce that clue's value.
3. **Late scarring alopecia (end-stage "burnt-out" scalp)** — once follicles are replaced by fibrous tracts, *neither* diagnosis may be assignable; the report reads "end-stage cicatricial alopecia." **Biopsy the active, inflamed peripheral margin, not the bald centre.** This is a practical rule worth remembering for life.

## 7. Why Any of This Matters Clinically

Both diseases can present as **violaceous, scaly papules and plaques**, both favour certain sun-exposed sites, both show interface dermatitis, and **both cause scarring alopecia on the scalp.** So why bother separating them?

1. **Prognosis of the skin lesion.** LP of glabrous skin is **self-limited and non-scarring** (it resolves with hyperpigmentation over months to ~1–2 years). DLE is **chronic and scarring** — atrophy, dyspigmentation and permanent alopecia. One reassures the patient; the other mandates aggressive early control and **rigorous photoprotection**.
2. **Different treatment ladders.** LP → **potent topical/intralesional corticosteroids, topical calcineurin inhibitors, oral steroids, acitretin, phototherapy, methotrexate/ciclosporin** for severe disease. DLE → **photoprotection + smoking cessation** (smoking reduces antimalarial efficacy) **+ antimalarials (hydroxychloroquine ± quinacrine, chloroquine)** as the therapeutic backbone, then thalidomide, methotrexate, mycophenolate, dapsone, retinoids, belimumab/anifrolumab in refractory cases. **Antimalarials do nothing for LP; they are first-line for DLE.** Getting the diagnosis wrong means getting the drug wrong.
3. **Systemic implications.** DLE may be a **cutaneous manifestation of, or a risk marker for, SLE** — roughly 5–20% of localised DLE patients progress (higher with widespread/generalised DLE), so the diagnosis obliges **ANA, anti-dsDNA, anti-Ro/La, complements, CBC, urinalysis/renal assessment and longitudinal follow-up.** LP instead prompts consideration of **hepatitis C serology** (in endemic/at-risk populations), drug and dental-amalgam review, and — for erosive oral/genital LP — **long-term surveillance for squamous cell carcinoma.**
4. **Malignant potential in scars.** Long-standing DLE plaques (especially on the lip and in scars) carry a recognised risk of **SCC** — a reason to biopsy any hyperkeratotic, indurated or ulcerating change.

## 8. Summary Anchor — The Core Idea in One Picture

Close your eyes and hold **two contrasting images**:

> **🟪 LICHEN PLANUS — "the thick, sharp, superficial blitz."**
> A **thickened** epidermis wearing a compact orthokeratotic cap, with **wedges of hypergranulosis** (= Wickham's striae), **saw-toothed rete ridges**, a **dense flat band of CD8⁺ lymphocytes hugging the junction and stopping dead**, **abundant Civatte bodies**, occasional **Max Joseph clefts**, melanophages beneath — and **nothing deep**: no plasma cells, no mucin, no fibrosis, no follicular plugs. DIF: **shaggy fibrinogen + globular IgM**.
> *One violent, short battle at the front door. The house thickens its walls and survives.*

> **🟥 DISCOID LUPUS — "the thin, deep, chronic siege."**
> An **atrophic** epidermis with **effaced rete ridges** and a **thick PAS-positive BMZ**, **plugged follicles** (= carpet-tack sign), interface damage extending **into the follicles**, lymphocytes **cuffing vessels and adnexa superficially *and* deeply**, **plasma cells** in the mix, **mucin** swelling the reticular dermis, and finally **fibrosis** that erases the adnexa. DIF: **granular IgG/IgM/C3 — the lupus band**.
> *A years-long occupation reaching every depth. The house thins, is patched repeatedly, floods, and ends up scarred.*

## 📌 Appendix — Verification and Source Notes Carried Forward

**Factual corrections made to the source table (pp. 794–795):**

1. **LP cornified layer** — source: "dense hyperkeratosis with ortho/parakeratosis." **Corrected to "dense orthokeratotic hyperkeratosis (without parakeratosis)."** Parakeratosis in a lichenoid infiltrate suggests lichenoid **drug** eruption, LP-like keratosis, or overlap — not classic idiopathic LP. *(Rook's — Histopathology of LP; Bolognia — Lichen Planus)*
