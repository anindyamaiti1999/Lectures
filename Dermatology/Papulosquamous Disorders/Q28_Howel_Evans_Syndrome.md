# Howel–Evans Syndrome — A Mini-Lecture

## 1. The Big Picture: Why This Rare Syndrome Deserves Your Attention

Let me start with the single sentence that makes this disease unforgettable:

> **Howel–Evans syndrome is the disease where a thickened sole predicts a lethal cancer of the food pipe.**

Most palmoplantar keratodermas (PPKs) are, frankly, nuisances — uncomfortable, cosmetically distressing, sometimes disabling, but they don't kill people. Howel–Evans is the glaring exception. Here, the keratoderma is not the disease; it is a **visible flag planted on the skin by a mutation that is simultaneously reprogramming the lining of the esophagus toward malignancy.** More than 95% of affected individuals will develop esophageal squamous cell carcinoma by age 65.

That is why this syndrome matters far beyond its rarity. It is the **cleanest proof of concept in all of dermatology** that the skin can be a window into internal disease — a genodermatosis in which recognising a callus on a teenager's foot can lead to endoscopic surveillance that saves their life twenty-five years later.

It is also a beautiful teaching case in molecular medicine, because we now know *exactly* why the palms, the soles, and the esophagus are affected together, and the answer turns out to be one protein acting as a chaperone for a pair of molecular scissors. We'll get there step by step.

---

## 2. Naming and History: Decoding the Vocabulary First

Students often stumble on this disease simply because of its names. Let's dismantle them.

| Term | What it literally means | Why it's used here |
|---|---|---|
| **Howel–Evans syndrome** | Eponym | Described in **1958** by **Howel-Evans, McConnell, Clarke and Sheppard** in two large **Liverpool families** (classically called Family A and Family B), traced over several generations |
| **Tylosis** | Greek *tylos* = "callus, knot" | Describes the clinical lesion: a **focal, non-epidermolytic thickening of the plantar skin**, looking like an exaggerated callus |
| **TOC** | **T**ylosis with **O**esophageal **C**ancer | The modern, mechanistically honest name — it tells you both halves of the syndrome |
| **TOC locus** | Chromosome **17q25** | The region mapped by linkage analysis long before the gene was found |
| ***RHBDF2* / iRhom2** | Rhomboid 5 homolog 2 | The actual culprit gene, identified in **2012** (Blaydon et al., *Am J Hum Genet*) |

**A small point of typography, since it appears in exam answers:** the correct rendering is **Howel–Evans** with an **en-dash**, because it joins **two separate surnames** (Dr. Howel-Evans and Dr. Evans are *not* the same — actually the convention is that the eponym honours multiple describers, and an en-dash rather than a hyphen is standard medical typography for joined surnames). Don't lose sleep over this, but it's a genuine convention.

Similarly, write **"missense mutation"** as one word — never "mis-sense." A missense mutation is a single base change that swaps one amino acid for another in the finished protein, leaving the protein intact but functionally altered. Remember that definition, because it is central to what follows: this is **not** a protein that is destroyed; it is a protein that is **hyperactive**.

---

## 3. The Genetics in Plain Language

- **Inheritance:** **Autosomal dominant.** One mutant copy is enough. Each child of an affected parent has a **50% risk**. Both sexes equally affected. Vertical transmission through generations is the classic pedigree pattern — and indeed the original Liverpool families showed exactly this over many generations.
- **Gene:** ***RHBDF2*** at **17q25**, encoding the protein **iRhom2**.
- **Mutation type:** **Missense**, clustering in a specific **N-terminal cytoplasmic proline-rich region** of iRhom2 (e.g., p.Ile186Thr, p.Asp188Asn, p.Pro189Leu).
- **Functional consequence:** **Gain of function**, not loss.

That last point is the intellectual heart of the disease, so let's linger on it.

Most genodermatoses you learn are **loss-of-function**: a keratin is broken, a transporter fails, an enzyme is absent, and tissue falls apart. Howel–Evans is the opposite. The mutation makes iRhom2 **too good at its job**, and the downstream machinery runs hot. Hyperactive growth signalling is exactly the kind of lesion that causes **both hyperkeratosis (too much epithelium) and carcinoma (uncontrolled epithelium)**. Once you see it as a "stuck accelerator" disease, every clinical feature becomes predictable.

## 4. The Mechanism, Step by Step

Here's where the source material needs correcting and expanding, because the commonly quoted line — "*RHBDF2* produces RHBDF2 protease" — is misleading.

### Step 0: Meet the players

**iRhom2 (product of *RHBDF2*)** belongs to the **rhomboid family of intramembrane proteases** — but here's the twist: **iRhom2 is catalytically dead.** It is a **pseudoprotease**. It has the shape of an enzyme but has lost the residues needed to cut anything. The lowercase "i" in iRhom stands for **inactive**.

So what does a dead enzyme do for a living? It works as a **chaperone and trafficking escort**.

**ADAM17**, also called **TACE (TNF-α Converting Enzyme)**, is the protein iRhom2 escorts. ADAM17 is a **"sheddase"** — a pair of molecular scissors anchored in the cell membrane whose job is to snip the extracellular portions off other membrane-bound proteins, releasing them as soluble signalling molecules. Its substrate list is a who's-who of biology:

- **TNF-α** (hence the name TACE) — inflammation
- **EGFR ligands**: **TGF-α, amphiregulin, epiregulin, HB-EGF** — proliferation and wound healing
- **Notch receptors, L-selectin, and dozens more**

### Step 1: The normal choreography

ADAM17 is synthesised in the **endoplasmic reticulum** as an immature pro-enzyme. It cannot leave on its own. **iRhom2 binds it in the ER and physically escorts it** through the Golgi to the plasma membrane, where the pro-domain is removed and ADAM17 becomes mature, active, surface-resident scissors.

> **Analogy — the bouncer and the guest list.** Think of ADAM17 as a skilled but unlicensed worker sitting in a back room (the ER). iRhom2 is the supervisor who signs the paperwork, walks the worker to the shop floor, and decides how many workers get out there. No iRhom2, no ADAM17 at the surface — the scissors never reach the workbench. **Too much iRhom2 activity, and the shop floor is flooded with scissors.**

This is why iRhom2 is described as regulating "the **maturation** of TACE" — it's about **licensing and delivery**, not about cutting.

### Step 2: The mutation

The missense change in the iRhom2 cytoplasmic tail makes it a **more efficient escort** (and also alters how iRhom2 itself is regulated and degraded). Net result:

**↑ mature ADAM17 at the cell surface = ↑ sheddase activity.**

### Step 3: The consequence — an EGFR feedback loop that never switches off

More ADAM17 at the surface means more cleavage of **amphiregulin, epiregulin and TGF-α** from the keratinocyte membrane. These are all **EGFR ligands**. Once released, they bind **EGFR on the same and neighbouring cells** — an **autocrine and paracrine loop**.

EGFR signalling does exactly three things relevant to us:
1. **Drives proliferation** (via RAS–MAPK)
2. **Promotes survival** (via PI3K–AKT, resisting apoptosis)
3. **Suppresses terminal differentiation** and promotes migration

And here's the vicious circle: **EGFR signalling itself upregulates ADAM17 activity.** The loop feeds itself.

### Step 4: The full cascade

```
Missense mutation in RHBDF2 (iRhom2), 17q25
                ↓  gain of function
Enhanced ER→surface trafficking & maturation of ADAM17 (TACE)
                ↓
↑↑ Shedding of EGFR ligands (amphiregulin, epiregulin, TGF-α)
   [± altered TNF-α release]
                ↓
Sustained, self-amplifying EGFR → RAS/MAPK + PI3K/AKT signalling
                ↓
     ┌──────────────────────┴──────────────────────┐
Hyperproliferation +                    Hyperproliferation +
impaired terminal                       genomic instability in
differentiation in                      oesophageal squamous
palmoplantar epidermis                  epithelium
     ↓                                          ↓
FOCAL PALMOPLANTAR                   Dysplasia → carcinoma in situ
KERATODERMA (tylosis)                → INVASIVE SQUAMOUS CELL CA
+ oral leukokeratosis                  (5th decade onward)
```

> **A lovely piece of corroborating evidence.** The mouse mutant **"curly bare" (*cub*)** carries a *Rhbdf2* mutation and shows wavy/absent hair, thickened skin, **and strikingly accelerated wound healing** — precisely what you'd predict from too much EGFR-ligand shedding. Conversely, humans with **loss-of-function ADAM17 deficiency** develop neonatal **inflammatory skin and bowel disease** with sparse hair. So ADAM17 activity is a dial: **turn it down → barrier and inflammatory disease; turn it up → hyperkeratosis and cancer.** Howel–Evans sits at the high end of that dial.

## 5. Why *These* Sites? Palms, Soles, Mouth, Esophagus

Students always ask: if the mutation is in every cell, why is the disease site-restricted? Two reasons, and they're worth understanding rather than memorising.

**Reason 1 — Shared tissue identity.** The **palmoplantar epidermis, the oral mucosa, and the esophageal lining are all stratified squamous epithelia** that depend heavily on EGFR signalling for their turnover. They are, developmentally and biochemically, cousins. A mutation that dysregulates squamous proliferation will announce itself in squamous tissue.

**Reason 2 — Mechanical load selects the sites.** Within the skin, the disease concentrates on **pressure-bearing points of the soles** (heel, metatarsal heads, and along the sides where the foot loads). Why? Because **mechanical trauma is itself a potent trigger of ADAM17-mediated ligand shedding and EGFR activation** — it's the normal "wound healing / callus" response. In Howel–Evans, that response is already running hot, so wherever there is friction, the epidermis over-responds and piles up keratin.

> **Analogy.** Everyone forms a callus where a shoe rubs — that's the normal, well-calibrated repair response. In Howel–Evans, the repair thermostat is set too high, so ordinary walking produces extraordinary callus. And in the esophagus, where the "friction" is the daily passage of hot food, acid, alcohol and tobacco, that same over-responsive repair programme, repeated for decades, accumulates mutations and tips into cancer.

This also explains a key morphological descriptor: the PPK is **focal and non-transgradient**.
- **Focal** = discrete islands over pressure points, *not* a uniform sheet (that would be *diffuse*).
- **Non-transgradient** = the thickening **stays on the palmar/plantar surface and does not spill over onto the dorsum** of the hands, feet, wrists or ankles. (Contrast with *transgradient* keratodermas such as Mal de Meleda, where it climbs over the edges in a "glove-and-stocking" fashion.)
- **Non-epidermolytic** = histologically there is orthohyperkeratosis without the keratinocyte lysis seen in keratin-mutation PPKs.

## 6. Clinical Features — The Complete Walkthrough

### (a) Focal palmoplantar keratoderma (tylosis) — the herald sign
- **Onset: late childhood to adolescence** (typically 5–15 years; some report from the first decade). It is **not** present at birth — a useful discriminator from congenital keratodermas.
- **Distribution:** **pressure/weight-bearing areas of the soles** predominantly; palms involved more variably and usually more mildly, especially at grip sites.
- **Morphology:** yellowish, firm, well-demarcated hyperkeratotic plaques; may fissure painfully.
- **Symptoms:** pain on walking, fissuring, sometimes secondary infection. Hyperhidrosis may accompany it.
- **What it is *not*:** it is **not** typically associated with constriction bands or digital autoamputation (unlike, say, Vohwinkel syndrome or Olmsted syndrome), and **nails and hair are characteristically normal** — remember this for the differential.

### (b) Oral leukokeratosis
- **Whitish, adherent, thickened mucosal plaques** — most often on the gingivae and buccal mucosa.
- Mechanistically identical to the skin lesion: squamous epithelium responding to over-driven EGFR signalling by thickening.
- **Clinically important because it mimics leukoplakia.** In a patient with tylosis, don't reflexively label these as tobacco-related premalignant leukoplakia — but equally, do not ignore mucosal change in a cancer-prone syndrome. Examine the mouth at every visit.

### (c) Follicular accentuation / follicular keratosis
- **Keratotic papules centred on hair follicles**, giving the skin a rough, "grater-like" feel — often on the trunk and extremities.
- Again, the same theme: follicular ostia are miniature keratinising units, and they too over-keratinise.
- Easily overlooked, but a helpful supportive sign when you're assembling the syndrome.

### (d) Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma — the reason we care
This is the defining feature, and the numbers deserve to be stated precisely:

| Parameter | Figure |
|---|---|
| **Histology** | **Squamous cell carcinoma** (NOT adenocarcinoma — this is not a Barrett's/reflux pathway) |
| **Typical site** | **Mid and lower esophagus** |
| **Age of onset** | Commonly quoted as the **5th decade**; mean onset ~45–65 years. Cases before 30 are rare but reported |
| **Cumulative risk** | **~40–50% by age 45; >90–95% by age 65** |
| **Effect of carcinogens** | Tobacco and alcohol are believed to **accelerate** onset — a crucial counselling point |

Understand the sequence: **normal squamous mucosa → hyperproliferation → low-grade dysplasia → high-grade dysplasia/carcinoma in situ → invasive SCC.** This is a multi-step progression taking years to decades, which is precisely **why surveillance works** — there is a long window in which a treatable precursor lesion is detectable.

### (e) An important clarification about "tylotic lesions progressing to SCC"

Older texts and some notes state that "tylotic lesions can progress to squamous cell carcinoma," which is easy to misread. Let me make this precise:

> **The palmoplantar keratoderma does not transform into the esophageal cancer.** These are **two separate organs expressing one shared genetic defect.** The tylosis is a *marker*, not a *precursor*, of the esophageal SCC.

That said, two caveats keep the original statement from being pure error: (i) both lesions arise from the *same* hyperproliferative EGFR-driven programme, so they are biologically linked; and (ii) chronically hyperkeratotic, fissured, inflamed squamous skin anywhere carries a small independent risk of cutaneous SCC over decades. But the classic, high-penetrance, life-threatening malignancy is **esophageal**, arising from **esophageal mucosa**.

## 7. Diagnosis

Diagnosis rests on three legs:

1. **Clinical recognition** — focal, pressure-point, non-transgradient plantar keratoderma beginning in late childhood, with normal nails and hair, plus oral leukokeratosis and follicular accentuation.
2. **Family history** — a dominant pedigree with keratoderma and/or early-onset esophageal cancer. **Always draw the pedigree.** A family history of "throat cancer" or "food-pipe cancer" in multiple generations is the single most valuable clue.
3. **Molecular confirmation** — **sequencing of *RHBDF2***. This is transformative, because it allows **presymptomatic testing** of at-risk relatives: those who test negative can be spared a lifetime of endoscopies, and those positive can be enrolled in surveillance before any symptom appears.

Skin biopsy is supportive but not diagnostic: **orthohyperkeratosis, acanthosis, hypergranulosis, without epidermolysis**.

## 8. Management — Two Parallel Tracks

Think of management as **two entirely separate agendas that happen to share a patient**: (A) making the skin comfortable, and (B) preventing death from cancer. Track B is the one that matters most, and it is the one candidates most often forget.

### Track A — Symptomatic care of the keratoderma
- **Keratolytics / emollients:** **urea 20–40%, salicylic acid 5–10%, lactic acid**, propylene glycol, under occlusion for stubborn plaques. These soften and thin the keratin and reduce painful fissuring.
- **Mechanical debridement / paring** by a podiatrist; **pressure-redistributing orthotics and well-fitted footwear** — this directly targets the mechanical trigger, so it is genuinely disease-modifying for the skin, not merely cosmetic.
- **Topical retinoids or vitamin D analogues** as adjuncts.
- **Oral retinoids (acitretin, isotretinoin)** for severe, disabling keratoderma and for the oral mucosal disease. Retinoids normalise keratinocyte differentiation and reduce hyperkeratosis.
  - *A note on "prevention of autoamputation":* this phrase is inherited from the treatment of **Papillon–Lefèvre** and mutilating keratodermas discussed alongside Howel–Evans in many texts. **Digital autoamputation is not a characteristic feature of Howel–Evans syndrome.** Use retinoids here for keratoderma severity and mucosal disease, not for a constriction-band risk that this syndrome doesn't really carry.
- Treat secondary **bacterial and dermatophyte infection**, which is common in fissured keratoderma.

### Track B — Cancer risk management (the priority)
- **Lifelong upper GI endoscopic surveillance.** Widely recommended to begin around **age 30** (earlier in families with early-onset cases) and repeat at **1–3 year intervals**, lifelong.
- **Enhanced imaging at endoscopy:** **Lugol's iodine chromoendoscopy** (dysplastic squamous mucosa fails to stain, appearing as pale "unstained islands") and narrow-band imaging, with **targeted plus random four-quadrant biopsies**. Random biopsy alone misses flat dysplasia.
- **Endoscopic therapy of dysplasia** — endoscopic mucosal resection or radiofrequency ablation for high-grade dysplasia/early mucosal cancer.
- **Prophylactic esophagectomy** has been debated and occasionally performed in selected high-risk individuals, but carries substantial morbidity; it is not routine, and modern practice favours intensive endoscopic surveillance with early intervention.
- **Risk-factor modification:** absolute **tobacco and alcohol cessation**, since these are the same carcinogens that drive sporadic esophageal SCC and plausibly accelerate onset in a genetically primed epithelium. Also attention to hot beverages and nutritional adequacy.
- **Oral cavity surveillance** at each dermatological visit.

### Track C — Genetic counselling (never optional)
- Explain **autosomal dominant inheritance, 50% recurrence risk per child**, and the near-complete penetrance of cancer.
- Offer **cascade genetic testing** to first-degree relatives.
- Discuss **predictive testing in minors** carefully — testing is usually deferred until the age at which surveillance would begin, unless the keratoderma itself declares the diagnosis.
- Address the very real **psychological burden** of knowing one carries a >90% cancer risk; involve clinical psychology and patient support.
- Discuss reproductive options where relevant (prenatal diagnosis, preimplantation genetic testing).

## 9. Differential Diagnosis — and, More Usefully, How to Tell Them Apart

The two classically paired differentials are pachyonychia congenita and Clouston syndrome. Here's *why* each is a contender and *what breaks the tie*:

| Condition | Why it mimics Howel–Evans | The discriminating feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Pachyonychia congenita** (*KRT6A/6B/16/17*) | **Focal, painful, pressure-point plantar keratoderma** + **oral leukokeratosis** — an almost identical skin/mucosa picture | **Nails are grossly abnormal** — thickened, wedge-shaped, hypertrophic **pachyonychia**. Plus follicular hyperkeratosis, cysts, natal teeth. **Nails are normal in Howel–Evans.** No esophageal cancer risk |
| **Clouston syndrome** (hidrotic ectodermal dysplasia, *GJB6*/connexin 30) | PPK + **nail dystrophy** | **Triad of PPK + nail dystrophy + diffuse alopecia/sparse wiry hair**, with **normal sweating and normal teeth**. Hair and nails are normal in Howel–Evans |
| **Focal hereditary PPK / keratosis palmoplantaris nummularis** | Identical morphology of the keratoderma alone | **No oral leukokeratosis, no cancer predisposition, negative family cancer history, *RHBDF2* normal** |
| **Acquired / mechanical callosities** | Pressure-point distribution | Confined to footwear/occupational sites, resolves with removal of trauma, no family history, no mucosal signs |
| **Olmsted syndrome** (*TRPV3*) | Palmoplantar keratoderma + periorificial plaques | **Mutilating**, with constriction and autoamputation; onset in infancy; periorificial keratotic plaques |
| **Tylosis-like PPK with other cancer syndromes** | Cancer + keratoderma | e.g., keratoderma in **Costello/other syndromes**; also consider **esophageal SCC risk in Fanconi anemia, Plummer–Vinson, achalasia, lye stricture** — but these lack focal PPK |

**Bottom line heuristic:** *Focal plantar keratoderma + oral white plaques → check the nails.*
- **Nails abnormal → Pachyonychia congenita.**
- **Nails and hair normal, but a family tree littered with esophageal cancer → Howel–Evans. Send *RHBDF2*, book the endoscopy.**

## 10. Common Misunderstandings — Let's Clear These Up

1. **"iRhom2 is a protease that cuts things."** ❌ It is a **catalytically inactive pseudoprotease** — a **chaperone/escort** for ADAM17. The gene is *RHBDF2*; the protein is **iRhom2**. Saying "RHBDF2 protease" conflates gene with protein and attributes an enzymatic activity that doesn't exist.

2. **"The keratoderma turns into the cancer."** ❌ Two organs, one mutation. The tylosis is a **marker**, not a **precursor**, of esophageal SCC.

3. **"It's an adenocarcinoma, like Barrett's."** ❌ **Squamous cell carcinoma**, arising from native squamous mucosa in the mid/lower esophagus. No relationship to reflux-driven intestinal metaplasia.

4. **"It's a loss-of-function keratin defect like most PPKs."** ❌ It's a **gain-of-function signalling defect**. Histology is **non-epidermolytic** precisely because no structural keratin is broken.

5. **"Present from birth."** ❌ Keratoderma appears in **late childhood/adolescence**; cancer in **middle age**. Age-dependent penetrance is the rule.

6. **"Treat it like Papillon–Lefèvre — retinoids to prevent autoamputation."** ❌ That line is borrowed from a neighbouring topic. Retinoids here address keratoderma and oral disease. **Autoamputation is not a feature.** And retinoids **do not prevent the esophageal cancer** — nothing replaces endoscopic surveillance.

7. **"The cancer risk is a vague 'increased risk.'"** ❌ It is **near-obligate: >90–95% by age 65**. Counsel with that number, not with vagueness.

8. **Forgetting the family.** ❌ In an autosomal dominant cancer syndrome, **diagnosing one patient means you have a duty to the whole pedigree.** Cascade testing is part of treatment.

---

## 11. The Core Idea in One Picture

> **One over-eager escort protein floods the cell surface with molecular scissors; those scissors release a stream of growth signals; and every squamous epithelium in the body that feels friction responds by growing too much.**
>
> **Where the friction is a shoe, you get a callus you can see. Where the friction is food, acid and smoke passing down the esophagus for forty years, you get a cancer you must go looking for.**
>
> **So: see the callus, screen the esophagus, and test the family.**

### One last thought to carry away

Howel–Evans syndrome is short on therapeutic options but enormous in principle. It teaches that a dermatologist's most powerful intervention is sometimes not a cream or a tablet, but **a correct diagnosis, a drawn pedigree, and a referral for endoscopy.** In this disease, the clinician who *looks at the soles and then asks about the family* does more to extend life than any prescription could.
