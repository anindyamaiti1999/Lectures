# 🎓 Palmoplantar Keratodermas — A Mini-Lecture

*"Why do the palms and soles so often turn to stone, and what does that stone tell us about the rest of the body?"*

---

## PART 1 — THE BIG PICTURE: Why Palms and Soles Are Special

Before we classify anything, let's understand **why this group of diseases exists at all**.

Your palms and soles are not ordinary skin. They are **volar (acral) skin**, and they differ from the rest of your body in five fundamental ways:

| Feature | Volar skin (palms/soles) | Rest of the body |
|---|---|---|
| **Stratum corneum** | Extremely thick (up to 40× thicker) | Thin |
| **Hair follicles** | **Absent** | Present |
| **Sebaceous glands** | **Absent** | Present |
| **Eccrine sweat glands** | Very dense | Sparse |
| **Keratin expression** | Unique — **K9** is expressed *only* here; K6/K16/K17 are constitutively high | K1/K10 dominant |

Think of volar skin as the **tire tread of the body**. It is engineered for load-bearing, shear stress, and grip. Because of that, it is built out of a *special reinforced material* — and any genetic defect in that special material will announce itself **most loudly on the palms and soles**, and often *nowhere else*.

This single insight explains the entire field:

> **Palmoplantar keratoderma (PPK) is what happens when the reinforced architecture of volar skin fails — either because a structural protein is defective (inherited), or because the skin's turnover is being pathologically driven (acquired).**

And here is the second insight, the one that makes PPK genuinely high-yield in exams and in clinics:

> Many of the proteins that build volar skin are **also used elsewhere in the body** — in the heart, the cochlea, the periodontium, the oesophagus, the teeth, the cornea. So a thick palm can be the **first visible sign of a lethal cardiomyopathy, a deafness gene, or an oesophageal cancer syndrome.**

The palm becomes a *window*. That's why we don't just describe PPK — we **classify** it, because classification is what tells you which window you are looking through.

---

## PART 2 — DEFINITION

> **Palmoplantar keratodermas (PPKs)** are a group of cutaneous disorders characterised by **hyperkeratosis of the palms and soles**. They may be **acquired** or **inherited**, and are subclassified by (a) the **morphological pattern** of hyperkeratosis, (b) the **distribution** (whether it stays within the volar margins), and (c) the presence of **associated extracutaneous features**.

Note the three axes in that definition — pattern, distribution, associations. Memorise those three, and the classification writes itself.

## PART 3 — THE MECHANISTIC FRAMEWORK (the part most books skip)

Rather than memorising 25 eponyms, understand that nearly every inherited PPK gene belongs to one of **six functional families**. This is the "periodic table" of keratodermas.

### 1️⃣ The Scaffolding — Keratin intermediate filaments
**Genes: *KRT1, KRT9, KRT16, KRT6A, KRT6B, KRT17***

Keratins are the **steel rebar** inside keratinocytes. Two keratins (one acidic type I + one basic type II) coil into a heterodimer, which assembles into a filament network anchored to desmosomes. Under shear stress, this network distributes force.

- **Cause → effect → outcome:** A missense mutation in the helical rod domain produces a *dominant-negative* keratin — a defective rebar that poisons the whole lattice. → Filaments clump instead of forming a network. → Cells rupture under mechanical stress (**cytolysis**) → the epidermis responds with reactive hyperproliferation → thick, hyperkeratotic skin. Histology shows **epidermolytic hyperkeratosis** (vacuolated keratinocytes, clumped tonofilaments, coarse keratohyalin granules).
- **Why palms/soles?** Because **K9 is expressed exclusively in volar epidermis** — so a *KRT9* mutation can produce disease in *no other site*. Beautiful genotype–phenotype logic.

**Analogy:** One bent reinforcing bar in a concrete slab doesn't weaken just itself — it prevents all the other bars from interlocking. That is dominant-negative inheritance, and it explains why keratin PPKs are **autosomal dominant**.

### 2️⃣ The Mortar — Cornified envelope / crosslinking proteins
**Genes: *LOR* (loricrin), *TGM1*, *SERPINB7***

Loricrin is the **major protein of the cornified envelope** (~70–85%), crosslinked by transglutaminases into an insoluble shell. It's the *mortar* between the keratin bricks.

- A frameshift in *LOR* creates a mutant protein with a **novel arginine-rich tail that carries a nuclear localisation signal** → the mutant loricrin is mis-trafficked into the nucleus instead of the envelope → defective envelope → retention hyperkeratosis and constricting bands (**loricrin keratoderma / Camisa's disease**).

### 3️⃣ The Intercom — Gap junctions (connexins)
**Genes: *GJB2* (connexin 26), *GJB6* (connexin 30)**

Connexins hexamerise into **connexons**, which dock with a neighbour's connexon to form a **gap junction** — a direct pore allowing passage of ions, ATP, IP₃ and small metabolites (<1 kDa) between adjacent cells. This is how the epidermis *coordinates* differentiation and how the cochlear supporting cells recycle **K⁺** back into the endolymph.

- **Cause → effect → outcome:** A dominant *GJB2* mutation yields a connexin that assembles but leaks abnormally (gain-of-function hemichannel) or fails to dock. → Epidermal differentiation becomes uncoordinated → keratoderma. → **Simultaneously**, cochlear K⁺ recycling fails → the endocochlear potential collapses → **sensorineural hearing loss**.
- **This is the single most elegant "aha" in the whole topic:** *connexin keratodermas come with deafness*, because skin and cochlea use the same intercom.
- **The GJB2 family:** Vohwinkel syndrome, Bart–Pumphrey syndrome, KID syndrome (keratitis–ichthyosis–deafness), PPK with deafness, Clouston syndrome (*GJB6*).

### 4️⃣ The Spot-Welds — Desmosomal proteins
**Genes: *JUP* (plakoglobin), *DSP* (desmoplakin), *DSG1* (desmoglein 1)**

Desmosomes are **rivets** linking neighbouring cells; the intracellular plaque (plakoglobin, plakophilin, desmoplakin) tethers cadherins to the keratin cytoskeleton. Both **epidermis** and **cardiac myocytes** (at the intercalated disc) depend on them, and **hair cortex** uses them too.

- **Cause → effect → outcome:** Defective desmosomes → tissues subjected to repetitive mechanical stress fail. → **Palms/soles** (friction) get PPK. → **Hair** loses cortical cohesion → *woolly, coarse, unruly hair*. → **Myocardium** (contracting ~2 billion times) suffers progressive myocyte detachment, death, and **fibrofatty replacement** → arrhythmias, heart failure, sudden death.
- **This is the "cardiocutaneous" axis** — Naxos and Carvajal. A thick palm plus woolly hair should trigger an **ECG and echocardiogram**, not just a jar of urea cream.

### 5️⃣ The Housekeepers — Proteases, protease inhibitors, and signalling receptors
**Genes: *CTSC* (cathepsin C), *SLURP1/ARS*, *TRPV3*, *SERPINB7*, *TAT***

Desquamation is a **controlled demolition**: proteases (kallikreins, cathepsins) must cut corneodesmosomes at exactly the right rate, restrained by inhibitors (LEKTI, serpins). Too little proteolysis → retention hyperkeratosis. Too much → barrier breakdown.

- ***CTSC*** encodes cathepsin C (dipeptidyl peptidase I), which **activates** neutrophil serine proteases (elastase, cathepsin G, proteinase 3). Loss of function → neutrophils can't kill periodontal bacteria → **Papillon–Lefèvre syndrome** (PPK + aggressive periodontitis + tooth loss + pyogenic liver abscess risk).
- ***SLURP1*** (secreted Ly-6/uPAR-related protein-1) is a **secreted allosteric modulator of the α7 nicotinic acetylcholine receptor** on keratinocytes. Non-neuronal acetylcholine signalling regulates keratinocyte adhesion, migration and apoptosis. Loss → **Mal de Meleda**.
- ***TRPV3*** is a **heat/chemical-activated calcium channel**. **Gain-of-function** → excessive Ca²⁺ influx → hyperproliferation *and* itch/pain via keratinocyte–nerve crosstalk → **Olmsted syndrome** (mutilating PPK with severe pain and itch).

### 6️⃣ The Metabolic accidents
**Gene: *TAT*** (tyrosine aminotransferase) → tyrosinaemia type II → tyrosine crystals deposit in cornea and volar epidermis → **Richner–Hanhart disease**.

> 🧠 **Teaching move:** When you see a PPK gene in an exam, first ask *"which of the six families does this belong to?"* The associated features then follow almost automatically — connexin ⇒ ear; desmosome ⇒ heart + hair; protease ⇒ gums; keratin ⇒ nails/oral mucosa.

### Understanding the three morphological patterns

| Pattern | What you see | Mental image |
|---|---|---|
| **Diffuse** | Uniform, **confluent** hyperkeratosis covering the entire palmoplantar surface | A **glove or sock of wax** |
| **Focal** | **Discrete plaques/columns** at pressure and friction sites (heels, metatarsal heads, digital flexion lines) | **Callus-like islands**; if linear along flexion creases = **striate** |
| **Punctate** | Multiple **tiny discrete keratotic papules or pits** scattered over the surface | **"Raindrops," "music-box spines,"** or a **cobblestone street** |

### Understanding "transgradient"

This is the single most misunderstood word in the topic, so let's nail it.

- **Transgradient** (Greek *trans* = across + *gradi* = to step) = the keratoderma **steps across the boundary** of the palm/sole onto the **dorsal surfaces** of hands, feet, wrists, ankles, and may extend to elbows, knees and the tendo Achilles.
- **Non-transgradient** = the hyperkeratosis stops **sharply at the volar margin**, often with an erythematous rim — the classic "**dipped in wax up to a line**" appearance.

**Why does this matter mechanistically?** Non-transgradient disease implies the defective protein is **volar-restricted** (e.g. K9) or the phenotype is purely **mechanically driven** — so it appears only where friction is greatest. Transgradient disease implies a defect in a **globally expressed** protein (loricrin, SLURP-1, connexins) where volar skin is simply the *worst-affected*, not the *only* affected site.

Also note the related term **progrediens / progressive** (e.g. **Greither syndrome**): the keratoderma **extends over time** with age.

## PART 5 — ACQUIRED PALMOPLANTAR KERATODERMAS

These matter enormously in real practice because they are **common, reversible, and sometimes a warning of internal malignancy**. Key clinical clue: **late onset, asymmetry, no family history, and relatively rapid evolution.**

### 1. Keratoderma climactericum (Haxthausen's disease)
- **What:** Hyperkeratotic, fissured plaques on **pressure-bearing** areas of palms and especially **heels**, in **peri-/post-menopausal women**, often obese and hypertensive.
- **Mechanism:** Falling oestrogen alters keratinocyte proliferation and the volar barrier; mechanical load on the weight-bearing heel converts this into painful fissuring.
- **Why it matters:** Extremely painful fissures; responds to **topical oestrogens, keratolytics, and weight/pressure offloading**.

### 2. Keratoderma associated with malignancy (paraneoplastic)
Three classic patterns you must distinguish:
- **Tylosis-like paraneoplastic PPK** — diffuse keratoderma appearing with internal cancer.
- **Acrokeratosis paraneoplastica (Bazex syndrome)** — psoriasiform violaceous scaling of **ears, nose, hands, feet** with PPK and nail dystrophy; overwhelmingly associated with **squamous cell carcinoma of the upper aerodigestive tract**.
- **Tripe palms (acanthosis palmaris)** — velvety, rugose, "honeycomb-tripe" palms; associated with **gastric** carcinoma (when with acanthosis nigricans) or **lung** carcinoma (when isolated).
- **Mechanism:** Tumour-derived growth factors — **TGF-α, EGF, IGF** — flood the circulation and drive keratinocyte hyperproliferation in the most metabolically responsive skin.
- **Why it matters:** **This is a cancer flag.** Acquired PPK of abrupt onset in an adult demands a malignancy work-up.

### 3. Keratoderma due to inflammatory, circulatory, infectious causes and drugs
- **Inflammatory / dermatoses:** **psoriasis** (the commonest mimic of all), chronic hand eczema, lichen planus, pityriasis rubra pilaris, **Reiter's / reactive arthritis** (keratoderma blenorrhagicum), mycosis fungoides.
- **Infectious:** **crusted (Norwegian) scabies**, chronic **tinea manuum/pedis** (classically "**two feet–one hand**" syndrome), **syphilis** (secondary syphilitic palmar keratoderma), **HPV-7 "butcher's warts."**
- **Circulatory / systemic:** myxoedema, **hypothyroidism**, lymphoedema, venous stasis, diabetes, chronic renal failure.
- **Nutritional:** vitamin A deficiency (**phrynoderma**), pellagra, **arsenic** exposure (arsenical keratoses — punctate, plus risk of Bowen's disease and internal malignancy).
- **Drugs:** **retinoid paradox**, glucan, lithium, **arsenic**, tegafur/capecitabine and other chemotherapeutics (hand–foot syndrome), **BRAF inhibitors** (vemurafenib), tyrosine kinase inhibitors, **verapamil**, venlafaxine, gold.
- **Teaching point:** Always **scrape and culture**, and always **examine the scalp, nails, elbows and body folds**. A "keratoderma" that is actually palmar psoriasis or tinea is a curable diagnosis missed by not looking.

### 4. Aquagenic keratoderma (aquagenic palmoplantar wrinkling / "hand-in-the-bucket sign")
- **What:** Transient, **whitish, translucent, oedematous papules and plaques on the palms appearing within minutes of water immersion**, resolving in 10–60 minutes.
- **Mechanism:** Abnormal water retention within the stratum corneum — occlusion of dilated eccrine ostia and increased hygroscopy of the corneocyte layer, likely driven by **altered sodium/chloride flux**.
- **Why it matters (huge pearl):** This is **markedly over-represented in cystic fibrosis and CF carriers**, and is exacerbated by **NSAIDs, aspirin, rofecoxib and spironolactone**. Aquagenic wrinkling in a young person should prompt consideration of **CFTR testing/sweat chloride**.

## PART 6 — INHERITED PPK: A. DIFFUSE

Diffuse PPK is **uniform, confluent hyperkeratosis of the whole volar surface**. It may:
- **extend beyond the palms and soles** → **transgradient keratoderma**, and
- **involve dorsal and flexor aspects of wrists, elbows, knees, and the tendo Achilles**.

### A(i) — Transgradient diffuse PPK

| Syndrome | Gene / protein | Mechanistic family | Hallmark features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Camisa's disease** (loricrin keratoderma) | **Loricrin (*LOR*)** | Cornified envelope | Transgradient, honeycomb PPK + **pseudoainhum**, ± mild ichthyosis, **NO deafness** |
| **Mal de Meleda** | **SLURP-1 (*SLURP1* / *ARS*, 8q24)** | ACh-receptor signalling | AR; **"glove-and-stocking"** transgradient erythematous PPK, **hyperhidrosis + malodour**, perioral erythema, koilonychia, brachydactyly |
| ***ARS* gene → SLURP-1** | *ARS = SLURP1* | — | ⚠️ **Same gene, same disease as Mal de Meleda** (see corrections) |
| **Papillon–Lefèvre syndrome** | **Cathepsin C (*CTSC*)** | Protease activation | AR; PPK + **aggressive periodontitis with premature loss of deciduous & permanent teeth**, ± **pyogenic liver abscess**, intracranial calcification |
| **Vohwinkel's syndrome** (classic, mutilating) | **Connexin 26 (*GJB2*)** | Gap junction | **Honeycomb** PPK + **starfish-shaped** keratoses on knuckles + **pseudoainhum** + **sensorineural deafness** |
| **Olmsted syndrome** | **TRPV3** (gain-of-function); X-linked form *MBTPS2* | Ca²⁺ channel | **Mutilating** transgradient PPK + **periorificial keratotic plaques**, severe **pain and itch**, alopecia, nail dystrophy, ↑SCC risk |
| **Huriez syndrome** | **SMARCAD1** (*not* SLURP-1 — see corrections) | Chromatin remodelling | **Scleroatrophy** of hands, **hypoplastic nails**, diffuse PPK, marked **↑ risk of aggressive cutaneous SCC** |
| **Clouston syndrome** (hidrotic ectodermal dysplasia) | **Connexin 30 (*GJB6*)** | Gap junction | AD; PPK + **hypotrichosis** + **severe nail dystrophy/thick nails**; teeth & sweating **normal** |
| **Nagashima-type PPK** | **SERPINB7** | Protease inhibition | AR, **East Asian**; mild transgradient PPK to wrists/ankles/Achilles; **macerated whitish skin after water**; histology **spongiotic**, not markedly hyperkeratotic |
| **Bart–Pumphrey syndrome** | **Connexin 26 (*GJB2*)** | Gap junction | PPK + **knuckle pads** + **leukonychia** + **sensorineural deafness** |

*(Supplementary, classically examined: **Greither syndrome** — "keratosis extremitatum progrediens" — *KRT1*, AD, transgradient and **progressive** PPK involving Achilles tendon, knees, elbows, with hyperhidrosis.)*

#### 🔍 Deep dive: pseudoainhum — the mechanism behind a lost finger
Both Vohwinkel and Olmsted syndromes cause **pseudoainhum**: a circumferential fibrotic constriction band at the base of a digit that progressively tightens, strangulating the blood supply until **autoamputation** occurs.

**Cause → effect → outcome:** Defective keratinisation produces a rigid, inelastic keratotic cuff around the digit. → As the child grows, the cuff cannot expand. → Chronic constriction → dermal fibrosis → venous and lymphatic obstruction → distal oedema → further fibrosis (a **vicious cycle**) → arterial compromise → ischaemic necrosis → the digit falls off.

**Clinical consequence:** This is why **early surgical release (Z-plasty)** and systemic retinoids are urgent, not cosmetic, interventions. "Mutilating keratoderma" is a *functional emergency*.

#### 🔍 Deep dive: the *GJB2* cluster and why deafness travels with keratoderma
Take three diseases: **Vohwinkel**, **Bart–Pumphrey**, **KID syndrome** — all *GJB2*. In each, the cochlear phenotype comes from failure of **potassium recycling**. Hair-cell transduction depends on K⁺ flooding in from the K⁺-rich endolymph; that K⁺ must be shuttled back via a **gap-junction-coupled syncytium** of supporting cells and fibrocytes into the stria vascularis. Break the connexin pores → K⁺ accumulates around hair cells → the endocochlear potential falls → hair cells become intoxicated and die → **irreversible sensorineural hearing loss**.

> **Practical rule:** Every child with a PPK and knuckle pads, honeycomb keratoderma, or leukonychia deserves **formal audiometry**.

### A(ii) — Non-transgradient diffuse PPK

| Syndrome | Gene / protein | Hallmark |
|---|---|---|
| **Vörner's keratoderma** (epidermolytic PPK) | **K9 (*KRT9*)**, less often *KRT1* | Diffuse, waxy, sharply demarcated PPK with erythematous rim; **epidermolytic hyperkeratosis on histology** |
| **Unna–Thost keratoderma** (non-epidermolytic PPK) | ***KRT1*** (source states "K1/K2" — see corrections) | Clinically identical to Vörner but **no epidermolysis** on histology |
| **Naxos disease** | **Plakoglobin (*JUP*)** | **PPK + woolly hair + arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy (ARVC)** |

> ### ⭐ High-Yield Point — **Vörner vs Unna–Thost**
> Historically these were considered **separate entities**, but molecular studies have shown substantial **overlap**.
> - **Vörner's** (epidermolytic PPK) is caused by mutations in ***KRT9*** — the **palmoplantar-specific keratin** — or in *KRT1*, and shows **epidermolytic hyperkeratosis** histologically.
> - **Unna–Thost** (non-epidermolytic PPK) was traditionally attributed to ***KRT1*** mutations *without* epidermolysis, though the distinction is now debated. Indeed, **re-biopsy of the original Thost pedigree revealed epidermolysis**, suggesting many historical "Unna–Thost" families were actually Vörner.
> - **Both are autosomal dominant, diffuse, and non-transgradient.**
> - **The only reliable discriminator is histopathology.** *(Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)*

> ### 💡 Clinical Pearl — **Naxos Disease** (the "heart in the hand")
> Named after the Greek island where it was described. Caused by mutations in the **plakoglobin (*JUP*)** gene, it presents with the triad of:
> 1. **Diffuse non-transgradient PPK**
> 2. **Woolly hair** (present from birth — the earliest sign)
> 3. **Arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy (ARVC)**
>
> It is the **autosomal recessive form of ARVC with cutaneous features** — a critical exam association linking dermatology to cardiology.
>
> **Why the sequence matters clinically:** woolly hair is evident at birth, PPK develops in infancy as the child begins to walk, but the **cardiomyopathy declares itself in adolescence**, often as **syncope, ventricular tachycardia, or sudden cardiac death**. The dermatologist therefore has a ~10-year head start to refer for cardiac surveillance (ECG, echo, cardiac MRI, Holter) and consider an **implantable defibrillator**. **Diagnosing the skin can literally save the life.** *(Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)*

## PART 7 — INHERITED PPK: B. FOCAL

Focal PPK involves **localised, non-confluent areas** of hyperkeratosis, typically over **pressure points or friction-bearing sites** — heels, metatarsal heads, and along the **lines of digital flexion** (when linear, it is called **striate** keratoderma).

**Mechanism in one line:** the defective protein is not catastrophically absent, so the tissue copes — *until* local mechanical stress exceeds its reduced tolerance. Hence the disease maps precisely onto the **load map of the foot and grip map of the hand**.

| With associated systemic features | Without associated systemic features |
|---|---|
| **Howel-Evans syndrome** (*RHBDF2*) | **Buschke–Fischer(-Brauer) disease** |
| **Focal keratoderma with oral leukokeratosis** (*KRT16*) | **Punctate keratoderma of the palmar creases** |
| **Pachyonychia congenita types 1 and 2** | **Marginal papular acrokeratoderma** |
| **Carvajal syndrome** (*DSP*) | |
| **Richner–Hanhart disease** (***TAT*** gene) | |

> ### ⭐ High-Yield Point — **Howel-Evans Syndrome** (tylosis with oesophageal cancer, TOC)
> Caused by mutations in ***RHBDF2*** (encoding **iRhom2**), this syndrome presents with **focal PPK of weight-bearing areas** and carries a **>95% lifetime risk of oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma**. **Endoscopic surveillance is mandatory.**
>
> **Mechanism worth knowing:** iRhom2 is the **trafficking chaperone that escorts ADAM17 (TACE)** from the ER to the cell surface. ADAM17 is the "sheddase" that releases **TGF-α, amphiregulin and TNF-α** from the membrane. Gain-of-function *RHBDF2* mutations → excess ADAM17 trafficking → excess EGFR-ligand shedding → **sustained EGFR signalling** → hyperproliferation in volar skin (tylosis) **and** in oesophageal squamous epithelium (carcinogenesis). One pathway, two organs — and one of the highest cancer penetrances in all of medicine.
>
> **Practical:** onset of tylosis in childhood/adolescence; cancer typically after the fourth decade; enrol in an upper GI endoscopy surveillance programme. *(Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)*

#### Focal PPK with oral leukokeratosis (*KRT16*)
Focal, painful, callus-like plantar keratoderma plus **white plaques on the oral mucosa** (tongue, buccal mucosa) — because **K16 is expressed in both volar epidermis and mucosa**. This sits on a **continuous spectrum with pachyonychia congenita**; some families blur the two.

#### Pachyonychia congenita (PC)
| | **PC type 1 (Jadassohn–Lewandowsky)** | **PC type 2 (Jackson–Lawler)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Genes** | ***KRT6A*, *KRT16*** | ***KRT6B*, *KRT17*** |
| **Nails** | **Wedge-shaped hypertrophic onychodystrophy** (pinch/pincer nails) | Same |
| **PPK** | **Severe focal, exquisitely painful plantar keratoderma** | Present, often milder |
| **Oral** | **Oral leukokeratosis** | Usually absent |
| **Other** | Follicular hyperkeratosis, angular cheilitis, laryngeal involvement (hoarse cry) | **Natal teeth**, **steatocystoma multiplex**, **pili torti**, hair abnormalities |

> **Modern nomenclature (worth a mark):** because genotype predicts phenotype better than the old numbering, PC is now classified by gene — **PC-K6a, PC-K6b, PC-K6c, PC-K16, PC-K17**.
>
> **Mechanism of the pain (commonly asked):** The dominant symptom of PC is not the nails — it is **disabling plantar pain**, disproportionate to the amount of keratin. It arises because keratinocyte cytolysis releases inflammatory mediators and because abnormal K6/K16 alters keratinocyte–nociceptor signalling; patients describe "walking on pebbles" and many use crutches or a wheelchair. Management therefore targets **pain** (offloading, paring, botulinum toxin for associated hyperhidrosis, sirolimus/statin trials), not just keratin.

> ### 💡 Clinical Pearl — **Carvajal Syndrome**
> Caused by mutations in **desmoplakin (*DSP*)**, Carvajal syndrome presents with:
> 1. **Striate PPK** (along the lines of digital flexion)
> 2. **Woolly hair**
> 3. **Dilated cardiomyopathy** (left-ventricular predominant)
>
> It is the autosomal recessive counterpart to Naxos disease, but with **left-sided** cardiac involvement (dilated cardiomyopathy) rather than **right-sided** (ARVC).
>
> **Why the difference?** Both plakoglobin and desmoplakin are desmosomal plaque proteins, but they contribute differently to the mechanical and signalling load of right versus left ventricular myocardium (and desmoplakin loss additionally destabilises the **desmosome–intermediate filament linkage** and perturbs **Wnt/β-catenin signalling**, promoting fibrofatty replacement). The practical message is identical: **woolly hair + keratoderma = image the heart.** Carvajal often presents in **childhood with heart failure**, earlier and more aggressively than Naxos. *(Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)*

> **🔗 Related for completeness:** **Striate PPK (SPPK)** also occurs without cardiac disease, from mutations in **desmoglein 1 (*DSG1*)**, **desmoplakin (*DSP*)** or **keratin 1 (*KRT1*)** — three genes, one linear-streaky phenotype along the flexor digital lines.

> ### 💡 Clinical Pearl — **Richner–Hanhart Disease**
> Also known as **tyrosinaemia type II (oculocutaneous tyrosinaemia)**, caused by mutations in the ***TAT*** gene (**tyrosine aminotransferase**, chromosome 16q22.2). It presents with:
> - **Painful, pseudo-dendritic corneal erosions** (mistaken for herpetic keratitis — a classic trap, because steroids/antivirals are given and the child worsens)
> - **Focal PPK** — painful, discrete keratoses on the fingertips, palms and soles
> - **Intellectual disability** / developmental delay in untreated cases
>
> **Mechanism:** Loss of tyrosine aminotransferase blocks the **second step** of tyrosine catabolism. → Plasma tyrosine rises dramatically (often >1200 µmol/L). → Tyrosine, being poorly soluble, **crystallises within the cornea and the volar stratum corneum**. → Crystals act as intracellular needles → cytotoxicity, inflammation, reactive hyperkeratosis, and corneal epithelial breakdown.
>
> **Elevated plasma tyrosine is diagnostic.** Treatment is **dietary restriction of tyrosine and phenylalanine**, which reverses both eye and skin disease — a rare instance of a **curable keratoderma**. *(Ref: Bolognia)*
>
> **Contrast with tyrosinaemia type I** (*FAH*, fumarylacetoacetate hydrolase): liver failure, renal tubulopathy, hepatocellular carcinoma, treated with **nitisinone** — no keratoderma.

#### Focal PPKs *without* systemic features
- **Buschke–Fischer(-Brauer) disease** — **keratosis punctata palmaris et plantaris**; AD; multiple firm keratotic papules that, when the central plug is pared away, leave a **crater/pit**. (See classification note below: most texts place this under **punctate**.)
- **Punctate keratoderma of the palmar creases** (keratosis punctata of the palmar creases) — tiny keratotic pits **confined to the palmar and digital creases**; much commoner in **skin of colour**; benign, often asymptomatic, sometimes associated with atopy. A pure "don't-worry" diagnosis.
- **Marginal papular acrokeratoderma** (acrokeratoelastoidosis of Costa / focal acral hyperkeratosis) — small, firm, umbilicated papules along the **lateral margins of the palms, fingers and the border of the feet**, at the junction of dorsal and volar skin. Distinguished histologically: **acrokeratoelastoidosis shows elastorrhexis** (fragmented elastic fibres) whereas **focal acral hyperkeratosis does not**. Benign, cosmetic concern only.

## PART 8 — INHERITED PPK: C. PUNCTATE

Punctate PPK features **tiny, discrete keratotic papules scattered across the palmoplantar surface**, giving a **"raindrop"** or **"music-box spine"** pattern.

- **Schopf–Schulz–Passarge syndrome** — ***WNT10A***
- **Punctate PPK with ankylosing spondylitis**
- **Punctate PPK with facial sebaceous hyperplasia**
- **Punctate PPK with spastic paralysis**

> ### ⭐ High-Yield Point — ***WNT10A***
> **Schopf–Schulz–Passarge syndrome (SSPS)** is caused by mutations in ***WNT10A*** and features:
> - **Punctate PPK**
> - **Eyelid cysts** (apocrine **hidrocystomas** along the lid margins — the signature sign)
> - **Hypodontia**
> - **Hypotrichosis**
> - **Nail dystrophy**
>
> ***WNT10A* mutations are now recognised as the most common genetic cause of selective tooth agenesis (hypodontia/oligodontia) worldwide** — an important cross-specialty association. *(Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)*
>
> **Mechanism that ties it together:** WNT10A is a ligand of the **canonical Wnt/β-catenin pathway**, which drives the **epithelial–mesenchymal cross-talk** that builds *all* ectodermal appendages — hair follicles, teeth, nails, and sweat/apocrine glands. Disable one Wnt ligand and you get a **pan-appendageal phenotype**: fewer teeth, sparse hair, dystrophic nails, cystic apocrine structures, and disordered volar keratinisation. SSPS therefore sits within the **ectodermal dysplasia** family and overlaps with odonto-onycho-dermal dysplasia (also *WNT10A*).
>
> **Note the family resemblance:** Clouston syndrome (*GJB6*) is the other classic "PPK + ectodermal dysplasia" — but Clouston spares the **teeth**, while SSPS is defined by **hypodontia** and **eyelid cysts**. That's your discriminator.

## PART 11 — COMMON MISUNDERSTANDINGS (clear these up and you're ahead)

1. **"Transgradient means severe."** ❌ No — it is purely a **statement about anatomical distribution**, not severity. **Nagashima-type PPK is transgradient but mild**; Vörner is non-transgradient but disabling.

2. **"Vörner and Unna–Thost are clinically distinguishable."** ❌ They are **clinically indistinguishable**. Only **histopathology** (presence of epidermolytic hyperkeratosis) separates them. In fact, the original Thost family was later shown to have epidermolysis.

3. **"Naxos and Carvajal are the same disease."** ❌ Same *concept* (desmosomal cardiocutaneous syndrome), different gene and **different ventricle**: **Naxos = plakoglobin = Right (ARVC)**; **Carvajal = desmoplakin = Left (dilated CM)**. Mnemonic: **"Na**xos — **N**orth/right; **C**arvajal — **C**ardiac dilatation, left."

4. **"Vohwinkel always includes deafness."** ❌ Only the **classic *GJB2* (connexin 26) form** has sensorineural deafness. The **loricrin variant (Camisa's disease)** has **ichthyosis instead of deafness**. This distinction is *the* classic MCQ.

5. **"Olmsted syndrome is a keratin disease."** ❌ It is a **TRPV3 calcium-channel gain-of-function** disease. *KRT1/KRT10* mutations cause **epidermolytic ichthyosis**, not Olmsted. (See corrections.)

6. **"Huriez syndrome is caused by SLURP-1."** ❌ **SLURP-1 (*SLURP1/ARS*) causes Mal de Meleda.** Huriez syndrome maps to the same chromosomal region (4q23) — which is *why* the confusion arose — but is caused by ***SMARCAD1*** mutations. (See corrections.)

7. **"A thick palm in an adult is just a callus."** ❌ **Acquired, late-onset, asymmetric or rapidly progressive PPK is a red flag** for malignancy, drug reaction, psoriasis, crusted scabies, or tinea. Always scrape, always survey the rest of the skin, always ask about drugs.

8. **"Punctate and focal are interchangeable."** ⚠️ Not quite — **focal** = larger discrete plaques at pressure sites; **punctate** = numerous tiny papules/pits independent of pressure. Some entities (e.g. Buschke–Fischer, keratosis punctata of the palmar creases) are **placed differently by different authors** — which is legitimate and worth acknowledging in an answer.

9. **"Genotype–phenotype is tidy."** ⚠️ It isn't. *KRT1* alone can give **diffuse (Unna–Thost), striate, and transgradient-progressive (Greither)** phenotypes; *GJB2* gives **four different syndromes**; *CTSC* gives both **Papillon–Lefèvre and Haim–Munk**. PPK nosology is genuinely in flux — say so, and you sound like you've read the literature.

## PART 12 — A PRACTICAL DIAGNOSTIC ALGORITHM (for the clinic and the viva)

**Step 1 — Acquired or inherited?**
Age of onset, family history, symmetry, rate of progression, drug history.

**Step 2 — If acquired:** scrape for fungus/scabies → examine scalp, nails, elbows, genitals for psoriasis → review drugs → screen thyroid → if abrupt in an adult, **look for malignancy** (chest imaging, upper GI endoscopy as indicated).

**Step 3 — If inherited: characterise the morphology.**
Diffuse / focal (striate?) / punctate.

**Step 4 — Define the border.** Does it cross onto the dorsa? → **transgradient** or not.

**Step 5 — Systematically hunt the associations** (this is the step that changes outcomes):
- **Hair** — woolly? sparse? → desmosome or ectodermal dysplasia
- **Nails** — thickened wedge-shaped? leukonychia? hypoplastic? 
- **Teeth/gums** — periodontitis? hypodontia? natal teeth?
- **Ears** — audiometry
- **Heart** — ECG + echo if woolly hair or striate PPK
- **Eyes** — slit lamp
- **Digits** — constriction bands, sclerodactyly
- **Mucosa** — oral leukokeratosis
- **Odour/sweating** — hyperhidrosis + malodour → Mal de Meleda

**Step 6 — Investigations:** skin biopsy (epidermolysis? spongiosis? elastorrhexis?), plasma tyrosine if corneal disease, sweat chloride if aquagenic, **targeted gene panel / next-generation sequencing**, and **genetic counselling**.

**Step 7 — Management principles:**
- **Mechanical/topical:** paring/debridement, **urea 20–40%**, salicylic acid, lactic acid, propylene glycol, topical retinoids, topical vitamin D analogues; soft insoles, offloading footwear; treat maceration and secondary **dermatophyte/bacterial** infection (always — it is the commonest cause of "treatment failure").
- **Systemic retinoids** (acitretin) — most useful in mutilating/transgradient disease and **pseudoainhum**; caution in epidermolytic (Vörner) PPK, where they may cause **increased blistering/erosion**.
- **Disease-specific:** dietary tyrosine/phenylalanine restriction (Richner–Hanhart); intensive periodontal care + **oral retinoids ± antibiotics** (Papillon–Lefèvre); **surgical Z-plasty** for constriction bands; **cardiac surveillance ± ICD** (Naxos/Carvajal); **endoscopic surveillance** (Howel-Evans); botulinum toxin for hyperhidrosis-related pain (pachyonychia); **skin cancer surveillance** (Huriez, Olmsted, KID); **hearing aids/cochlear implant** (connexin disorders).
- **Emerging:** siRNA against mutant *KRT6A*, rapamycin/sirolimus and statins in pachyonychia, **erlotinib (EGFR inhibition) in Olmsted syndrome** — mechanistically elegant, since TRPV3 activation drives EGFR-mediated hyperproliferation.

## PART 13 — CORRECTIONS & VERIFICATION NOTES

*(These reconcile the source page with current authoritative texts.)*

1. **"Marginal popular acrokeratoderma"** (p. 182, focal PPK table, right column) → **"Marginal *papular* acrokeratoderma."** The source reads "popular," a typographical/OCR substitution. The correct entity is **marginal papular acrokeratoderma** (**acrokeratoelastoidosis of Costa**), a benign condition with small papular keratoses along the **lateral margins of the palms and fingers**. *(Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)*

2. **"Camissa syndrome"** → **Camisa's disease / loricrin keratoderma.** This is a variant of **loricrin keratoderma** caused by ***LOR*** mutations: transgradient, mutilating PPK with **honeycomb hyperkeratosis and pseudoainhum**, often with mild ichthyosis, and importantly **WITHOUT deafness** — unlike classic Vohwinkel syndrome (*GJB2*/connexin 26), which **includes sensorineural hearing loss**. The source spelling is an alternate transliteration. *(Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)*

3. **"ARS gene → SLURP-1" listed separately from Mal de Meleda** → **Clarification: these are the same disorder.** The ***ARS*** gene (now designated ***SLURP1***) on **chromosome 8q24** encodes **secreted Ly-6/uPAR-related protein 1**. **Mal de Meleda** (*keratosis palmoplantaris transgradiens of Siemens*) results from homozygous or compound heterozygous *SLURP1/ARS* mutations. The separate listing likely emphasises the gene→protein relationship rather than a distinct syndrome. *(Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)*

4. **"Olmsted syndrome (K1/K10, TRPV3)"** → **Correction.** The primary cause of Olmsted syndrome is **gain-of-function mutations in *TRPV3***, **not** *KRT1/KRT10*. **KRT1/KRT10 mutations cause epidermolytic hyperkeratosis / epidermolytic ichthyosis**, a different entity. Olmsted syndrome = **bilateral mutilating transgradient PPK + periorificial keratotic plaques + alopecia + nail dystrophy**, with severe pain/pruritus. An **X-linked recessive form** is linked to ***MBTPS2***. The K1/K10 entry is likely an error or a row-alignment artefact. *(Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)*

5. **"Huriez syndrome — SLURP-1"** → **Correction.** **Huriez syndrome** (scleroatrophic and keratotic dermatosis of the limbs) is caused by mutations in ***SMARCAD1*** (4q22–q23) — adjacent to, but distinct from, the *SLURP1* locus, which explains the historical confusion. Features: **congenital scleroatrophy of the hands, nail hypoplasia, diffuse PPK, and a markedly elevated risk of aggressive cutaneous SCC.** **SLURP-1 belongs to Mal de Meleda.** *(Ref: Rook's; Bolognia; OMIM #181600)*

6. **"Unna–Thost syndrome — K1/K2"** → **Clarification/likely error.** Non-epidermolytic diffuse PPK of Unna–Thost type is attributed to ***KRT1***. ***KRT2*** mutations cause **superficial epidermolytic ichthyosis (ichthyosis bullosa of Siemens)**, not Unna–Thost PPK. Similarly, **Vörner's** is best attributed to ***KRT9*** (with a minority from *KRT1*), as printed. *(Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)*

7. **"TATc gene"** (Richner–Hanhart) → **"*TAT* gene."** The gene for **tyrosinaemia type II** is ***TAT*** (tyrosine aminotransferase), on **chromosome 16q22.2**. The lowercase "c" is a typographical/OCR artefact. *(Ref: Rook's; Bolognia; OMIM #276600)*

8. **"Punctate keratoderma of palmar creases" placed under Focal PPK** → **Clarification.** Traditionally, **punctate keratoderma (keratosis punctata palmaris et plantaris / Buschke–Fischer–Brauer)** is grouped under the **punctate** category. The source's placement under focal (without systemic features) reflects an alternative convention treating palmar-crease-restricted keratoses as focal. **Both classifications exist in the literature** and either is defensible if stated. *(Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)*

9. **Nagashima-type PPK — gene not listed in source** → **Supplement.** **Nagashima-type palmoplantar keratosis (NPPK)** is caused by mutations in ***SERPINB7*** (serpin peptidase inhibitor, clade B, member 7). It is **autosomal recessive**, especially prevalent in **East Asian** populations, and characterised by **mild transgradient PPK** (extending to wrists, ankles, Achilles) with **spongiotic** rather than markedly hyperkeratotic histopathology, and conspicuous **whitish maceration after water exposure**. It is the **commonest inherited PPK in Japan/China** and was long misclassified as mild Mal de Meleda. *(Ref: Bolognia)*

10. **Clouston syndrome classified as transgradient** → **Clarification.** Clouston syndrome (**hidrotic ectodermal dysplasia**, *GJB6*/connexin 30) features **palmoplantar keratoderma with hypotrichosis and severe nail dystrophy**; the keratoderma is usually **diffuse but not classically transgradient**. Its placement in the transgradient column is a convention of this source; the clinically essential facts are the **gene (GJB6/Cx30)** and the **triad: hair + nails + PPK, with normal teeth and normal sweating**. *(Ref: Rook's; Bolognia)*

## PART 14 — 🌟 SUMMARY ANCHOR: The Core Idea in One Picture

> **Picture a single palm.**
>
> Its thick horny layer is built from four materials: **keratin rebar (K9/K1/K6/K16)**, **cornified-envelope mortar (loricrin)**, **desmosomal rivets (plakoglobin, desmoplakin, desmoglein)**, and **gap-junction intercoms (Cx26, Cx30)** — all maintained by a **protease/inhibitor balance (cathepsin C, SERPINB7, SLURP-1)** and tuned by **ion channels (TRPV3)** and **Wnt signalling (WNT10A)**.
>
> **Break any one of them, and the palm thickens.**
>
> Then ask **one question**: *"Where else in the body is that same material used?"*
> - Keratin → **nails and oral mucosa** → *pachyonychia congenita*
> - Loricrin → **whole epidermis** → *transgradient, mutilating keratoderma*
> - Connexins → **the cochlea** → *deafness* (Vohwinkel, Bart–Pumphrey, KID)
> - Desmosomes → **heart and hair** → *woolly hair + cardiomyopathy* (Naxos, Carvajal)
> - Cathepsin C → **neutrophils and gums** → *periodontitis* (Papillon–Lefèvre)
> - iRhom2/ADAM17 → **oesophageal epithelium** → *cancer* (Howel-Evans)
> - Wnt10a → **teeth, hair, glands** → *ectodermal dysplasia* (Schopf–Schulz–Passarge)
> - Tyrosine aminotransferase → **cornea** → *pseudodendritic keratitis* (Richner–Hanhart)
>
> And if the keratoderma began **late, asymmetrically, and without family history** — it is **acquired**, and your job is to find the **psoriasis, the fungus, the scabies, the drug, the menopause, or the hidden cancer**.
>
> **That is the whole subject:** *the palm is a billboard. Learn to read the six building materials, and the billboard tells you which organ to examine next — sometimes in time to save a life.*
