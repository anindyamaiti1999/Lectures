# PART 0 — Why this question deserves a whole lecture

Let me set the stage before we start naming diseases, because if you begin with the names you will drown.

The word **ichthyosis** comes from the Greek *ichthys* — **fish** — because the skin looks like it is covered in **fish scales**. That etymology is a lovely mnemonic, but it is also a trap, because it tempts you to think of ichthyosis as *one* disease with *one* look. It is not. The ichthyoses are a **large, genetically heterogeneous family of Mendelian disorders of cornification (MeDOC)** whose only shared feature is **generalised, persistent scaling of the skin**. Under that single visual umbrella sit more than fifty distinct entities caused by mutations in more than fifty different genes, plus an entirely separate **acquired** group in which the skin is reacting to systemic disease or drugs.

So the examiner asking you to "classify the ichthyoses" is really asking three questions at once:

1. **Can you impose order on chaos?** (Do you know the 2009 consensus scaffold?)
2. **Do you know which ichthyoses are just skin, and which are the tip of a systemic iceberg?** (nonsyndromic vs syndromic — this is the clinically dangerous distinction)
3. **Do you understand *why* the skin scales?** (barrier biology)

This question is the **roadmap question** for the whole ichthyosis block. In the source text, Q16 sits at the bottom of page 173 and functions as the master framework onto which Q17–Q24 hang their detail (epidermolytic hyperkeratosis and harlequin ichthyosis, collodion baby, X-linked ichthyosis, lamellar ichthyosis, erythrokeratoderma variabilis, CIE, Netherton syndrome, Refsum disease). The same hierarchical architecture you saw with Q14 → Q15–Q38. **Memorise this classification first**; every later fact then has somewhere to live.

# PART 1 — The physiology you must own before the classification makes sense

Every single ichthyosis in the list below is a **barrier failure**. If you understand the barrier, the classification becomes almost self-generating — you can practically derive it.

## 1.1 The "brick wall" model of the stratum corneum

Picture the stratum corneum as a **brick wall with mortar**, about 15–20 layers thick, sitting on top of the living epidermis.

| Wall component | Biological counterpart | Key molecules | Ichthyoses when it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The bricks** | Corneocytes — dead, flattened, anucleate cells | Keratin filaments inside; **cornified cell envelope** (CCE) outside made of loricrin, involucrin, small proline-rich proteins, cross-linked by **transglutaminase‑1** | *TGM1* (lamellar ichthyosis, CIE), *LOR* (loricrin keratoderma) |
| **The rebar inside each brick** | Keratin intermediate filament cytoskeleton | **K1/K10** (suprabasal), **K2** (upper spinous/granular) | *KRT1*, *KRT10*, *KRT2* → **keratinopathic ichthyoses** |
| **The glue that bundles the rebar** | Filaggrin — aggregates keratin filaments; later degraded to natural moisturising factor (NMF) | **Filaggrin (*FLG*)**, stored in keratohyalin granules | *FLG* → **ichthyosis vulgaris** |
| **The mortar** | Intercellular **lipid lamellae** — ceramides : cholesterol : free fatty acids in roughly equimolar ratio | Delivered by **lamellar bodies** (Odland bodies) secreted at the granular–corneal interface | *ABCA12*, *ALOX12B*, *ALOXE3*, *SLC27A4*, *ABHD5*, *ALDH3A2*, *STS*, cholesterol‑pathway genes |
| **The masonry ties holding bricks together** | **Corneodesmosomes** | **Desmoglein‑1**, desmocollin‑1, **corneodesmosin (CDSN)** | *CDSN* (inflammatory peeling skin disease), *TGM5* (peeling skin syndrome type B) |
| **The demolition crew** | Serine proteases that dissolve corneodesmosomes so cells can shed | **Kallikrein‑5 and ‑7**, restrained by **LEKTI** (*SPINK5*) | *SPINK5* → **Netherton syndrome** |
| **The site foremen (intercellular communication)** | Gap junctions coordinating differentiation | **Connexins 26, 30.3, 31, 43** (*GJB2*, *GJB4*, *GJB3*, *GJA1*) | KID syndrome, erythrokeratoderma variabilis |
| **The delivery trucks** | Vesicle trafficking machinery that gets lamellar bodies to the surface | **SNAP29** (SNARE), **AP1S1** (adaptor protein complex 1) | CEDNIK, MEDNIK |

**Now the key inference.** Damage *any* of those components and you get the same final common pathway:

> **Defective barrier → increased transepidermal water loss (TEWL) → the epidermis senses "I am leaking" → compensatory hyperproliferation and/or failure of normal desquamation → thickened, retained, visible scale.**

That is *why* fifty different genes produce one clinical word. The skin has only a small vocabulary of responses.

## 1.2 Two mechanisms of scale — a distinction examiners love

| | **Retention hyperkeratosis** | **Hyperproliferative hyperkeratosis** |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | Corneocytes are made at normal rate but **won't let go** | Corneocytes are churned out **too fast** |
| Transit time | Normal production, delayed shedding | Accelerated epidermal turnover |
| Examples | **Ichthyosis vulgaris, X‑linked ichthyosis** | **Epidermolytic ichthyosis, lamellar ichthyosis, CIE** |
| Clinical clue | Thick adherent scale, little/no erythema | Erythroderma, thick scale, higher metabolic demand |

Analogy: retention hyperkeratosis is a **conveyor belt at the end of a factory that has jammed** — product piles up. Hyperproliferative ichthyosis is a **factory running triple shifts** — product piles up for the opposite reason. Both look like a pile of boxes at the loading dock; the fix is different.

## 1.3 Why some ichthyoses are syndromic — the single most important conceptual point

Ask yourself: *why should a skin scaling disorder come with deafness, spasticity, or liver failure?*

Two answers, and between them they explain **every** syndromic ichthyosis on the list:

**Answer A — The gene is expressed in more than one tissue.**
*GJB2* (connexin 26) is needed in the epidermis **and** in the cochlear supporting cells **and** in corneal epithelium → **K**eratitis, **I**chthyosis, **D**eafness. *SNAP29* is needed for lamellar body exocytosis in keratinocytes **and** for synaptic vesicle fusion in neurons → ichthyosis plus cerebral dysgenesis and neuropathy. The skin and the nervous system share their love of vesicle trafficking and of lipid handling; this is why **ichthyosis + neurological disease** is such a recurring couple.

**Answer B — The gene sits in a metabolic pathway whose substrate or product poisons other organs.**
In **Refsum disease** the problem is not really "skin"; it is **phytanic acid** which cannot be degraded, accumulates everywhere, and displaces normal fatty acids from membrane lipids — including the stratum corneum lipid lamellae (ichthyosis), the photoreceptors (retinitis pigmentosa), peripheral nerve myelin (neuropathy), and cerebellum (ataxia). In **Sjögren–Larsson** the fatty aldehydes and alcohols that cannot be oxidised are both barrier lipids *and* neuronal membrane lipids.

> **Teaching line to remember:** *The skin is a window onto lipid metabolism. Whenever the body cannot process a lipid, the stratum corneum — the most lipid‑dependent tissue in the body — complains first and loudest.*

A third, mechanically different route to "syndromic" deserves its own flag:

**Answer C — Contiguous gene deletion.** Here the *pathway* is not shared at all; the genes are simply **neighbours on the chromosome**, and a large deletion takes out several at once. This is the situation at **Xp22.3** (see §5.1). Analogy: a single lightning strike takes out three houses on the same street, not because they share plumbing, but because they share a street.

# PART 2 — The classification scaffold (2009 First Ichthyosis Consensus Conference)

## 2.1 Why we needed a new classification

Before 2009 the nomenclature was a museum of historical accidents: "bullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma of Brocq," "non‑bullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma," eponyms layered on eponyms, and earlier schemes (Frost & Van Scott 1966, based on histological hyper‑proliferative vs retention; Williams & Elias 2003, based on pathophysiology) that did not survive the genomic era. In 2009, 55 experts met in **Sorèze, France**, and published the consensus (Oji et al., 2010). **This is the classification you write in the exam.** It supersedes all earlier schemes.

Its governing principles:
- **Primary split: is the phenotype limited to the skin (nonsyndromic) or does it involve other organs (syndromic)?** This is a *clinical*, bedside‑usable split — deliberately so, because it maps onto what the clinician must do next.
- Descriptive, pathogenesis‑neutral names preferred; "**bullous**" replaced by "**epidermolytic**" (because the blisters reflect keratinocyte **cytolysis**, not a subepidermal split).
- Acquired ichthyosis kept conceptually separate — it is not a MeDOC at all.

# PART 3 — Acquired ichthyosis

## 3.1 What it is and why it matters

**Acquired ichthyosis** is generalised ichthyosiform scaling arising **de novo in an adult (or older child) with no personal or family history of ichthyosis**. That single sentence is the whole diagnostic power of the concept: *new-onset generalised fish-scale skin in an adult is a red flag, not a cosmetic complaint.*

Clinically it favours the **trunk and extremities**, tends to be **symmetrical**, with adherent polygonal scales and prominent **rhomboidal fissuring**, often sparing flexures. It looks very like severe ichthyosis vulgaris — which is exactly the trap: **you must ask "why now?"**

## 3.2 The five categories (source, p. 173)

| Category | Examples | Mechanism (the "why") |
|---|---|---|
| **Endocrine / metabolic** | **Malnutrition**, malabsorptive disorders (coeliac, post‑bariatric), **sarcoidosis**, **chronic renal failure**, **hyperparathyroidism**, **diabetes mellitus**; classically also **hypothyroidism** | Deficiency of the raw materials of barrier lipids and of essential fatty acids; uraemic toxins; vitamin A/zinc deficiency; reduced sebaceous and eccrine output |
| **Lymphoproliferative & solid malignancy** | **Hodgkin lymphoma — the classic, highest-yield association**; non‑Hodgkin lymphoma, mycosis fungoides, multiple myeloma, leiomyosarcoma, and carcinomas of lung, breast, cervix | True **paraneoplastic** dermatosis: tumour‑derived cytokines and altered vitamin A metabolism disturb keratinocyte differentiation. May **precede, parallel, or follow** the tumour, and often **remits with successful treatment** of the malignancy — the best proof of causality |
| **Infections** | **Leprosy**, **HIV** | Chronic inflammatory/nutritional/cytokine effects; in HIV also multifactorial (wasting, drugs, ART) |
| **Drugs** | **Retinoids**, **clofazimine**, **HMG‑CoA reductase inhibitors (statins)**, **hydroxyurea**, **nicotinic acid (niacin)**, cimetidine, butyrophenones, allopurinol | Direct interference with keratinocyte differentiation or with barrier lipid synthesis |
| **Connective tissue disease (CTD)** | **SLE**, **dermatomyositis** (also systemic sclerosis, mixed CTD) | Circulating autoantibodies/immune complexes and chronic interface inflammation disturb epidermal differentiation |

> **Mnemonic for acquired ichthyosis — "HIV‑MALT‑DC":** **H**odgkin (and other malignancy), **I**nfection (HIV, leprosy), **V**itamin/nutritional deficiency, **M**etabolic (renal, thyroid, parathyroid, diabetes), **A**utoimmune/CTD, **L**ymphoma/leukaemia, **T**reatment (drugs), **D**ietary malabsorption, **C**learance failure (renal). Or simply remember the five headings: **endocrine/metabolic, malignancy, infection, drugs, CTD.**

## 3.3 Mechanism worth dwelling on: statins and the mevalonate pathway

This is a genuinely instructive mechanism, and it links to another exam topic.

Cholesterol makes up roughly **25% of stratum corneum intercellular lipid** and is synthesised **locally** in the epidermis (the epidermis is one of the most active sites of *de novo* cholesterol synthesis in the body). **HMG‑CoA reductase** is the rate‑limiting enzyme of the **mevalonate pathway** that produces it.

> **Cause → effect → outcome:** statin inhibits HMG‑CoA reductase → epidermal cholesterol synthesis falls → the ceramide : cholesterol : free fatty acid ratio of the lipid lamellae is distorted → lamellae fail to form organised bilayers → mortar becomes crumbly → TEWL rises → compensatory hyperkeratosis → **acquired ichthyosis**.

Note the elegant symmetry: **the same mevalonate pathway is central to the pathogenesis of porokeratosis** (covered in Q34, where *MVK*, *MVD*, *PMVK*, *FDFT1* mutations cause disordered cornification). One pathway, two exam questions. And note the paradox worth mentioning aloud: **topical cholesterol supplementation** can actually improve statin‑ and lovastatin‑induced scaling — proving the mechanism.

### ⚠ Correction from the source text
The source writes "**HMG‑COA**" as though it were a drug. **It is not a drug — it is a substrate.** The correct expansion is **HMG‑CoA reductase inhibitors**, i.e. **statins**; **HMG‑CoA** = **3‑hydroxy‑3‑methylglutaryl coenzyme A** (note the capitalisation: CoA, not COA). Write "statins (HMG‑CoA reductase inhibitors)" in the exam.

### ⚠ Clarification from the source text
The source lists "**CTD**" without expansion. **CTD = connective tissue disease**, principally **SLE and dermatomyositis**. Distinguish this carefully from two look‑alikes that are *not* acquired ichthyosis: the **dry, rough, cool skin of hypothyroidism** (reduced sebaceous/eccrine activity — though this is often classified with acquired ichthyosis), and the **bound-down, parchment-like skin of systemic sclerosis** (dermal fibrosis, not a cornification defect).

## 3.4 Practical approach to a suspected acquired ichthyosis

1. Confirm **adult onset** and **no family history** (examine parents' and siblings' skin, ask about "dry skin since infancy").
2. Full systems review + examination: lymph nodes, liver, spleen, weight loss, night sweats.
3. Baseline: FBC, ESR, LDH, renal and liver function, **TFTs**, calcium/PTH, glucose, HIV serology, chest radiograph.
4. Drug history — including recently started statins and niacin.
5. If lymphadenopathy or B symptoms → **imaging and node biopsy** looking for Hodgkin lymphoma.
6. Skin biopsy is usually non‑specific (compact orthokeratosis, thinned or absent granular layer — mimicking ichthyosis vulgaris) and serves mainly to exclude mycosis fungoides.

# PART 4 — Inherited ichthyoses: NONSYNDROMIC forms

These are the ichthyoses in which the phenotype is, for practical purposes, **confined to the skin and its appendages**. Four subgroups.

---

## 4.1 Group 1 — "Common" ichthyoses

Why "common"? Because these two account for the overwhelming majority of all ichthyosis seen in clinic — the rest are individually rare. They are grouped by *epidemiology*, not by mechanism, and their mechanisms are in fact entirely different. Comparing them is a perennial exam favourite.

| | **Ichthyosis vulgaris (IV)** | **Recessive X‑linked ichthyosis (XLI)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Gene / locus** | ***FLG*** (filaggrin), 1q21 | ***STS*** (steroid sulfatase), **Xp22.31** |
| **Inheritance** | **Autosomal semidominant** (heterozygotes mild, homozygotes/compound heterozygotes severe) | **X‑linked recessive** — males affected, female carriers usually mild/normal |
| **Prevalence** | ~**1 : 250** — the commonest genetic ichthyosis | ~**1 : 2,000–6,000 males** |
| **Defect** | Loss of filaggrin → keratin filaments not aggregated; loss of **NMF** (filaggrin breakdown products: urocanic acid, pyrrolidone carboxylic acid) → dry, poorly hydrated corneocytes | Steroid sulfatase cannot cleave **cholesterol sulfate** → cholesterol sulfate accumulates in the stratum corneum |
| **Why scale forms** | **Retention hyperkeratosis** + impaired hydration | **Retention hyperkeratosis**: cholesterol sulfate is a **potent inhibitor of the kallikrein proteases** needed for corneodesmosome degradation → cells cannot detach; it also disrupts lamellar architecture |
| **Scale morphology** | **Fine, white, "cracked porcelain"/branny** scale | **Large, adherent, polygonal, "dirty‑brown"** scale — the classic **"dirty neck"** |
| **Distribution** | Extensors, shins, abdomen; **flexures spared**; **hyperlinear palms**; keratosis pilaris | **Involves flexures and neck**; spares palms/soles and central face |
| **Onset** | After 2 months, rarely at birth | Within the **first weeks of life** |
| **Histology — key contrast** | **Reduced or absent granular layer**, compact orthokeratosis | **Normal or thickened granular layer** with hyperkeratosis |
| **Associations** | **Atopic dermatitis (major risk allele), asthma, allergic rhinitis, peanut allergy, atopic keratoconjunctivitis** | **Cryptorchidism (~20%, ↑ testicular cancer risk)**, **corneal comma‑shaped stromal opacities (~50%, asymptomatic)**, **ADHD/autism**, and in mothers: **placental STS deficiency → low maternal oestriol → failure of labour to initiate, prolonged labour** |
| **Course** | Improves in summer/humidity; often improves with age | Persists lifelong; improves in summer |

> **Why "hyperlinear palms" in IV?** Filaggrin is heavily expressed in palmar skin; its loss exaggerates the normal dermatoglyphic creases. Combine hyperlinear palms + keratosis pilaris + flexural eczema and you have essentially diagnosed *FLG* haploinsufficiency at the bedside.

> **Clinical pearl:** A newborn male with generalised scaling whose mother had **failure to progress in labour** and required caesarean section — think **XLI** immediately. This is one of the few dermatological diagnoses you can make from the obstetric notes. Diagnosis is confirmed by **absent steroid sulfatase activity** in leukocytes/fibroblasts, elevated plasma cholesterol sulfate, or **FISH/MLPA showing the Xp22.31 deletion** (~90% have a complete gene deletion).

## 4.2 Group 2 — Autosomal Recessive Congenital Ichthyosis (ARCI)

**Conceptual framing:** ARCI is not a disease; it is an **umbrella term for a clinical–genetic spectrum** of autosomal recessive ichthyoses that almost all present at birth as a **collodion baby** and are caused by defects in the **lipid‑processing machinery of the stratum corneum**. Historically the two poles were called "lamellar ichthyosis" and "non‑bullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma"; we now know these are **ends of a continuum**, and the same gene (*TGM1*) can produce either. Hence the unifying name.

**The genes and what they do — note the theme:**

| Gene | Protein | Barrier role |
|---|---|---|
| ***TGM1*** (~32–55% of ARCI) | Transglutaminase‑1 | Cross‑links the cornified envelope AND covalently attaches ω‑hydroxyceramides to it (the **corneocyte lipid envelope**) — the "primer coat" that lets mortar stick to bricks |
| ***ABCA12*** | ATP‑binding cassette lipid transporter | Loads **glucosylceramides into lamellar bodies** — the truck‑loading dock |
| ***ALOX12B*** / ***ALOXE3*** | 12R‑lipoxygenase / epidermal lipoxygenase‑3 | Sequential oxidation of linoleate in ceramides — required to make the lipid envelope |
| ***NIPAL4*** (ichthyin) | Membrane receptor | Lamellar body formation |
| ***CYP4F22*** | ω‑hydroxylase | Synthesis of ultra‑long‑chain ω‑hydroxy fatty acids for acylceramides |
| ***PNPLA1***, ***CERS3***, ***LIPN***, ***SDR9C7***, ***CASP14*** | Various | Ceramide synthesis/processing, filaggrin degradation |

> Notice: **every one of these is about lipid or envelope chemistry.** ARCI = "the mortar group."

### The ARCI entities (source, p. 173)

**(a) Harlequin ichthyosis (HI) — *ABCA12* — the most severe ichthyosis known**
Complete loss of ABCA12 function means lamellar bodies are essentially empty — no mortar at all. The neonate is encased in **thick, armour‑plate hyperkeratosis fissured into geometric polygonal plates** (hence "harlequin," from the diamond‑patterned costume). Consequences follow mechanically from the rigid casing and the absent barrier:
- **Severe ectropion** (eyelids everted) and **eclabium** (lips everted, "fish mouth") → corneal exposure, feeding failure
- **Flattened/absent ears and nose**, **hypoplastic digits**, **pseudo‑ainhum** (constriction bands → autoamputation)
- **Massive TEWL** → hypernatraemic dehydration, temperature instability, **hypermetabolic state**
- **Sepsis** through fissures — the leading cause of death
Management: **neonatal intensive care, high‑humidity incubator, meticulous emollients, ophthalmic lubrication, nutritional support, and early systemic retinoids (acitretin)** — which have markedly improved survival. Survivors evolve into a **severe CIE‑like erythroderma**.
⚠ **Do not confuse "harlequin ichthyosis" with "harlequin colour change,"** a benign transient vasomotor phenomenon of neonates in which one half of the body flushes and the other blanches. Same adjective, unrelated conditions.

**(b) Lamellar ichthyosis (LI) — *TGM1* in ~90%**
Born as a collodion baby, then evolves into **large, dark, plate‑like ("lamellar") brown scales with minimal erythema**, generalised including flexures, with **ectropion, eclabium, scarring alopecia, palmoplantar keratoderma, and hypohidrosis with heat intolerance**.

**(c) Congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma (CIE) — *TGM1*, *ALOX12B*, *ALOXE3*, *NIPAL4*, *CYP4F22* and others**
The opposite pole: **prominent, generalised bright erythroderma with fine, white, superficial scale**. Also ectropion and scarring alopecia. (Formerly "non‑bullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma," NBCIE.)

> **LI vs CIE in one line:** **LI = lots of dark scale, little redness; CIE = lots of redness, fine pale scale.** Many patients sit in between. Same genes, differing residual enzyme activity and modifiers.

**(d) Self‑improving collodion ichthyosis (SICI)**
Presents as a classic **collodion baby** — the neonate encased in a taut, shiny, yellowish **collodion membrane** like cellophane or a varnished parchment — which desquamates over 2–4 weeks to reveal **near‑normal or only mildly dry skin**. Usually *ALOX12B*, *ALOXE3*, *TGM1*, or *CYP4F22*.

**(e) Acral self‑healing collodion baby**
Same favourable biology but the membrane is **restricted to the extremities** (acral), with spontaneous resolution.

**(f) Bathing suit ichthyosis (BSI) — temperature‑sensitive *TGM1* mutations**
This is the most beautiful genotype–phenotype mechanism in all of dermatology, and a guaranteed mark if you explain it properly.

> **Cause → effect → outcome:** The mutant transglutaminase‑1 is **thermolabile**. At the **warm core temperature of the trunk, scalp and neck (~37 °C)** the enzyme misfolds and is **inactive** → no cornified envelope cross‑linking → ichthyosis. At the **cooler temperature of the distal extremities (~33–35 °C)** the same enzyme **retains activity** → normal skin. Result: thick scaling confined to a **"bathing suit" distribution** over the trunk (and often scalp/neck), with **strikingly spared arms, legs, hands and feet**.

Corollaries that prove the mechanism: affected neonates present as collodion babies (in utero the whole body is at core temperature — 37 °C everywhere — so the whole body is affected), and the limbs clear only *after* birth once they cool. BSI is reported most often in patients of **South African/sub‑Saharan African** ancestry.

### The collodion baby — a presentation, not a diagnosis
⚠ **This is a classic misunderstanding.** "Collodion baby" describes a *newborn phenotype*, not an aetiology. Its differential includes **lamellar ichthyosis, CIE, self‑improving collodion ichthyosis, bathing suit ichthyosis, Netherton syndrome, Gaucher disease type 2, trichothiodystrophy, Conradi–Hünermann–Happle, and Sjögren–Larsson**. Therefore **every collodion baby requires a systematic diagnostic work‑up** (see Part 7) — you cannot counsel the family until you know which of these it is, and the prognoses range from "completely normal by one month" to "fatal by age two."

## 4.3 Group 3 — Keratinopathic ichthyoses (KPI)

**Unifying concept:** these are caused by mutations in the **suprabasal keratin genes *KRT1*, *KRT2*, *KRT10***, and they share one histological signature: **epidermolytic hyperkeratosis (EHK)**.

**Mechanism, step by step:**
1. Keratins are **obligate heterodimers**: a type II basic keratin (K1, K2) pairs with a type I acidic keratin (K10). K1/K10 form the tonofilament network of the suprabasal epidermis; K2 takes over in the upper spinous/granular layers.
2. A **missense mutation in a highly conserved helix‑boundary motif** produces a protein that incorporates into filaments and **poisons the whole network** — a **dominant‑negative** effect (which is why these are usually **autosomal dominant**, with many cases sporadic/de novo).
3. The cytoskeleton can no longer resist **mechanical shear**. Keratinocytes **lyse** — this is *cytolysis within the epidermis*, i.e. **epidermolysis**, hence the term. Clinically: **neonatal blistering and erosions**.
4. Blistering diminishes over months as the skin adapts, and is **replaced by thick, verrucous/cobblestone hyperkeratosis**, characteristically **most severe in flexures**, with a **distinctive malodour** from bacterial overgrowth in the thick scale.
5. **Histology:** hypergranulosis with **coarse, clumped keratohyalin granules**, perinuclear vacuolisation, clumped tonofilaments on EM, and suprabasal cytolysis — pathognomonic **epidermolytic hyperkeratosis**.

⚠ **Terminology warning:** **"EHK" is a histological pattern, not a disease name.** It is also seen in epidermolytic acanthoma, epidermolytic naevi, some seborrhoeic keratoses, and incidentally in normal skin. The disease formerly called "bullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma of Brocq" is now **epidermolytic ichthyosis**.

### The KPI entities (source, p. 173 — seven entities)

| Entity | Gene | Distinguishing feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Epidermolytic ichthyosis (EI)** | ***KRT1*** / ***KRT10*** | Prototype. **KRT1 mutations → prominent palmoplantar keratoderma** (because K1 pairs with K9 in palmoplantar skin); **KRT10 mutations → palms/soles spared**. A neat genotype–phenotype rule worth quoting. |
| **Superficial epidermolytic ichthyosis** (Siemens' ichthyosis bullosa) | ***KRT2*** | K2 is expressed only in the **upper** granular layer → cytolysis is **superficial** → **mild**, no erythroderma, characteristic **"moulting"/Mauserung phenomenon** (circumscribed areas of superficial peeling leaving normal skin). Milder than EI. |
| **Annular epidermolytic ichthyosis** | ***KRT1*** / ***KRT10*** | **Migratory, annular and polycyclic erythematous plaques with double‑edged scale**, coming and going — can be mistaken for erythrokeratoderma variabilis or figurate erythema |
| **Congenital reticular ichthyosiform erythroderma (CRIE) = "ichthyosis with confetti"** | ***KRT10*** (frameshift/splice → dominant negative with **arginine‑rich mislocalised protein**) | See detailed note below — **revertant mosaicism** |
| **Ichthyosis Curth–Macklin** | ***KRT1*** (frameshift in the tail/V2 domain) | Severe **spiny/verrucous hyperkeratosis** with PPK. **EM shows a unique perinuclear shell of aggregated keratin filaments** (giving a binucleate‑like appearance) **rather than the classic clumping/cytolysis** of ordinary EI |
| **Autosomal recessive epidermolytic ichthyosis** | ***KRT10*** (true loss of function, homozygous/compound heterozygous) | Rare; proves that both dominant‑negative and biallelic‑null mechanisms exist for the same gene |
| **Epidermolytic naevi** | **Postzygotic mosaic *KRT1* / *KRT10*** | Linear/Blaschkoid verrucous plaques showing EHK on biopsy — see genetics note below |

### ⚠ Correction — the eponym
The source writes "**Ichthyosis Curth Macklin**." The correct form uses an **en‑dash**: ***Ichthyosis Curth–Macklin***, after **Henrietta Curth** and **Madge Thurlow Macklin**. Gene: ***KRT1***.

### ✅ Verification — "Congenital reticular ichthyosiform erythroderma" *is* correctly placed under keratinopathic ichthyosis, and it is a high‑yield entity
This is **ichthyosis with confetti (IWC)**. The story:
- Born with **generalised erythroderma and ichthyosiform scaling** (often with palmoplantar keratoderma and hypertrichosis).
- From childhood onward, the patient develops **hundreds to thousands of small, sharply defined, 1–10 mm spots of completely normal skin** — the "confetti" — which **increase in number and size with age**.
- **Mechanism (this is the beautiful part):** the *KRT10* mutation is heterozygous and dominant‑negative. In individual keratinocyte stem cells, **spontaneous mitotic recombination** between the maternal and paternal chromosome 17 arms results in **loss of heterozygosity** — the mutant allele is replaced by a copy of the normal allele. That single corrected stem cell then clonally expands into a macroscopic patch of **genetically normal, phenotypically normal skin**. This is **revertant mosaicism** — "natural gene therapy."
- Therefore: the confetti spots are **pathognomonic**, and they are a *good* sign, not a treatment response. Do not mistake them for vitiligo, pityriasis versicolor, or clearance from therapy.

### The genetics point behind "epidermolytic naevi" — mosaicism and gonadal transmission
The source lists epidermolytic naevi separately, and the reason is conceptually important.
- A **postzygotic** (somatic) *KRT1*/*KRT10* mutation occurring during embryogenesis produces a **mosaic** individual with disease limited to the affected clone — a linear/Blaschkoid verrucous naevus. This is **type 1 segmental mosaicism**.
- **Type 2 segmental mosaicism** occurs when a person with a germline heterozygous mutation suffers post‑zygotic **loss of heterozygosity** in one clone, producing a **segment of unusually severe disease superimposed on generalised milder disease**.
- **The clinical bombshell:** if the somatic mutation arose **early enough to involve the germ cells (gonadal mosaicism)**, the individual — who has only a localised naevus — can father or mother a child with **full‑blown generalised epidermolytic ichthyosis**. Hence **every patient with an epidermolytic naevus needs genetic counselling about reproductive risk.** This is a frequently tested concept and a genuine medico‑legal trap.

# PART 5 — Inherited ichthyoses: SYNDROMIC forms

**The clinical reason this category exists:** if a scaling neonate has a syndromic ichthyosis, the skin is the *least* of the child's problems. Your job is to recognise the extracutaneous clues early enough to (a) get the right metabolic test, (b) institute disease‑modifying therapy where it exists (diet in Refsum, enzyme replacement in Gaucher), and (c) counsel accurately.

The 2009 consensus divides syndromic ichthyoses by **mode of inheritance first (X‑linked vs autosomal)**, then, within the autosomal group, by **the dominant extracutaneous system involved**.

---

## 5.1 X‑linked syndromic ichthyoses

### (a) Recessive X‑linked ichthyosis with contiguous gene deletion syndromes

### ⚠ Correction and clarification — the source's "Recessive X‑linked IS"
The source lists "**Recessive X‑linked IS**" under the *syndromic* group. The abbreviation "IS" means "ichthyosis syndrome." This needs careful explanation, because at first glance it looks like a duplication — XLI already appeared under **nonsyndromic common ichthyoses** (§4.1). Both placements are correct, and the reason is the single most elegant teaching point in this whole classification:

> **XLI appears twice because the *same enzyme deficiency* can be either nonsyndromic or syndromic, depending on the *size of the deletion*.**

- The gene is ***STS*** (steroid sulfatase) at **Xp22.31**, and ~90% of patients have a **complete deletion** of the gene.
- If the deletion is **confined to *STS***, the patient has **isolated, nonsyndromic XLI** (plus its recognised minor associations: cryptorchidism, corneal opacities, and increased rates of ADHD/autism).
- If the deletion is **large and extends into neighbouring genes at Xp22.3**, the patient has a **contiguous gene deletion syndrome** — ichthyosis *plus* the phenotypes of whichever neighbours were lost:

| Neighbouring gene lost | Resulting additional phenotype |
|---|---|
| ***ANOS1*** (formerly ***KAL1***) | **Kallmann syndrome** — hypogonadotropic hypogonadism with **anosmia** (failed migration of GnRH neurons and olfactory axons) |
| ***ARSL*** (formerly ***ARSE***) | **X‑linked recessive chondrodysplasia punctata** — stippled epiphyses, short stature, nasal hypoplasia, brachytelephalangy |
| ***NLGN4X*** | **Intellectual disability / autism spectrum disorder** |
| ***MID1*** (more distal) | **Opitz G/BBB syndrome** features |
| ***SHOX*** region | Short stature |

**Mechanistic point to state explicitly:** unlike every other syndrome in this section, the extracutaneous features here are **not** caused by a shared pathway. They are caused by **physical chromosomal proximity**. This is why the *clinical* consequence is so specific: **a boy with XLI plus anosmia, delayed puberty, short stature or developmental delay needs cytogenetic/MLPA testing to define the deletion size**, not just an enzyme assay.

### (b) IFAP syndrome — **I**chthyosis **F**ollicularis, **A**trichia, **P**hotophobia
- **Gene: *MBTPS2*** (membrane‑bound transcription factor peptidase, site 2) — X‑linked; encodes **S2P**, the protease that, together with S1P, cleaves and activates **SREBP** transcription factors and ATF6. SREBP is the master regulator of **cholesterol and fatty‑acid synthesis** — so once again we are back in lipid metabolism, now at the level of transcriptional control.
- **Triad:** **(1) Ichthyosis follicularis** — spiny, thorn‑like follicular keratotic projections giving the skin a "nutmeg‑grater" feel; **(2) universal atrichia** — absent scalp hair, eyebrows, eyelashes, with **absent/rudimentary hair follicles**; **(3) photophobia** with corneal vascularisation/erosions.
- Additional: growth retardation, seizures, nail dystrophy, enamel defects. Severe in males; females show **Blaschko‑linear** involvement due to X‑inactivation mosaicism.
- Allelic with **keratosis follicularis spinulosa decalvans (KFSD)** and **BRESHECK syndrome** — same gene, graded severity.

### (c) Conradi–Hünermann–Happle syndrome (X‑linked dominant chondrodysplasia punctata, CDPX2)
- **Gene: *EBP*** — **emopamil‑binding protein**, i.e. **3β‑hydroxysteroid‑Δ8,Δ7‑isomerase**, an enzyme of the **post‑squalene cholesterol biosynthesis pathway**.
- **Mechanism:** blocked distal cholesterol synthesis → deficiency of cholesterol (needed for barrier lipids, for Hedgehog signalling in bone, and for lens transparency) **and** accumulation of toxic sterol precursors (**8‑dehydrocholesterol, 8(9)‑cholestenol** — the diagnostic biochemical markers).
- **Phenotype in females (X‑linked dominant, usually male‑lethal):**
  - **Neonatal ichthyosiform erythroderma in a striking Blaschko‑linear/whorled pattern** (functional mosaicism from random X‑inactivation), which resolves over months to leave
  - **Follicular atrophoderma ("ice‑pick" or orange‑peel pits) and linear/whorled cicatricial alopecia along Blaschko lines**
  - **Chondrodysplasia punctata** — stippled calcification of epiphyses on neonatal radiographs, resolving by ~age 2, leaving **asymmetric limb shortening and scoliosis**
  - **Sectorial cataracts**, flat nasal bridge, frontal bossing
- **Beware Happle's rule:** the pattern follows **Blaschko's lines**, reflecting the clonal migration paths of ectodermal cells — it is *not* dermatomal.

### (d) CHILD syndrome — **C**ongenital **H**emidysplasia with **I**chthyosiform naevus and **L**imb **D**efects
- **Gene: *NSDHL*** — NAD(P)H steroid dehydrogenase‑like protein, a **3β‑hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase** also in the **post‑squalene cholesterol pathway**, X‑linked dominant, male‑lethal.
- **The defining clinical feature is STRICT LATERALISATION** — the pathognomonic **"midline cut‑off."** One entire half of the body is affected, with the demarcation running exactly down the midline, sparing the other half.
  - **Ichthyosiform naevus:** unilateral, waxy, yellowish, sharply demarcated verrucous erythrokeratoderma with a peculiar affinity for **body folds (ptychotropism)** — axillae, groin, gluteal cleft
  - **Ipsilateral limb defects**, from hypoplasia to complete amelia; ipsilateral **stippled epiphyses**
  - Ipsilateral hypoplasia of brain, lung, kidney, heart
- **Histology:** verruciform xanthoma‑like changes with **foamy histiocytes in the papillary dermis** — a nice clue reflecting the underlying lipid defect.
- **Treatment insight straight from mechanism:** because the defect is **deficient cholesterol plus toxic precursor accumulation**, topical therapy combining **2% cholesterol + a statin (e.g. 2% lovastatin/simvastatin)** works remarkably well — the cholesterol replaces what is missing, and the statin shuts off the upstream pathway to stop precursors accumulating. Understanding this earns you the "pathophysiology‑based therapy" mark.

> **Group them and remember:** **Conradi–Hünermann–Happle (*EBP*) and CHILD (*NSDHL*) are both X‑linked dominant, male‑lethal, post‑squalene cholesterol biosynthesis disorders presenting with mosaic ichthyosis in females** — one **Blaschkoid/whorled**, the other **strictly hemilateral**. Together with IFAP (*MBTPS2*, SREBP pathway) and XLI (*STS*, cholesterol sulfate), **the entire X‑linked syndromic group is about sterol metabolism.** That is a sentence worth writing in an exam.

## 5.2 Autosomal syndromic ichthyoses — A. With prominent hair abnormalities

**Why hair and cornification travel together:** the hair shaft is itself a keratinised structure, built by the same machinery — trichocyte keratins, transglutaminases, and disulfide‑rich keratin‑associated proteins. A gene that disorders cornification in the interfollicular epidermis will often disorder it in the hair cortex and cuticle too. **Therefore: in every child with ichthyosis, pluck and examine hairs under light and polarised microscopy.** It is cheap, fast, and can be diagnostic.

### (a) Netherton syndrome — *the most important of this group* (elaborated in Q23)
- **Gene: *SPINK5***, encoding **LEKTI** (lympho‑epithelial Kazal‑type‑related inhibitor). Autosomal recessive.
- **Mechanism — a chain worth reciting:**
  1. LEKTI is the physiological **inhibitor of kallikrein‑related peptidases KLK5, KLK7 (and KLK14)**, the serine proteases that degrade corneodesmosomes during desquamation.
  2. No LEKTI → **unopposed kallikrein activity** in the stratum corneum.
  3. Kallikreins **cleave desmoglein‑1 and corneodesmosin** → corneocytes detach prematurely and the barrier is stripped. (Note the mechanistic kinship with **staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome**, where exfoliative toxin also targets DSG‑1, and with **CDSN‑mutant peeling skin disease**.)
  4. KLK5 also activates **PAR‑2** → NF‑κB → release of **TSLP, TNF‑α, IL‑8** → intense **Th2 inflammation**, driving atopy and **hyper‑IgE**.
  5. The stripped barrier → massive **TEWL**, allowing **hypernatraemic dehydration**, failure to thrive, recurrent **staphylococcal sepsis**, and enhanced **percutaneous absorption of topical drugs**.
- **Clinical triad:**
  1. **Ichthyosis linearis circumflexa (ILC)** — migratory, serpiginous/polycyclic erythematous plaques with a pathognomonic **double‑edged scale** at the advancing margin. (In neonates the presentation is usually **generalised congenital erythroderma with scaling**, and ILC emerges later.)
  2. **Trichorrhexis invaginata ("bamboo hair")** — the pathognomonic hair defect: the soft, incompletely keratinised proximal hair shaft **invaginates into itself** producing a **ball‑and‑socket / bamboo node**. Best sought in **eyebrow and eyelash hairs**, and may require repeated sampling over time — a single negative examination does not exclude the diagnosis. (Also seen: trichorrhexis nodosa, pili torti.)
  3. **Atopic diathesis** — severe atopic dermatitis‑like disease, food allergies, asthma, **markedly elevated IgE and eosinophilia**, and **immune dysregulation** with recurrent infection.
- **Therapeutic warnings that flow directly from the mechanism:**
  - **Avoid/limit topical calcineurin inhibitors and potent topical steroids** in neonates — the defective barrier causes **dangerous systemic absorption** (documented toxic tacrolimus blood levels).
  - **Systemic retinoids often worsen** Netherton (they further thin an already fragile stratum corneum) — unlike their usefulness in ARCI. A classic distinguishing exam point.
  - IVIG and biologics targeting the IL‑4/IL‑13 or IL‑17 axes (dupilumab, secukinumab) are emerging options.
  - Monitor sodium, growth, and for infection.

### (b) Trichothiodystrophy (TTD) — congenital and non‑congenital forms
- **Genes: *ERCC2* (XPD)**, ***ERCC3* (XPB)**, ***GTF2H5* (TTDA)**, ***MPLKIP* (TTDN1)**, ***RNF113A***.
- **The name tells you the biochemistry:** *tricho‑* (hair) *thio‑* (sulfur) *dystrophy* — the hair contains **only ~15–50% of normal cysteine/sulfur content**, because the **cysteine‑rich keratin‑associated proteins (KAPs)** that cross‑link the hair cortex are not made. Fewer disulfide bridges → structurally weak hair.
- **Diagnostic sign: "tiger‑tail" banding** — alternating light and dark transverse bands along the hair shaft under **polarising light microscopy**. Also trichoschisis (clean transverse fractures) and trichorrhexis nodosa. Hair is **brittle, sparse, short**.
- **Systemic features (mnemonic: PIBIDS)** — **P**hotosensitivity, **I**chthyosis, **B**rittle hair, **I**ntellectual impairment, **D**ecreased fertility, **S**hort stature. Also microcephaly, ataxia, cataracts, receding chin, protruding ears, dental caries, and in severe forms recurrent infection with neutropenia.
- Many patients present as **collodion babies** — hence TTD belongs in the collodion baby differential.
- **⭐ The classic exam distinction:** *ERCC2/ERCC3* are **nucleotide excision repair (NER)** genes — **the very same genes mutated in xeroderma pigmentosum group D and B**. Yet:

| | **Xeroderma pigmentosum (XPD)** | **Photosensitive trichothiodystrophy (also ERCC2/XPD)** |
|---|---|---|
| Photosensitivity | Marked | Marked |
| **Skin cancer risk** | **Enormously increased** | **NOT increased** |
| Hair | Normal | Brittle, sulfur‑deficient, tiger‑tail |
| Why the difference? | Mutations impair **global genome NER** → unrepaired mutagenic photoproducts → carcinogenesis | Mutations impair the **transcription (TFIIH) function** of XPD → defective transcription of sulfur‑rich KAP genes and other cell‑type‑specific genes → developmental/structural phenotype **without** the mutagenic burden |

XPD/ERCC2 is a subunit of **TFIIH**, which has **dual roles: DNA repair *and* basal transcription**. Which function a given mutation cripples determines whether you get **cancer (XP)**, **brittle hair and developmental defects (TTD)**, or **both (XP–TTD overlap)**. One gene, two functions, two diseases — this is a favourite viva question.

### (c) Ichthyosis–hypotrichosis syndrome (IHS)
- **Gene: *ST14*** (encoding **matriptase**, a membrane serine protease) — and note the pleasing logic: **matriptase activates prostasin, which participates in the protease cascade of profilaggrin processing and desquamation.** Congenital ichthyosis with diffuse **hypotrichosis** (sparse, slow‑growing, fragile hair), with normal intellect and no systemic disease.

### (d) Ichthyosis–hypotrichosis–sclerosing cholangitis syndrome (IHSC / NISCH syndrome)
- **Gene: *CLDN1*** — **claudin‑1**, an integral **tight junction** protein.
- **Mechanism:** tight junctions form the **second barrier** of the epidermis (beneath the stratum corneum) and also seal the **bile canaliculi** in the liver. Loss of claudin‑1 → leaky epidermal tight junctions (ichthyosis, hypotrichosis) **and** leaky biliary epithelium → **bile leakage and neonatal sclerosing cholangitis**.
- **Phenotype (NISCH = Neonatal Ichthyosis–Sclerosing Cholangitis):** neonatal scalp‑sparing ichthyosis, **scarring alopecia/hypotrichosis**, **oligodontia/hypodontia**, and **progressive cholestatic liver disease** with jaundice, pruritus, and eventual cirrhosis.
- **Clinical imperative:** this is why **every neonate with ichthyosis plus hypotrichosis needs liver function tests.** Missing it costs a liver.

## 5.3 Autosomal syndromic ichthyoses — B. With prominent neurological signs

The recurring theme, as promised: **shared dependence on lipid metabolism.** The brain is ~60% lipid by dry weight; the stratum corneum is the most lipid‑dependent epithelium. A lipid enzyme defect therefore hits both.

### (a) Sjögren–Larsson syndrome (SLS) ⭐

### ⚠ Correction of spelling — the source writes "Sjogren Larson syndrome"
The correct form is ***Sjögren–Larsson syndrome*** — named after the Swedish physicians **Karl Gustaf Torsten Sjögren** (psychiatrist/geneticist) and **Tage Konrad Leopold Larsson**. Note: **two s's in Larsson**, umlaut on Sjögren, en‑dash between. And note it is **entirely unrelated to Sjögren's syndrome** (the autoimmune sicca syndrome) — a different Swedish Sjögren (Henrik). Confusing them is a common and costly error.

- **Gene: *ALDH3A2*** at **17p11.2**, encoding **fatty aldehyde dehydrogenase (FALDH)**, a component of the **fatty alcohol : NAD⁺ oxidoreductase (FAO)** complex. Autosomal recessive.
- **Mechanism, step by step:**
  1. FALDH oxidises **long‑chain fatty aldehydes → fatty acids**, the second step of fatty alcohol catabolism.
  2. Deficiency → accumulation of **long‑chain fatty aldehydes and fatty alcohols**, plus abnormal **leukotriene B4** metabolism (LTB4 is also FALDH‑degraded) and accumulation of **ether lipids/aldehyde‑modified lipids**.
  3. Reactive aldehydes form **Schiff‑base adducts with membrane phospholipids and proteins**, damaging membranes; accumulating fatty alcohols are incorporated into **abnormal barrier lipids**.
  4. In skin → defective lamellar membranes and **abnormal lamellar body contents** → **ichthyosis**. In CNS → abnormal myelin lipids → **leukoencephalopathy and spasticity**. In retina → **crystalline deposits in the macula**.
- **The classic triad:**
  1. **Congenital ichthyosis** — usually generalised, fine to plate‑like, brownish, with a **characteristically intense pruritus** (unusual among ichthyoses — attributed to accumulated **leukotriene B4**), often with accentuation in flexures and **lichenification**; the **face and central abdomen are often relatively spared**. Neonates may show mild erythroderma but rarely a full collodion membrane.
  2. **Spastic diplegia or tetraplegia** — pyramidal signs, evident as delayed motor milestones and scissoring gait; many never walk independently.
  3. **Intellectual disability** — usually moderate to severe; also **seizures** in ~40%, and **speech delay** out of proportion.
- **⭐ The pathognomonic ophthalmic sign: "glistening white dots"** — **crystalline macular dystrophy**: multiple tiny, refractile, glistening yellow‑white dots in the perifoveal region, visible on **fundoscopy** and exquisitely well demonstrated on **OCT** (hyperreflective foci in the inner retina). Usually appears after 1–2 years of age and is **highly specific for SLS**. Also **photophobia** and progressive visual loss. *Learn this sign — it converts a vague "ichthyosis plus spasticity" into a confident diagnosis.*
- **MRI:** delayed/dysmyelination, periventricular white‑matter T2 hyperintensity; **MR spectroscopy shows an abnormal lipid peak**.
- **Diagnosis:** deficient FALDH/FAO activity in cultured fibroblasts or leukocytes; *ALDH3A2* sequencing.
- **Management:** emollients/keratolytics, physiotherapy and spasticity management; **zileuton (5‑lipoxygenase inhibitor)** reduces LTB4 and has improved pruritus in trials — mechanism‑derived therapy again.

### (b) Refsum disease ⭐ (elaborated in Q24)
- **Genes: *PHYH*** (phytanoyl‑CoA hydroxylase / phytanoyl‑CoA 2‑hydroxylase, ~90%) or ***PEX7*** (peroxin‑7, the receptor that imports PTS2‑containing enzymes such as PHYH into peroxisomes) — both autosomal recessive, both producing the same biochemical block.
- **Mechanism:**
  1. **Phytanic acid** is a **branched‑chain (3‑methyl) fatty acid** obtained **exclusively from the diet** — from **ruminant fat, dairy products, and certain fish** (derived from chlorophyll's phytol). Its methyl branch at C‑3 **blocks β‑oxidation**, so it must first be **α‑oxidised in peroxisomes** — the step catalysed by PHYH.
  2. Enzyme (or import) deficiency → **phytanic acid accumulates in plasma and all lipid‑rich tissues**.
  3. Phytanic acid is then **incorporated into membrane and barrier lipids in place of straight‑chain fatty acids**. Because the branched chain cannot pack into ordered bilayers, **membranes and stratum corneum lamellae become structurally disorganised** — and phytanic acid additionally displaces cholesterol and disrupts retinoid and PPAR signalling.
- **The classic tetrad:**
  1. **Ichthyosis** — often late‑onset (childhood/adolescence, unusual for a "congenital" ichthyosis), fine ichthyosis‑vulgaris‑like scaling, trunk and extremities, hyperkeratotic palms/soles. **It fluctuates with plasma phytanic acid levels** — a wonderful demonstration of causality, since the scaling improves on dietary restriction.
  2. **Retinitis pigmentosa** — the usual presenting complaint: **night blindness** and progressive **concentric visual field constriction (tunnel vision)**; also **cataract, miosis, and an abnormal electroretinogram (often the earliest detectable abnormality)**.
  3. **Chronic progressive peripheral polyneuropathy** — distal sensorimotor, often with **palpably enlarged nerves**; raised CSF protein with normal cell count (albuminocytological dissociation).
  4. **Cerebellar ataxia**.
  Plus: **anosmia** (very early and near‑universal), **sensorineural deafness**, **cardiac conduction defects and cardiomyopathy** (a cause of sudden death), **skeletal dysplasia with shortened 4th metatarsal**, and epiphyseal dysplasia.
- **Diagnosis:** **elevated plasma phytanic acid** (with normal very‑long‑chain fatty acids and normal plasmalogens in classic adult Refsum), then gene testing.
- **Management — and this is *why* recognising it matters:** **dietary restriction of phytanic acid** (avoid dairy, ruminant meat and fat, certain fish) with **adequate calorie intake to avoid mobilising phytanic acid from adipose stores**, plus **plasmapheresis/lipid apheresis** for acute deterioration. **The ichthyosis and the neuropathy improve; the retinitis pigmentosa and deafness do not reverse** — hence early diagnosis is everything.
- ⚠ **Do not confuse:** **classic (adult) Refsum disease** (*PHYH*/*PEX7*, isolated α‑oxidation defect) with **infantile Refsum disease** (*PEX1*, *PEX2*, *PEX26* — a **peroxisome biogenesis disorder** in the **Zellweger spectrum**, presenting in infancy with hypotonia, dysmorphism, hepatomegaly, and raised VLCFAs). Also note *PEX7* mutations more commonly cause **rhizomelic chondrodysplasia punctata type 1**; Refsum‑like phenotypes are the milder allelic end.

### (c) MEDNIK syndrome
### ✅ Verification of the acronym and gene
- **"MR" in MEDNIK stands for Mental Retardation** — a term now superseded by **intellectual disability**, but retained here because it is embedded in the historical acronym. Expand it fully: **M**ental retardation (intellectual disability), **E**nteropathy, **D**eafness, **N**europathy, **I**chthyosis, **K**eratoderma.
- **Gene: *AP1S1*** — the **σ1A subunit of adaptor protein complex 1 (AP‑1)**, which mediates **clathrin‑coated vesicle trafficking between the trans‑Golgi network and endosomes**. Autosomal recessive, extremely rare (originally described in a French‑Canadian founder population from Quebec).
- **Mechanism — one defect, six organs:** AP‑1 is required to traffic cargo to the right membrane. Loss of σ1A disrupts:
  - **Lamellar body and desmosomal protein trafficking in keratinocytes** → **ichthyosis, erythroderma and palmoplantar keratoderma**
  - **Trafficking of the copper transporters ATP7A and ATP7B** → a **combined Menkes‑like and Wilson‑like copper metabolism disorder** with **low serum copper and ceruloplasmin (Menkes‑like) yet hepatic copper accumulation and raised hepatic/urinary copper (Wilson‑like)** → liver disease
  - **Intestinal epithelial trafficking** → **congenital diarrhoea/enteropathy**
  - **Neuronal and cochlear vesicle trafficking** → **sensorineural deafness, peripheral neuropathy, intellectual disability**
- **Therapeutic corollary:** zinc acetate has been used to limit copper absorption, mirroring Wilson's disease management.

> **Pattern recognition for the neurological group:** **SLS = ichthyosis + spasticity + intellectual disability + glistening macular dots. Refsum = ichthyosis + retinitis pigmentosa + neuropathy + ataxia + anosmia (and it is *treatable by diet*). MEDNIK = ichthyosis + deafness + neuropathy + enteropathy + intellectual disability (copper).**

## 5.4 Autosomal syndromic ichthyoses — C. Fatal course

This subgroup exists for a sober clinical reason: **in these three conditions, the ichthyosis is the visible harbinger of a rapidly lethal metabolic or neurodegenerative disease.** Recognising them changes the conversation from "here is your emollient regimen" to palliative planning and urgent genetic counselling.

### (a) Gaucher disease type 2 (acute neuronopathic Gaucher)
- **Gene: *GBA*** (*GBA1*), encoding **acid β‑glucocerebrosidase (glucosylceramidase)** — the lysosomal enzyme that hydrolyses **glucosylceramide → ceramide + glucose**.
- **Why the skin is involved — and it is a lovely piece of logic:** in the epidermis, the final maturation of barrier lipids requires **β‑glucocerebrosidase to convert glucosylceramides (delivered in lamellar bodies) into ceramides** at the stratum granulosum–corneum interface. **Ceramides are the backbone of the lipid lamellae.** No glucocerebrosidase → glucosylceramides accumulate and ceramides are not generated → the lamellae never form → **barrier failure at birth**.
- **Hence the distinctive presentation:** **type 2 Gaucher is the only form of Gaucher disease with skin disease**, and it presents as a **collodion baby** or with an ichthyosiform/"dry, taut, shiny" neonatal skin. (In some infants the skin changes are subtle and transient — you must look for them.)
- **Systemic features:** **hepatosplenomegaly**, thrombocytopenia and anaemia, and **devastating brainstem neurodegeneration** — the classic triad of **oculomotor apraxia/strabismus, bulbar signs (stridor, dysphagia), and opisthotonic head retroflexion**, with seizures and spasticity. **Death typically by 2 years of age.**
- **Diagnosis:** low leukocyte/fibroblast glucocerebrosidase activity; Gaucher cells (lipid‑laden macrophages with "crumpled tissue paper" cytoplasm) in marrow; elevated chitotriosidase; *GBA* sequencing.
- **Critical caveat:** **enzyme replacement therapy rescues the visceral disease but does not cross the blood–brain barrier**, so it does **not** alter the neurological course of type 2. Hence "fatal course."
- ⚠ Note the extreme end of the spectrum: the **"collodion baby" presentation of Gaucher** overlaps with **hydrops fetalis/perinatal‑lethal Gaucher** — a recognised cause of non‑immune hydrops with ichthyosis.

### (b) Multiple sulfatase deficiency (MSD, Austin disease)
- **Gene: *SUMF1***, encoding **formylglycine‑generating enzyme (FGE)**, also called **sulfatase‑modifying factor 1**.
- **Mechanism — the "master switch" concept:** every human sulfatase (there are ~17) requires **post‑translational conversion of an active‑site cysteine into C‑α‑formylglycine** in order to work at all. FGE performs that conversion. **Lose FGE and you lose every sulfatase simultaneously.**
- **Therefore MSD is a combined phenotype**, and you can literally predict it by listing which sulfatase does what:

| Sulfatase lost | Disease phenocopied | Clinical contribution |
|---|---|---|
| **Arylsulfatase A** | **Metachromatic leukodystrophy** | Progressive **leukodystrophy**: developmental regression, spasticity, seizures, blindness, deafness |
| **Arylsulfatase B, iduronate‑2‑sulfatase, heparan‑N‑sulfatase, GALNS, G6S** | **Mucopolysaccharidoses** (Maroteaux–Lamy, Hunter, Sanfilippo, Morquio) | **Coarse facies, dysostosis multiplex, hepatosplenomegaly, corneal clouding, joint stiffness, hernias, cardiac valve disease, raised urinary GAGs** |
| **Steroid sulfatase (STS)** | **X‑linked ichthyosis** | **Ichthyosis** — this is exactly why MSD scales |
| **Arylsulfatase E** | **Chondrodysplasia punctata** | Stippled epiphyses, nasal hypoplasia |
- So: **MSD = metachromatic leukodystrophy + mucopolysaccharidosis + ichthyosis + chondrodysplasia punctata, all in one child.** Onset usually late infancy with developmental regression; **progressive, untreatable, fatal in childhood.**
- **Diagnosis:** assay of multiple sulfatases in leukocytes/fibroblasts (all reduced), urinary GAGs, *SUMF1* sequencing.
- **Teaching value:** MSD is the single best illustration in dermatology of a **post‑translational modification disorder** — the enzymes are present and correctly sequenced, but never "switched on."

### (c) CEDNIK syndrome
### ✅ Verification — correctly placed under "fatal course," and here is the full mechanism
- **Acronym:** **CE**rebral **D**ysgenesis, **N**europathy, **I**chthyosis, palmoplantar **K**eratoderma.
- **Gene: *SNAP29*** (synaptosomal‑associated protein 29, 22q11.2) — autosomal recessive; originally described in Israeli Arab kindreds. *SNAP29* lies within the **22q11.2** region, so it can also be affected in 22q11 deletion syndrome patients with a second‑allele mutation.
- **What SNAP29 does:** it is a **SNARE protein** (soluble NSF attachment protein receptor). SNAREs are the molecular machines that **dock and fuse vesicles with target membranes** — think of them as **zippers that pull two membranes together until they merge**. SNAP29 also participates in **autophagosome–lysosome fusion**.
- **One molecular defect → the entire phenotype:**

| Tissue | Vesicle event requiring SNAP29 | Consequence of loss |
|---|---|---|
| **Keratinocyte (granular layer)** | **Exocytosis of lamellar bodies** at the granular/corneal interface | Lipids never reach the intercellular space → **no lipid lamellae → ichthyosis and palmoplantar keratoderma**; abnormal lamellar granule secretion is visible on EM |
| **Neuron** | **Synaptic vesicle fusion**, and vesicle trafficking during neuronal migration | **Cerebral dysgenesis** — agenesis/hypoplasia of the corpus callosum, cortical dysplasia, pachygyria, hypoplastic cerebellum; **microcephaly** |
| **Peripheral nerve** | Membrane trafficking in Schwann cells/axons | **Sensorimotor neuropathy** |
| Other | Autophagy | Contributes to neurodegeneration |
- **Clinical course:** ichthyosis from birth/infancy, profound developmental failure, microcephaly, hypotonia progressing to spasticity, seizures, optic atrophy, sensorineural deafness, failure to thrive. **Most patients die in early childhood.**
- **Why this is worth learning:** together with **MEDNIK (*AP1S1*)**, CEDNIK establishes a whole mechanistic class: **"vesicle trafficking ichthyoses."** Both share ichthyosis + neuropathy + palmoplantar keratoderma, precisely because **skin barrier formation and neuronal function are the body's two most trafficking‑dependent processes.** If you can say that sentence, you have understood the section.

## 5.5 Autosomal syndromic ichthyoses — D. Others

### (a) KID syndrome — **K**eratitis, **I**chthyosis, **D**eafness ⭐
- **Gene: *GJB2*** — **connexin 26**; usually the recurrent **p.D50N (Asp50Asn)** mutation. Autosomal dominant, mostly **sporadic/de novo**. (A rarer *GJB6*/connexin‑30 form exists; the related **HID syndrome — hystrix‑like ichthyosis with deafness** — is allelic and simply a more hyperkeratotic variant.)
- **The triad:**
  1. **Keratitis** — progressive **vascularising keratitis** with photophobia, corneal neovascularisation and scarring → **progressive visual loss and blindness**; may not appear until later childhood, so **serial ophthalmology review is mandatory**.
  2. **Ichthyosis** — actually an **erythrokeratoderma**: at birth erythroderma or a leathery, thickened skin; then **well‑demarcated verrucous/hystrix‑like ("porcupine") hyperkeratotic plaques** with a characteristic **grainy/stippled ("leather‑grain") surface**, **reticulated palmoplantar keratoderma**, perioral furrowed hyperkeratosis, **scarring alopecia**, nail dystrophy and follicular occlusion.
  3. **Congenital bilateral profound sensorineural hearing loss** — present from birth; cochlear implantation is often required.
- **Additional and clinically critical:** **recurrent mucocutaneous candidiasis and bacterial infection**, and a **markedly increased risk of squamous cell carcinoma** — typically of the **tongue and mucocutaneous junctions and of the hyperkeratotic skin**, occurring in young adults. **Lifelong surveillance for SCC is essential.**
- **⭐ The high‑yield genetics point — same gene, opposite mutation mechanism, different disease:**

| | **Non‑syndromic autosomal recessive deafness (DFNB1)** | **KID syndrome** |
|---|---|---|
| Gene | ***GJB2*** (connexin 26) | ***GJB2*** (connexin 26) |
| Mutation type | **Loss of function** (e.g. 35delG) — biallelic | **Gain of function / dominant negative** (e.g. D50N) — monoallelic |
| Inheritance | Autosomal recessive | Autosomal dominant (usually de novo) |
| Functional consequence | Gap junction simply **absent** in the cochlea | Mutant connexin forms **"leaky" aberrant hemichannels** with abnormal permeability and aberrant Ca²⁺/ATP flux — **actively toxic** to keratinocytes and cochlear cells, and also disrupts keratinocyte growth control |
| Phenotype | **Deafness only** | **Deafness + skin + cornea + cancer risk** |

Why does loss of function spare the skin while gain of function destroys it? Because in the epidermis, **other connexins (Cx30, Cx31, Cx43) can compensate for absent Cx26** — but **nothing can compensate for a dominant, leaky, toxic channel** that also poisons the partner connexins it co‑assembles with. That single explanation earns the mark. (The same principle underlies the other *GJB2*‑related disorders: **Vohwinkel syndrome, palmoplantar keratoderma with deafness, Bart–Pumphrey syndrome** — all dominant *GJB2* missense mutations.)

> Note the recurring **connexin theme**: *GJB2* → KID/HID; *GJB3*, *GJB4*, *GJA1* → erythrokeratoderma variabilis; *GJB6* → Clouston hidrotic ectodermal dysplasia. **Connexinopathies = erythrokeratodermas ± deafness.**

### (b) Neutral lipid storage disease with ichthyosis (NLSDI) = **Chanarin–Dorfman syndrome**
- **Gene: *ABHD5*** (also called ***CGI‑58***) — α/β‑hydrolase domain‑containing protein 5, autosomal recessive. It is the **co‑activator of adipose triglyceride lipase (ATGL/PNPLA2)**, and also supplies acyl groups for **acylceramide synthesis via PNPLA1**.
- **Mechanism:** without ABHD5, **triacylglycerol cannot be hydrolysed** → **neutral lipid (triglyceride) droplets accumulate in the cytoplasm of virtually every cell type**, and the fatty acids trapped in those droplets are **unavailable for acylceramide/barrier lipid synthesis** → dual hit: storage **plus** barrier lipid deficiency.
- **Phenotype:** **congenital non‑bullous ichthyosiform erythroderma** (may be a collodion baby) persisting lifelong, plus **hepatomegaly with steatosis** progressing to fibrosis/cirrhosis, **myopathy with raised CK**, **ataxia, sensorineural deafness, cataracts, nystagmus, intellectual disability**, and short stature.
- **⭐ The diagnostic test you must know — the *Jordans' anomaly*:** a simple **peripheral blood smear** stained with **Oil Red O / Sudan black (or even careful examination on May–Grünwald–Giemsa)** reveals **multiple small lipid vacuoles within the cytoplasm of granulocytes (neutrophils and eosinophils) and monocytes**. This is **inexpensive, rapid, and essentially diagnostic** — a beautiful example of a bedside/laboratory shortcut in a complex metabolic disease. *Any child with congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma plus hepatomegaly or myopathy deserves a blood film looking for Jordans' anomaly.*
- **Distinguish:** **NLSD with myopathy (NLSDM)** is caused by ***PNPLA2*** mutations and has **myopathy and cardiomyopathy but NO ichthyosis** — because PNPLA2 is not required in the skin. One pathway, two genes, and the presence or absence of ichthyosis tells you which.

### (c) Ichthyosis prematurity syndrome (IPS)
- **Gene: *SLC27A4***, encoding **fatty acid transport protein 4 (FATP4)** — a very‑long‑chain acyl‑CoA synthetase essential for incorporating VLCFAs into barrier ceramides. Autosomal recessive; **founder mutations in Scandinavia (Norway, Sweden)**, hence the historical name "self‑healing ichthyosis of the Norwegian type."
- **The distinctive story, in order — and it is unmistakable once heard:**
  1. **Polyhydramnios** with **opaque amniotic fluid** containing **abundant floating cutaneous debris** (desquamated corneocytes and vernix‑like material), which may be mistaken for meconium.
  2. **Premature delivery**, typically at **30–34 weeks**, with **neonatal asphyxia/respiratory distress** — because the shed keratinous debris is **aspirated into the lungs**, and there is often eosinophilia.
  3. At birth: a **thick, caseous, "cottage cheese"/vernix‑like coating** over the entire body, notably on scalp and eyebrows — not a tight collodion membrane but a soft, crumbly casing.
  4. **Rapid improvement over days to weeks** into a **lifelong but mild** phenotype: **dry skin, follicular hyperkeratosis, itching, mild ichthyosis, heat intolerance**, and a **strong atopic tendency with raised IgE**.
- **Why it matters:** it is one of the very few ichthyoses with an **obstetric presentation**, and the combination of *prematurity + opaque amniotic fluid + neonatal asphyxia + thick caseous skin* should prompt immediate recognition so that the family is reassured about the excellent cutaneous prognosis rather than being told their child has a severe ARCI.

# PART 7 — Putting it to work: the bedside algorithm

Classification is only worth memorising if it changes what you do. Here is how this framework converts into action.

## 7.1 Approach to the scaling neonate

**Step 1 — Stabilise.** High‑humidity incubator, meticulous emollients, fluid/electrolyte balance (watch for **hypernatraemic dehydration**), temperature control, ocular lubrication, infection surveillance, nutrition (these babies are hypermetabolic).

**Step 2 — Characterise the skin.** Collodion membrane? Armour plates (harlequin)? Erosions/blisters (keratinopathic)? Caseous coating (IPS)? Mosaic/Blaschkoid or strictly hemilateral (X‑linked dominant mosaic)? Serpiginous double‑edged scale (Netherton)?

**Step 3 — Hunt for the extracutaneous clues, because these decide syndromic vs nonsyndromic:**

| Clue found | Think of |
|---|---|
| **Hair examined under light/polarised microscopy** | **Bamboo hair → Netherton**; **tiger‑tail → TTD**; sparse hair → IHS, IHSC |
| **Deafness** | **KID** (*GJB2*), MEDNIK, Refsum, MSD |
| **Eye: keratitis** | KID |
| **Eye: glistening macular dots** | **Sjögren–Larsson** |
| **Eye: retinitis pigmentosa / night blindness** | **Refsum** |
| **Eye: cataract + stippled epiphyses** | **Conradi–Hünermann–Happle** |
| **Spasticity + intellectual disability** | **Sjögren–Larsson** |
| **Hepatosplenomegaly + neurodegeneration in a neonate** | **Gaucher type 2** |
| **Hepatomegaly + myopathy + raised CK** | **Chanarin–Dorfman → blood film for Jordans' anomaly** |
| **Cholestatic jaundice** | **IHSC/NISCH (*CLDN1*)** |
| **Prematurity + opaque amniotic fluid** | **Ichthyosis prematurity syndrome** |
| **Anosmia / undescended testes / delayed puberty** | **XLI contiguous gene deletion (Kallmann)** |
| **Strictly unilateral skin + limb defects** | **CHILD syndrome** |
| **Atrichia + photophobia + spiny follicular papules** | **IFAP** |
| **Enteropathy + copper abnormalities** | **MEDNIK** |
| **Coarse facies + dysostosis + regression** | **Multiple sulfatase deficiency** |

**Step 4 — Targeted investigations.**
- **Always:** skin biopsy (light microscopy; consider **EM** for keratinopathic/Curth–Macklin/CEDNIK), **hair microscopy (light + polarised)**, ophthalmology, audiology, LFTs, U&E, CK, FBC with **blood film**.
- **Biochemistry when indicated:** steroid sulfatase activity / plasma cholesterol sulfate (XLI); **plasma phytanic acid** (Refsum); FALDH activity in fibroblasts (SLS); leukocyte glucocerebrosidase (Gaucher); multiple sulfatase assay + urinary GAGs (MSD); plasma sterols — **8‑dehydrocholesterol** (Conradi–Hünermann–Happle); serum copper/ceruloplasmin (MEDNIK); IgE/eosinophils (Netherton, PSS, IPS).
- **Genetics:** targeted panel or exome sequencing is now first‑line in most centres — but the clinical categorisation above determines how you interpret the variants.
- **Skeletal survey** if chondrodysplasia punctata is suspected; **MRI brain** if neurological signs.

## 7.2 General principles of management (shared across ichthyoses)

Because the final common pathway is **barrier failure with excess scale**, therapy is largely shared:
- **Hydrate then occlude:** bathing/soaking, then **emollients** (petrolatum‑based) — restore the mortar physically.
- **Keratolytics:** urea (10–40%), lactic acid/ammonium lactate, propylene glycol, salicylic acid. **Caution in infants and in extensive disease — systemic absorption** (salicylism; propylene glycol toxicity; lactic acidosis).
- **Topical retinoids** (tazarotene) and **topical calcipotriol** for localised disease.
- **Systemic retinoids (acitretin, isotretinoin)** — transformative in ARCI, harlequin ichthyosis, and lamellar ichthyosis. But note two exceptions: **worsen Netherton syndrome**, and their **long‑term toxicity** (teratogenicity — acitretin requires 3 years of contraception after stopping; hyperlipidaemia; **skeletal: premature epiphyseal closure, DISH**) demands monitoring.
- **Barrier‑lipid replacement based on mechanism:** **cholesterol + statin for CHILD syndrome** (and cholesterol for statin‑induced ichthyosis).
- **Disease‑specific:** **phytanic‑acid‑restricted diet in Refsum**; **zileuton in SLS**; **cochlear implant in KID**; SCC surveillance in KID; hearing aids, physiotherapy, ophthalmic care.
- **Always:** genetic counselling (remember **gonadal mosaicism** in epidermolytic naevi, **X‑linked carrier testing**, and prenatal diagnosis options), psychosocial support, and referral to patient support organisations (e.g. FIRST).

# PART 8 — Common misunderstandings: a checklist

Run through these before the exam. Each is a mark someone else will lose.

1. **"Ichthyosis is one disease."** No — it is a barrier‑failure *phenotype* produced by ≥50 genes plus acquired causes.
2. **"Collodion baby is a diagnosis."** No — it is a neonatal *presentation* with a differential that runs from self‑resolving to fatal. Always work it up.
3. **"Harlequin ichthyosis = harlequin colour change."** Unrelated. The latter is a benign neonatal vasomotor phenomenon.
4. **"EHK is the name of a disease."** No — **epidermolytic hyperkeratosis is a histological pattern**; the disease is **epidermolytic ichthyosis**.
5. **"Bullous CIE" is current terminology.** No — the 2009 consensus replaced "bullous" with "**epidermolytic**" because the split is **intraepidermal cytolysis**, not a subepidermal blister.
6. **Granular layer confusion.** **Ichthyosis vulgaris = reduced/absent granular layer. X‑linked ichthyosis = normal or thickened granular layer. Keratinopathic ichthyoses = hypergranulosis with coarse clumped granules.**
7. **"XLI is always nonsyndromic."** It is nonsyndromic when the deletion is confined to *STS*; it becomes **syndromic via contiguous gene deletion** at Xp22.3 (Kallmann, chondrodysplasia punctata, intellectual disability).
8. **"Lamellar ichthyosis and CIE are separate diseases with separate genes."** They are **poles of the ARCI spectrum** and share genes (notably *TGM1*).
9. **"Trichothiodystrophy is a form of xeroderma pigmentosum, so these patients get skin cancer."** **No.** Same genes (*ERCC2/ERCC3*), but TTD reflects the **transcription** defect of TFIIH, not the repair defect → **photosensitivity WITHOUT increased skin cancer risk.**
10. **"GJB2 mutations cause deafness, so KID syndrome is recessive."** No — **DFNB1 = biallelic loss of function (deafness only); KID = monoallelic dominant gain‑of‑function/dominant‑negative (leaky toxic hemichannels) → deafness + skin + cornea + SCC risk.**
11. **"Netherton syndrome responds to retinoids like other ichthyoses."** Retinoids typically **worsen** it. And **topical calcineurin inhibitors/steroids can reach toxic blood levels** through the defective barrier.
12. **"Sjögren–Larsson is related to Sjögren's syndrome."** Completely unrelated; different Sjögren. And spell it **Larsson** (double s), with an en‑dash.
13. **"Refsum disease is untreatable."** The **ichthyosis and neuropathy improve with a phytanic‑acid‑restricted diet**; retinopathy and deafness do not reverse — so **early diagnosis is the treatment**. Also distinguish **classic adult Refsum (*PHYH*)** from **infantile Refsum (*PEX1*, Zellweger spectrum)**.
14. **"Confetti spots in CRIE mean the treatment is working."** They are **revertant mosaicism** — spontaneous genetic self‑correction — and are **pathognomonic**.
15. **"An epidermolytic naevus is purely cosmetic."** It may signify **gonadal mosaicism** with risk of generalised epidermolytic ichthyosis in offspring. Counsel.
16. **"Acquired ichthyosis is just dry skin in the elderly."** New generalised scaling in an adult mandates a search for **malignancy (especially Hodgkin lymphoma)**, endocrinopathy, HIV, drugs and CTD. Distinguish from **asteatotic eczema/xerosis** and from the **bound‑down skin of scleroderma**.
17. **"HMG‑CoA is a drug."** It is a substrate; the drugs are **HMG‑CoA reductase inhibitors (statins)**.
18. **"MR in MEDNIK means something else."** It is **mental retardation** (now intellectual disability), retained in the historical acronym.
19. **"Chanarin–Dorfman needs sophisticated testing."** A **simple peripheral blood film showing Jordans' anomaly** (lipid vacuoles in granulocytes) is nearly diagnostic.
20. **"Bathing suit ichthyosis is a distribution quirk."** It is a **temperature‑sensitive *TGM1* mutation** — enzyme dead at 37 °C (trunk), functional at cooler limb temperatures. Mechanism, not coincidence.

# PART 9 — Summary anchor: the core idea in one picture

> **Hold this single image in your head.**
>
> The stratum corneum is a **brick wall**: **corneocyte bricks** reinforced by **keratin rebar (K1/K10/K2)** and bundled by **filaggrin**, sealed inside a **transglutaminase‑1 cross‑linked envelope**, bedded in **lipid mortar (ceramide : cholesterol : fatty acid)** delivered by **lamellar‑body trucks (ABCA12, SNAP29, AP1S1)**, tied together by **corneodesmosome masonry ties (DSG‑1, corneodesmosin)** that are demolished on schedule by **kallikrein proteases restrained by LEKTI**, with **connexin gap junctions** as the site foremen coordinating the work.
>
> **Ichthyosis is what you see when any one of those components fails: the wall leaks, water escapes, the epidermis panics, and scale accumulates.**
>
> Then ask the one question that organises the entire classification:
>
> **"Is the broken component used ONLY in skin, or is it used elsewhere too?"**
>
> - **Only in skin** → **nonsyndromic** ichthyosis (*FLG*, *STS* alone, *TGM1*, *ABCA12*, *KRT1/2/10*, *LOR*, *CDSN*).
> - **Used elsewhere too** → **syndromic** ichthyosis, and the *elsewhere* tells you the syndrome:
>   - a **lipid/sterol pathway** shared with brain, retina, nerve → **Sjögren–Larsson, Refsum, Chanarin–Dorfman, IPS, CHILD, Conradi–Hünermann–Happle, IFAP, Gaucher 2, MSD**
>   - a **vesicle‑trafficking machine** shared with neurons → **CEDNIK, MEDNIK**
>   - a **gap junction** shared with cochlea and cornea → **KID**
>   - a **protease/adhesion system** shared with hair and immune regulation → **Netherton**
>   - a **DNA‑repair/transcription complex** shared with hair and development → **trichothiodystrophy**
>   - a **tight junction** shared with bile canaliculi → **IHSC/NISCH**
>   - or nothing shared at all, just **chromosomal neighbours deleted together** → **XLI contiguous gene syndromes**
> - And if the wall was **built perfectly but is now failing in an adult** → **acquired ichthyosis**: look for **Hodgkin lymphoma**, endocrine/metabolic disease, HIV or leprosy, drugs (especially **statins**), or connective tissue disease.

Memorise the **skeleton diagram in §2.2** first, then hang the entities on it, then attach the genes, then the clinical clues. Do it in that order and a bewildering list of fifty diseases becomes a single, logical, and — dare I say — rather beautiful story about a brick wall.
