# Hailey–Hailey Disease: A Guided Lecture

## 1. The Big Picture — Where This Disease Lives in Your Mental Map

Let's start by orienting ourselves, because Hailey–Hailey disease (HHD) only makes sense when you see the family it belongs to.

The epidermis is not a pile of loose cells. It is a **riveted wall**. Each keratinocyte is bolted to its neighbours by **desmosomes** — protein rivets that link the cytoskeletons of adjacent cells so that mechanical force applied to the skin surface is distributed across the whole sheet rather than tearing any single cell free. When those rivets fail, cells separate from one another. That separation has a name: **acantholysis**.

Now, acantholysis can happen in two fundamentally different ways:

- **Acquired/autoimmune acantholysis** — antibodies attack the rivets (the pemphigus group).
- **Inherited/genetic acantholysis** — the cell is genetically incapable of *building and maintaining* the rivets properly (the hereditary acantholytic genodermatoses).

Hailey–Hailey disease sits firmly in the second group, alongside its cousin **Darier disease**. Both are autosomal dominant, both are caused by mutations in **calcium pumps**, and both produce acantholysis — but in different organelles, different body sites, and with different morphology. That pairing (HHD vs Darier) is the single most examined comparison in this topic, so we will build toward it deliberately.

**Why it matters clinically:** HHD is a chronic, painful, foul-smelling, relapsing flexural disease that devastates quality of life, is repeatedly misdiagnosed as "resistant intertrigo" or "inverse psoriasis" for years, is frequently over-treated with immunosuppression because of the misleading word "pemphigus" in its old name, and carries a small but real risk of squamous cell carcinoma.

---

## 2. The Name — and Why It Is a Trap

**Hailey–Hailey disease = familial benign chronic pemphigus.**

Dissect the old name, because every word of it teaches or misleads:

- **Familial** — true. Autosomal dominant.
- **Benign** — true in the sense that it is *not* the lethal autoimmune pemphigus vulgaris of the pre-steroid era. But do not tell the patient it is "benign" and expect them to be reassured; it is miserable.
- **Chronic** — true. Lifelong, relapsing, worse in summer.
- **Pemphigus** — **false and dangerous.** There is no autoantibody. Direct immunofluorescence is **negative**. The histology shows acantholysis, which is why 19th–20th century dermatologists lumped it with pemphigus, but the mechanism is a genetic defect in desmosome *assembly*, not immune destruction. The practical consequence: high-dose long-term immunosuppression is not the answer, and slapping a patient on chronic steroids will mainly buy you candidiasis and skin atrophy in the folds.

> **Teaching point:** "Pemphigus" in the old name describes the *histological appearance*, not the *pathogenesis*. Same picture, different cause — like a collapsed wall that fell because the mortar was never mixed properly, versus one that fell because someone chiselled the mortar out.

---

## 3. Definition (Exam-Ready)

> Hailey–Hailey disease is an **autosomal dominant inherited acantholytic disorder** caused by mutations in *ATP2C1*, characterised by **recurrent vesicles, erosions, fissures, macerated plaques and vegetations in the flexures and at sites of friction and trauma**, with the histological hallmark of widespread partial acantholysis producing a **"dilapidated brick wall"** appearance.

---

## 4. Epidemiology — and the Puzzle of Late Onset

- **Inheritance:** autosomal dominant, with **high but incomplete penetrance** and marked variability in severity even within one family.
- **Onset:** typically the **2nd to 4th decade** — adolescence to middle age. Rarely in childhood.
- **Sex:** roughly equal.
- About **two-thirds** of patients report a positive family history; the remainder appear sporadic (new mutations, or mildly affected relatives who were never diagnosed).

Here is the question a good student always asks: *if you are born with the mutation, why does the disease wait twenty or thirty years?*

**Answer — the threshold model.** The mutation halves your functional pump capacity (haploinsufficiency). That is enough to build desmosomes adequately *under low mechanical and thermal stress*. Disease appears only when environmental load exceeds the reduced reserve. Post-pubertal changes supply exactly that load: **apocrine and eccrine sweating increases, axillary and groin hair appears, body habitus changes, skin folds deepen, friction rises**. So the genotype is present from conception, but the *phenotype* waits for the flexures to become hot, wet, and rubbing. This is also precisely why the treatment ladder begins with weight loss and antiperspirants rather than drugs — you are lowering the load back below the threshold.

## 5. Pathophysiology — The Core of the Disease, Step by Step

This is the part worth understanding deeply, because every clinical feature, trigger and treatment falls out of it.

### Step 1 — The gene

**Missense or nonsense mutations in *ATP2C1*, on chromosome 3q21–q24.** Over 150 mutations are described; most are loss-of-function, and there is **no strong genotype–phenotype correlation** (a family member with the identical mutation may be far milder).

### Step 2 — The protein

*ATP2C1* encodes **SPCA1 — secretory pathway Ca²⁺/Mn²⁺-ATPase type 1**. Unpack the name:

- **Secretory pathway** → it lives in the membrane of the **Golgi apparatus**.
- **Ca²⁺/Mn²⁺-ATPase** → it burns ATP to pump calcium (and manganese) *from the cytosol into the Golgi lumen*.

So SPCA1 is the **Golgi's calcium loading dock**. Its job is to keep the Golgi lumen full of Ca²⁺.

### Step 3 — Why the Golgi needs calcium

The Golgi is the cell's **post office and finishing workshop**. Proteins arrive half-made, and inside the Golgi they are folded, glycosylated, sorted, packaged into vesicles, and addressed to their destination. A very large fraction of these enzymatic and chaperone reactions are **calcium-dependent** — and, critically, **desmosomal cadherins are calcium-dependent adhesion molecules whose extracellular domains only fold into their correct, adhesive conformation in a high-Ca²⁺ environment**.

**Analogy:** think of the Golgi as a factory whose glue only sets at the right humidity. SPCA1 is the humidifier. Knock the humidifier down to half power and the factory still runs — but the parts it ships out are poorly finished and the glue doesn't cure.

### Step 4 — The downstream consequences

With reduced Golgi Ca²⁺:

1. **Impaired trafficking and processing of desmosomal proteins** — notably **desmoplakin** (the plaque protein that anchors desmosomes to keratin intermediate filaments) and **desmoglein-3 (Dsg-3)** (the transmembrane cadherin that actually grips the neighbouring cell). Desmoglein-3 does not reach the membrane in adequate amounts or correct conformation; desmoplakin fails to be properly recruited to the plaque.
   → **Result: desmosomes are fewer, smaller, and mechanically weak, and they are not properly tied into the keratin cytoskeleton.**
2. **Altered actin reorganisation** — desmosome and adherens-junction assembly requires calcium-dependent remodelling of the cortical actin cytoskeleton to drive cell membranes together. Disordered actin dynamics means keratinocytes cannot form or re-form adhesive contacts efficiently after they are disturbed. Notice the significance: it is not just that the rivets are weak — the **repair crew is also slow**. That explains chronicity and poor healing.
3. **Increased vulnerability to ER stress** — Golgi and ER calcium pools are continuous and interdependent. Disturbed Ca²⁺ handling causes accumulation of misfolded protein, activating the **unfolded protein response**, generating oxidative stress and predisposing keratinocytes to **apoptosis/dyskeratosis**. This is why you can see a few dyskeratotic cells in HHD, and it links the disease mechanistically to Darier disease.
4. **Impaired Notch signalling and abnormal differentiation** — contributing to the epidermal hyperplasia and the papillomatous/vegetating clinical lesions.

### Step 5 — From molecule to lesion

Weak rivets + poor repair + mechanical shear in a fold + heat + moisture
→ keratinocytes pull apart (**acantholysis**) at multiple levels of the epidermis
→ intraepidermal clefts and flaccid vesicles form
→ the fragile roof of macerated skin is immediately rubbed off
→ **erosions and painful fissures**, because vesicles in intertriginous skin essentially never survive to be seen intact
→ chronic erosion + chronic inflammation + reactive proliferation → **vegetating, papillomatous plaques**
→ exudate + bacterial overgrowth in a warm anaerobic fold → **malodour**

That single causal chain explains virtually the entire clinical picture. Hold onto it.

### Step 6 — The pivotal molecular contrast

| | Hailey–Hailey | Darier |
|---|---|---|
| Gene | ***ATP2C1*** | ***ATP2A2*** |
| Pump | **SPCA1** | **SERCA2** |
| Organelle | **Golgi** | **Endoplasmic reticulum** |

Both are Ca²⁺-ATPases of the secretory system; both cripple calcium-dependent desmosomal assembly; but they do it at **different stations of the same assembly line**. HHD fails at *packaging and shipping* (Golgi) → the dominant defect is **adhesion failure = acantholysis**. Darier fails at *initial folding and quality control* (ER) → the dominant defect is **abnormal keratinisation with dyskeratosis (corps ronds and grains)** on top of acantholysis.

**Mnemonic:** *Hailey = **H**igher up the pathway is fine, the **G**olgi fails → think **G** for Golgi and **G**roin (flexures).* Darier: **ATP2A2**, **A** for **A**cid... simpler: *2A2 = ER; 2C1 = Golgi* — "A comes before C, ER comes before Golgi." That ordering mnemonic is reliable and worth memorising.

## 6. Triggers and Exacerbating Factors — All Explainable

Every trigger is simply "something that raises mechanical, thermal, osmotic or inflammatory load on a wall with weak mortar."

| Trigger | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Heat** | Increases sweating and macerates the stratum corneum; also directly destabilises weak desmosomal adhesion. Hence the classic **summer flare**. |
| **Friction** | Direct shear force on mechanically incompetent desmosomes — the fundamental provocateur. Explains flexural and waistband/bra-line distribution. |
| **Sweating** | Maceration swells corneocytes, reduces barrier integrity, and creates the moist milieu for microbial overgrowth. |
| **Infection** (bacterial, candidal, HSV, HPV) | Microbial proteases and the inflammatory infiltrate further degrade adhesion; inflammation amplifies the lesion. Infection is both a *consequence* and a *driver* — a vicious cycle. |
| **UVB exposure** | Experimentally reproduces lesions; UVB alters keratinocyte calcium handling and desmoglein expression and induces ER stress. Useful clue: HHD can flare on sun-exposed skin, unlike ordinary intertrigo. |
| **Contact allergy** (adhesive dressings, ECG electrodes) | Allergic contact dermatitis provides the inflammatory "second hit"; the adhesive also strips a fragile epidermis on removal. Practically: **avoid strongly adhesive tapes on these patients.** |
| **Scabies** | Itch–scratch trauma plus inflammation; a classic reported precipitant. |
| **Pre-existing inflammatory dermatoses** | Lesions may localise to sites of **psoriasis, seborrhoeic dermatitis or drug eruptions** — an isotopic/"second-hit" phenomenon: pre-existing inflammation lowers the local threshold for acantholysis. |

## 7. Clinical Features

### The classic presentation

- **Painful, pruritic and often malodorous** lesions of the flexures. Note that triad: HHD hurts, itches, *and* smells — which is why patients are socially withdrawn and why the "benign" label rings hollow.
- **Sites:** axillae, groins, inframammary and submammary folds, perineum, perianal area, natal cleft, and lateral/posterior neck. Also sites of pressure and friction — waistband, bra straps, inner thighs.
- **Primary lesion:** flaccid vesicles or small blisters on an erythematous base — which are **rarely seen intact**, because they rupture instantly in a moist fold.
- **What you actually see:** well-demarcated, moist, red plaques with **characteristic linear fissures running along the depths of the fold** — sometimes described as "cut with a knife" or resembling the surface of a *dried river bed*. These **linear rhagades in the fold are a strong diagnostic clue**.
- **Evolution:** with chronicity → **maceration, erosions, crusting, and soft papillomatous/vegetating plaques**.
- **Course:** relapsing and remitting, worse in hot weather, with spontaneous remissions; may improve somewhat in later life.
- **Extent:** usually **localised**, but may be **generalised**, and rarely progresses to **erythroderma**.

### Nails — a small sign with big exam value

- **Longitudinal (linear) white bands = longitudinal leukonychia.** These are often **asymptomatic and may be the only sign** in a carrier relative, so examine the nails of the family.
- Contrast with Darier disease: **red *and* white longitudinal bands (the "candy-cane" nail) plus distal V-shaped nicking/notching and subungual hyperkeratosis.** HHD nails are *white bands only* — no V-nicks, no palmar pits.

### Mucosae

**Rare.** Oral, oesophagogastric, laryngeal, conjunctival and vaginal involvement have been reported but are uncommon — another useful separator from true pemphigus vulgaris, where mucosal disease is usually the *first* and dominant feature.

---

## 8. Clinical Variants

1. **Papular acantholytic dermatosis of the genitocrural area** — discrete whitish papules of the vulva, groin or perianal skin, often in women, without typical flexural erosions. Many cases carry *ATP2C1* mutations and are regarded as a **localised/forme fruste HHD**. Frequently misdiagnosed as genital warts or lichen sclerosus — think of it when "warts" fail to respond to destructive therapy.
2. **Relapsing linear acantholytic dermatosis** — recurrent acantholytic lesions arranged in **linear/Blaschkoid** streaks.
3. **Mosaic (segmental) variants** — reflecting post-zygotic mutation:
   - **Type 1 segmental:** localised unilateral disease on otherwise normal skin, in a patient with no germline mutation (mosaic only).
   - **Type 2 segmental:** a heterozygous patient who suffers **loss of heterozygosity** in one segment → generalised mild disease with a **superimposed band of severe disease**. Recognising type 2 matters because it implies transmissible germline disease.

## 9. Severity Classification (Grades 0–3)

This grading is not academic decoration — it maps directly onto the therapeutic ladder.

| Grade | Description | Practical translation |
|---|---|---|
| **Grade 0** | Subclinical carrier; asymptomatic, no lesions, or **nail changes only** | Reassure, counsel, genetic advice, preventive measures |
| **Grade 1** | **Mild**: localised to perineum or axillae; intermittent; **responds to topical treatment** | Lifestyle + topicals |
| **Grade 2** | **Moderate**: chronic lesions at **more than two body sites**; locally severe and **refractory to topicals** | Escalate to systemic therapy |
| **Grade 3** | **Severe**: extensive or chronically disabling **despite topical and systemic treatment** | Consider surgical/ablative and destructive options, botulinum toxin, breast reduction |

## 10. Complications — and the Logic Behind Each

The eroded, macerated, immunologically busy flexure is an ideal niche for trouble.

- **Secondary bacterial infection** — *Staphylococcus aureus*, streptococci, and anaerobes. The commonest cause of a sudden painful flare with increased odour and exudate. **Always consider infection before escalating immunosuppression.**
- **Candidal infection** — warm, moist, occluded folds; steroid use amplifies it.
- **Herpes simplex virus** → may produce **Kaposi varicelliform eruption (eczema herpeticum)** — a diffuse, monomorphic, punched-out erosive eruption with systemic upset. This is the **emergency** in HHD: abrupt worsening with fever and grouped punched-out ulcers demands PCR/Tzanck and **prompt systemic aciclovir**.
- **HPV infection** — verrucous lesions in the affected folds; also relevant to carcinogenesis.
- **Squamous cell carcinoma** — the key long-term risk, arising in **chronically eroded, inflamed, vegetating flexural plaques (especially perianal, vulval and axillary)**, often HPV-associated. Clinical rule: **any non-healing, indurated, exophytic or ulcerated nodule within a long-standing HHD plaque must be biopsied.** Do not assume "it's just her Hailey–Hailey."
- **Allergic contact dermatitis** — from the many topicals, antiseptics, fragrances and adhesive dressings these patients accumulate. A patient whose disease worsens *while* on treatment may be reacting to the treatment; patch testing is legitimate.

## 11. Investigations

### A. Histopathology — the diagnostic cornerstone

Biopsy the **edge of an active lesion**, ideally including intact adjacent skin, and handle it gently (the epidermis is fragile).

Findings:

1. **Suprabasilar clefting**, with **acantholytic cells lining and lying free within the clefts**. The basal cells remain attached to the basement membrane and stand up like a **"row of tombstones"** — the same term used in pemphigus vulgaris, hence the historical confusion.
2. **Widespread *partial* acantholysis at multiple levels of the epidermis** — this is the defining feature. Cells are separated but many remain loosely connected by residual slender bridges, so the epidermis looks like a **wall whose bricks are loose, crooked, and in places missing — the "dilapidated brick wall."**

   > **Why this metaphor is mechanistically perfect:** in pemphigus, antibodies cleave one specific plane, so you get a *clean split* — a wall neatly demolished along one mortar line. In HHD, *every* desmosome everywhere in the epidermis is intrinsically weak, so separation is **diffuse, partial and multi-level** — a wall that is *crumbling all over*, not cut in half. **Full-thickness partial acantholysis = intrinsic genetic defect; single-plane clean acantholysis = targeted autoimmune attack.**
3. **Epidermal hyperplasia** (acanthosis, papillomatosis) — the reactive proliferative response to chronic erosion, corresponding to the clinically vegetating plaques. Villous projections of dermal papillae lined by a single layer of basal cells may extend into the cleft.
4. **Corps ronds and grains — present only rarely, and few in number** (in contrast to Darier disease, where they are abundant and diagnostic). Their occasional presence reflects the shared ER-stress/dyskeratosis mechanism, but dyskeratosis is *not* the story in HHD.
5. **Neutrophils — numerous**, found within the vesicles/clefts or in the overlying **parakeratotic crust**, reflecting erosion and secondary bacterial colonisation. (Their prominence is a clue to always culture for infection.)
6. **Direct immunofluorescence: NEGATIVE.** No intercellular IgG or C3. **This single test excludes pemphigus** and is the investigation to order when you are unsure. Indirect IF and ELISA for Dsg-1/Dsg-3 are likewise negative.

### B. Dermoscopy

- **Polymorphous vessels — glomerular, linear, looped, spiral and coiled — over a pinkish-yellow background.**
- *Interpretation:* the pinkish-yellow background reflects erosion, serous exudate and crust; the varied vessel morphologies reflect the reactive angiogenesis and papillomatous epidermal hyperplasia over dilated papillary capillaries. Practically, dermoscopy helps separate HHD from **inverse psoriasis** (which shows uniformly distributed regular dotted/glomerular vessels on a light-red background) and from **extramammary Paget disease** (milky-red areas with clustered irregular vessels).

### C. Molecular

- **Commercial *ATP2C1* gene sequencing is available.** It confirms the diagnosis in atypical, segmental or purely papular presentations, enables **genetic counselling** (50% transmission risk, autosomal dominant), and identifies subclinical relatives. It is confirmatory, not routine — the diagnosis is usually clinical plus histological.

### D. Supporting/ancillary

- **Bacterial, fungal and viral swabs/PCR** at every flare — because infection is the commonest reason for sudden deterioration.
- **Patch testing** in patients worsening on topicals.
- **Biopsy of any suspicious nodule** for SCC.

## 12. Differential Diagnosis (Practical Bedside Thinking)

| Mimic | How to tell it apart |
|---|---|
| **Inverse (flexural) psoriasis** | Sharply demarcated shiny red plaques, less fissuring, no acantholysis on HPE, often psoriasis elsewhere/nail pitting |
| **Candidal intertrigo** | Satellite pustules, responds fully to antifungals, KOH positive |
| **Pemphigus vegetans** | Oral involvement common, **DIF positive**, single-plane acantholysis with eosinophilic abscesses |
| **Darier disease** | Seborrhoeic distribution, greasy keratotic papules, palmar pits, corps ronds/grains, red-white nail bands with V-nicks |
| **Extramammary Paget disease** | Unilateral, well-defined, may be pruritic; Paget cells, CK7+ |
| **Frictional/allergic intertrigo** | History of contact, resolves with avoidance |
| **Granular parakeratosis, glucagonoma syndrome, acrodermatitis enteropathica** | Consider in atypical flexural eruptions; biopsy and metabolic screen |

## 13. Treatment — A Ladder Built on Mechanism

The governing principle: **you cannot fix the pump, so you reduce the load on the wall and control the amplifiers (friction, heat, sweat, microbes, inflammation).**

### First line — load reduction (never skip this; it is genuinely effective)
- **Weight loss** — fewer and shallower folds → less friction, less occlusion, less sweat trapping. Mechanistically the most rational intervention available.
- **Emollients and topical antiseptics** — restore barrier, reduce microbial burden and odour. Dilute bleach baths, chlorhexidine or povidone-iodine washes are commonly used.
- **Reduce heat, friction and sweating** — loose breathable cotton clothing, air conditioning, absorbent barriers between skin surfaces, antiperspirants, drying powders, avoidance of occlusive adhesive dressings, treating hyperhidrosis. Counsel about summer flares.

### Second line — suppress inflammation and infection
- **Topical corticosteroids (TCS)**, alone or in combination with antimicrobials/antifungals. Potent steroids work quickly; use short bursts and be mindful of **atrophy, striae and candidiasis** in the folds.
- **Oral antibiotics / antifungals / antivirals** — directed by swab results; treating the microbial "second hit" often produces disproportionate clinical improvement.
- **Oral prednisolone** — for acute severe flares only. Remember: this works as an **anti-inflammatory**, not as immunosuppression for an autoimmune process. Short courses, then taper.

### Third line — for grade 2–3 / refractory disease
- **Topical tacrolimus (0.1%)** — steroid-sparing calcineurin inhibitor; particularly valuable for **long-term maintenance in thin flexural skin** where steroid atrophy is a real hazard. A highly favoured option in practice.
- **Low-dose long-term antibiotics — doxycycline, minocycline** — exploiting their **anti-inflammatory and matrix-metalloproteinase-inhibiting** properties rather than pure antibacterial action; useful for maintenance suppression.
- **Systemic agents:**
  - **Ciclosporin** — rapid control in severe flares.
  - **Methotrexate** — anti-inflammatory maintenance.
  - **Retinoids (acitretin/isotretinoin)** — variable: may help the hyperkeratotic/vegetating component but can worsen fragility in some patients.
  - **Naltrexone (low dose)** — reported benefit, attributed to modulation of opioid-receptor/TLR signalling and improved keratinocyte wound healing and autophagy.
  - **Vitamin D (oral or topical calcitriol)** — modulates keratinocyte calcium handling and differentiation; a mechanistically attractive adjunct.
  - **Magnesium chloride** — oral magnesium is thought to reduce intracellular calcium mobilisation/competing calcium signalling and has anecdotal benefit.
  - Other reported options include dapsone, thalidomide, apremilast, and biologics in case reports.
- **Refractory flexural disease — definitive local measures:**
  - **Surgical excision with grafting, dermabrasion, or ablation with CO₂/erbium laser.** *Rationale:* destroy or remove the genetically incompetent epidermis; re-epithelialisation occurs from adnexal epithelium or the graft, and the resulting fibrotic/grafted surface is thicker, drier and less mobile, so it resists shearing. This can produce durable, sometimes permanent, local remission.
  - **Breast reduction (reduction mammoplasty)** — mechanically abolishes the deep, moist inframammary fold. Pure load reduction, surgically achieved.
  - **Botulinum toxin injection** — chemodenervates eccrine sweat glands → dramatically reduces axillary/inguinal sweating → removes maceration, one of the principal amplifiers. Often strikingly effective and now a mainstream option in axillary disease.

### Also essential to the "treatment" of HHD
- **Genetic counselling:** autosomal dominant, 50% risk to offspring; examine relatives' nails.
- **Psychosocial support:** pain, odour and genital involvement cause depression, sexual dysfunction and social isolation. Address it explicitly.
- **Long-term surveillance** for SCC in chronic plaques.

## 14. The Master Comparison: Hailey–Hailey vs Darier

| Feature | **Hailey–Hailey Disease** | **Darier Disease** |
|---|---|---|
| **Gene / locus** | ***ATP2C1***, 3q21–24 | ***ATP2A2***, 12q23–24 |
| **Protein / organelle** | **SPCA1 — Golgi** Ca²⁺ pump | **SERCA2 — ER** Ca²⁺ pump |
| **Dominant defect** | **Adhesion failure → acantholysis** | **Keratinisation failure → dyskeratosis** (+ acantholysis) |
| **Distribution** | **Flexural / intertriginous**, friction sites | **Seborrhoeic** — central chest, back, face, scalp, neck |
| **Primary lesion** | Flaccid vesicles → **macerated erosions, linear fissures, vegetations** | **Greasy, warty, keratotic red-brown papules** coalescing into plaques |
| **Odour/pain** | Prominent (malodorous, painful) | Malodour possible in flexural involvement, less fissuring |
| **HPE hallmark** | **"Dilapidated brick wall"** — widespread *partial* multi-level acantholysis | **Corps ronds and grains** — prominent dyskeratosis with suprabasal clefts, villi |
| **Corps ronds/grains** | **Rare, sparse** | **Abundant, diagnostic** |
| **Nails** | **Longitudinal leukonychia (white bands only)** | **Red and white longitudinal bands + distal V-shaped nicking** |
| **Palmar pits** | **Absent** | **Present** (with punctate keratoses) |
| **Mucosal lesions** | Rare | Cobblestone white papules on palate/oral mucosa |
| **Neuropsychiatric associations** | Not typical | **Depression, epilepsy, learning difficulty, suicidal ideation, schizophrenia** |
| **DIF** | Negative | Negative |
| **Key triggers** | Friction, heat, sweat, infection, UVB | Heat, sweat, **UVB**, lithium, infection |
| **Distinctive therapy** | Botulinum toxin, surgical ablation, weight loss | Oral retinoids (mainstay) |

## 15. Common Misunderstandings — Let's Clear Them Up

1. **"It's a type of pemphigus, so give immunosuppressants."** No. DIF is negative; there is no antibody. Immunosuppression has only an adjunctive anti-inflammatory role and carries real infection risk in eroded, colonised folds.
2. **"Dilapidated brick wall and tombstoning mean the same thing."** No. *Tombstoning* = retained basal cells standing on the basement membrane (seen in both HHD and pemphigus vulgaris). *Dilapidated brick wall* = **diffuse, partial, multi-level** loss of cohesion throughout the epidermis, characteristic of HHD.
3. **"Corps ronds and grains rule out Hailey–Hailey."** They are **rare** in HHD but not impossible. It is their **abundance and diagnostic prominence** that signals Darier. Judge by the dominant pattern, distribution and nails.
4. **"Both diseases have a calcium pump defect, so they're basically the same disease."** Same *class* of defect, **different organelle** — and that difference determines whether the cell fails at *adhesion* (Golgi/HHD) or at *differentiation* (ER/Darier), which in turn determines flexural versus seborrhoeic distribution and erosive versus keratotic morphology.
5. **"A worsening plaque just means the disease is flaring."** Not necessarily. Three alternatives must be actively excluded: **bacterial/candidal superinfection, HSV (Kaposi varicelliform eruption), and squamous cell carcinoma.** Also consider contact allergy to the treatment.
6. **"Weight loss and antiperspirants are token advice."** They are, mechanistically, among the most powerful interventions available, because HHD is a *threshold* disease driven by mechanical and thermal load.
7. **"Onset in adulthood means it isn't genetic."** Autosomal dominant inheritance with adult onset is entirely typical here — the mutation is congenital, but the phenotype awaits post-pubertal friction, sweat and fold formation.
8. **"Nail changes are a Darier-only sign."** HHD has its own nail sign — **asymptomatic longitudinal white bands** — which may be the *only* manifestation in a carrier, making nail examination a cheap family-screening tool.

## 16. The Core Idea in One Picture

> Picture a **brick wall built with defective mortar**. The bricks are keratinocytes; the mortar is desmosomes; and the mortar only sets properly in a calcium-rich Golgi workshop. In Hailey–Hailey disease, a mutation in ***ATP2C1*** halves the **SPCA1** pump that loads calcium into the Golgi, so desmoplakin and desmoglein-3 are never properly finished or delivered, actin cannot reorganise to rebuild lost contacts, and ER stress adds insult. The wall stands — until it is placed somewhere **hot, wet and constantly rubbed**: the axilla, the groin, the inframammary fold. There it **crumbles diffusely at every level**, which is exactly what the pathologist sees as the **"dilapidated brick wall,"** and what the patient experiences as **painful, itchy, malodorous fissured erosions** that flare every summer, get infected, and occasionally harbour a carcinoma. Because you cannot rebuild the pump, therapy is **load reduction** (lose weight, stay cool, stay dry, stop the friction, stop the microbes), then **inflammation control** (steroids, tacrolimus, tetracyclines), then **mechanical solutions** (botulinum toxin to stop the sweat, surgery to abolish the fold or resurface the skin). And remember the family resemblance: **ER pump (ATP2A2) → Darier, keratotic and seborrhoeic; Golgi pump (ATP2C1) → Hailey–Hailey, eroded and flexural.**
