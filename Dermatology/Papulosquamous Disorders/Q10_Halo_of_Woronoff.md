# The Halo of Woronoff (Woronoff's Ring)

## A Mini-Lecture

---

## 1. First, the Big Picture: Why Should Anyone Care About a Pale Ring?

Let me start by placing this topic on the map, because a "short note" question hides something genuinely beautiful.

Psoriasis sits in the chapter on **disorders of keratinization** — a group of diseases in which the epidermis makes the wrong amount of the wrong kind of keratin at the wrong speed. Psoriasis is the flagship member: a chronic, immune-driven, T-cell–mediated inflammatory disease in which keratinocytes proliferate too fast, differentiate incompletely, and are accompanied by a dilated, tortuous dermal vasculature and a neutrophil-rich infiltrate.

Now, most signs we teach in psoriasis describe **what happens inside the plaque** — Auspitz sign, Koebner phenomenon, the silvery scale. The Woronoff ring is different and far more interesting: it describes what the plaque **does to the skin around it.**

That single shift in perspective is the whole point. The Woronoff ring tells us that a psoriatic plaque is not a passive patch of abnormal skin sitting inertly on a normal background. It is a **biochemically active factory** that secretes diffusible mediators which travel outward into clinically normal-looking skin and change its behaviour. The plaque has a *sphere of influence* that extends beyond its visible border.

So the Woronoff ring is, in effect, a **naturally occurring experiment in anti-inflammatory pharmacology**, conducted on the patient's own skin, free of charge. That is why dermatologists have been fascinated by it for a century.

---

## 2. Setting the Clinical Scene: The Plaque That Casts the Halo

Before we can describe a halo, we must describe the thing it surrounds. In an examination, *always* open a Woronoff-ring answer with two or three lines of plaque psoriasis morphology — it shows the examiner you understand context, not just trivia.

### The plaque itself

Plaque-type (chronic stationary) psoriasis presents as **erythematous, scaly plaques with sharply demarcated borders**. The sharpness of the edge is diagnostically important — you can almost trace the lesion with a pen, unlike eczema, whose borders fade away.

These lesions **vary** in size, shape, and distribution (note: *vary*, the verb — a common transcription slip turns this into "very"). They are typically **symmetrical**, favouring the **scalp, trunk, and extensor surfaces of the limbs** — elbows, knees, lumbosacral region.

### Lesional characteristics

| Feature | What it is | Why it happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Red plaque with silvery-white, easily shed scale** | The classic lesion | Erythema = dilated, elongated dermal capillaries in the papillary dermis; scale = parakeratotic, loosely adherent stratum corneum from accelerated, incomplete keratinocyte maturation |
| **Auspitz sign positive** | Pinpoint bleeding when scale is gently scraped off | The suprapapillary epidermis is thinned, so removing the scale unroofs the dilated dermal papillary vessels |
| **Koebner phenomenon** | New psoriatic lesions arising at sites of trauma | Trauma releases antimicrobial peptides and cytokines that recruit the same T-cell/dendritic-cell circuit, "seeding" disease in susceptible skin |
| **Post-inflammatory hyper- or hypopigmentation on healing** | Residual colour change after the plaque flattens | Inflammation disturbs melanocyte–keratinocyte melanin transfer; direction depends on constitutive pigment and inflammation intensity |

### Symptoms — the patient's experience

* **Pruritus** — often the *dominant* symptom, contrary to the old teaching that "psoriasis doesn't itch." It substantially impairs quality of life and sleep. (Spelling note: it is **pruritus**, from Latin *prurire*, "to itch" — not "pruritis.")
* **Skin tightness and burning** — characteristic of **unstable, erythrodermic, or pustular** psoriasis, where the barrier is failing and inflammation is acute.
* **Pain in fissured areas** — deep cracks in **palmoplantar** or **flexural** psoriasis. This is a high-yield point: these subtypes involve a tiny body surface area yet cause disproportionate disability, because fissured palms and soles hurt with every step and every grip.
* **Embarrassment and social withdrawal** from constant shedding of scale.

Hold that image in your mind: a sharply bordered red plaque. Now look at the skin *just outside* the border.

## 3. What the Woronoff Ring Actually Is

> **Definition:** The halo of Woronoff is a **pale, blanched, hypopigmented ring or halo of apparently healthy skin immediately surrounding a psoriatic plaque**, seen in both **treated and untreated** psoriasis.

**Historical anchor:** It was described in **1926 by Woronoff**, originally in patients being treated with **chrysarobin** — a plant-derived anthraquinone antipsoriatic agent, the historical ancestor of today's **dithranol (anthralin)**.

### Three details that separate a good answer from an average one

**(a) It is *pale*, not depigmented.** The pallor has **two superimposed components**, and you must mention both:
1. A **vascular** component — reduced vasodilatation, so less erythema than the surrounding normal skin.
2. A **pigmentary** component — genuinely reduced melanin content in the epidermis.

**(b) It occurs in untreated as well as treated lesions.** This is conceptually crucial. If the halo appeared only after therapy, we could dismiss it as a drug artifact — bleaching by dithranol, vasoconstriction by a steroid. Because it occurs **spontaneously**, it must reflect an **intrinsic, plaque-generated, peri-lesional immunological phenomenon**. The plaque itself is the source.

**(c) It is a marker of a *quiescent or resolving* lesion.** Clinically, the appearance of a Woronoff ring around plaques during therapy is generally read as a **favourable sign** — evidence that local inflammation is being suppressed.

## 4. The Mechanism: A Diffusible Anti-Inflammatory Gradient

Here is the heart of the topic. The exact pathophysiology remains **incompletely understood**, but the dominant and best-supported hypothesis is **local prostaglandin inhibition with consequent reduction of inflammation.**

### 4.1 Groundwork: what prostaglandins do in skin

To understand the ring, you must understand the molecule whose absence creates it.

Prostaglandins — especially **PGE₂** and **PGI₂ (prostacyclin)** — are arachidonic-acid metabolites generated by cyclo-oxygenase. In skin they are the principal **"turn-on-the-redness" signals**:

* They cause **arteriolar vasodilatation** → increased cutaneous blood flow → **erythema and warmth**
* They increase **vascular permeability** → oedema
* They **sensitise nociceptors** → pain and itch
* They are the chief mediators of **UV-induced erythema (sunburn)**

So: *prostaglandin present → vessels open → skin looks red.* And by simple logic, *prostaglandin absent → vessels stay closed → skin looks pale.*

### 4.2 The chain of causation

Now the mechanism, step by step, as a cause → effect → outcome chain:

```
Psoriatic plaque (metabolically hyperactive tissue)
        │
        ▼
Generates / releases a PROSTAGLANDIN-SYNTHESIS INHIBITOR
(a small, diffusible, as-yet incompletely characterised substance)
        │
        ▼
Substance DIFFUSES CENTRIFUGALLY from the plaque edge
into the immediately adjacent perilesional skin
(highest concentration nearest the plaque, falling off with distance)
        │
        ▼
Within that narrow zone, local prostaglandin synthesis is SUPPRESSED
        │
        ├──► Loss of prostaglandin-mediated vasodilatation
        │          → dermal vessels remain constricted/undilated
        │          → BLANCHING (pallor)
        │
        ├──► Local inflammation is PREVENTED / damped
        │          → no acanthosis, no parakeratosis, no neutrophils
        │          → skin remains architecturally near-normal
        │
        └──► Melanogenesis / melanin transfer is downregulated
                   → REDUCED MELANIN
                   → HYPOPIGMENTATION
        │
        ▼
Net clinical result: a PALE, HYPOPIGMENTED HALO of
non-inflamed skin hugging the plaque border
```

### 4.3 The analogy that makes it click

Think of the psoriatic plaque as a **wildfire**, and the Woronoff ring as a **firebreak**.

A wildfire consumes and blackens its centre. But imagine this particular fire also *exhales a fire-retardant vapour* that drifts outward and soaks the ground immediately around it. That collar of treated ground cannot catch fire. It looks conspicuously different from both the burning centre and the untouched forest beyond — not because nothing is happening there, but because something **actively suppressive** is happening there.

A second analogy: think of a **drop of ink falling into water**. The concentration is highest at the point of entry and fades with distance. That is exactly why the ring is **narrow and hugs the plaque edge** — the inhibitor obeys a diffusion gradient. Beyond a few millimetres, the concentration falls below the threshold needed to suppress prostaglandin synthesis, and skin resumes its normal colour.

### 4.4 The two pieces of evidence you must be able to quote

This hypothesis is not hand-waving. Two elegant observations support it, and citing **both** makes an examination answer compelling.

**Evidence 1 — The PGE₂ injection experiment (the classic).**
If you **inject prostaglandin E₂ directly into the blanched Woronoff ring, the erythema is restored** — the pale skin turns red.

Why is this so decisive? Because it distinguishes between two rival explanations for the pallor:
* *Hypothesis A (structural):* the vessels in that zone are damaged, obliterated, or permanently constricted. → If true, adding PGE₂ would achieve nothing; there would be no functional vasculature to dilate.
* *Hypothesis B (biochemical):* the vessels are perfectly healthy and fully capable of dilating — they are simply **not receiving the signal** to do so, because prostaglandin production is locally switched off.

Since PGE₂ restores redness, **Hypothesis B is correct**. The pallor is a **functional, mediator-deficiency state, not a structural vascular defect.** The plumbing is intact; the tap has been turned off. Put the mediator back and the tap opens.

**Evidence 2 — Failure to develop erythema during phototherapy.**
When a psoriatic patient receives UV phototherapy, the surrounding normal skin develops the expected UV erythema, but the **Woronoff ring fails to redden**. It behaves as though it were shielded.

Why does this support the same conclusion? Because **UV-induced erythema is itself substantially prostaglandin-mediated** — UV damages keratinocytes, arachidonic acid is liberated, cyclo-oxygenase generates prostaglandins, vessels dilate, and you get sunburn. If a zone of skin *cannot* mount UV erythema, the most parsimonious explanation is that **its prostaglandin-generating machinery is blocked**.

Notice the logical symmetry, which is what makes the argument so satisfying:
* **Add prostaglandin → redness appears** (you can rescue it)
* **Demand prostaglandin (via UV) → no redness appears** (it cannot supply it)

Both arrows point to the same conclusion: *local prostaglandin deficiency.*

## 5. Histopathology: What the Microscope Shows

If you biopsy the Woronoff ring, you find skin that is **"more normal than normal"** — that is the phrase to remember. Four findings are classically listed, and each maps neatly onto the mechanism.

| HPE finding | What it means | Why it happens / significance |
|---|---|---|
| **Minimal epidermal acanthosis** | Only slight epidermal thickening; the epidermis is nearly normal in depth | Acanthosis in psoriasis is driven by inflammatory cytokines (IL-17, IL-22, TNF-α) pushing keratinocyte proliferation. With inflammation suppressed, the proliferative drive is largely absent. Some minimal thickening persists because the zone still lies within the plaque's biochemical reach. |
| **Reduced melanin content** | Less pigment in the epidermis | This is the histological correlate of the **clinically visible hypopigmentation**. Melanocytes are present but under-producing / transferring less melanin — inflammation and its mediators normally *stimulate* melanogenesis, so their suppression reduces it. |
| **Absence of parakeratosis** | No retained keratinocyte nuclei in the stratum corneum | This is the single most telling finding. **Parakeratosis is a histological hallmark of psoriasis** — it reflects accelerated transit through the epidermis so keratinocytes are extruded before they can shed their nuclei. Its **complete absence** in the ring proves that epidermal turnover here is *normal, not accelerated*. The ring is genuinely not psoriatic skin. |
| **Decreased endoglin (CD105)** | Reduced expression of a vascular glycoprotein | **Endoglin** is a transmembrane glycoprotein that acts as an accessory receptor within the **TGF-β receptor complex**, expressed strongly on **proliferating vascular endothelium**. It is a marker and mediator of **angiogenesis** and it **modulates inflammation**. Its reduction in the Woronoff ring indicates **decreased angiogenic and endothelial activation** — the vessels here are quiescent. This is a sophisticated point that elevates an answer. |

> **Conceptual summary of the histology:** The Woronoff ring is not "damaged" skin, "scarred" skin, or "partially psoriatic" skin. It is **immunologically and proliferatively silenced skin** — architecturally normal epidermis, quiescent vasculature, hypoactive melanocytes. A zone of enforced peace.

## 6. The Geometry: Reading the Concentric-Ring Diagram

The standard textbook figure depicts three concentric zones. Here it is as reproduced in the source:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
│         Psoriatic Lesion (center)       │
│  ┌───────────────────────────────────┐  │
│  │        Normal Skin (middle)       │  │
│  │  ┌─────────────────────────────┐  │  │
│  │  │  Area of Hypopigmentation   │  │  │
│  │  │  and Woronoff Ring (outer)   │  │  │
│  │  └─────────────────────────────┘  │  │
│  └───────────────────────────────────┘  │
└─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**An honest word of caution, because this labelling confuses students.** Taken literally, the diagram appears to place a band of "normal skin" *between* the plaque and the Woronoff ring. Clinically, that is not how it works: the **Woronoff ring lies immediately adjacent to the plaque margin**, directly abutting it. The label "normal skin" in the figure is best understood as the **clinically uninvolved skin beyond the halo** — the outermost territory, unreached by the diffusing inhibitor.

So the anatomically faithful sequence, from centre outward, is:

```
CENTRE  →  Active psoriatic plaque
              (erythematous, acanthotic, parakeratotic,
               dilated vessels, high endoglin)
   │
   ▼
RING    →  WORONOFF RING  ← the halo
              (pale + hypopigmented, minimal acanthosis,
               NO parakeratosis, reduced melanin,
               low endoglin, prostaglandin-deficient)
   │
   ▼
OUTSIDE →  Truly normal, uninvolved skin
              (normal colour, normal vessels,
               normal UV erythema response)

        ◄────── diffusion gradient of inhibitor ──────►
        (concentration high at plaque edge, falling outward)
```

Think of it as a **moat around a castle**, or the **cordon sanitaire** around a quarantined city. The ring is a **buffer zone** — a transition territory where the plaque's chemical influence is strong enough to suppress inflammation but where the disease itself has not taken hold.

For diagram-based questions, reproduce the three-zone model with clear labels and add a single arrow labelled *"centrifugal diffusion of prostaglandin-synthesis inhibitor."* That one arrow converts a drawing into an explanation.

## 7. Clinical and Scientific Significance

### 7.1 With which treatments is it seen?

The Woronoff ring has been observed after essentially every classical antipsoriatic modality:

* **UV light** (phototherapy — and remember, the ring itself resists UV erythema)
* **Topical corticosteroids**
* **Coal tar**
* **Dithranol (anthralin)** — and its historical predecessor **chrysarobin**, in which Woronoff first saw it

Look at that list and notice the unifying thread: **every one of these agents is anti-inflammatory, and several directly or indirectly interfere with the arachidonic-acid/prostaglandin cascade.** Corticosteroids inhibit phospholipase A₂ (via lipocortin/annexin-1), cutting off arachidonic acid at source. Coal tar and dithranol modulate keratinocyte proliferation and inflammatory mediator generation. UV is immunosuppressive.

This is why the Woronoff ring is more than a curiosity: **it appears to be a visible, macroscopic readout of successful local anti-inflammatory action.** Nature has drawn us a picture of what our drugs are doing.

### 7.2 Why it matters

* **As a clinical sign:** its presence indicates **localised reduction in inflammation** and is generally interpreted as a marker of lesional quiescence or therapeutic response.
* **As a research window:** if we could fully characterise the endogenous prostaglandin-synthesis inhibitor that the plaque itself manufactures, we would be holding a **naturally occurring, skin-generated anti-inflammatory molecule**. Elucidating the molecular mechanisms of the Woronoff ring would therefore offer insights not only into psoriasis therapy but into **inflammatory skin disease in general.** The body is already making an anti-psoriatic drug at the plaque edge; we simply have not finished identifying it.

That last sentence is the intellectual payoff of the entire topic. Do not omit it.

## 8. Other Conditions With a Woronoff-Ring–Like Phenomenon

A Woronoff-like pale halo has been **rarely described** around lesions of:

* **Lichen planus**
* **Granuloma annulare**
* **Pityriasis rosea**
* **Eczemas**
* **Secondary syphilis**
* **Molluscum contagiosum**
* **Sézary syndrome**

### How to interpret this list intelligently

Do not memorise it as a random string. Notice the pattern: **almost all of these are conditions with an active, ongoing immunological process in the skin** — lymphocyte-mediated (lichen planus, pityriasis rosea, eczema, secondary syphilis), granulomatous (granuloma annulare), or viral-immunomodulatory (molluscum).

The inference is important: the Woronoff ring may not be a psoriasis-specific quirk at all, but rather a manifestation of a **general biological principle — perilesional immunosuppression.** Wherever a focus of intense cutaneous immune activity exists, it may generate diffusible mediators that damp inflammation in a narrow surrounding collar. Psoriasis is simply where the phenomenon is most conspicuous, because psoriatic plaques are sharply bordered and intensely erythematous, providing maximum contrast against a pale ring.

**Two entries deserve individual comment:**

* **Sézary syndrome** — the erythrodermic, leukaemic variant of **cutaneous T-cell lymphoma (CTCL)**. A Woronoff-like phenomenon here is particularly striking, because it implies that even a **neoplastic** lymphocytic infiltrate can create a perilesional immunomodulatory zone. Malignant T cells are well known to secrete suppressive cytokines; this may be that suppression made visible.
* **Molluscum contagiosum** — an unusual inclusion, and worth flagging as such. It likely refers to the pale halo sometimes seen around molluscum papules (a *"Woronoff-like"* or pseudo-halo sign), plausibly reflecting local immune modulation by the poxvirus, which encodes multiple immune-evasion proteins. This is **not a classical Woronoff ring**, but it is occasionally described.

## 9. Common Confusions — Let's Clear Them Up

Students reliably trip over the following. Fix them now.

**① "The Woronoff ring is depigmentation, like vitiligo."**
No. It is **hypopigmentation plus vascular blanching** — melanocytes are present and functioning, merely under-producing. It is **reversible** and resolves as the plaque clears. Vitiligo involves autoimmune **destruction** of melanocytes and produces chalk-white, completely depigmented macules. Different mechanism, different reversibility, different Wood's-lamp appearance.

**② "It's the same as post-inflammatory hypopigmentation."**
Related but distinct. Post-inflammatory hypopigmentation appears **where the plaque used to be**, *after* it has resolved. The Woronoff ring appears **around a plaque that is still there** — it is *peri*-lesional, not *post*-lesional.

**③ "It must be caused by the drug bleaching the skin."**
No — and this is exactly why the "**treated *and* untreated**" point is examined. Its occurrence in untreated psoriasis proves it is plaque-generated, not drug-generated.

**④ "The vessels in the ring are damaged."**
No — the PGE₂ injection experiment refutes this directly. The vessels are structurally normal and fully responsive; they merely lack the dilating signal.

**⑤ Confusing it with a halo nevus (Sutton's nevus).**
A halo nevus has a depigmented ring around a melanocytic nevus caused by a **cytotoxic T-cell attack on melanocytes** — an *immune-activated*, destructive halo. The Woronoff ring is the conceptual **opposite**: an *immune-suppressed*, non-destructive halo. Same geometry, opposite biology.

**⑥ Confusing it with the Meyerson phenomenon.**
Meyerson's is an **eczematous, red, inflamed halo** around a lesion (classically a nevus) — inflammation *increased* around the lesion. Woronoff's is inflammation *decreased*. A useful contrasting pair.

**⑦ Mixing up the psoriasis signs.**
* **Auspitz sign** — pinpoint bleeding *within* the plaque after scraping scale.
* **Koebner phenomenon** — *new* lesions at sites of trauma.
* **Woronoff ring** — pale halo *around* the plaque.
Three different signs, three different locations, three different mechanisms.

**⑧ Terminology housekeeping.**
It is **pruritus** (not "pruritis"); plaques **vary** (not "very") in size; painful cracks are **fissures** (not "fishers"); and the compound term is **parakeratosis**, one word — a hallmark of psoriasis that is conspicuously **absent** in the Woronoff ring.

## 10. The Core Idea in One Picture

If you remember nothing else, remember this:

> **A psoriatic plaque secretes a diffusible prostaglandin-synthesis inhibitor that seeps outward into the adjacent skin. In that narrow collar, prostaglandins cannot be made — so the vessels do not dilate, inflammation cannot ignite, and melanin production falls. The result is a pale, hypopigmented, histologically near-normal halo hugging the plaque: the halo of Woronoff. Inject PGE₂ and the redness returns; shine UV on it and no redness appears. The ring is not a scar and not a drug artifact — it is the visible footprint of the plaque's own anti-inflammatory exhaust.**

**The one-line mnemonic:** *The plaque builds its own moat.*

## 11. Examination-Ready Short Note (model answer structure)

Use this skeleton under time pressure — roughly 6–8 marks' worth:

1. **Context (1 line):** Plaque psoriasis = sharply demarcated erythematous plaques with silvery scale; Auspitz sign and Koebner phenomenon positive.
2. **Definition:** Halo of Woronoff = pale, hypopigmented ring of apparently healthy skin surrounding a psoriatic plaque; described by **Woronoff, 1926** (originally with chrysarobin); seen in **both treated and untreated** psoriasis.
3. **Pathogenesis:** Exact mechanism unclear; leading hypothesis is a **diffusible prostaglandin-synthesis inhibitor** released from the plaque into perilesional skin → suppressed prostaglandin synthesis → loss of vasodilatation (pallor) + prevention of inflammation + reduced melanogenesis. Draw the 3-step flowchart.
4. **Supporting evidence:** (a) **Intradermal PGE₂ restores erythema** in the blanched ring; (b) the ring **fails to develop erythema during phototherapy**, and UV erythema is prostaglandin-mediated.
5. **Histopathology:** minimal acanthosis; reduced melanin; **absence of parakeratosis**; decreased **endoglin (CD105)**, a TGF-β–complex glycoprotein modulating angiogenesis and inflammation.
6. **Diagram:** three concentric zones — plaque → Woronoff ring (hypopigmented) → normal skin, with an arrow for centrifugal diffusion.
7. **Significance:** seen after **UV, topical corticosteroids, coal tar, dithranol**; indicates **local reduction of inflammation**; a marker of lesional quiescence; studying it may yield insights into treating psoriasis and other inflammatory disorders.
8. **Also described (rarely) in:** lichen planus, granuloma annulare, pityriasis rosea, eczemas, secondary syphilis, molluscum contagiosum, **Sézary syndrome** — suggesting a general mechanism of perilesional immunosuppression.

### A closing thought

There is a lovely lesson buried in this small sign. For a century, dermatologists have known that psoriatic skin manufactures a potent local anti-inflammatory substance — we can *see* its effect with the naked eye, in every clinic, on countless patients — and yet we have never fully identified it. The Woronoff ring is a reminder that the skin is not merely a canvas on which disease is painted; it is a **chemically communicating organ**, and sometimes it writes its own prescriptions in the margins.
