# The Isomorphic (Koebner) Response — A Mini‑Lecture

---

## 1. Let's start with the big picture: why this phenomenon matters at all

Before we dissect anything, I want you to appreciate *why dermatologists care so much about a bruise or a scratch*.

Most of medicine assumes that disease lives where disease lives. A psoriatic plaque is on the elbow; the forearm next to it looks normal, so we call it "uninvolved skin." The Koebner phenomenon demolishes that comfortable assumption. It tells us something profound:

> **In these diseases, the "normal-looking" skin is not normal at all. It is *primed*. It is loaded gun-skin. Trauma is merely the trigger that pulls.**

That single insight reorganises how we think about psoriasis, lichen planus and vitiligo. These are not focal skin diseases — they are **systemic immunological states expressed focally**, and the site of expression is decided largely by where the skin gets injured. The lesion tells you *where*; the immune system decides *whether*.

This has three practical consequences we will build toward:
1. It is a **diagnostic clue** (seeing it supports psoriasis, LP, vitiligo).
2. It is an **activity marker** (if skin Koebnerizes, the disease is unstable *right now*).
3. It is a **therapeutic warning** (don't operate, graft, tattoo, scrub or burn this patient today).

So keep this frame in mind: *Koebnerization is the re-ignition of the patient's own disease cascade, at a new address, using trauma as the match.*

---

## 2. The name and the words — decode them and you've half-learned the concept

**Heinrich Koebner (1838–1904)**, a German dermatologist, reported in **1876** that psoriatic patients grew *new psoriasis* precisely where their skin had been injured — his famous examples being **excoriations (scratch marks), horse bites, and tattoos**. (Yes — *horse bites*. Nineteenth-century dermatology had a different set of occupational hazards. Examiners love this detail, so keep the triad: **excoriation, horse bite, tattoo**.)

Now the term **isomorphic**:
- *iso-* = same
- *-morph* = form

**Isomorphic response = "the same-form response."** The new lesion arising in the traumatised skin is not some vague inflammatory blotch — it is a **carbon copy** of the patient's existing disease, both **clinically** (same colour, scale, border, morphology) and **histopathologically** (same microscopic architecture).

Hold on to the word "same," because when we later contrast this with **isotopic** ("same *place*") and **pathergy** ("exaggerated *suffering* from injury"), the etymology will keep you from mixing them up.

---

## 3. The formal definition, unpacked clause by clause

> **The Koebner (isomorphic) phenomenon is the development of isomorphic pathological lesions in *traumatised uninvolved skin* of a patient with an existing cutaneous disease, such that the new lesions are *clinically and histopathologically identical* to the lesions of the diseased skin.**

Let's take that apart, because every phrase is doing work:

| Clause | What it means | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| "**in a patient with an existing cutaneous disease**" | The patient must already *have* the disease (or the predisposition) | Trauma does not *create* psoriasis in a normal person. It **reveals** it. Trauma is permissive, not causative. |
| "**traumatised uninvolved skin**" | The lesion appears in skin that looked clinically normal before injury | This is the surprising part — the disease "spreads" into apparently healthy territory |
| "**isomorphic … clinically and histopathologically identical**" | Same disease, not a nonspecific scar or reactive change | Distinguishes a true Koebner from ordinary post-traumatic inflammation, keloid, or pigmentary change |

**Analogy I want you to carry:** Think of the patient's skin as a **field soaked in petrol** (the systemic immune predisposition). It looks like an ordinary field. A struck match (trauma) anywhere on that field produces a fire *of the same kind* — and the fire is identical wherever it starts, because the fuel is identical everywhere. In a person without the petrol, the same match produces only a small scorch mark that heals.

### A practical footnote: what counts as "enough" trauma, and how long does it take?

Two features you should be able to state:
- **Depth matters.** Superficial insults that don't breach the epidermis often fail; injury that reaches at least the **papillary dermis** (tape-stripping to glistening, curettage, needle prick, surgical incision, burn) reliably works in responders. *More severe injury → more extensive lesion.*
- **Time matters.** The Koebner lesion is **not immediate**. There is a latent period — typically about **10–20 days** (roughly 1–3 weeks) between injury and the appearance of the isomorphic lesion. This delay is itself a clue to mechanism: it is the time required to recruit, activate and expand an **adaptive** (T-cell) response, not just an innate one. A reaction appearing in minutes to hours is *not* Koebner — that's urticaria/dermographism or pathergy.

## 4. The classification — the Boyd & Nelder (1990) four tiers

This four-tier scheme is the single most examinable part of the topic. Notice that the tiers are graded by **reproducibility**, moving from "always" → "mimic" → "sometimes" → "doubtful."

### Tier 1 — **True isomorphic phenomenon (true Koebner's)**
Diseases that **reproducibly** Koebnerize.

- **Psoriasis**
- **Lichen planus**
- **Vitiligo**

**This is your classic triad. Memorise it as a block.**

A beautiful teaching point buried here: **vitiligo is the only non-inflammatory, non-papulosquamous member** of the triad. Its "lesion" is *absence* of pigment, not presence of scale. That tells you the Koebner phenomenon is **not fundamentally about inflammation** — it is about **any primed immunological target being unmasked by injury**. In psoriasis the target is the keratinocyte compartment; in vitiligo it is the melanocyte.

### Tier 2 — **Pseudo-isomorphic phenomenon (the infectious mimics)**
Here trauma produces a lesion at the wound — but by a *completely different mechanism*: the **infectious agent is mechanically inoculated** into the breach. The immune priming of the host is irrelevant; what matters is that a virus fell into an open door.

- **Warts** (HPV — hence wart spread along shaving lines, in nail-biters, in swimmers' abrasions)
- **Molluscum contagiosum** (poxvirus — hence linear spread along scratch tracks)

*(Per the source, also listed here: **Behçet's disease** and **pyoderma gangrenosum** — see the important clarification below.)*

**Analogy:** True Koebner is *setting fire to a petrol-soaked field*. Pseudo-Koebner is *planting a seed in freshly tilled soil*. In one, the injury releases something already present; in the other, the injury lets something new in.

> ### ⚠️ An important clarification: pseudo-Koebner vs **pathergy**
>
> The source groups **Behçet's disease** and **pyoderma gangrenosum** under "pseudo-isomorphic phenomenon (infectious diseases)." This follows the **original Boyd–Nelder (1990) classification**, so it is defensible for the exam — but it needs qualifying, because **Behçet's and PG are not infectious**, and their mechanism is different again.
>
> What Behçet's and PG show is **pathergy**: an **exaggerated, disproportionate, neutrophil-driven inflammatory response** to minimal injury — a needle prick producing a sterile pustule (the Behçet's *pathergy test*), or a debridement producing a spreading necrotic ulcer (PG — the reason PG ulcers *worsen* with surgery).
>
> **How to answer in a viva:**
> - Asked about **pseudo-Koebner** → say **warts and molluscum** (direct inoculation), and then add: *"Behçet's and PG are grouped here in the original Boyd–Nelder classification, though they are more accurately examples of pathergy."*
> - Asked about **pathergy** → say **Behçet's disease and pyoderma gangrenosum** (also Sweet's syndrome, some neutrophilic dermatoses).
>
> That single sentence shows the examiner you know both the classification *and* its limitation. Rook's and Bolognia treat pathergy as a separate entity.

### Tier 3 — **Occasionally occurring isomorphic phenomenon**
Diseases that **sometimes** localise to trauma sites — real, but not reproducible in every patient.

- **Darier's disease** (keratosis follicularis)
- **Hailey–Hailey disease** (benign familial pemphigus)
- **Kyrle's disease**
- **Perforating folliculitis**
- **Erythema multiforme**
- **Pellagra**

Notice the internal logic: **Darier's and Hailey–Hailey** are **genetic acantholytic disorders** (ATP2A2 and ATP2C1 — calcium-pump defects). Their keratinocyte adhesion is *intrinsically fragile everywhere*; friction, heat and sweat simply expose that fragility where mechanical stress is highest — hence flexural and friction-site disease. That's a "mechanical unmasking of a genetic defect," which is conceptually close to Koebner but not immunological.

**Kyrle's disease and perforating folliculitis** belong to the **perforating dermatoses**, where scratching and transepidermal elimination of dermal material are integral to lesion formation — so trauma is almost part of the pathology itself.

**Pellagra** localises to **sun-exposed and friction/pressure sites** (Casal's necklace, dorsal hands) — here the "trauma" is UV and mechanical stress on a metabolically vulnerable epidermis.

*(For revision economy: Darier's, Hailey–Hailey and Kyrle's disease all reappear later in this chapter — cross-link them.)*

### Tier 4 — **Questionable isomorphic phenomenon**
Where the relationship to trauma is **debated or inconsistent**.

- **Anaphylactoid purpura** (Henoch–Schönlein)
- **Bullous pemphigoid (BP)**
- **Dermatitis herpetiformis (DH)**
- **Discoid lupus erythematosus (DLE)**
- **Eczema**
- **Lichen amyloidosis / lichen nitidus**
- **Pityriasis rubra pilaris**
- **Urticaria pigmentosa**
- **Morphea**

Two of these deserve comment:
- **Lichen nitidus** is historically interesting because **Koebner himself** described trauma-induced lesions in it. Many modern texts would place it higher than "questionable."
- **Morphea** at the foot of the list is a genuine point of controversy. There is now good evidence for **post-radiation morphea, post-surgical and post-injection morphea**, and several modern references **promote morphea from "questionable" to "occasional."** Flag this in an exam — it shows reading beyond the list.

Also note **urticaria pigmentosa**: stroking a lesion produces **Darier's sign** (whealing from mast-cell degranulation). That is a *pharmacological* response to trauma, not new isomorphic lesion formation — which is precisely why it sits in the "questionable" bin rather than the "true" bin.

## 5. What actually causes it — the three practical trigger categories

Clinically you need to be able to *counsel* a patient, so group triggers by what you can advise against:

**1. Mechanical and thermal trauma**
Excoriations and scratching (the commonest of all), friction, pressure, **surgical wounds and incisions**, burns, sunburn and **phototherapy overdose**, insect bites, venepuncture, adhesive tape stripping, tight clothing/belts, vigorous scrubbing, acupuncture needles, shaving.

**2. Concurrent dermatoses (the "disease-as-trauma" group)**
Furuncles, **carbuncles**, **dermatophytosis**, **scabies**, impetigo — anything that damages the epidermis or causes intense itching. Here the mechanism is often double: direct epidermal injury **plus** the itch–scratch cycle.

> **This is why point 3 of "clinical significance" says *treat the underlying concurrent disease*.** If a psoriatic patient has scabies, you cannot control the psoriasis while the patient excoriates himself nightly. **Break the itch, and you break the Koebner cycle.** Treating the scabies is anti-psoriatic therapy.

**3. Allergic and irritant reactions**
**BCG vaccination**, tattoos, hair dyes/tints, hairspray, topical irritants, contact allergens, harsh keratolytics.

> **The BCG point is not trivia — it's regionally vital.** In countries with universal BCG programmes, the vaccination site is a reliable spot for a psoriasiform flare in predisposed children. Similarly, **tattoos** are a modern, high-frequency, entirely avoidable trigger — and worth mentioning in counselling any young patient with psoriasis, LP or vitiligo.

## 6. Pathogenesis — the mechanism, step by step

Here is where we earn our marks. The old view was "trauma somehow spreads psoriasis." The modern view is precise and immunological, and it turns out Koebnerization is simply **psoriasis pathogenesis in fast-forward, with a defined start time**. That is why the Koebner model is such a beloved *experimental tool*: it lets researchers watch a psoriatic plaque assemble from day zero.

Some orienting numbers first:
- In psoriasis, the Koebner response is positive in **11–75%** of patients across studies — most clinical series cluster around **24–51%**.
- Why such a huge range? Because **it depends entirely on whether the patient's disease is active and unstable at the moment of testing.** The variable is not the stimulus — it's the patient's immunological weather that week. (Remember this; it explains the all-or-none phenomenon below.)

### The central idea in one sentence

> **A wound behaves like a fake infection. Injured skin releases antimicrobial peptides and self-DNA/RNA; these combine into a complex that the innate immune system cannot distinguish from a virus; the resulting interferon alarm launches the full Th17 cascade — and psoriasis appears.**

### The molecular cascade

```
                        SKIN INJURY
                             │
                             ▼
      Antimicrobial peptide LL-37 (cathelicidin) released
      — from keratinocytes and infiltrating neutrophils
                             │
                             ▼
      LL-37 FORMS COMPLEXES with self nucleic acids
      (DNA and RNA) liberated from dying/damaged cells
                             │
                             ▼
      Complexes are transported into intracellular
      (endosomal) compartments of plasmacytoid
      dendritic cells (pDCs)
                             │
                             ▼
      Activation of pDCs via TLR9 (DNA complexes)
                      and TLR7 (RNA complexes)
                             │
                             ▼
              Massive release of IFN-α and IFN-β
                             │
                             ▼
       Maturation/triggering of (myeloid) dendritic cells
                             │
                             ▼
             Stimulation of AUTOREACTIVE T cells
                             │
                             ▼
                    Th17 cell generation
                             │
                             ▼
                      IL-17  and  IL-22
                             │
        ┌────────────────────┼────────────────────┐
        ▼                    ▼                    ▼
 ↑ AMP expression    Inhibition of         Keratinocyte
 in keratinocytes    terminal              HYPERPROLIFERATION
        │            differentiation
        │
        └──────────── POSITIVE FEEDBACK ──────────┐
                  (more LL-37 → restart cascade)  │
                             ▲                    │
                             └────────────────────┘
```

Now let me narrate each step properly, because a flowchart memorised without mechanism evaporates in a viva.

**Step 1 — Injury breaches the barrier and kills cells.**
Two things are now loose in the tissue that should never be loose: (a) **antimicrobial peptides**, poured out by stressed keratinocytes and arriving neutrophils as an emergency antibacterial measure, and (b) **self nucleic acids**, spilled from the nuclei and cytoplasm of dying cells. Individually, neither is dangerous. Together, they are.

**Step 2 — LL-37 forms complexes with self-DNA and self-RNA.**
**LL-37 (cathelicidin)** is a short, strongly **cationic** (positively charged) peptide. Nucleic acids are strongly **anionic** (negatively charged). They therefore bind avidly by simple electrostatics, forming **condensed, aggregated, nuclease-resistant complexes**.

Two consequences:
- The nucleic acid is now **protected from degradation** by extracellular DNases/RNases (normally the body's way of silencing self-DNA).
- The complex is now **efficiently internalised** into pDC endosomes, because LL-37 acts as a molecular ferry.

> **Analogy:** Self-DNA alone is a **whisper in an empty room** — nobody hears it, and it's quickly swept away. LL-37 is a **megaphone strapped to the whisper and carried straight into the sentry's guardroom.** Same message, catastrophically amplified and delivered to exactly the wrong audience.

**Step 3 — TLR7 and TLR9 in pDCs are fooled.**
**TLR9** sits inside endosomes and is designed to detect **viral/bacterial DNA**; **TLR7** detects **single-stranded viral RNA**. Crucially, these receptors are *endosomal* precisely so that they only meet nucleic acid that has been *swallowed* — a design that normally guarantees "if I'm seeing DNA in here, it must be a pathogen I ingested."

LL-37 subverts that safeguard. It delivers **self** nucleic acid into the endosome. TLR7/TLR9 cannot tell self from viral sequence — they only check *location and structure*. So the pDC concludes, with complete conviction and complete inaccuracy: **"We are being invaded by a virus."**

**This step is the conceptual heart of the whole topic: Koebnerization is a case of mistaken identity — sterile injury misread as viral infection.**

**Step 4 — Type I interferons are released (IFN-α, IFN-β).**
pDCs are the body's **professional interferon factories** — a single pDC can make 100–1000× more type I IFN than any other cell. The wound is now flooded with the cytokines of antiviral warfare.

Type I IFN does three relevant things:
- **Matures myeloid dendritic cells** into potent antigen-presenting cells.
- Upregulates **MHC and costimulatory molecules**, lowering the threshold for T-cell activation.
- Creates an inflammatory, chemokine-rich milieu that recruits more leukocytes.

**Step 5 — Dendritic cells activate autoreactive T cells.**
Now the mature myeloid DCs present antigen to T cells — and in a predisposed host (think **HLA-Cw6**), the T cells that respond are **autoreactive**: they recognise self-antigens of the skin. (Recognised candidate autoantigens in psoriasis include **LL-37 itself** and **ADAMTSL5** from melanocytes — a delightful irony: the alarm peptide becomes the target.)

This is the step where the response becomes **adaptive, specific and self-sustaining**, and it is the reason for the **1–3 week latency**: clonal expansion takes time.

**Step 6 — Th17 differentiation.**
Driven by DC-derived **IL-23** (with IL-1β, IL-6, TGF-β), naïve T cells commit to the **Th17** lineage. This is the famous **IL-23/Th17 axis** — the axis targeted by ustekinumab, guselkumab, risankizumab, secukinumab, ixekizumab.

**Step 7 — IL-17 and IL-22 act on the keratinocyte.**
Now the effector cytokines hit their target cell, and produce the **three hallmark effects**:

| Cytokine effect | Cellular consequence | What you see clinically / histologically |
|---|---|---|
| **↑ AMP expression** (LL-37, β-defensins, S100 proteins, lipocalin-2) plus **↑ chemokines (CXCL1/CXCL8)** | Neutrophil recruitment; more LL-37 available | Neutrophil microabscesses (Munro's in the stratum corneum, spongiform pustule of Kogoj); intense erythema |
| **Inhibition of terminal differentiation** (largely IL-22 via STAT3; loss of keratin 1/10, filaggrin, loricrin) | Corneocytes formed incompletely, retain nuclei | **Parakeratosis**, loss of granular layer (hypogranulosis), **silvery scale** |
| **Hyperproliferation** of keratinocytes | Massive increase in basal mitoses, shortened transit time | **Acanthosis with regular elongation of rete ridges**, thinned suprapapillary plates, **Auspitz sign** when scale is lifted (dilated papillary vessels bleed) |

### Step 8 — The positive feedback loop: why the lesion doesn't just heal

This is the elegant, examinable finish. Look at the first row of that table: **IL-17/IL-22 induce keratinocytes to make MORE antimicrobial peptides, including LL-37.**

But LL-37 was **step 2** of the cascade.

So the output of the pathway is also its own input:

```
 LL-37 + self-nucleic acid → pDC (TLR7/9) → IFN-α/β → DC → Th17
        ↑                                                    │
        └──────────── IL-17 / IL-22 induce more AMPs ────────┘
```

> **This closed loop is the answer to "why doesn't the Koebner lesion resolve like an ordinary wound?"** An ordinary wound's inflammation is self-limiting because the stimulus (bacteria, debris) is cleared. Here, the *response manufactures the stimulus*. There is nothing to clear. The loop is self-sustaining, which is why Koebnerized lesions **persist and expand centrifugally** into surrounding skin long after the wound itself has healed.

**Analogy:** A **fire alarm whose siren is loud enough to trigger the neighbouring alarm** — and that one triggers the first one back. The original smoke is long gone; the building is still screaming.

### Two mechanisms worth adding for completeness

**(a) The neurogenic contribution.** Injured skin also means injured nerve endings. Cutaneous nerves release **substance P** and **CGRP**, and psoriatic keratinocytes upregulate **nerve growth factor (NGF)**, causing nerve fibre proliferation in developing plaques. These neuropeptides are pro-inflammatory, degranulate mast cells, and upregulate endothelial adhesion molecules. This helps explain the classic clinical observations that **psoriasis can clear in a denervated limb** and that **emotional stress** provokes flares — the nervous system is a genuine participant, not a bystander. Trauma also upregulates **ICAM-1** on keratinocytes and **tenascin** in the dermis, which help the incoming lymphocytes stick and stay.

**(b) Disease-specific variations — because the mechanism is not identical in all three "true" diseases.**

- **Psoriasis** — as described above: innate alarm → IL-23/Th17 → keratinocyte hyperproliferation. Trauma provides the LL-37/self-nucleic-acid ignition.
- **Lichen planus** — a **CD8⁺ cytotoxic T-cell attack on basal keratinocytes**. Trauma is thought to alter/expose basal-cell antigens and upregulate adhesion molecules, drawing the primed cytotoxic infiltrate to a new site. Clinically this is the **beautifully linear row of violaceous papules along a scratch mark** — arguably the most visually striking Koebner of all, and the classic bedside demonstration.
- **Vitiligo** — the target is the **melanocyte**, which in vitiligo is intrinsically fragile and poorly adherent (**Gauthier's "melanocytorrhagy" hypothesis**: friction causes melanocytes to detach and be lost transepidermally). Add stress-induced **HSP70** release, which activates dendritic cells and amplifies anti-melanocyte **CD8⁺** autoimmunity. This elegantly explains why vitiligo favours **bony prominences, elbows, knees, knuckles, belt-line and bra-strap areas** — the body's high-friction map.

## 7. The "zone of local immunity" — a fascinating exception

Here is an observation that seems paradoxical until you think about it:

> **The involuted central portion of an established psoriatic plaque is *refractory* to experimental induction of psoriasis. You cannot Koebnerize the middle of an old plaque.**

Injure the centre of a long-standing, resolving plaque, and you do **not** get a new lesion there. The centre behaves as an immunologically **"spent" or protected** zone — hence "zone of local immunity."

**Why does this matter conceptually?** Because it proves that Koebner susceptibility is **not uniformly distributed** across the skin. There is a local, microenvironmental layer of control on top of the systemic predisposition. The plaque centre has presumably acquired an **immunosuppressive/resolution microenvironment** — plausibly **regulatory T cells**, pro-resolution lipid mediators, local exhaustion of the pDC/DC compartment, or altered tissue-resident memory T-cell distribution.

Clinically, this is also the mechanistic explanation for the familiar pattern of **central clearing with active advancing margins** in annular psoriasis: the edge is Koebner-competent, the middle is not.

**Why does it matter methodologically?** It is exactly why all experimental Koebner studies must use **uninvolved skin** for testing — testing on an old plaque would give a false negative every time.

> **Analogy:** A forest that has already burned cannot burn again — the fuel is consumed and the ground is changed. The fire only advances at the **unburned edge**.

## 8. The "all-or-none" phenomenon — arguably the most conceptually important point

Experimental studies (Eyre & Krueger) tested multiple different stimuli on the same patient — tape-stripping, curettage, injections, chemicals — and found something striking:

> **If a patient responds to *one* experimental stimulus, he responds to *all* of them. If he fails to respond to a known Koebner-inducing stimulus, that failure predicts failure with every other stimulus in the series.**

### What this means (state this in the exam — it is the punchline)

> **Koebner positivity is a property of the PATIENT'S immunological state at that moment — not a property of the stimulus.**

The trauma is merely a **non-specific trigger**; it carries no information. The decision about whether a plaque appears is made centrally, by the patient's current systemic immune activity, not locally by the type of injury.

Practical corollaries:
- A patient in an **active, unstable phase** is a "Koebner responder" — *everything* will provoke him: the surgeon's scalpel, the tattooist's needle, the venepuncture, the sunburn, his own fingernails.
- A patient in a **stable/remitting phase** may be a non-responder — and the same insults pass without consequence.
- **The same patient can switch categories over time**, which is precisely why the reported response rate ranges so widely (**11–75%**). The studies weren't disagreeing; they were sampling patients in different phases.

> **Analogy:** It's not about *what kind of match* you strike. It's about whether the field is soaked in petrol today. Any match will do when it is; no match works when it isn't.

## 9. Reverse Koebner — the mirror image (the "Renbök phenomenon")

Nature, being contrary, also provides the opposite:

> **Reverse Koebner** = **existing disease *clears* at the site of injury.**

The playful synonym is the **Renbök phenomenon** — *Koebner* spelled backwards. (Coined originally in the setting of alopecia areata and psoriasis interacting: hair regrowing within psoriatic plaques on an alopecic scalp.)

Two examples you must be able to quote:

1. **Psoriasis:** a psoriatic plaque **clears** at a site of injury — classically along a **surgical scar** running through a plaque, or at sites of dermabrasion, burn, or excision. Mechanistically, the wound-healing programme (TGF-β–rich, fibrotic, with altered keratinocyte differentiation, destruction of the resident memory T-cell population and remodelled vasculature) **overwrites** the psoriatic programme locally.

2. **Vitiligo:** **spontaneous repigmentation of vitiligo patches at sites distant from an autologous skin graft.** This is remarkable — a graft placed here causes repigmentation *over there*. The implication is that the grafting procedure produces a **systemic immunomodulatory effect**, presumably shifting the anti-melanocyte autoimmune response, perhaps via release of melanocyte growth factors or induction of tolerance.

> **Therapeutic pay-off:** this observation underpins and encourages **autologous melanocyte transplantation** techniques (split-thickness grafting, suction blister grafting, non-cultured epidermal cell suspension), where the benefit may extend beyond the grafted patch itself.

**The deep lesson:** trauma is not intrinsically "pro-disease." Trauma **resets** the local immunological programme. Whether that reset yields disease (Koebner) or clearance (Renbök) depends on the disease, the site and the patient's state. Trauma is a *switch*, not a *direction*.

## 10. The confusable cousins — please get these straight

This is where most candidates lose marks. Build the table once and never mix them again.

| Phenomenon | Meaning of the name | What happens | Classic examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Isomorphic (Koebner)** | "same **form**" | Traumatised *uninvolved* skin develops **the same disease** the patient already has | **Psoriasis, lichen planus, vitiligo** |
| **Pseudo-Koebner** | looks like Koebner | Lesion at trauma site because the **organism is inoculated** into the wound | **Warts, molluscum contagiosum** |
| **Pathergy** | "suffering from injury" | **Exaggerated, disproportionate** (often pustular/ulcerative, neutrophilic) response to minimal trauma | **Behçet's disease** (pathergy test), **pyoderma gangrenosum**, Sweet's syndrome |
| **Isotopic (Wolf's) response** | "same **place**" | A **NEW, DIFFERENT** disease arises at the site of an **already-healed**, unrelated dermatosis | **Granuloma annulare or other eruption in a healed herpes zoster scar** |
| **Reverse Koebner (Renbök)** | Koebner backwards | Existing disease **clears** at the injury site | Psoriasis clearing in a scar; vitiligo repigmenting after grafting |
| **Locus minoris resistentiae** | "place of least resistance" | Generic term: a site of pre-existing altered tissue is preferentially targeted by any later process | Umbrella concept covering several of the above |

**Memory hook:**
- **Iso*morph*ic = same *form*** (same disease, new site).
- **Iso*topic* = same *topos* (place)** — new disease, old site. *"Topographic = place."*
- **Path*ergy* = an *over*-reaction** — think "allergy-like word, exaggerated response."

## 11. Clinical significance — the three reasons this earns its place in the textbook

### 1. Diagnostic value
The presence of Koebnerization — lesions in a **linear, geometric, or otherwise clearly traumatic distribution** — is a strong supportive clue for **psoriasis, lichen planus and vitiligo**. A row of violaceous flat-topped papules along a scratch line is nearly pathognomonic of LP. Psoriasis appearing exactly along a surgical scar, at a tattoo, or at a BCG site tells you a great deal. It also helps explain odd distributions that might otherwise confuse you (why is the psoriasis only along the belt line? Because that's where the friction is).

### 2. Marker of disease activity
> **Active Koebnerization = active, unstable disease.**

This is a bedside activity index that costs nothing. Combined with the all-or-none principle, it tells you the patient's immune system is currently "hot," and therefore:
- expect further spread,
- expect poorer tolerance of provocative therapy,
- consider systemic rather than purely topical control.

In **vitiligo**, Koebnerization at friction sites is one of the accepted signs of **unstable/progressive disease** — and stability is the key determinant of whether surgical therapy is appropriate.

### 3. It guides management — practically and specifically

**(a) Avoid physical and chemical irritants during active disease.** This is real, actionable counselling:
- **Defer elective surgery and cosmetic procedures.** A patient in an active Koebner-positive phase may develop psoriasis in the surgical wound. If surgery is unavoidable, control the disease first.
- **In unstable vitiligo, do not graft.** Not only may the recipient site fail — the **donor site itself may depigment**. Stability (typically 6–12 months of no new/expanding lesions and no Koebner) is a prerequisite for surgical vitiligo therapy.
- **In pyoderma gangrenosum, do not debride or graft** an active ulcer — pathergy will enlarge it. Immunosuppress first.
- **No tattoos.** Counsel young patients explicitly.
- **Avoid aggressive topicals**, high-concentration keratolytics on normal skin, harsh scrubbing, loofahs, and vigorous descaling.
- **Avoid phototherapy overdose** — a phototherapy burn is a trauma and can Koebnerize; hence careful MED-based dosing.
- Advise **loose clothing**, avoidance of tight belts/straps in vitiligo, gentle emollient-based skin care, and **nail care/antipruritics to break the itch–scratch cycle** (the single commonest Koebner trigger of all).

**(b) Treat concurrent disease.** Aggressively treat **scabies, dermatophytosis, furunculosis, eczema** — any condition that damages skin or drives scratching. In this framework, **an antiscabietic or an antifungal is an antipsoriatic drug**, because it removes the trauma engine.

## 12. Common misunderstandings — let me pre-empt them

1. **"Trauma causes psoriasis."** ❌ No. Trauma **localises** psoriasis in someone who already has the predisposition. A normal person who is scratched gets a scratch.

2. **"Koebner means the disease spreads by contact/contagion."** ❌ No. Nothing is transmitted in true Koebner. (That *is* essentially what happens in **pseudo**-Koebner, which is why keeping the two apart matters.)

3. **"A reaction at the injury site is Koebner."** ❌ Only if it is **isomorphic** — clinically *and* histologically the same disease. Ordinary post-traumatic erythema, post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, keloid, or a pustule is not Koebner.

4. **"It happens immediately."** ❌ There's a **1–3 week latency**. Immediate responses are dermographism, urticaria or pathergy.

5. **"A negative Koebner rules out psoriasis."** ❌ Absolutely not. Only some **11–75%** respond, and the same patient can be negative today and positive in three months. **Negative Koebner excludes nothing; positive Koebner is informative.**

6. **"Behçet's pathergy test is a Koebner phenomenon."** ❌ Different mechanism (exaggerated neutrophilic reaction, not reproduction of an existing dermatosis). Know that Boyd–Nelder grouped it with pseudo-Koebner, but be able to say *why* that grouping is criticised.

7. **"You can test for Koebner by scratching a plaque."** ❌ Test **uninvolved** skin. Plaque centres are refractory (zone of local immunity) — you'd get a meaningless negative.

8. **"The type of trauma determines whether you get a lesion."** ❌ The **all-or-none phenomenon** says otherwise. Severity of injury affects **how extensive** the resulting lesion is, but **whether** you respond at all is decided by the patient's state.

9. **"Isomorphic and isotopic are interchangeable."** ❌ Same **form** vs same **place**. Koebner vs Wolf.

## 13. The core idea in one picture

Hold this single image and the whole answer reconstructs itself:

> **The patient's "normal" skin is a petrol-soaked field (systemic immune priming, e.g. HLA-Cw6 psoriasis, primed cytotoxic T cells in LP, fragile melanocytes in vitiligo).**
>
> **Trauma is a struck match — any match will do (all-or-none).**
>
> **The match works because injury makes a wound look like a virus: LL-37 grabs spilled self-DNA/RNA, smuggles it into pDC endosomes, and TLR7/TLR9 sound a false antiviral alarm → IFN-α/β → dendritic cells → autoreactive T cells → Th17 → IL-17/IL-22 → hyperproliferating, poorly differentiating keratinocytes = a new plaque, identical to the old ones.**
>
> **The fire won't go out, because IL-17/IL-22 make more LL-37 — the response manufactures its own trigger (positive feedback).**
>
> **Where the field has already burned (the involuted plaque centre), no new fire starts (zone of local immunity). And occasionally, injury doesn't ignite but instead firebreaks the field, clearing disease (reverse Koebner / Renbök).**
>
> **Clinically, therefore: Koebner seen = diagnosis supported + disease is active + hands off the skin (no surgery, no grafts, no tattoos, no scrubbing) until you have treated the disease — and the concurrent itchy dermatosis driving the scratching.**

## 14. Rapid-fire viva pearls

| Question | One-line answer |
|---|---|
| Who and when? | **Heinrich Koebner, 1876**, in psoriasis |
| His three classic triggers? | **Excoriations, horse bites, tattoos** |
| Meaning of "isomorphic"? | Same **form** — new lesion identical clinically **and histologically** |
| Classification used? | **Boyd & Nelder (1990)** — true / pseudo / occasional / questionable |
| The "true Koebner" triad? | **Psoriasis, lichen planus, vitiligo** |
| Which of the triad is non-inflammatory? | **Vitiligo** |
| Pseudo-Koebner examples & why? | **Warts, molluscum** — direct inoculation of the agent |
| Pathergy examples? | **Behçet's disease, pyoderma gangrenosum** (± Sweet's) |
| Latency period? | About **10–20 days** (1–3 weeks) |
| Frequency in psoriasis? | **11–75%** (most series 24–51%); depends on disease activity |
| Key innate trigger molecule? | **LL-37 (cathelicidin)** complexed with self-DNA/RNA |
| Receptors on pDCs? | **TLR9** (DNA) and **TLR7** (RNA) |
| Key cytokine axis? | **IFN-α/β → IL-23 → Th17 → IL-17/IL-22** |
| Why self-sustaining? | IL-17/IL-22 **induce more AMPs**, restarting the loop |
| Zone of local immunity? | **Involuted centre of a plaque is refractory** to Koebnerization |
| All-or-none means? | Reflects the **patient's systemic immune state**, not the stimulus type |
| Reverse Koebner synonym? | **Renbök phenomenon** |
| Reverse Koebner examples? | Psoriasis **clearing** in a scar; vitiligo **repigmenting distant** to an autologous graft |
| Wolf's isotopic response? | **New, different** disease at the site of a **healed** dermatosis (e.g. GA in a zoster scar) |
