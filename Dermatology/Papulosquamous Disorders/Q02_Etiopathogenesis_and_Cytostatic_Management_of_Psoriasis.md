# 🎓 Mini-Lecture: Psoriasis — Etiopathogenesis & Cytostatic Management

*(Chapter 10: Disorders of Keratinization | Rook's • Bolognia • Andrews')*

---

## PART 0 — The Big Picture: Why This Question Is Really *Two* Questions

Before we dive in, let me frame what the examiner is actually asking, because this question has a hidden structure.

**"Etiopathogenesis"** = *Why does this disease happen, and by what molecular chain of events?* It splits into:
- **Etiology** → the *causes*: genes (the loaded gun) + environmental triggers (the finger on the trigger)
- **Pathogenesis** → the *mechanism*: the immune cascade that converts a trigger into a red scaly plaque

**"Cytostatic management"** = a deliberately narrow, old-school pharmacological term. The examiner is *not* asking for the whole treatment ladder. They want the **antiproliferative systemic drugs** — above all **methotrexate** and **cyclosporine** — and they want you to explain *why slowing down cell division helps a disease of runaway cell division and inflammation.*

And here is the sentence that ties the whole answer together — memorise this as your thesis statement:

> **Psoriasis is not a disease of the skin that became inflamed; it is a disease of the immune system that happens to be read out through the skin. The keratinocyte hyperproliferation is the *consequence*, not the cause — and that single insight explains every single drug we use.**

Hold that thought. It will make the pharmacology feel inevitable rather than memorised.

---

## PART 1 — DEFINITION & SCALE OF THE PROBLEM

**Psoriasis is an immune-mediated, polygenic, chronic inflammatory disorder of the skin**, elicited by environmental triggers in genetically predisposed individuals, characterised by **sharply demarcated, erythematous plaques surmounted by silvery-white scale**, of highly variable extent and severity.

Let me unpack every word of that definition, because each word is a clue to the pathogenesis:

| Word in the definition | What it tells you about the mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Immune-mediated** | T cells and dendritic cells are the drivers; this is why immunosuppressants and biologics work |
| **Polygenic** | Many genes each contribute a small risk (PSORS1–9, ERAP1, IL23R, CARD14) — no single-gene inheritance pattern |
| **Chronic** | Self-amplifying feedback loops + tissue-resident memory T cells → it does not "burn out" |
| **Sharply demarcated** | The inflammatory process has a hard border — unlike eczema, which fades imperceptibly into normal skin. A clinical fingerprint of a *localised, self-sustaining* immune circuit |
| **Erythematous** | VEGF-driven angiogenesis → dilated, tortuous dermal papillary capillaries |
| **Silvery-white scale** | Massively accelerated, *incomplete* keratinocyte maturation → parakeratosis with air trapped between loose corneocyte layers (air + light scatter = silver) |

**Epidemiological burden:** affects roughly **2% of the world population** (regional range ~0.5–4%). That is not a rare disease — in a busy dermatology OPD it is one of the top three diagnoses, and because of its systemic comorbidities it is a *cardiovascular* disease as much as a *cutaneous* one.

## PART 2 — ETIOLOGY, PART A: THE GENETIC SUBSTRATE ("The Loaded Gun")

### 2.1 Age of Onset Is **Bimodal** — And That Is a Genetic Clue

Psoriasis has **two age peaks**, and this is not a statistical accident. It reflects **two genetically distinct diseases wearing the same clinical clothes.**

| Feature | **Type 1 (Early onset)** | **Type 2 (Late onset)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Peak age** | **16–22 years** | **57–62 years** |
| Family history | Strongly positive | Usually negative/sporadic |
| **HLA-Cw6** | **Strongly positive** | Usually negative |
| Course | More extensive, more relapsing, more severe | Milder, more stable |
| Classic associations | Guttate psoriasis, strep-triggered flares | Nail and joint involvement relatively more prominent |

> ⚠️ **Important source correction (examiners love this):** The source page places "HLA-C:0602 → **Type II** psoriasis and guttate psoriasis." This is a **typographical/conceptual error.** Per **Rook's (10th ed.)** and **Bolognia (4th ed.)**, **HLA-C\*06:02 (HLA-Cw0602 / Cw6)** is most strongly associated with **Type 1 = EARLY-onset** psoriasis and with **guttate psoriasis**. The handwritten margin note on the same page — *"HLA Cw6: early age of onset (Type 1)"* — is the correct version. **Write Type 1 in your exam.**

**Sex distribution:** **Male = Female** (no significant sex predilection). Onset tends to be slightly earlier in females.

### 2.2 The Genetics — Explained So It Actually Makes Sense

**PSORS1 (Psoriasis susceptibility locus 1)** — chromosome **6p21.3**, within the MHC region. This is the **single strongest genetic determinant**, accounting for ~35–50% of the heritability. Sitting inside PSORS1 are three candidate genes: **HLA-C**, **CCHCR1**, and **CDSN (corneodesmosin)** — and the winning candidate is **HLA-C**, specifically the allele **HLA-C\*06:02**.

> 📌 *This is exactly what the handwritten margin note means when it says "found on PSORS1 gene" beside HLA-Cw6 — HLA-Cw6 is not a separate finding from PSORS1; **it IS the PSORS1 signal.*** Don't list them as two unrelated bullet points; show that you know they are the same locus.

**PSORS2–PSORS9** — additional loci of smaller effect. Worth knowing one by name: **PSORS2 = CARD14** (chromosome 17q25), gain-of-function mutations in which cause constitutive **NF-κB activation in keratinocytes** → familial plaque psoriasis, generalised pustular psoriasis, and PRP-like presentations. (Bonus mark: **IL36RN** loss-of-function → **DITRA** → generalised pustular psoriasis.)

**ERAP1 (Endoplasmic Reticulum Aminopeptidase 1)** — and here is where the genetics becomes beautiful.

🔑 **Why HLA-C and ERAP1 in the same disease? Because they are two workers on the same assembly line.**

> **Analogy — the sandwich shop.** MHC class I molecules (like HLA-C) are *sandwich trays* that display peptide "fillings" to CD8⁺ T cells. **ERAP1 is the knife in the kitchen** that trims long protein fragments down to exactly the 8–10 amino acid length that fits the tray. If you inherit an *unusual tray* (HLA-C\*06:02) **and** an *unusual knife* (a risk ERAP1 variant), you end up presenting an unusual self-peptide to CD8⁺ T cells — and autoreactivity begins.

This is a genuine **gene–gene (epistatic) interaction**: the ERAP1 risk effect is seen **only in individuals who carry HLA-C\*06:02.** This is the molecular reason we now believe psoriasis is, at its root, a **CD8⁺ T-cell-mediated autoimmune response against a keratinocyte self-antigen** — the leading candidate antigens being **ADAMTSL5** (a melanocyte antigen presented by HLA-C\*06:02), **keratin 17**, and **LL37 itself**.

## PART 3 — ETIOLOGY, PART B: TRIGGERS ("The Finger on the Trigger")

A trigger is anything that delivers a **danger signal** to the innate immune system of predisposed skin. Never just list them — always give the mechanism.

| Trigger | Mechanism (say this, don't just name it) |
|---|---|
| **Streptococcal infection** (β-haemolytic strep, pharyngitis/tonsillitis) | **Molecular mimicry** — streptococcal **M protein** shares sequence homology with **keratin 17**; strep superantigens also cause massive non-specific T-cell activation. Classically → **guttate psoriasis** 1–3 weeks post-sore throat, especially in HLA-Cw6⁺ children/young adults |
| **COVID-19 & other respiratory infections** | Viral RNA → TLR7/8 on pDCs → large **type I interferon (IFN-α)** surge → new-onset or flare of psoriasis; also reported post-vaccination |
| **HIV** | The **paradox**: psoriasis *worsens* as CD4 count falls. Explanation: loss of CD4⁺ **regulatory T cells** removes the brakes, while a relative **expansion of CD8⁺ memory T cells** (the true effectors in the epidermis) drives disease. Severe/erythrodermic psoriasis in a young adult = **test for HIV** |
| **Drugs** — **L**ithium, **A**ntimalarials, **B**eta-blockers, **I**nterferon-α, **N**SAIDs, ACE inhibitors, CCBs, tetracyclines, **TNF-α inhibitors** | • **Lithium** — inhibits inositol monophosphatase & GSK-3β → ↓intracellular inositol → keratinocyte proliferation + enhanced neutrophil function (worst offender; may precipitate pustular disease)<br>• **Beta-blockers** — ↓ epidermal **cAMP** → cAMP normally restrains proliferation → hyperproliferation<br>• **Antimalarials** — inhibit **transglutaminase**, disturbing cornification<br>• **IFN-α** — directly supplies the initiating cytokine<br>• **TNF-α inhibitors = "paradoxical psoriasis"** — blocking TNF removes TNF's physiological suppression of pDCs → **unopposed IFN-α** production → new psoriasiform eruption (classically palmoplantar pustular) |
| **Alcohol** | Immune dysregulation + ↑ proinflammatory cytokines + poor adherence; also a relative **contraindication to methotrexate** — so this trigger directly constrains your therapy |
| **Smoking** | Nicotine activates pDCs, ↑oxidative stress, ↑Th17 polarisation. Strongest association with **palmoplantar pustulosis** |
| **Stress** | The **neuro-immuno-cutaneous axis** — cutaneous nerves release **substance P**, **CGRP**, **NGF** → mast cell degranulation, keratinocyte activation. Explains flares before exams and weddings alike |
| **Sunlight** | UV helps **~80–90%** of patients (it is a therapy!) — but **triggers or worsens ~5–20%** (*photosensitive psoriasis*, often with a **Koebner response in sunburnt skin**). Do not present sunlight as purely beneficial |
| **Physical trauma → KOEBNER (isomorphic) PHENOMENON** | New psoriatic lesions arising **at sites of injury** (scratches, surgical scars, BCG sites, tattoos, belt lines). Mechanism: trauma → keratinocyte damage → release of **LL37 + self-DNA** and **NGF/substance P** → local pDC activation → new plaque in 10–20 days. This is a *marker of unstable/active disease* and has huge practical consequences (see Part 6) |
| **Hypocalcaemia** (and hypoparathyroidism) | **Calcium is the master switch of keratinocyte differentiation** — the epidermal calcium gradient drives terminal maturation. Remove the gradient and keratinocytes proliferate without maturing → classically precipitates **generalised pustular psoriasis (von Zumbusch)**. Always check serum calcium in an acute pustular flare |

> 🚩 **Add the trigger the source omits but every examiner expects: abrupt withdrawal of systemic (or potent topical) corticosteroids → rebound, often pustular or erythrodermic psoriasis.** This is *the* reason systemic steroids are avoided in psoriasis.

## PART 4 — PATHOGENESIS: THE IMMUNE CASCADE, TOLD AS A STORY

This is the heart of the answer. Rather than reciting cytokines, I want you to see psoriasis as a **three-act play**: **Initiation → Amplification → Maintenance**, with the keratinocyte as the stage on which the drama is displayed.

> 🔁 **The one-line master mechanism:**
> **Trigger → innate danger signal (LL37 + self-nucleic acid) → pDC-derived IFN-α → myeloid DC maturation → IL-12/IL-23 → Th1/Th17/Th22 → IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-17, IL-22 → keratinocyte activation, hyperproliferation & chemokine output → recruitment of MORE Th17 cells and neutrophils → self-amplifying loop = chronic plaque.**

---

### ACT I — **INITIATION**: How a trigger becomes an immune reaction

**Step 1 — Tissue stress releases LL37.**
Injured or triggered keratinocytes release **LL37 (cathelicidin)**, an antimicrobial peptide, together with **self-DNA and self-RNA** liberated from dying cells.

**Step 2 — LL37 converts self-nucleic acid into an immune trigger.**
Normally, your own DNA floating in tissue is ignored. **LL37 is cationic; DNA is anionic.** They form a **condensed LL37–DNA complex** that is now able to enter the endosome of a plasmacytoid dendritic cell and engage **TLR9** (RNA complexes engage **TLR7/8**).

> **Analogy:** Self-DNA alone is like a letter with no stamp and no address — the postal system ignores it. **LL37 is the stamp and envelope.** Suddenly the immune system reads a letter it was never meant to see, and interprets "my own DNA outside the cell" as "viral infection."

**Step 3 — pDCs release a torrent of IFN-α.**
**Plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs)** are the professional type I interferon factories. Activated pDCs pour out **IFN-α** — and this is the **critical initiating cytokine of psoriasis** (which is exactly why injecting IFN-α as a drug, or blocking TNF and thereby disinhibiting pDCs, can *cause* psoriasis).

**Step 4 — IFN-α matures myeloid dendritic cells.**
IFN-α licenses **myeloid/dermal DCs** to mature into potently T-cell-stimulatory, proinflammatory cells. The source's note that DCs differentiate into proinflammatory forms producing **TNF-α and iNOS** refers to these — the so-called **TIP-DCs (TNF- and iNOS-producing dendritic cells)**, which are massively expanded in psoriatic plaques.

📌 **A crucial subtlety from the source:** *DCs are present in both involved AND uninvolved skin* — but in **lesional skin they are increased in number and enhanced in their ability to activate T cells.** This tells you that clinically normal skin in a psoriatic patient is **not immunologically normal** — it is *primed*. That is the immunological basis of the **Koebner phenomenon** and of relapse at the same anatomical sites.

---

### ACT II — **AMPLIFICATION**: The IL-23 / Th17 axis (the engine room)

Mature DCs migrate to lymph nodes and present antigen. They secrete two master polarising cytokines:

- **IL-12** → drives **Th1** differentiation → **IFN-γ**, TNF-α
- **IL-23** → drives **Th17** differentiation and survival → **IL-17A/IL-17F**, and **IL-22** (Th22)

**The IL-23/Th17 axis is the central pathogenic pathway of psoriasis.** Everything modern in psoriasis therapeutics (ustekinumab, guselkumab, risankizumab, secukinumab, ixekizumab, bimekizumab) is an attempt to cut this axis.

Now meet the effector cytokines and know exactly what *each* does — because "a cytokine soup" earns fewer marks than "a division of labour":

| Cytokine | Source | Precise action | What you see clinically |
|---|---|---|---|
| **IFN-α** | pDC | Initiates; matures mDC | The "spark" |
| **IFN-γ** | Th1, CD8⁺ | Activates endothelium & keratinocytes; ↑MHC & chemokines | Immune cell influx |
| **TNF-α** | T cells, DCs, keratinocytes | **The central amplifier.** Endothelial activation, adhesion molecules, synergy with IL-17, NF-κB activation | Erythema, systemic inflammation, comorbidities |
| **IL-23** | Myeloid DC, macrophages | Sustains and expands Th17 cells | The "upstream master switch" |
| **IL-17A/F** | **Th17, Tc17, γδ T cells, ILC3, mast cells, neutrophils** | Acts **on keratinocytes** → AMPs, chemokines, cytokines | Neutrophil recruitment, scale, sustained inflammation |
| **IL-22** | Th22, Th17 | Via **STAT3** → keratinocyte **hyperproliferation + blocked terminal differentiation** | **Acanthosis + parakeratosis** — the thick plaque itself |
| **VEGF** | Keratinocytes | Angiogenesis: dilated, tortuous, leaky dermal papillary capillaries | **Erythema + Auspitz sign** |

**The key synergy the source highlights — commit this to memory:**
> **IL-17 + TNF-α act synergistically** (not merely additively) on keratinocytes to drive proliferation and upregulate psoriasis-associated genes. This is why dual-pathway blockade is so effective, and why a single cytokine knocked out in isolation may be insufficient.

---

### ACT III — **MAINTENANCE**: The self-amplifying loop (why psoriasis is chronic)

Here the **keratinocyte stops being a victim and becomes a participant.** Activated by IL-17/IL-22/TNF-α, keratinocytes become **active immune cells** in their own right.

#### The Keratinocyte's Innate-Immune Toolkit

Keratinocytes express **Toll-like receptors** and secrete **IL-6, IL-8 (CXCL8), TNF-α**, plus a battery of **antimicrobial proteins (AMPs)**:

| AMP (with source corrections) | Role |
|---|---|
| **hBD-1** — human β-defensin 1 *(source writes "BLD-1" — non-standard/typo)* | Constitutive antimicrobial defence |
| **hBD-2** — human β-defensin 2 *(source writes "LBD2" — typo)* | **IL-17-inducible**; a biomarker of IL-17 pathway activity; also chemotactic |
| **SLPI** — secretory leukocyte protease inhibitor *(source writes "SLP-1")* | Antiprotease + antimicrobial; protects epidermis |
| **LL37 / cathelicidin** | Antimicrobial **AND** the initiator of Act I — the loop closes here |
| **S100 proteins, lipocalin-2** | Additional IL-17-induced AMPs |

> 🔍 **This is the single most elegant idea in psoriasis pathogenesis, so let me make it explicit.** The AMPs are why **psoriatic plaques almost never get secondarily infected** despite a grossly disrupted barrier — compare this with atopic dermatitis, which is AMP-*deficient* and constantly superinfected with *S. aureus*. **Psoriasis = over-armed skin; eczema = under-armed skin.** But that same over-armament (LL37) is what restarts the inflammation. **The defence molecule is also the autoantigen.** That is the vicious circle.

#### The Chemokine Network (the "GPS system")

Cytokines *activate*; **chemokines direct traffic.** Their expression is driven by **IL-17 and IFN-γ**:

| Chemokine | Receptor | Cell recruited |
|---|---|---|
| **CXCL9, CXCL10, CXCL11** | CXCR3 | **Th1 cells, CD8⁺ T cells** (IFN-γ-inducible) |
| **CCL2 (MCP-1)** | CCR2 | **Monocytes/macrophages** |
| **CCL20** | **CCR6** | **Th17 cells** and DCs |
| **CXCL1, CXCL8 (IL-8)** | CXCR1/2 | **Neutrophils** |

**The feedback loop, stated as a chain of cause and effect:**

```
   Dendritic cell
        │ IL-23
        ▼
   Th17 differentiation
        │ IL-17A/F
        ▼
   Keratinocyte activation ──► CCL20 (+ hBD-2, CXCL1/8, IL-6, S100)
        │                              │
        │                              ▼  (CCR6⁺)
        │                        MORE Th17 cells recruited from blood
        │                              │
        └──────────◄──────────────────┘
                 SELF-AMPLIFYING LOOP
                          │
                          ▼
              CHRONIC, PERSISTENT PLAQUE
```

> **Analogy — the microphone and the speaker.** Hold a microphone (keratinocyte) too close to a speaker (Th17 cell) and you get **feedback howl**: the speaker's sound enters the mic, is amplified, comes out louder, re-enters the mic. The initial noise that started it (the streptococcal throat infection) is long gone, but the howl continues indefinitely. **Treatment = either turn the speaker down (biologics/immunosuppression) or move the microphone away (cytostatics reducing the keratinocyte response).**

Two more maintenance mechanisms worth a mark each:
- **Tissue-resident memory T cells (T_RM)** — CD8⁺ T cells bearing **CD103** and **α1β1 integrin** (binding collagen IV) become *permanent residents* of the epidermis. They survive clinical clearance. **This is why psoriasis relapses at exactly the same sites, and why "cure" is not currently achievable.**
- **Loss of regulatory T-cell function** — Tregs are present but functionally defective and can even **convert into IL-17 producers** in the psoriatic milieu.

### 4.1 The Neutrophil: Why Pustular Psoriasis Is a Different Beast

The source notes: **"Neutrophils are a prominent feature in acute/pustular psoriasis."** Explain *why*:
- **IL-17** (from T cells) → keratinocytes → **CXCL1/CXCL8** → powerful neutrophil chemotaxis into epidermis
- Neutrophils then release **more IL-17 and LL37** (neutrophil extracellular traps, NETs) → further amplification
- **IL-36 family cytokines** are pivotal here; loss-of-function of the antagonist **IL36RN** → **DITRA → generalised pustular psoriasis**

**Histological correlate — say these names:**
- **Munro's microabscess** = neutrophils in the **parakeratotic stratum corneum**
- **Spongiform pustule of Kogoj** = neutrophils in the **stratum spinosum** (more specific for pustular disease)

### 4.2 The Signalling Pathways — The Intracellular "Wiring"

Cytokines bind receptors; receptors must talk to the nucleus. Three highways do that job, and each is a drug target.

| Pathway | How it is activated | Downstream effect | Therapeutic relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NF-κB** | TLRs, TNF receptor, IL-1R, **CARD14 gain-of-function** | ↑**TNF-α**, IL-1, IL-6, IL-8, adhesion molecules, anti-apoptotic genes | The master inflammatory switch; **glucocorticoids, dimethyl fumarate** act here |
| **JAK–STAT** | IL-23, IL-12, IL-6, IL-22, IFN-α/γ receptors | ↑**IL-6, IL-23, IFN-γ**; **STAT3** → keratinocyte proliferation | **Tofacitinib, deucravacitinib (TYK2 inhibitor)** |
| **MAPK** (ERK/JNK/p38) | Growth factors, stress, cytokines | Drives **rapid cell-cycle turnover** and proliferation | Explains the accelerated epidermal kinetics; **apremilast (PDE4) raises cAMP and dampens this** |

> **Special emphasis on STAT3** (the source flags it, and rightly): **STAT3 is the transcription factor that translates IL-22/IL-6 signals into hyperproliferation and impaired apoptosis.** In fact, transgenic mice overexpressing STAT3 in keratinocytes develop spontaneous psoriasiform lesions **only when the skin is wounded** — a beautiful experimental model of the **Koebner phenomenon**.

**The complete trigger-to-pathway flow:**

```
TRIGGERS (strep, drugs, trauma, stress, smoking, hypocalcaemia)
        │
        ▼  sensed by PATTERN RECOGNITION RECEPTORS (PRRs) & TOLL-LIKE RECEPTORS
        │
        ▼
CYTOKINE & CHEMOKINE RELEASE (IFN-α, TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6, IL-23)
        │
        ▼
INTRACELLULAR SIGNALLING: NF-κB │ JAK-STAT │ MAPK
        │
        ▼
CHRONIC INFLAMMATORY CASCADE + EPIDERMAL HYPERPROLIFERATION
```

### 4.3 🔗 Closing the Loop: From Cytokine to Clinical Sign

This is where you earn the top marks — **prove you understand the mechanism by explaining the physical findings.**

| Clinical / histological finding | Mechanistic explanation |
|---|---|
| **Epidermal turnover falls from ~28 days to 3–5 days** | IL-22/IL-17 + MAPK/STAT3-driven proliferation; transit time collapses |
| **Silvery-white scale; Grattage / candle-grease sign** | No time for terminal differentiation → **parakeratosis** (retained nuclei), loss of granular layer, loosely stacked corneocytes with trapped air |
| **Regular acanthosis with elongated rete ridges + suprapapillary thinning** | Hyperproliferation of basal keratinocytes downward, epidermis stretched thin over the expanded dermal papilla |
| **Auspitz sign** (pinpoint bleeding after removing scale) | Suprapapillary thinning **+ VEGF-driven dilated tortuous papillary capillaries** sitting immediately below. *(Caveat: not specific, and it is best NOT elicited — trauma can Koebnerise!)* |
| **Bright red colour** | VEGF-mediated angiogenesis and vasodilatation |
| **Sterile pustules** | IL-17/CXCL8/IL-36-driven neutrophil influx → Munro's microabscess / Kogoj's spongiform pustule |
| **Koebner phenomenon** | Trauma → LL37 + self-DNA release in *primed* non-lesional skin → restarts Act I |
| **Nail pitting, oil-drop sign, onycholysis** | The same process in the nail matrix and nail bed |
| **Psoriatic arthritis, metabolic syndrome, MI, NAFLD, depression** | Systemic spillover of **TNF-α and IL-17** → "psoriatic march": insulin resistance → endothelial dysfunction → atherosclerosis. *(The source's page-157 note "myocardial infection" should read **myocardial infarction**.)* |

## PART 5 — THE CONCEPT OF "CYTOSTATIC" THERAPY

### 5.1 Definition — and the Distinction That Earns the Mark

> **Cytostatic agents are substances that slow or arrest cell growth and division WITHOUT killing the cell.**

**Cytostatic vs cytotoxic — do not confuse these:**

| | **Cytostatic** | **Cytotoxic** |
|---|---|---|
| Effect | Halts proliferation (cell survives, stops dividing) | Kills the cell outright |
| Reversibility | **Reversible** on stopping the drug | Irreversible cell death |
| Dermatological use | **Low-dose weekly methotrexate**, hydroxyurea, 6-TG | High-dose oncological chemotherapy |
| Clinical implication | Disease **relapses** after withdrawal — it is control, not cure | — |

This distinction explains the single most important expectation-setting conversation you will have with a psoriasis patient: **these drugs suppress; they do not cure. Relapse on stopping is the rule, not treatment failure.**

### 5.2 The Three Targets of Cytostatics in Psoriasis

Going back to our pathogenesis, cytostatics intervene at three points:

1. **Keratinocyte hyperproliferation** — directly slow the accelerated cell cycle (the DNA-synthesis-dependent S phase is the vulnerable point)
2. **Inhibition of inflammatory cells** — suppress the proliferation and tissue migration of activated T cells, and the recruitment of neutrophils
3. **Immunomodulation** — dampen cytokine production and shift the immune balance away from Th1/Th17

> ⚠️ **Conceptual clarity (a common exam trap):** Not every systemic psoriasis drug is a cytostatic. **Acitretin** is a *differentiating agent* (normalises keratinisation via RAR); **apremilast** is a *PDE4 inhibitor* (raises cAMP); **biologics** are *targeted cytokine blockers*. Strictly, the **cytostatics are methotrexate, hydroxyurea, 6-thioguanine, mercaptopurine, azathioprine**, with **cyclosporine, sulfasalazine and leflunomide** conventionally grouped alongside them as antiproliferative/immunosuppressive systemics. If you mention this distinction, you demonstrate that you understand the *question*, not just the drugs.

## PART 6 — STABLE vs UNSTABLE PSORIASIS: WHY THIS PARAGRAPH IS NOT FILLER

Students often skip the "criteria for stable psoriasis" as a random list. **It is actually a therapeutic decision-making tool** — it determines which drug you may safely give.

### Criteria for **STABLE** psoriasis
- **No active (new) lesions** developing
- **No new lesions**, and existing lesions **remaining the same size** — *even if the total affected area is extensive* (extensive ≠ unstable!)
- **Scaling predominates**, and the plaques are **light red / dull pink**, as opposed to the **bright red, "angry," minimally scaly, expanding** plaques of active disease
- Absent Koebner phenomenon

### Why it matters — the clinical logic

| | **Stable psoriasis** | **Unstable / active psoriasis** |
|---|---|---|
| Koebner phenomenon | Absent | **Present** |
| Safe to use | **Dithranol (anthralin), coal tar/Goeckerman, PUVA, NB-UVB**, potent topical steroids | These may cause **irritation → Koebnerisation → generalised flare or erythroderma** |
| Preferred approach | Topicals, phototherapy, MTX | **Rapidly acting systemic agent — CYCLOSPORINE** is the classical choice; bland emollients only topically; admit if erythrodermic/pustular |
| Key caution | — | **Never give or abruptly stop systemic steroids**; avoid irritants |

> 💡 **So the elegant link between the two halves of this question is:** *stability determines drug choice.* **Unstable/erythrodermic/pustular → cyclosporine (fast, weeks). Stable, chronic plaque with joints or nails → methotrexate (slow, months, cheap, once weekly).**

## PART 7 — METHOTREXATE: THE WORKHORSE CYTOSTATIC

### 7.1 What It Is
A **folic acid antagonist** (4-amino-10-methyl folic acid), structurally analogous to folate, giving **excellent therapeutic control of psoriasis**. Introduced for psoriasis in the 1950s and still, worldwide, the **most widely used systemic agent** — cheap, once weekly, effective on skin *and* joints.

### 7.2 Mechanism of Action — Built Up Step by Step

**First, the normal physiology.** Folate must be reduced to its active form to donate one-carbon units for nucleotide synthesis:

```
Folic acid ──(DHFR)──► Dihydrofolate (DHF) ──(DHFR)──► TETRAHYDROFOLATE (THF)
                                                            │
                                                            ▼
                                       one-carbon donor for PURINE synthesis
                                       and (via thymidylate synthase) for
                                       dTMP → THYMIDINE → DNA
```

**Now the drug.** Methotrexate is taken up by the **reduced folate carrier (RFC1)** and, critically, is **polyglutamated** inside the cell by folylpolyglutamate synthase. These **MTX-polyglutamates cannot leave the cell** — they are trapped, giving prolonged intracellular action and explaining **once-weekly dosing** for a drug with a plasma half-life of only a few hours.

MTX then acts at **three enzymes**:

```
METHOTREXATE (polyglutamated)
   │
   ├─(1)─► DIHYDROFOLATE REDUCTASE (DHFR) ── potently inhibited
   │        • Binds with ~1000× the affinity of the natural substrate
   │        • Blocks DHF → THF  ⇒  no reduced folate pool
   │        • ⇒ ↓ purine + ↓ thymidylate synthesis
   │        • ⇒ ARREST OF DNA SYNTHESIS — cell stalls in the S PHASE
   │
   ├─(2)─► THYMIDYLATE SYNTHASE ── partially inhibited
   │        • Directly blocks dUMP → dTMP ⇒ further ↓ thymidine ⇒ ↓ DNA
   │
   └─(3)─► AICAR TRANSFORMYLASE (ATIC) ── inhibited  ★ THE ANTI-INFLAMMATORY ARM ★
            • ⇒ accumulation of AICAR (5-aminoimidazole-4-carboxamide ribonucleotide)
            • ⇒ AICAR blocks adenosine deaminase & AMP deaminase
            • ⇒ intracellular adenine nucleotides ↑, released and converted by
                 ECTO-5'-NUCLEOTIDASE (CD73) to EXTRACELLULAR ADENOSINE
            • ⇒ Adenosine acts on A2a receptors on neutrophils/monocytes/T cells
            • ⇒ ↓ TNF-α, ↓ IL-6, ↓ IL-8, ↓ neutrophil adhesion & chemotaxis
                 = POTENT ANTI-INFLAMMATORY EFFECT
```

**So methotrexate is a dual-action drug, and you must say both halves:**

| Arm | Mechanism | Dose-dependence | Onset |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Antiproliferative (cytostatic)** | DHFR + thymidylate synthase inhibition → S-phase arrest of rapidly cycling keratinocytes and activated lymphocytes | Requires higher doses | Slower |
| **Anti-inflammatory / immunomodulatory** | **AICAR → adenosine → A2a receptor → ↓TNF-α, ↓IL-17 pathway**; suppression of activated T-cell proliferation and their **migration into tissues** | Operates at **low weekly doses** | Dominant at psoriasis doses |

> 🔑 **The single most important conceptual point about MTX:** at the **low weekly doses** used in psoriasis (7.5–25 mg/week), the **anti-inflammatory/immunomodulatory effect via adenosine predominates** over the pure antiproliferative effect. This is why MTX works in psoriasis without behaving like cancer chemotherapy, and why it also works in rheumatoid arthritis where there is no hyperproliferating epidermis at all.
>
> 📝 **Source correction:** the note says DHFR inhibition is "irreversible." Strictly, MTX binding to DHFR is **competitive and extremely high-affinity — effectively/"pseudo-irreversible" at therapeutic concentrations — but not covalent.** It is therefore *reversible* in principle, which is precisely why **folinic acid (leucovorin), a pre-reduced folate that bypasses DHFR, can rescue MTX toxicity.** Say "potent, tight-binding (pseudo-irreversible) competitive inhibition" and you will be exactly right.

> **Analogy — the counterfeit key.** DHFR is a lock; folate is the real key. Methotrexate is a **counterfeit key that fits the lock perfectly but cannot turn it — and then jams in place.** The production line behind the door (DNA synthesis) shuts down. **Leucovorin is the emergency back door** that lets folate in without needing the jammed lock at all.

### 7.3 Indications in Psoriasis

1. **Severe psoriasis:**
   - **Generalised pustular psoriasis** (von Zumbusch)
   - **Erythrodermic psoriasis**
   - **Psoriatic arthritis (PsA)** — ★ MTX treats skin *and* joints, its unique advantage over phototherapy and acitretin
   - **Nail psoriasis** (significant involvement)
   - Extensive **palmoplantar** or other disabling localised disease
2. **Psoriasis not responding to** topical treatments, **phototherapy**, and/or **systemic retinoids**
3. **Chronic plaque psoriasis with BSA > 10–15%**
4. (Additionally) psoriasis with major impact on quality of life (high DLQI) or involving special sites — face, genitals, palms/soles

### 7.4 Practical Use, Monitoring & Safety — The Marks Everyone Forgets

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Dose** | **7.5–25 mg ONCE WEEKLY**, oral/IM/SC. Give a **test dose of 2.5–5 mg** and check CBC at 1 week (to detect idiosyncratic myelosuppression) |
| **⚠️ Deadly error** | **NEVER daily dosing** — inadvertent daily administration causes fatal pancytopenia and mucositis. Write "once weekly" in words on the prescription |
| **Folic acid** | **5 mg/day on non-MTX days** (or 5 mg once weekly, 24–48 h after the dose). Reduces GI upset, mucositis, hepatotoxicity, cytopenias **without abolishing efficacy** |
| **Baseline workup** | CBC, LFT, RFT, viral markers (HBV/HCV/HIV), chest X-ray, TB screening, pregnancy test |
| **Monitoring** | CBC + LFT + RFT at 1 week, then 2-weekly for 1–2 months, then every 2–3 months. **Serum procollagen-III N-terminal peptide (PIIINP)** where available for hepatic fibrosis; **FibroScan/transient elastography** has largely replaced the old **liver biopsy at cumulative dose 1.5 g** |
| **Toxicities** | Nausea/mucositis (most common), **myelosuppression**, **hepatotoxicity/fibrosis**, **pneumonitis** (idiosyncratic, may be fatal), nephrotoxicity, alopecia, "MTX-induced ulceration of psoriatic plaques" (a warning sign of toxicity) |
| **Absolute contraindications** | **Pregnancy & lactation (teratogen)**, significant hepatic or renal disease, active infection/TB, severe cytopenias, alcoholism, unreliable patient |
| **Conception** | Stop **at least 1–3 months** before conception (both sexes) with adequate folate supplementation |
| **Key interactions** ★ | **Co-trimoxazole/trimethoprim (additive antifolate → pancytopenia — a classic killer), NSAIDs, salicylates, probenecid, penicillins, sulphonamides, retinoids (hepatotoxicity), phenytoin, PPIs** — all reduce excretion or add toxicity |
| **Antidote** | **Folinic acid (leucovorin) rescue** |

## PART 8 — CYCLOSPORINE: THE RAPID-ACTION AGENT

### 8.1 What It Is
A **cyclic undecapeptide** (11 amino acids) isolated from the fungus ***Tolypocladium inflatum*** (formerly *Trichoderma polysporum*), from a soil sample famously collected in Norway. It is a **calcineurin inhibitor** — and it was cyclosporine's dramatic efficacy in psoriasis (discovered serendipitously in transplant patients) that **proved psoriasis is a T-cell-mediated disease**, overturning the older view that it was a primary keratinocyte disorder. *(Historically, one of the great "accidental experiments" in dermatology.)*

### 8.2 Mechanism of Action

**Normal T-cell activation:** TCR engagement → ↑intracellular Ca²⁺ → Ca²⁺-calmodulin activates the phosphatase **calcineurin** → calcineurin **dephosphorylates NFAT** (Nuclear Factor of Activated T cells) → NFAT translocates to the nucleus → transcription of **IL-2** (plus IL-4, IFN-γ, GM-CSF) → clonal T-cell proliferation.

**Cyclosporine interrupts this at the phosphatase step:**

```
CYCLOSPORINE (CsA)
     │ binds
     ▼
CYCLOPHILIN (an immunophilin, intracellular)
     │
     ▼
CsA–CYCLOPHILIN COMPLEX
     │ binds and INHIBITS
     ▼
CALCINEURIN (Ca²⁺-dependent phosphatase)
     │
     ▼
NFAT remains PHOSPHORYLATED → cannot enter the nucleus
     │
     ├──► ↓↓ IL-2 TRANSCRIPTION
     │        ⇒ ↓ activated CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells
     │        ⇒ NO T-CELL CLONAL PROLIFERATION
     │
     ├──► ↓ IFN-γ (and IL-4, GM-CSF, TNF-α)
     │
     └──► ↓ ICAM-1 (and other adhesion molecules) on endothelium/keratinocytes
              ⇒ ↓ lymphocyte adhesion, trafficking and dermal/epidermal infiltration
              ⇒ ↓ INFLAMMATION

PLUS direct non-T-cell effects on:
   • MAST CELLS         (↓ degranulation, ↓ mediator release)
   • LANGERHANS CELLS   (↓ antigen presentation / migration)
   • KERATINOCYTES      (↓ proliferation, ↓ cytokine output)
```

> 📝 **Source correction:** The source says CsA "reduces activity of transcription factor NFAT1." More precisely: **CsA inhibits calcineurin**, which prevents dephosphorylation of the **NFAT family (NFATc1–c4)**. **NFAT1 = NFATc2** is one member. The drug does not bind NFAT itself.

> **Analogy — the ignition key.** NFAT is the car key; **calcineurin is the hand that turns it.** Cyclosporine doesn't remove the key — it **handcuffs the hand.** IL-2 is never transcribed, the T-cell army is never conscripted, and the inflammatory war effort collapses. Notice how *upstream* this is: blocking IL-2 blocks T-cell expansion, which blocks IL-17 and IFN-γ production. That upstream position is exactly why cyclosporine acts so **fast**.

### 8.3 Indications in Psoriasis

- **Severe psoriasis requiring rapid control** — **because of its rapid onset (visible improvement in 2–4 weeks; often the fastest of the conventional systemics)**
- **Unstable, erythrodermic, and generalised pustular psoriasis** → "crisis management" / induction therapy
- **When conventional therapies are ineffective** or contraindicated (e.g., methotrexate contraindicated by liver disease)
- Severe **palmoplantar** and **nail** psoriasis
- As a **short-course / intermittent** agent, or as a bridge to a slower-acting long-term drug

### 8.4 Practical Use & Safety

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Dose** | **2.5–5 mg/kg/day** in two divided doses (microemulsion formulation); start low and escalate, or start high for rapid control then taper |
| **Duration** | ⚠️ **Intermittent short courses (12–16 weeks) preferred; limit continuous use to 1 year (maximum 2)** because of cumulative, irreversible nephrotoxicity |
| **Monitoring** | **Blood pressure and serum creatinine** (at baseline, 2-weekly ×2 months, then monthly), eGFR, **potassium, magnesium, uric acid, lipids**, LFT. **Reduce dose or stop if creatinine rises >30% above baseline** |
| **Adverse effects** | **Nephrotoxicity** (dose-limiting), **hypertension**, hyperkalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, hyperuricaemia/gout, hyperlipidaemia, **gingival hyperplasia**, **hypertrichosis**, tremor, paraesthesiae, headache, GI upset, ↑ infection and **malignancy (especially skin cancer and lymphoma)** risk |
| **Critical caution** ★ | **Avoid combining with PUVA or in patients with extensive prior PUVA/UVB** — markedly increased risk of **cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma** |
| **Contraindications** | Renal impairment, **uncontrolled hypertension**, malignancy, active infection, live vaccines, pregnancy (relative — it is actually among the safer options in pregnancy if essential), poor compliance |
| **Interactions** | Metabolised by **CYP3A4** — levels ↑ by **macrolides, azoles, diltiazem/verapamil, grapefruit juice**; levels ↓ by **rifampicin, phenytoin, carbamazepine, St John's wort**. Avoid concurrent nephrotoxins (NSAIDs, aminoglycosides) |
| **Withdrawal** | **Taper — abrupt cessation causes rebound.** Plan the sequential/transition therapy before stopping |

### 8.5 Methotrexate vs Cyclosporine — The Comparison Table Examiners Love

| Feature | **Methotrexate** | **Cyclosporine** |
|---|---|---|
| Class | Folate antagonist (antimetabolite, cytostatic) | Calcineurin inhibitor (immunosuppressant) |
| Onset of action | **Slow** (4–8 weeks, full effect 3–6 months) | **Rapid** (2–4 weeks) |
| Best for | Stable chronic plaque, **psoriatic arthritis**, nail disease | **Unstable, erythrodermic, pustular**; rapid rescue |
| Joint disease | **Effective** | Less effective for axial disease |
| Main organ toxicity | **Liver** (fibrosis), marrow, lung | **Kidney**, blood pressure |
| Long-term use | Feasible for years with monitoring | **Limited to ~1–2 years** |
| Pregnancy | **Absolutely contraindicated (teratogen)** | Relatively safe if essential |
| Cost/availability | Very cheap, once weekly | Expensive, twice daily |
| Combination | ✗ Avoid with retinoids (hepatotoxicity) | ✗ **Avoid with PUVA (SCC risk)** |

## PART 9 — THE OTHER CYTOSTATIC / IMMUNOSUPPRESSIVE AGENTS

These are second- and third-line, but you should be able to say one sentence of mechanism for each.

| Drug | Mechanism | Notes & practical points |
|---|---|---|
| **Azathioprine** | Purine analogue → converted to **6-mercaptopurine → 6-thioguanine nucleotides**, which incorporate into DNA → inhibition of lymphocyte proliferation | **Check TPMT enzyme activity/genotype before starting** (low TPMT → severe myelosuppression). **Never co-prescribe with allopurinol** (xanthine oxidase blockade → toxicity). Modest efficacy in psoriasis; ~1–3 mg/kg/day |
| **Hydroxyurea** | Inhibits **ribonucleotide reductase** → blocks conversion of ribonucleotides to deoxyribonucleotides → **S-phase arrest** | ~0.5–1.5 g/day. Useful when MTX/CsA contraindicated (e.g., liver or renal disease). Toxicity: **megaloblastic anaemia**, leg ulcers, hyperpigmentation, dermatomyositis-like eruption |
| **6-Thioguanine** *(source's "6-thioqnanine" is an OCR error)* | Purine analogue (thiopurine) → thioguanine nucleotides incorporated into DNA | Pulse dosing (e.g., 80–120 mg twice weekly) reduces myelosuppression; also TPMT-dependent |
| **Mercaptopurine (6-MP)** | The active metabolite of azathioprine; same thiopurine pathway | Rarely used for psoriasis today |
| **Sulfasalazine** | Cleaved to **sulfapyridine + 5-ASA**; inhibits **NF-κB**, folate-dependent enzymes, and neutrophil chemotaxis | Modest skin benefit; **useful for peripheral psoriatic arthritis**. Watch G6PD deficiency, sulfa allergy, reversible oligospermia |
| **Leflunomide** | Inhibits **dihydroorotate dehydrogenase (DHODH)** → blocks *de novo* **pyrimidine** synthesis → arrests activated lymphocyte proliferation | Approved for **psoriatic arthritis**; some skin benefit. **Strongly teratogenic with a very long half-life** (enterohepatic recirculation → washout with cholestyramine) |

> 🧠 **Unifying insight — see the pattern, don't memorise the list.** Look at the mechanisms: MTX blocks **folate** → purines + thymidine. Azathioprine/6-MP/6-TG are **purine** analogues. Leflunomide blocks **pyrimidine** synthesis. Hydroxyurea blocks **ribonucleotide reductase** → deoxyribonucleotides. **Every single one of these cytostatics converges on the same choke point: the supply of nucleotides for DNA synthesis in rapidly dividing keratinocytes and activated lymphocytes.** One idea, five drugs.

## PART 10 — COMMON MISUNDERSTANDINGS (Clear These Up Now)

1. **"Psoriasis is a disease of keratinocyte hyperproliferation."** ❌ It is an **immune-mediated** disease; hyperproliferation is the *downstream consequence* of IL-17/IL-22/TNF-α. Cyclosporine's efficacy (a pure T-cell drug) proved this decisively.
2. **"Cytostatic = cytotoxic = chemotherapy."** ❌ Cytostatics *arrest* division reversibly. Low-dose weekly MTX in psoriasis is nothing like oncological dosing.
3. **"Methotrexate works in psoriasis mainly by killing dividing keratinocytes."** ❌ At psoriasis doses, the **anti-inflammatory adenosine (AICAR/ATIC) pathway** predominates.
4. **"Folic acid will neutralise methotrexate."** ❌ **Folic** acid reduces toxicity without significant loss of efficacy. It is **folinic** acid (leucovorin) that is the rescue agent for overdose.
5. **"Cyclosporine inhibits NFAT directly."** ❌ It inhibits **calcineurin**, the phosphatase upstream of NFAT.
6. **"HLA-Cw6 → late-onset psoriasis."** ❌ **Early-onset (Type 1)** and **guttate** psoriasis. (The source page has this inverted.)
7. **"Sunlight always helps psoriasis."** ❌ It helps most, but **5–20% are photo-aggravated**, and sunburn can Koebnerise.
8. **"Give systemic steroids for a bad flare."** ❌ **Avoid** — withdrawal precipitates rebound **pustular or erythrodermic** psoriasis.
9. **"Extensive disease = unstable disease."** ❌ **Extent ≠ activity.** A patient can have 60% BSA of stable, dull-red, heavily scaling plaques — that is *stable*, and dithranol/PUVA are permissible.
10. **"Always demonstrate Auspitz sign."** ❌ It is neither specific nor sensitive, and the trauma of eliciting it can itself Koebnerise.
11. **"Elicit and treat only the skin."** ❌ Screen every patient for **psoriatic arthritis, metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular risk, NAFLD, and depression** — the "psoriatic march."
12. **"Cyclosporine can be continued indefinitely like MTX."** ❌ Cumulative **irreversible nephrotoxicity** caps continuous use at ~1–2 years; it is an *induction/rescue* agent.

### The Three Sentences You Must Be Able to Say

1. **Etiology:** *Psoriasis arises when an environmental trigger meets a genetically primed immune system — the key genes being **HLA-C\*06:02 within PSORS1** and **ERAP1**, which together distort self-peptide presentation to CD8⁺ T cells.*
2. **Pathogenesis:** *LL37–self-DNA complexes drive pDC-derived **IFN-α**, which matures dendritic cells to secrete **IL-23**, driving a **Th17** response whose **IL-17** activates keratinocytes to hyperproliferate and to secrete **CCL20** and antimicrobial peptides — recruiting yet more Th17 cells in a **self-amplifying loop** that makes the disease chronic.*
3. **Cytostatic management:** *Because the plaque is sustained by both **proliferating keratinocytes** and **proliferating T cells**, drugs that arrest cell division control it — **methotrexate** (antifolate; slow, cheap, weekly, also treats arthritis; watch liver/marrow) and **cyclosporine** (calcineurin inhibitor; fast, ideal for unstable/erythrodermic/pustular disease; watch kidney/BP, limit to 1–2 years) — supported by azathioprine, hydroxyurea, 6-thioguanine, mercaptopurine, sulfasalazine and leflunomide.*
