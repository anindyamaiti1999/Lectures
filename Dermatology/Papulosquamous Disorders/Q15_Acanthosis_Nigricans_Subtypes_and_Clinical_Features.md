# Acanthosis Nigricans — A Mini-Lecture

## Opening Orientation: Why This Topic Deserves Your Attention

Let me start with a story that plays out in clinics every single day.

A 14-year-old girl comes in because her mother is worried about "dirt" on the back of her neck that won't wash off. The skin there is dark, thick, and has a soft, suede-like feel. Nothing itches. Nothing hurts.

Now, a dermatologist who only *looks* at skin will say: "It's cosmetic, use a cream." But a dermatologist who *thinks* will say something quite different: "This velvety darkening is not the disease — it's a **billboard**. Your skin is advertising what your pancreas is screaming about. Let's check her insulin."

That is the entire intellectual point of acanthosis nigricans (AN). It is the classic example of skin as a **window into internal medicine**. In a young or middle-aged patient, it usually announces **insulin resistance** — pre-diabetes, obesity, PCOS, metabolic syndrome. In an elderly patient with sudden, explosive, widespread disease, it may announce something far more sinister: an **internal adenocarcinoma**.

So AN sits at the crossroads of dermatology, endocrinology, and oncology. That is precisely why examiners love it — and why we will take it apart mechanistically rather than memorize lists.

Where does it sit taxonomically? AN belongs to the **disorders of keratinization** — conditions where the epidermis makes keratin abnormally (too much, too fast, or with abnormal architecture). Most keratinization disorders you learn (the ichthyoses, palmoplantar keratodermas, Darier disease) are *inherited* and *structural*. AN is the great **acquired, signal-driven** member of the family: the keratinocytes are genetically normal, but they are being *shouted at* by growth factors. Remember that framing — it explains everything that follows.

---

## 1. Definition — Read It Word by Word

> **Acanthosis nigricans** is an **acquired** disorder of keratinization characterized by **asymptomatic**, **symmetrical**, **velvety**, **thickened**, **hyperpigmented** plaques affecting predominantly **intertriginous (flexural) skin**, often studded with **acrochordons** (skin tags).

Let me unpack each word, because each one is doing real diagnostic work:

| Word | What it means | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Acquired** | Appears during life; not present at birth (except in rare familial forms) | Immediately separates AN from congenital ichthyoses |
| **Asymptomatic** | No itch, no pain, no burning | If it itches or burns, reconsider your diagnosis (think eczema, tinea, inverse psoriasis) |
| **Symmetrical** | Both axillae, both groins, both sides of the neck | Asymmetry is a red flag → think of the rare **unilateral nevoid** variant |
| **Velvety** | Feels like suede or peach skin; fine, soft roughness | This texture comes from *papillomatosis* — micro-hills on the surface |
| **Thickened** | Palpably raised, sometimes rugose (deeply folded) | Reflects hyperkeratosis + papillomatosis, not inflammation |
| **Hyperpigmented** | Brown to grey-black | **This is the great trap** — the darkness is *optical*, not melanocytic (we will prove this later) |
| **Intertriginous** | Neck, axillae, groins, inframammary, antecubital, knuckles | Sites of **friction + moisture + insulin-sensitive fat** |
| **Acrochordons** | Skin tags | Same growth-factor drive causing fibroepithelial outgrowths — a *co-sign* of the same hormonal message |

**The anatomical clue is enormous.** Why flexures? Because flexures combine three things: mechanical **friction**, trapped **perspiration**, and a locally **hormone-responsive** epidermis. Friction and moisture act like a "primer coat" that lowers the threshold for the growth-factor signal to produce visible thickening. This is why obese patients get it worst — more skin-on-skin contact, more sweat, *and* more hyperinsulinemia. Three hits in one.

## 2. Epidemiology — The Numbers That Frame Your Suspicion

- **Benign ≫ Malignant.** Malignant (paraneoplastic) AN is genuinely rare — **less than 5%** of all AN cases. More than **90%** of AN you will ever see is driven by insulin resistance, obesity, or drugs.
  > ⚠️ **Correction to a commonly circulated figure:** Some notes state malignant AN is "20%" of cases. That is **wrong** and unsupported by any standard reference. Fix this in your memory as **<5%**. Overestimating this number leads to unnecessary panic and unnecessary CT scans.
- **Any age** can be affected — but *which* age you see it in changes your whole differential (see §4).
- **Male = Female.** No sex predilection overall. (Note, however, that the *HAIR-AN* variant is by definition female.)
- **Most common in darker skin phototypes.** Partly because the baseline epidermal melanin makes the optical darkening far more conspicuous, and partly because of genuinely higher rates of insulin resistance in certain populations (South Asian, African, Hispanic).

**The clinical takeaway:** when you see AN, your first instinct should be *metabolic*, not *malignant*. Malignancy is the exception you must consciously exclude, using the red-flag criteria we'll build in §4.

## 3. Pathophysiology — The Heart of the Lecture

Everything about AN — its appearance, its associations, its treatment, its variants — falls out of **one single idea**:

> **The keratinocyte is a cell with growth-factor receptors on its surface. If you over-stimulate those receptors, the keratinocyte proliferates and the epidermis thickens and folds. AN is what over-stimulated epidermis looks like.**

There are essentially **four ways** to over-stimulate those receptors. Learn them as four "channels" into the same final common pathway.

### Channel 1 — Insulin spillover onto IGF-1 receptors *(the common one)*

Here is the elegant mechanism.

1. The patient is obese and/or insulin-resistant. Their muscle and fat tissue no longer respond well to insulin.
2. The pancreas compensates by pumping out **enormous** amounts of insulin → **hyperinsulinemia**.
3. Now, insulin's *own* receptor is defective/downregulated in target tissues. But insulin is **structurally homologous** to IGF-1 (insulin-like growth factor-1), and at very high concentrations it **cross-reacts with and activates IGF-1 receptors**.
4. Keratinocytes and dermal fibroblasts are **loaded with IGF-1 receptors** — and these receptors are **overexpressed** in hyperinsulinemic patients, making the tissue even more sensitive.
5. IGF-1R activation is a potent **proliferative** signal (it is a growth pathway, not a metabolic one). Keratinocytes divide; fibroblasts proliferate.
6. Result: a thickened, papillomatous epidermis → **acanthosis nigricans**. And proliferating fibroepithelial units → **skin tags**.

> **Analogy — the wrong key in the neighbour's lock.** Insulin's proper key fits the insulin receptor. In insulin resistance, that lock is jammed, so the body makes thousands of copies of the key and floods the building. With that many keys jangling around, some of them start turning the *IGF-1 receptor* lock next door — a lock that opens the door marked "GROW." The metabolic door stays shut (hence the diabetes), but the growth door swings wide open (hence the thick skin).

This single analogy explains why AN is a **visible marker of hyperinsulinemia**, why AN is accompanied by **skin tags**, and why **weight loss and metformin** make it fade — remove the excess insulin, remove the inappropriate growth signal.

### Channel 2 — Direct EGFR stimulation by tumour-derived TGF-α *(malignant AN)*

1. A visceral adenocarcinoma (classically **gastric**) secretes large amounts of **TGF-α (transforming growth factor-alpha)**.
2. TGF-α is **structurally homologous to EGF** and binds the **EGF receptor (EGFR)** on keratinocytes.
3. EGFR activation → keratinocyte hyperproliferation → rapid, florid, widespread AN, plus **tripe palms** and often the **sign of Leser–Trélat** (explosive crop of seborrheic keratoses) — all three being manifestations of the *same* circulating growth factor acting on *different* epidermal structures.
4. Treat the tumour → the circulating TGF-α falls → the skin findings **regress**. Tumour recurs → skin findings return. The skin behaves like a **biological assay** for tumour burden.

> ⚠️ **Do NOT confuse TGF-α with TGF-β.** They are unrelated despite the shared name.
> - **TGF-α** → EGF-family ligand → binds EGFR → **stimulates** epithelial proliferation → the driver of malignant AN.
> - **TGF-β** → entirely different superfamily → generally **inhibits** epithelial proliferation and drives fibrosis/immunosuppression.
> If an examiner asks "which growth factor mediates malignant AN?", the answer is **TGF-α**, and saying "TGF" alone is incomplete.

### Channel 3 — Constitutively activated FGFR3 *(the genetic one)*

Here the receptor doesn't wait for a ligand at all. A **gain-of-function mutation in FGFR3** (fibroblast growth factor receptor 3) leaves the receptor **permanently switched on**, signalling growth even with zero ligand around.

> **Analogy — the light switch taped down.** Channel 1 and 2 are people repeatedly flicking the switch. Channel 3 is a switch physically taped in the "ON" position. The bulb (keratinocyte proliferation) burns continuously.

This explains three related entities:

| Entity | Mutation | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| **Familial (benign) AN** | **FGFR3** activating mutation, **autosomal dominant** | AN appearing in **childhood**, often *without* obesity or insulin resistance, accentuating at puberty |
| **SADDAN syndrome** | **FGFR3** p.Lys650Met | **S**evere **A**chondroplasia with **D**evelopmental **D**elay and **A**canthosis **N**igricans |
| **Beare–Stevenson cutis gyrata syndrome** | Primarily **FGFR2** (some FGFR3-related phenotypic overlap) | Cutis gyrata (deep corrugated skin folds), AN, craniosynostosis, digital and genital anomalies |

Note the beautiful internal logic: FGFR3 is a *growth-plate* receptor, so its constitutive activation gives you **skeletal dysplasia (achondroplasia-spectrum) plus AN** in the same patient. One receptor, two organ systems. That is why SADDAN exists as a syndrome at all.

### Channel 4 — Other growth factor receptors

**FGF receptors** and **EGF receptors** more broadly are implicated in sporadic disease. The unifying theme stands: **AN = receptor tyrosine kinase over-activity in the epidermis.**

### The convergence

```
   HYPERINSULINEMIA ──→ IGF-1R ┐
   TUMOUR TGF-α ─────→ EGFR    ├──→ KERATINOCYTE + FIBROBLAST
   FGFR3 mutation ───→ FGFR3   │     HYPERPROLIFERATION
   Drugs/hormones ───→ (mixed) ┘              │
                                              ▼
                            HYPERKERATOSIS + PAPILLOMATOSIS
                                              │
                          ┌───────────────────┴───────────────────┐
                          ▼                                       ▼
                 VELVETY THICKENING                    APPARENT DARKENING
                 (palpable texture)                   (optical, not melanin)
                          │
                          + ACROCHORDONS (fibroblast proliferation)
```

**Different doorways, one room.** That is why benign and malignant AN look *qualitatively* similar — the final effector pathway is shared. The difference is in *speed, extent, age,* and *company kept*.

## 4. Classification — Benign vs. Malignant

This is the single most examined table in the topic, so let's not just memorize it — let's make it *derivable*.

| Feature | **Benign AN** | **Malignant (Paraneoplastic) AN** |
|---|---|---|
| **Age** | Children, adolescents, adults | **Older** patients (typically >40) |
| **Underlying driver** | Hyperinsulinemia / obesity / drugs / inherited FGFR3 | Tumour-secreted **TGF-α** |
| **Onset** | **Insidious**, over months to years | **Abrupt**, over weeks; rapidly progressive |
| **Extent** | Limited to flexures (neck, axillae, groins, knuckles) | **Widespread**; involves **mucosae, lips, oral cavity, eyelids, conjunctivae, palms, nails** |
| **Severity of lesions** | Velvety, moderate | **Rugose hypertrophy, warty/verrucous thickening**, florid |
| **Associated signs** | Skin tags | **Tripe palms**, **Leser–Trélat sign**, pruritus (unusual for benign AN), weight *loss* |
| **Genetics** | Can be **genetically transmitted** (familial AD, FGFR3) | **No genetic component** |
| **Course** | **Chronic**; primarily a **cosmetic** and *metabolic-marker* concern | **Paraneoplastic**; parallels the tumour |
| **Prognosis** | **Good** (skin-wise) | **Poor** — average survival ≈ **2 years** |
| **Management** | Treat the **insulin resistance** | **Find and treat the tumour** |

### The red flags that should make you hunt for cancer

Build a mental checklist. Any of these should trigger a malignancy workup:

1. **Older patient** (>40) with **new-onset** AN
2. **Rapid onset** — weeks, not years
3. **Absence of obesity or insulin resistance** (no metabolic explanation)
4. **Mucosal involvement** — lips, mouth, conjunctiva, oesophagus
5. **Palmar/nail involvement**, especially **tripe palms**
6. **Pruritus** (malignant AN can itch — benign AN classically does not)
7. **Concurrent Leser–Trélat sign** or unexplained **weight loss**
8. **Florid, verrucous, rugose** morphology

> **Analogy — the smoke detector.** Benign AN is the detector beeping because someone made toast (obesity/insulin): annoying, expected, fixable. Malignant AN is the same detector going off at 3 a.m. with no toast in sight, screaming, in every room of the house. Same alarm, radically different meaning. **Context, speed, and extent** are what distinguish them.

### Timing relative to cancer diagnosis

An important subtlety: malignant AN may **precede** the cancer diagnosis (often by months — hence its value as an early warning), may appear **simultaneously**, or may **follow** it (sometimes heralding recurrence). Because it can precede diagnosis, a negative initial workup does **not** end your responsibility — such patients need **ongoing surveillance**.

## 5. Predisposing Factors — The "Four Fs" of AN

Four classic predisposing factors, and each maps onto mechanism:

| Factor | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **1. Insulin resistance** | The IGF-1R cross-activation pathway (Channel 1) — the *biochemical* driver |
| **2. Obesity** | Amplifies hyperinsulinemia *and* creates skin folds |
| **3. Friction** | Mechanical trauma → reactive epidermal hyperplasia; **lowers the threshold** for growth signals to manifest visibly |
| **4. Perspiration** | Maceration and occlusion in folds → further epidermal reaction |

Notice how neatly factors 2–4 explain the **flexural distribution**: the biochemical signal (insulin) is *systemic and everywhere*, but it only produces **visible** disease where the mechanical co-factors (friction and moisture) are present. This is a lovely example of a general dermatological principle: **a systemic signal plus a local co-factor produces a regional eruption.**

## 6. Associations — Turning a Terrifying List into Logic

The textbook list of associations looks like brute memorization. It isn't. Sort it into **five mechanistic buckets** and it becomes almost self-generating.

### Bucket A — Insulin resistance / hyperinsulinemia (the biggest bucket)
- **Diabetes mellitus** (especially type 2)
- **Obesity**
- **PCOS**
- **HAIR-AN syndrome** — see below
- **Prader–Willi syndrome** (hyperphagia → obesity → insulin resistance)
- **Laurence–Moon–Bardet–Biedl syndrome** (obesity, retinal dystrophy, polydactyly, hypogonadism, renal disease)
- **Rud syndrome** (ichthyosis, hypogonadism, epilepsy, intellectual disability)

### Bucket B — Growth-hormone / IGF-1 excess
- **Acromegaly** (adult GH excess)
- **Gigantism** (childhood GH excess)

Why these? Because **GH acts largely through IGF-1**. Flood the body with IGF-1 and you are directly turbo-charging the IGF-1 receptor pathway — the exact final pathway of Channel 1. AN in acromegaly is mechanistically *inevitable*.

### Bucket C — Genetic/receptor and progeroid/RASopathy syndromes
- **Familial AN** (AD, FGFR3)
- **Beare–Stevenson syndrome** (FGFR2/FGFR-family)
- **SADDAN** (FGFR3)
- **Costello syndrome** (HRAS — a **RASopathy**; the MAPK pathway is *downstream* of all these growth factor receptors, so activating it mimics receptor over-activity)
- **Werner syndrome** (adult progeria, WRN; insulin resistance and diabetes are core features)
- **Bloom syndrome** (BLM helicase; insulin resistance and diabetes occur)
- **Wilson syndrome** (as listed in the source text)

### Bucket D — Autoimmune / connective tissue disease
- **Dermatomyositis** (note: DM is *itself* paraneoplastic-prone, so AN + DM demands a hard cancer search)
- **SLE**
- **Hashimoto thyroiditis**
- Mechanism: autoantibodies against the **insulin receptor** (type B insulin resistance) can produce severe AN — a genuinely beautiful mechanistic link between autoimmunity and this skin sign.

### Bucket E — Drug-induced
- **Systemic corticosteroids** (induce insulin resistance)
- **Insulin itself** (exogenous hyperinsulinemia — AN can appear at injection sites!)
- **Oral contraceptives / hormones**
- Also described: nicotinic acid, diethylstilbestrol, triazinate, fusidic acid, protease inhibitors

### Bucket F — "Mimics and neighbours" (conditions the source lists that are worth understanding differently)
- **Phenylketonuria**
- **Addison disease** — diffuse hyperpigmentation from ACTH/MSH excess, accentuated in **flexures, scars, and mucosae**. This can *look* like AN but is **flat**, not velvety or thickened.
- **Pellagra** — niacin deficiency; the **Casal necklace** is a hyperpigmented, thickened, desquamating collar around the neck that genuinely mimics neck AN.
- **Hemochromatosis** — bronze hyperpigmentation + **diabetes** ("bronze diabetes"), so it can produce *both* mimicry and true insulin-resistance-driven AN.

> **Teaching point about Bucket F:** these are best remembered as conditions in which the *colour change* mimics AN. The discriminator is **palpation**. Run your finger over the lesion. **AN is thick and velvety; pigmentary mimics are flat and smooth.** Texture beats colour, every time.

### 🔑 HAIR-AN Syndrome — Memorize This One

| Letter | Component |
|---|---|
| **HA** | **H**yper**A**ndrogenism (hirsutism, acne, oligomenorrhoea) |
| **IR** | **I**nsulin **R**esistance |
| **AN** | **A**canthosis **N**igricans |

A subset of PCOS in young women. The mechanism closes a beautiful loop: **hyperinsulinemia** stimulates ovarian theca cells to produce **androgens** *and* suppresses hepatic **SHBG** (raising free testosterone) *and* drives **IGF-1R** in the skin. So the *same* excess insulin simultaneously produces the hirsutism, the menstrual irregularity, and the velvety neck. One hormone, three clinical signs. Treat the insulin resistance (weight loss, metformin) and **all three** improve — which is exactly why metformin is used in PCOS.

## 7. Malignancies Associated with AN

The list as given:

- **Gastric adenocarcinoma** ← **the most common by far (~50–60% of malignant AN cases)**
- Ovarian adenocarcinoma
- Breast adenocarcinoma
- Bladder adenocarcinoma
- Renal cell carcinoma
- Thyroid adenocarcinoma

**The single most useful pattern to extract:** every entry is an **adenocarcinoma** (i.e., glandular epithelium). The rule to carry into the exam and the clinic is:

> **Malignant acanthosis nigricans = intra-abdominal adenocarcinoma until proven otherwise — and the stomach until proven otherwise.**

Why adenocarcinomas? Glandular epithelial tumours are prolific producers of **EGF-family ligands including TGF-α**. Squamous cell cancers and lymphomas, lacking that secretory profile, only rarely cause AN.

### The paraneoplastic triad — recognize the family resemblance

Three signs, one shared mechanism (circulating TGF-α acting on epidermal EGFR):

| Sign | Description | Association |
|---|---|---|
| **Malignant AN** | Florid, rugose, mucosal-involving AN | Gastric adenocarcinoma foremost |
| **Tripe palms** (acanthosis palmaris / pachydermatoglyphia) | Exaggerated, thickened, velvety **ridging of palmar dermatoglyphics**, resembling the rugose lining of bovine stomach ("tripe") | Present in **~75%** of malignant AN; with AN → **gastric**; **alone** → **pulmonary (lung)** carcinoma |
| **Sign of Leser–Trélat** | **Sudden eruption of multiple seborrheic keratoses** ("explosion of SKs") | Gastrointestinal adenocarcinoma, lymphoproliferative disease |

> **Exam pearl:** **Tripe palms alone → think lung. Tripe palms + AN → think stomach.** This is a classic two-step discriminator.

Also worth noting: **"florid cutaneous papillomatosis"** (sudden eruption of numerous wart-like papules) is a fourth member of this family, driven by the same pathway.

### Practical workup when you suspect malignant AN

Since you cannot scan the whole body indefinitely, be directed:
1. **History and full examination** including lymph nodes, breast, pelvic/rectal exam
2. **Stool occult blood**; **upper GI endoscopy** (the stomach is priority number one)
3. **CBC, LFT, tumour markers** as guided
4. **CT chest/abdomen/pelvis**
5. **Mammography and pelvic imaging** in women
6. **Repeat surveillance** if initially negative, because AN may precede the tumour by months

## 8. Clinical Features — What You Actually See and Feel

### Classic sites (in rough order of frequency)
- **Posterior and lateral neck** — the most common and most easily overlooked site (patients think it's dirt)
- **Axillae**
- **Groins** and inner thighs
- **Knuckles** and dorsal interphalangeal joints (a very useful site — easily seen, often the earliest sign in children)
- Inframammary, antecubital and popliteal fossae, umbilicus, perianal skin
- Elbows, knees

### Morphology
- **Velvety, dark brown to grey-black patches and plaques**
- **Ill-defined margins** blending into normal skin (a key discriminator from well-demarcated conditions)
- **Symmetrical**
- **Skin lines accentuated**, with a faintly warty or **papillomatous** surface
- **Acrochordons** scattered within and around the plaques
- In advanced/malignant disease: **rugose hypertrophy** — deep, corrugated, brain-like folds — and **verrucous (warty) thickening**

### Extended involvement (a marker of severity, and a red flag)
- **Mucosal surfaces** — oral cavity, lips, tongue (velvety papillomatous thickening), oesophagus, larynx, anogenital mucosa
- **Eyelids** and **conjunctivae** (papillomatous conjunctival thickening)
- **Nail changes** — leukonychia, brittleness, hyperkeratosis of the nail bed
- **Palms** — tripe palms

> **Clinical rule:** mucosal, conjunctival, palmar, or nail involvement pushes you *hard* toward the **malignant** end of the spectrum. Benign, insulin-driven AN stays politely in the flexures.

## 9. Clinical Variants — Six You Must Know

| Variant | Defining features | Mechanism / key point |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Benign / obesity-associated ("pseudo-AN")** | The everyday form; flexural, insidious, with obesity and insulin resistance | IGF-1R cross-activation; **reversible with weight loss** |
| **2. HAIR-AN syndrome** | **H**yperandrogenism + **I**nsulin **R**esistance + **A**canthosis **N**igricans; in young women, with PCOS | Severe insulin resistance driving both ovarian androgens and skin changes |
| **3. Familial AN** | **Autosomal dominant**; presents in **childhood**; **FGFR3** mutations; *no* obesity or endocrinopathy required; often accentuates at puberty | Constitutively active receptor ("switch taped on"). Also seen in **Beare–Stevenson** and **SADDAN** |
| **4. Drug-induced AN** | Follows drug exposure; resolves on withdrawal | Culprits: **systemic corticosteroids, insulin, oral contraceptives/hormones**, nicotinic acid, diethylstilbestrol, protease inhibitors |
| **5. Acral AN (acral acanthotic anomaly)** | Velvety hyperkeratosis on **dorsa of hands and feet**, over knuckles, with **no** flexural or systemic involvement | Most common in **otherwise healthy patients with dark skin**; benign, no workup needed beyond basic screening |
| **6. Unilateral nevoid AN** | **Rare**; a **unilateral, fragmented plaque following the lines of Blaschko**; onset in childhood/adolescence | A **mosaic/somatic mutation** in a clone of keratinocytes → hence unilateral and Blaschko-linear, with typical AN histology. Emphatically **NOT** associated with endocrinopathy or malignancy |
| **7. Malignant AN** | Abrupt, florid, widespread; **rugose hypertrophy** and **warty thickening including mucosae**; **tripe palms**; older patient; may itch | Tumour-derived **TGF-α** → keratinocyte EGFR |

> **Why does unilateral nevoid AN follow Blaschko's lines?** Because Blaschko's lines trace the migration paths of ectodermal cells during embryogenesis. A post-zygotic (somatic) mutation in one embryonic keratinocyte produces a clone of mutated cells that migrates along that path. So a *linear, Blaschko-following* lesion is essentially a visible map of a **mosaic mutation** — a genetic fingerprint you can see with the naked eye. This is why it's "nevoid": it behaves like a birthmark, not like a systemic disease.

## 10. Investigations

### A. Histopathology (HPE) — And the Most Important Trap in the Topic

The classic triad on biopsy:

1. **Hyperkeratosis** — a thickened, compact **stratum corneum**. *This is the dominant feature.*
2. **Papillomatosis** — upward, **finger-like (church-spire) projections of the dermal papillae**, giving a peaked, undulating epidermal surface.
3. **Minimal acanthosis** — only *mild* thickening of the viable epidermis. Note the delightful irony: the disease is named "**acanthosis** nigricans," yet **acanthosis is the least prominent feature**. The epidermal thickening that you *feel* clinically is mostly **hyperkeratosis + papillomatosis**, not true acanthosis.
4. Additional features: mild dermal papillary fibrosis; the "valleys" between papillae are filled with keratin; occasional mild basal layer hyperpigmentation.

### 🔑 The "nigricans" is an OPTICAL illusion

This is the **most frequently examined single fact** about AN, so let's establish it properly.

> **The dark colour of acanthosis nigricans is due to HYPERKERATOSIS, not to increased melanin.**
> **Melanocyte number is NORMAL. Melanin production is NOT significantly increased.**

**Why does thick keratin look dark?** Three contributing optical effects:

1. **Light scattering and absorption:** A thick, compact keratin layer absorbs and scatters incident light rather than reflecting it back. Less returned light = darker appearance.
2. **Shadowing from papillomatosis:** The surface is not flat — it is a landscape of micro-hills and valleys. Those valleys cast **shadows**, and shadows read as "darker" to the eye.
3. **Retained keratinocyte debris and melanin *within* the thickened corneum:** the normal amount of melanin is packed into a much thicker layer, concentrating it optically without any increase in production.

> **Analogy — wet sand and stacked glass.** Take a sheet of clear glass: it looks colourless. Stack fifty sheets: the edge looks distinctly green-black. Nothing about the glass changed — only its **thickness**. Or think of dry beach sand (pale) versus wet sand (dark brown): the sand grains have identical pigment; the change in surface optics changes the perceived colour. AN is your patient's skin becoming "optically thicker."

**A closely related phenomenon:** **terra firma-forme dermatosis** ("Duncan's dirty dermatosis") — dirty-appearing brown patches from retained, compacted keratinocytes, which **wipe off completely with 70% isopropyl alcohol**. The colour there is *purely* retained keratin. AN shares the same optical principle but will *not* wipe off, because the keratin is firmly adherent and the papillomatosis is structural.

**A nuance worth knowing:** some authors note that mild basal-layer hyperpigmentation *can* coexist. But the examinable and mechanistically dominant answer is: **the darkening is optical/structural, not melanocytic.**

**Practical consequence:** this is why **bleaching agents like hydroquinone are useless in AN**. You cannot lighten pigment that isn't excessive. The rational therapy is **keratolytic and antiproliferative** — thin the keratin, and the darkness resolves. Mechanism dictates treatment. Every time.

### B. Screening for the Underlying Cause — Mandatory, Not Optional

> **A diagnosis of AN is never complete at the skin. The skin finding is the *beginning* of the workup, not the end.**

**For all patients:**
- **Fasting plasma glucose**, **HbA1c**, **OGTT** if indicated
- **Fasting insulin** → for HOMA-IR
- **Lipid profile**; **BMI and waist circumference**; blood pressure
- **LFTs** (NAFLD frequently coexists)

**If hyperandrogenism is suspected (young woman):**
- Total and free testosterone, DHEAS, LH/FSH ratio, SHBG, prolactin
- Pelvic ultrasound for polycystic ovaries

**If acromegaly is suspected:**
- **IGF-1 level**; growth hormone suppression test; pituitary MRI

**If red flags for malignancy are present:**
- Full malignancy workup as detailed in §7

**Thyroid:** TSH, anti-TPO (Hashimoto association)

### C. HOMA-IR — Quantifying Insulin Resistance

The **Ho**meostatic **M**odel **A**ssessment for **I**nsulin **R**esistance gives you a single number for how insulin-resistant your patient is.

$$\text{HOMA-IR} = \frac{\text{Fasting glucose (mg/dL)} \times \text{Fasting insulin (µIU/mL)}}{405}$$

**Interpretation:**

| HOMA-IR | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **0.7 – 2.0** | **Normal** |
| **> 2.0** | **Insulin resistance present** |

> ⚠️ **Unit correction:** insulin must be in **µIU/mL** (micro-international units per millilitre). Some notes write "MIU," which would be *milli*-international units — a thousand-fold error. Write **µIU/mL**.

**Where does 405 come from?** It is not arbitrary. It is the product of the *normal* fasting values:

$$90 \text{ mg/dL (normal glucose)} \times 4.5 \text{ µIU/mL (normal insulin)} = 405$$

So the formula is really asking: *"How many times greater is this patient's glucose × insulin product than a healthy person's?"* A perfectly normal person scores **1.0**. That is why the normal range hovers around 1 and why the framework is so intuitive.

**SI-unit version** (if glucose is reported in mmol/L):

$$\text{HOMA-IR} = \frac{\text{Glucose (mmol/L)} \times \text{Insulin (µIU/mL)}}{22.5}$$

And again the constant derives from normals: 5.0 mmol/L × 4.5 µIU/mL = 22.5.

*(Original description: Matthews et al., Diabetologia 1985.)*

**The conceptual beauty:** HOMA-IR captures the *essence* of insulin resistance. A normal person has normal glucose **with** normal insulin. An insulin-resistant person has normal-ish glucose **only because** insulin is sky-high. Looking at glucose alone would miss them entirely; **multiplying** the two unmasks the compensatory effort. HOMA-IR is therefore the perfect laboratory partner to AN — both detect **hyperinsulinemia before frank diabetes appears.** The skin and the number are telling you the same story.

## 11. Differential Diagnosis

| Condition | Distinguishing feature |
|---|---|
| **Terra firma-forme dermatosis** ("Duncan's dirty dermatosis") | Dirty-brown patches that **wipe off entirely with 70% isopropyl alcohol** — the single best bedside test. AN does not wipe off. |
| **Confluent and reticulated papillomatosis of Gougerot–Carteaud** | Reticulated, scaly brown papules/plaques on the **trunk** (upper back, chest, intermammary) rather than flexures; **responds dramatically to minocycline** (or azithromycin) — a therapeutic diagnostic test. |
| **Dowling–Degos disease** | **Reticulate** pigmentation of the flexures; **AD inheritance (KRT5)**; pigment macules, **not thickened/velvety**; comedo-like lesions and pitted perioral scars. |
| **Macular amyloidosis** | Rippled/reticulate brown pigmentation, typically upper back/interscapular, **pruritic**, amyloid on Congo red. |
| **Addison disease** | **Flat**, diffuse hyperpigmentation accentuated in flexures, scars, mucosae; hypotension, fatigue, hyponatraemia. |
| **Pellagra (Casal necklace)** | Photodistributed, well-demarcated, desquamating collar; diarrhoea + dementia; niacin deficiency. |
| **Inverse psoriasis / intertrigo / erythrasma / tinea** | **Erythematous, symptomatic (itchy/sore), often scaly or macerated**; AN is asymptomatic and skin-coloured-to-brown. |
| **Pemphigus vegetans, Hailey–Hailey** | Erosive, vegetating, symptomatic flexural plaques. |
| **Acanthosis nigricans-like drug eruption (nicotinic acid)** | History-dependent. |

> **The unifying bedside discriminator: PALPATE and WIPE.** *Velvety + thickened* points to AN. *Flat* points to a pigmentary mimic. *Wipes off* means terra firma-forme. *Itchy and red* means inflammatory/infective. You can get 80% of the way there before ordering a single test.

## 12. Treatment — Built on Mechanism

The guiding principle, derived directly from pathophysiology:

> **AN is a symptom, not a disease. Treat the *driver* first; treat the *keratin* second.**

### Step 0 — Treat the underlying cause (the only truly curative step)
- **Malignant AN → treat the tumour.** Successful resection can clear the skin completely; recurrence of skin lesions may signal tumour recurrence.
- **Drug-induced → withdraw the offending drug.**
- **Endocrinopathy → treat it** (acromegaly, thyroid disease, PCOS).

### Step 1 — Lifestyle modification *(first and most important intervention in benign AN)*
- **Weight loss** and **dietary modification** (low glycaemic load, calorie restriction)
- **Exercise** — improves insulin sensitivity independent of weight loss
- **Mechanism:** less adiposity → improved insulin sensitivity → lower circulating insulin → less IGF-1R cross-activation → the epidermis quiets down and thins. Weight loss can produce **complete resolution**. Nothing else in the list is as effective.
- Also reduces **friction** and **perspiration** (two of our four predisposing factors).
- **Set expectations:** this is slow — months, not weeks.

### Step 2 — First line topical: **Retinoids**
- **Tretinoin 0.1%** (and lower strengths), **adapalene**
- **Mechanism:** retinoids bind nuclear RAR/RXR receptors → **normalize keratinocyte differentiation**, **reduce corneocyte cohesion** (keratolytic), and **thin the hyperkeratotic stratum corneum**.
- **Because the pigment is optical, thinning the keratin literally removes the darkness.** This is the clearest example of mechanism-directed therapy in the whole topic.
- Counsel about irritation, dryness, and photosensitivity — and **never** in pregnancy.

### Step 3 — Second line: **Topical vitamin D analogues**
- **Calcipotriene (calcipotriol)**
- **Mechanism:** binds the vitamin D receptor → **inhibits keratinocyte proliferation** and **promotes differentiation**; mildly anti-inflammatory.
- Better tolerated than retinoids in thin flexural skin; useful in combination.

### Step 4 — Third line: **Alpha hydroxy acids** and **oral isotretinoin**
- **AHAs (glycolic, lactic acid):** keratolytic — dissolve corneodesmosomes, reduce corneocyte adhesion, thin the corneum.
- **Oral isotretinoin:** for extensive, severe, or refractory disease. Systemic normalization of keratinization. Requires strict **pregnancy prevention** and monitoring of lipids/LFTs.

### Step 5 — Additional and adjunctive options

| Agent / modality | Rationale |
|---|---|
| **Metformin** (oral) | **Insulin sensitizer** — lowers hepatic gluconeogenesis and circulating insulin → removes the IGF-1R drive at its source. **Treats the cause, not just the skin.** First-line systemic choice in insulin-resistant AN and HAIR-AN. |
| **Rosiglitazone** (thiazolidinedione, PPAR-γ agonist) | Also an insulin sensitizer; improves peripheral insulin sensitivity. (Use is limited in practice by cardiovascular safety concerns.) |
| **Acitretin** (oral retinoid) | Systemic retinoid for widespread disease; same mechanism as topical retinoids, systemically. Teratogenic — 3-year contraception requirement. |
| **Urea-based creams / ammonium lactate 12%** | **Keratolytic + humectant**; breaks hydrogen bonds in keratin, softening and thinning the stratum corneum. Gentle and well tolerated for maintenance. |
| **Chemical peels — trichloroacetic acid, salicylic acid** | Controlled chemical exfoliation → physical removal of hyperkeratosis → rapid visible lightening (again: optical). Salicylic acid is a **lipophilic beta-hydroxy acid**, ideal for penetrating thick keratin. |
| **Podophyllin 20%** | **Antimitotic** — binds tubulin, arresting keratinocyte mitosis. An older, second-tier option; use with caution (systemic toxicity). |
| **Retinol-based serums (up to 5%), creams, peels (10%)** | Cosmeceutical retinoids — gentler, better tolerated for long-term maintenance and for flexural skin. |
| **Long-pulsed alexandrite laser (755 nm), 4–8 week intervals** | For resistant, cosmetically distressing disease. Targets and reduces the thickened, hyperkeratotic tissue; also useful for associated hypertrichosis. |
| **Fish oil / omega-3 fatty acids** | Emerging evidence: **reduce hyperkeratosis**; improve insulin sensitivity and exert anti-inflammatory effects via resolvins/protectins. Adjunctive. |
| **Sun protection** | Prevents any additional true melanogenic darkening superimposed on the optical darkening; also mandatory alongside retinoid use. |
| **Reduce friction** | Loose clothing, weight loss, absorbent/antiperspirant measures, treating co-existing intertrigo — removing predisposing factors 3 and 4. |

### A therapeutic reality check
Be honest with patients: topical therapy **improves but rarely cures** AN while the underlying driver persists. The dramatic responders are those who lose substantial weight or whose tumour is removed. **Topicals are cosmetic adjuncts; the metabolic work is the real treatment.**

## 13. Common Misunderstandings — Let's Clear Them All

| ❌ Misconception | ✅ Reality |
|---|---|
| "The dark colour means increased melanin, so I'll prescribe hydroquinone." | **The darkening is optical**, from hyperkeratosis and papillomatosis. Melanocyte number and melanin production are normal. **Bleaching agents do not work.** Use keratolytics and retinoids. |
| "It's called *acanthosis* nigricans, so acanthosis must be the main histological feature." | **Acanthosis is minimal.** The dominant findings are **hyperkeratosis** and **papillomatosis**. The name is historically misleading. |
| "Malignant AN accounts for about 20% of cases." | **Wrong.** Malignant AN is **<5%**; >90% of AN is benign/insulin-related. |
| "AN means the patient has diabetes." | AN indicates **insulin resistance / hyperinsulinemia**, which often **precedes** overt diabetes by years. Many AN patients are **euglycaemic** but hyperinsulinemic. This is precisely what makes AN valuable — it is an **early warning**, before the glucose goes abnormal. |
| "TGF causes malignant AN." | Be specific: **TGF-α** (an EGF-family ligand acting on EGFR). **TGF-β** is a different molecule with largely *inhibitory* epithelial effects. |
| "AN is purely cosmetic; reassure and discharge." | AN is a **cutaneous marker of systemic disease**. Every patient needs metabolic screening; high-risk patients need malignancy screening. |
| "Beare–Stevenson is an FGFR3 disease." | Beare–Stevenson cutis gyrata is **primarily FGFR2**. **SADDAN** and **familial AN** are the **FGFR3** entities (though FGFR-family phenotypes overlap). |
| "Unilateral nevoid AN needs a malignancy workup." | **No.** It is a **mosaic/somatic** disorder following Blaschko's lines — no endocrine or neoplastic association. |
| "All AN is symmetrical and flexural." | Remember the exceptions: **acral AN** (dorsa of hands/feet), **unilateral nevoid AN** (Blaschko-linear), and **malignant AN** (widespread, including mucosae, palms, nails). |
| "Insulin therapy is unrelated to AN." | Exogenous **insulin is a recognized cause** of drug-induced AN — sometimes localized to injection sites. |
| "A negative cancer workup rules out malignant AN." | AN may **precede** the tumour by months. High-risk patients need **ongoing surveillance**, not a single negative scan. |
| "Benign AN can't itch, so pruritus is irrelevant." | Pruritus is uncharacteristic of benign AN but **does occur in malignant AN** — treat it as a red flag, not a nuisance. |

## 14. High-Yield Exam Distillate

Commit these to memory:

1. **Definition:** acquired, asymptomatic, symmetrical, velvety, thickened hyperpigmented flexural plaques with acrochordons.
2. **Epidemiology:** benign ≫ malignant (**<5%**); any age; M = F; commoner in dark skin.
3. **Core mechanism:** hyperinsulinemia → insulin cross-activates **IGF-1 receptors** on keratinocytes → proliferation.
4. **Malignant mechanism:** tumour-derived **TGF-α** → keratinocyte **EGFR**.
5. **Genetic:** **FGFR3** activating mutations → familial AN (AD), **SADDAN**; Beare–Stevenson (mainly FGFR2).
6. **Predisposing factors:** insulin resistance, obesity, friction, perspiration.
7. **HAIR-AN:** **H**yperandrogenism + **I**nsulin **R**esistance + **A**canthosis **N**igricans (± PCOS).
8. **Commonest associated malignancy:** **gastric adenocarcinoma** (~50–60%). Malignant AN prognosis: **~2-year** average survival.
9. **Tripe palms:** ~75% of malignant AN; **with AN → gastric; alone → lung**. **Leser–Trélat** may co-occur.
10. **Histology:** **hyperkeratosis + papillomatosis + minimal acanthosis**. **Pigmentation is optical; melanocytes are normal.**
11. **HOMA-IR** = [glucose (mg/dL) × insulin (µIU/mL)] / **405**; normal **0.7–2.0**; **>2 = insulin resistance**. SI version: ÷ **22.5**.
12. **Treatment ladder:** lifestyle → **topical retinoids (tretinoin 0.1%, adapalene)** → **calcipotriene** → **AHAs / isotretinoin**; plus **metformin**, peels, urea/ammonium lactate 12%, alexandrite laser, omega-3s.
13. **Bedside discriminators:** wipes off with alcohol → terra firma-forme; truncal reticulate + minocycline-responsive → Gougerot–Carteaud; flat pigment in flexures → Dowling–Degos or Addison.

**Three sentences to carry out of this lecture:**
