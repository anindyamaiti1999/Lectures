# 🎓 Mini-Lecture: The Management of Stable Psoriasis, with a Deep Dive into Phototherapy

---

## PART 0 — Setting the Stage: Why This Question Is Really Two Questions

Let me start by telling you what the examiner is actually asking, because students often lose marks here by answering only half.

"**Discuss the management of stable psoriasis**" wants a *therapeutic ladder* — an ordered, severity-matched strategy. "**Add a note on phototherapy**" wants *mechanistic and practical depth* on ultraviolet light as a treatment modality. So your answer must have two gears: a **wide-angle lens** (the whole ladder) and then a **zoom lens** (UV light in fine detail).

And before either, we need to understand one word in the question that carries enormous clinical weight.

### What does "stable" mean, and why does it matter so much?

"Stable" plaque psoriasis means the disease is **quiescent in behaviour**, even if extensive in area. Plaques are:

- **Well-demarcated** — sharp borders, not blurring outward
- **Not spreading rapidly**
- **Not erythrodermic** (not >90% confluent redness)
- **Not pustular**
- **Not tender, oedematous, or "angry"**
- **No active Koebner phenomenon** — i.e., trauma is not seeding new lesions

Contrast that with **unstable psoriasis**: plaques with indistinct, advancing edges, new lesions erupting, marked erythema, systemic upset. This distinction is not academic pedantry — it **determines what you are allowed to do to the patient.**

> **Why it matters mechanistically:** In unstable disease, the skin is in a hyper-reactive, Koebner-positive state. Any irritant or injurious stimulus — dithranol, strong coal tar, a phototoxic UV burn — can act as a *trauma signal* and trigger an explosive flare, potentially tipping the patient into erythroderma or generalised pustular psoriasis. This is why **phototherapy, dithranol, and irritant topicals are essentially contraindicated in unstable psoriasis**, while they are mainstays in stable psoriasis.

**Analogy:** Stable psoriasis is a *smouldering* fire — you can approach it, poke it, apply targeted measures. Unstable psoriasis is a fire in a room full of petrol vapour — strike a match (UV burn, irritant) and the whole room goes up. So the very first cognitive step in any psoriasis management question is: *Is this fire smouldering or is it about to explode?*

### A 60-second refresher on the pathogenesis — because every drug maps onto it

You cannot reason about psoriasis therapy without the immunological circuit diagram. Here it is in cause→effect form:

1. **Trigger** (genetic predisposition — *HLA-Cw6*, *IL23R*, *CARD14* — plus environment: stress, trauma, streptococcal infection, drugs, smoking, alcohol).
2. **Plasmacytoid dendritic cells** are activated, partly by **LL-37 (cathelicidin)–self-DNA complexes**, releasing **interferon-α**.
3. **Myeloid dendritic cells** mature and secrete **IL-23** and **IL-12**.
4. IL-23 drives **Th17 / Tc17 cells** (and IL-12 drives Th1), which pour out **IL-17A, IL-22, IL-17F, TNF-α, IFN-γ**.
5. These cytokines hit the **keratinocyte**, which responds by:
   - **Proliferating** (turnover time falls from ~28 days to ~4 days → clinical **thick plaque**)
   - **Failing to differentiate properly** (parakeratosis, loss of granular layer → clinical **silvery scale**)
   - **Secreting chemokines** (CXCL1/8 → neutrophils → **Munro's microabscesses**, spongiform pustules of Kogoj) and **antimicrobial peptides**
   - Driving **angiogenesis** via VEGF (dilated, tortuous dermal papillary vessels → clinical **erythema** and **Auspitz sign**)
6. The whole thing is a **self-amplifying feed-forward loop**: keratinocyte products recruit more immune cells, which make more cytokines, which stimulate more keratinocytes.

**Hold that loop in your mind**, because every single therapy we discuss works by cutting one of its wires:

| Where we cut the loop | Therapy |
|---|---|
| Barrier/scale (physical layer) | Emollients, keratolytics (salicylic acid, urea) |
| Generic inflammation (transcriptional) | Corticosteroids |
| Keratinocyte proliferation & differentiation | Vitamin D analogues, tazarotene, dithranol, tar |
| T-cell survival (delete the effector) | **Phototherapy**, ciclosporin |
| DNA synthesis of proliferating cells | Methotrexate, PUVA |
| Specific cytokines | Biologics (TNF, IL-12/23, IL-17, IL-23) |
| Intracellular signalling | Apremilast (PDE4), deucravacitinib (TYK2) |

Notice where phototherapy sits: it is a **T-cell–deleting therapy delivered non-invasively through the skin surface**. That single sentence explains almost everything about its efficacy, its dosing logic, and its risks.

## PART 1 — THE MANAGEMENT LADDER

### Step 0: Assess before you treat

Before prescribing, you must answer three questions, because they select the rung of the ladder:

1. **How severe?**
   - **BSA (body surface area)** — palm + digits ≈ 1%.
   - **PASI (Psoriasis Area and Severity Index)** — 0–72, scores erythema, induration (thickness), desquamation (scale) across four body regions weighted by area. **PASI-75** = 75% reduction from baseline, the classic trial endpoint.
   - **DLQI (Dermatology Life Quality Index)** — 0–30, the *patient's* verdict.
   - **"Rule of tens"**: BSA >10%, or PASI >10, or DLQI >10 ⇒ **severe** ⇒ think systemic/phototherapy.
   - But also remember **site-based severity**: 3% BSA on the palms, soles, face, scalp, or genitals can be *functionally devastating* and justifies systemic therapy even with a low PASI.

2. **What subtype and is it stable?** (chronic plaque, guttate, inverse/flexural, palmoplantar, scalp, nail, erythrodermic, pustular)

3. **Any comorbidities or red flags?**
   - **Psoriatic arthritis** (screen all patients — PEST/PsAQ questionnaires). Its presence *pulls you up the ladder* to methotrexate or biologics, because topicals and phototherapy do nothing for joints.
   - **Metabolic syndrome, obesity, NAFLD, dyslipidaemia, type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular risk** — psoriasis is a systemic inflammatory disease; the "psoriatic march" links skin inflammation to insulin resistance and atherosclerosis.
   - **Depression, anxiety, alcohol excess, smoking.**
   - **Pregnancy plans** — vetoes methotrexate, acitretin, and modifies everything.

### Step 1: Lifestyle, supportive care, and the foundations

These are listed first in the source and are *not* filler — they are the substrate on which everything else works.

**Regular moisturisation (emollients).**
*What:* Bland occlusives/humectants — white soft paraffin, emulsifying ointment, urea 5–10%, glycerol-based creams.
*Mechanism:* Psoriatic skin has a defective barrier with high transepidermal water loss. Dry, fissured scale is itchy and *irritating* — and irritation is a pro-inflammatory, Koebner-like stimulus. Emollients restore the lipid barrier, hydrate the corneocytes, **soften and loosen scale**, reduce pruritus, and reduce the need for steroid.
*Why it also matters technically:* Hydrated, descaled skin is **optically clearer** — which, as we'll see, directly improves UV penetration.
*Analogy:* You cannot paint a wall well until you have sanded off the flaking old paint. Emollients are the sanding.

**Avoid known triggers.**
- **Stress** — activates the HPA axis and cutaneous neuropeptides (substance P, CGRP) that amplify neurogenic inflammation. Hence relaxation techniques, CBT, sleep hygiene.
- **Smoking** — strongly associated with psoriasis incidence and severity, and especially with **palmoplantar pustulosis**; also blunts response to therapy.
- **Alcohol** — worsens disease, impairs adherence, and is a hard contraindication-modifier for methotrexate (hepatotoxicity).
- **Trauma/friction** — the **Koebner phenomenon**; avoid scratching, harsh scrubbing, tight clothing over plaques.
- **Drugs** — the classic mnemonic list: **β-blockers, lithium, antimalarials (hydroxychloroquine), NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, interferons, and abrupt systemic corticosteroid withdrawal** (which can precipitate pustular psoriasis — a major reason we avoid oral steroids in psoriasis).
- **Infection** — streptococcal pharyngitis (guttate flares), HIV.
- **Obesity** — adipose tissue is an IL-6/TNF-α factory; weight loss measurably improves PASI and improves biologic response.

**Patient education.**
*Why it matters:* Psoriasis is **chronic, remitting–relapsing, and currently not curable**. Patients who expect cure become therapeutic nomads; patients who understand "control, not cure" engage with maintenance. Education must explicitly cover: it is **not contagious**, it is **not caused by poor hygiene**, it is **genetically influenced**, and it is a **systemic** disease (hence the joint and cardiometabolic screening).

**Adherence to therapy.**
*Why it matters so much:* Adherence to topical psoriasis therapy in real life is dismal — often 50–60%, falling with treatment duration. Reasons: greasy vehicles, time burden, large BSA, "steroid phobia," and the fact that the disease returns. Strategies: choose acceptable vehicles (foams, gels, sprays for scalp; ointments for thick plaques), simplify to once-daily where possible, use fixed-dose combinations, schedule early review (2–4 weeks) to reinforce, and address steroid fears explicitly.

> **The recurring theme:** an elegant prescription that the patient does not apply has zero efficacy. Adherence is not a soft skill — it is pharmacology's rate-limiting step.

### Step 2: Topical Therapies — first-line for mild-to-moderate disease

Topicals are appropriate as **sole therapy for mild-to-moderate stable plaque psoriasis (roughly <5–10% BSA)** and as **adjuncts at every higher rung**. Their fundamental advantage is that the drug goes where the disease is, with negligible systemic exposure; their fundamental limitation is that you cannot practically treat 40% of the body surface with a cream twice daily.

#### 2a. Topical corticosteroids

*What they are:* Synthetic glucocorticoids, classified by **potency** (the source specifies medium/high/low, mapping onto the WHO/Stoughton–Cornell classes I–VII or the UK mild/moderate/potent/very potent scheme).

- **Mild** — hydrocortisone 1%
- **Moderate** — clobetasone butyrate 0.05%, betamethasone valerate 0.025%
- **Potent** — betamethasone valerate 0.1%, mometasone furoate 0.1%, fluocinonide
- **Very potent** — clobetasol propionate 0.05%, betamethasone dipropionate in optimised vehicle

*Mechanism — a genuinely multi-layered story:*
1. **Genomic (slow, hours–days):** The steroid diffuses through the membrane, binds the cytoplasmic **glucocorticoid receptor**, displaces HSP90, and the complex translocates to the nucleus. There it (a) **transactivates** anti-inflammatory genes (annexin A1/lipocortin, IκB, IL-10) via GRE binding, and (b) more importantly, **transrepresses** NF-κB and AP-1, shutting down transcription of TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6, IL-8, IL-17, and iNOS.
2. **Antiproliferative:** Inhibits keratinocyte and fibroblast mitosis → **thins the plaque** (and, with overuse, thins the normal skin too — same mechanism, unwanted site).
3. **Vasoconstrictive:** Reduces capillary dilatation → visibly **blanches the erythema** (this is literally how potency is assayed — the *vasoconstrictor assay*).
4. **Immunosuppressive:** Reduces Langerhans cell number and function, induces T-cell apoptosis.

*Net clinical effect:* fastest reduction of **erythema, induration, and pruritus** of any topical. This is why steroids are the workhorse for *rapid* control.

*Why combine with salicylic acid (as the source notes):* Salicylic acid (2–6%) is a **β-hydroxy acid keratolytic**. It dissolves intercellular cement and reduces corneocyte cohesion, **shedding the hyperkeratotic scale**. Two consequences follow: (1) the plaque immediately looks and feels better, and (2) — crucially — **removing the scale removes the diffusion barrier**, so the steroid actually reaches the living epidermis. On a thick knee or elbow plaque, a keratolytic can be the difference between a steroid working and not working.

*Practical rules and pitfalls:*
- **Site-match potency:** very potent on palms/soles/scalp/thick plaques; mild only on face, flexures, genitals (thin stratum corneum + occlusion = high absorption).
- **Fingertip unit (FTU):** ~0.5 g, covers two adult palms — use it to quantify prescriptions.
- **Tachyphylaxis:** diminishing response with continuous use, partly receptor downregulation, partly adherence decay. Combat with **weekend/pulse ("weekend therapy") regimens** or rotation with a vitamin D analogue.
- **Local adverse effects:** atrophy, striae (irreversible), telangiectasia, purpura, hypertrichosis, hypopigmentation, perioral dermatitis, acneiform eruption, tinea incognito, delayed wound healing, **and steroid-induced rebound/pustular flare on abrupt withdrawal**.
- **Systemic effects with very potent steroid over large BSA:** HPA-axis suppression, iatrogenic Cushing's, growth retardation in children. Rough guidance: >50 g/week of very potent steroid for prolonged periods is risky.
- **Never** use systemic corticosteroids to control chronic plaque psoriasis — withdrawal can precipitate **generalised pustular psoriasis (von Zumbusch)** or erythroderma.

#### 2b. Vitamin D analogues

*What:* **Calcipotriol (calcipotriene)**, **calcitriol**, **tacalcitol**, **maxacalcitol** — synthetic analogues of 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D₃ engineered to retain skin activity while minimising calcaemic effect.

*Mechanism (cause → effect → outcome):*
- The analogue binds the nuclear **vitamin D receptor (VDR)**, which heterodimerises with **RXR** and binds VDREs in DNA.
- **Effect 1 — antiproliferative:** Downregulates c-myc and cyclins, upregulates p21/p27 ⇒ **keratinocytes stop dividing**.
- **Effect 2 — pro-differentiating:** Upregulates involucrin, transglutaminase, filaggrin ⇒ keratinocytes **mature properly**, restoring the granular layer and normal cornification. This directly corrects the parakeratosis that generates scale.
- **Effect 3 — immunomodulatory:** Inhibits dendritic cell maturation, suppresses **IL-17/IL-23 axis** signalling, promotes regulatory T cells.
- **Outcome:** progressive flattening of the plaque and normalisation of the scale — but **slowly**, over 4–8 weeks. It is a *remodelling* drug, not a *firefighting* drug.

*Why the source says "maintain remission and [reduce] persistent plaque buildup":* Exactly — vitamin D analogues shine in **maintenance**. They have **no atrophogenic potential**, so they can be used continuously for months without thinning the skin. Steroids get you clear; vitamin D analogues keep you clear.

*The best-evidence strategy — fixed-dose combination:*
**Calcipotriol + betamethasone dipropionate** (ointment, gel, foam) is superior to either agent alone and is arguably the single most effective topical regimen for plaque psoriasis. Why it works synergistically:
- The steroid gives rapid anti-inflammatory and anti-erythema effect; the analogue gives antiproliferative/differentiating effect. **Different mechanisms, additive efficacy.**
- The steroid **suppresses the irritation** that calcipotriol alone can cause.
- The analogue **counteracts the steroid's atrophogenic effect** (vitamin D stimulates collagen synthesis).
- Formulating them together solves a chemical problem: calcipotriol is **unstable at acid pH** and betamethasone at alkaline pH, so they were traditionally applied at separate times of day; the combination vehicle stabilises both at an intermediate pH.

*Cautions:*
- **Irritation**, especially on the face and flexures — avoid there.
- **Dose ceiling** to avoid hypercalcaemia: conventionally **≤100 g/week** of calcipotriol 50 µg/g in adults.
- ⚠️ **High-yield interaction with phototherapy:** **Calcipotriol is degraded by UV light, especially UVA and broadband UVB.** If a patient is on combined calcipotriol + phototherapy, the analogue must be applied **after** the UV session (or at least several hours before is avoided) — otherwise you are simply photodegrading your drug. Many units instruct: *no calcipotriol on the skin at the time of irradiation.*

#### 2c. Topical calcineurin inhibitors

*What:* **Tacrolimus 0.03–0.1% ointment**, **pimecrolimus 1% cream**.

*Mechanism:* They bind cytosolic immunophilins (**FKBP-12** for tacrolimus, macrophilin-12 for pimecrolimus). The drug–immunophilin complex inhibits **calcineurin**, the Ca²⁺/calmodulin-dependent phosphatase that dephosphorylates **NFAT**. Without dephosphorylation, NFAT cannot enter the nucleus, so transcription of **IL-2** (and IL-4, IL-5, IFN-γ) fails ⇒ **T cells cannot be activated or clonally expand**.

*Why specifically for face and genitals (the source's point):*
- Calcineurin inhibitors cause **no skin atrophy, no striae, no telangiectasia, no glaucoma/cataract risk** — the exact adverse effects that make potent steroids dangerous on thin, occluded, cosmetically critical skin.
- They therefore become the **steroid-sparing agent of choice for facial, intertriginous/inverse, and genital psoriasis**, and for periocular disease.
- **Pharmacokinetic point:** tacrolimus is a large molecule (MW ~822) and penetrates normal thick stratum corneum poorly — which is precisely why it works better on **thin skin and moist flexures** (where absorption is high and scale is minimal) than on thick knee plaques. The anatomy explains the indication.

*Adverse effects:* transient **burning/stinging** in the first few days (mediated by substance P release from sensory nerves — it typically settles, and counselling about this dramatically improves adherence), alcohol-related flushing, folliculitis. There is a **black-box warning** regarding theoretical lymphoma/skin cancer risk; long-term registry data have not confirmed it, but it is prudent to **avoid concomitant phototherapy on calcineurin-inhibitor–treated skin**.

#### 2d. Topical coal tar

*What:* Crude coal tar (1–5%), or refined preparations (liquor picis carbonis, tar shampoos, tar-in-oil bath additives).

*Mechanism:* Tar is a complex mixture of >10,000 polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons. Its key molecular action is **activation of the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR)** in keratinocytes, which:
- **Induces terminal differentiation** and upregulates **filaggrin** — literally repairing the barrier defect (this was shown elegantly in atopic dermatitis but applies to keratinisation broadly);
- **Inhibits DNA synthesis** ⇒ antiproliferative, thinning the plaque;
- Suppresses **Th2/Th17 cytokine** signalling;
- Provides **antipruritic** and **antiseptic** effects.

*So the source's "reduce scaling and inflammation" is accurate*, and now you know the receptor doing it.

*Downsides:* smell, staining of skin/clothes/bath, **folliculitis ("tar acne")**, irritation, **photosensitivity** (relevant below!), and theoretical carcinogenicity (PAHs are mutagenic; however, decades of cohort data in psoriasis patients have not demonstrated a clear increase in cutaneous malignancy at therapeutic doses).

*Historical regimens worth knowing for exams:*
- **Goeckerman regimen** = crude coal tar + **UVB**. Tar is a photosensitiser and antiproliferative; UVB is lymphocytotoxic. Highly effective for severe stable plaque psoriasis, now mostly confined to day-treatment centres because of the labour and mess.
- **Ingram regimen** = tar bath + **dithranol (anthralin)** paste + **UVB**.

#### 2e. Other topicals the examiner will reward you for adding

| Agent | Mechanism | Key practical point |
|---|---|---|
| **Dithranol (anthralin)** | Generates free radicals, inhibits mitochondrial respiration and DNA replication; antiproliferative | Very effective, **long remissions**; but stains and burns normal skin — hence "**short-contact therapy**" (10–30 min then wash off). **Contraindicated in unstable/pustular psoriasis and on the face/flexures.** |
| **Tazarotene 0.05–0.1%** | Third-generation retinoid → RAR-β/γ → normalises differentiation, reduces proliferation and inflammation | Longer remission than steroid alone; **irritant** — combine with a steroid to improve tolerability. **Teratogenic — avoid in pregnancy.** |
| **Keratolytics: salicylic acid, urea 10–40%, lactic acid, propylene glycol** | Reduce corneocyte cohesion, hydrate | Descale *before* other actives. ⚠️ **Salicylic acid absorbs UV** — it must be washed off before phototherapy or it will block your treatment. Avoid extensive SA in children (salicylism). |
| **Roflumilast 0.3% cream** (newer) | Topical PDE-4 inhibitor → ↑cAMP → ↓TNF/IL-17 | Non-steroidal, safe in **intertriginous** sites |
| **Tapinarof 1% cream** (newer) | Topical **AhR agonist** — the "designer tar" | Cosmetically acceptable AhR pathway therapy; folliculitis is the main issue |

**Site-specific pearls:**
- **Scalp:** steroid shampoos/lotions/foams, tar shampoo, salicylic-acid oil overnight to descale, calcipotriol+betamethasone gel.
- **Flexural/inverse:** mild steroid + calcineurin inhibitor; avoid tar/dithranol/tazarotene.
- **Nail:** intralesional triamcinolone, potent steroid + calcipotriol under occlusion; poor topical response generally means **systemic** therapy (nail disease also predicts psoriatic arthritis).
- **Palmoplantar:** very potent steroid under occlusion, **acitretin**, **local PUVA** (hand-and-foot units).

### Step 3: Phototherapy — the bridge rung

Phototherapy occupies a strategic position on the ladder: **more effective and more practical than topicals for widespread disease, but without the systemic organ toxicity of drugs.**

**Indications (the "when to step up to light"):**
- Moderate-to-severe **stable** plaque psoriasis that has failed or is impractical for topical therapy
- Extensive **guttate** psoriasis (responds beautifully)
- Patients who wish to avoid systemic immunosuppression
- **Pregnancy** — ⭐ **NB-UVB is one of the safest options in pregnancy**, since there is no systemic drug exposure (though folate levels should be monitored, as UV degrades folate)
- Combination with topicals or low-dose systemics to spare doses of both

We will unpack the mechanism, dosing, and risks in exhaustive detail in Part 2. For now, note where it sits in the sequence, and note the source's framing: **NB-UVB "maintains clearance and prevents flares"**, while **PUVA is used more selectively** ("rare but effective in some cases" — reflecting modern practice, where PUVA has largely been displaced to second line by NB-UVB's superior safety profile, and by biologics).

### Step 4: Conventional Systemic Therapy

Indicated for **severe** disease (rule of tens), **phototherapy failure or inaccessibility**, **psoriatic arthritis**, **erythrodermic/pustular** variants, or psoriasis at disabling sites.

#### Methotrexate
*Mechanism:* Competitively inhibits **dihydrofolate reductase** ⇒ ↓tetrahydrofolate ⇒ ↓thymidylate and purine synthesis ⇒ inhibits DNA synthesis in rapidly dividing cells (keratinocytes and, importantly, activated lymphocytes). Its dominant **anti-inflammatory** action at psoriasis doses is via inhibition of **AICAR transformylase** ⇒ intracellular AICAR accumulates ⇒ **adenosine release** ⇒ adenosine A2a-receptor-mediated suppression of neutrophil and T-cell function. It also induces T-cell apoptosis.
*Dose:* 7.5–25 mg **once weekly** (never daily — fatal dosing errors occur here), oral or subcutaneous, with **folic acid 5 mg** supplementation on non-MTX days.
*Advantage:* treats **skin *and* joints**; cheap.
*Monitoring:* FBC (myelosuppression), LFTs, U&E; **procollagen-III-N-peptide (PIIINP)** or **transient elastography/FibroScan** for hepatic fibrosis; consider baseline CXR (pneumonitis).
*Contraindications:* **pregnancy and conception (both sexes — teratogenic; wash-out required)**, significant liver disease, alcohol excess, severe renal impairment, cytopenias, active infection. ⚠️ **Drug interactions:** trimethoprim/co-trimoxazole (pancytopenia), NSAIDs, probenecid, penicillins (↓renal clearance).

#### Acitretin
*Mechanism:* A second-generation **systemic retinoid**, the active metabolite of etretinate. Binds **RAR** ⇒ normalises keratinocyte proliferation, differentiation, and cornification; anti-inflammatory; reduces neutrophil chemotaxis.
*Best indications:* ⭐ **pustular psoriasis** (including palmoplantar) and **erythrodermic psoriasis** — it is arguably the drug of choice here; also hyperkeratotic palmoplantar psoriasis and psoriasis in HIV-positive patients (**not immunosuppressive** — a big advantage).
*Dose:* 10–50 mg/day (often start low, 10–25 mg, to avoid the initial "pseudo-flare").
*Adverse effects:* mucocutaneous dryness (cheilitis is near-universal), hair thinning, **hyperlipidaemia**, transaminitis, myalgia, and with long-term use **DISH/skeletal hyperostosis**; **benign intracranial hypertension** if combined with tetracyclines (⚠️ never co-prescribe).
*Critical counselling:* ⭐⭐ **Severely teratogenic. Acitretin is re-esterified to etretinate (especially with alcohol), which has a very long half-life. Pregnancy must be avoided for 3 years (2 years in some jurisdictions) after stopping**, and alcohol must be avoided during and for 2 months after therapy. Blood donation is prohibited for 3 years.

#### Ciclosporin
*Mechanism:* Calcineurin inhibition (as above, but systemic) ⇒ ↓IL-2 ⇒ profound T-cell suppression.
*Role:* Fast, powerful **"rescue"/induction** therapy for severe unstable or erythrodermic flares; typically 2.5–5 mg/kg/day for **short courses (≤1–2 years)**.
*Toxicity:* dose-dependent **nephrotoxicity** (interstitial fibrosis), **hypertension**, hypertrichosis, gingival hyperplasia, tremor, hypomagnesaemia, hyperkalaemia, hyperuricaemia, and **↑skin cancer risk**.
⚠️ **Never combine ciclosporin with phototherapy or PUVA** — the additive carcinogenic risk (immunosuppression + UV mutagenesis) is unacceptable. Also avoid using it in patients with a heavy prior PUVA load.

#### Others
- **Fumaric acid esters / dimethyl fumarate** — widely used in Europe; Nrf2 activation, Th2 shift; flushing, GI upset, lymphopenia.
- **Apremilast** — oral **PDE-4 inhibitor** ⇒ ↑cAMP ⇒ ↓TNF-α, IL-17, IL-23; modest efficacy but very safe (no lab monitoring); nausea, diarrhoea, weight loss, headache, depression.
- **Deucravacitinib** — oral allosteric **TYK2** inhibitor binding the pseudokinase domain ⇒ selectively blocks IL-23/IL-12/type-I-IFN signalling; more effective than apremilast, favourable safety.
- **Hydroxyurea, 6-thioguanine, azathioprine** — historical/rare.

### Step 5: Biologics — targeted cytokine blockade

These are for **moderate-to-severe psoriasis failing, contraindicated to, or intolerant of phototherapy and conventional systemics** (and first-line in some settings with significant PsA).

| Target | Agents | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **TNF-α** | Etanercept (receptor fusion), infliximab, adalimumab, certolizumab | Excellent for **joints**; certolizumab (lacks Fc, minimal placental transfer) is preferred in **pregnancy**. Risk: **reactivation of latent TB**, hepatitis B, demyelination, paradoxical psoriasis. |
| **IL-12/23 (p40)** | Ustekinumab | Convenient 12-weekly dosing; very durable |
| **IL-17A** | Secukinumab, ixekizumab | Very rapid, very high PASI-90/100 rates. ⚠️ **Worsens inflammatory bowel disease** (IL-17 is gut-protective) and predisposes to **mucocutaneous candidiasis** (IL-17 guards mucosal antifungal defence) |
| **IL-17 receptor A** | Brodalumab | Blocks A, C, E, F; black-box suicidality warning |
| **IL-17A/F** | Bimekizumab | Highest skin clearance rates; more candidiasis |
| **IL-23 (p19)** | Guselkumab, risankizumab, tildrakizumab | ⭐ Best current **efficacy-to-safety-to-durability** ratio; hits the *master cytokine upstream* of the Th17 loop, so remissions outlast drug levels |

**Mandatory pre-biologic screening:** **latent TB** (IGRA/Mantoux + CXR), **hepatitis B and C**, **HIV**, vaccination status (**live vaccines contraindicated once on therapy** — give them before), pregnancy status, malignancy history, and for IL-17 agents, **history of IBD**.

**Why the ladder order matters:** biologics are extraordinarily effective but expensive, require injections, need infection surveillance, and lose efficacy over time through anti-drug antibodies. Hence the sequence: exhaust the safer, cheaper rungs first — **unless** severity, PsA, or comorbidity justifies going straight up.

### Step 6: Excimer Laser (308 nm) — *targeted* phototherapy

The source lists this separately, and rightly so, because conceptually it is a hybrid: it is phototherapy, but it behaves like a *localised procedure*.

- **What it is:** A xenon-chloride (XeCl) **excimer** ("excited dimer") laser or lamp emitting a **monochromatic, coherent 308 nm** UVB beam — which sits almost exactly on the **peak of the therapeutic action spectrum** (311–313 nm) while being deliverable through a handpiece.
- **The core trick:** because the beam is aimed **only at the plaque**, surrounding normal skin receives **zero dose**. This decouples the two things that normally limit UVB dosing. In whole-body cabinets, the dose is capped by the tolerance of the *normal* skin between plaques. Remove the normal skin from the equation and you can escalate to **supra-erythemogenic doses** — several multiples of MED — that would blister a whole-body-irradiated patient.
- **Why that matters biologically:** Psoriatic plaques are **relatively UV-resistant** (thick, scaly, high turnover). They need a *bigger* dose than normal skin can tolerate. The excimer laser delivers exactly that mismatch.
- **Dosing:** the source's "**about 3 MED**" is correct and standard (typical range 2–4 MED, titrated by plaque thickness and response); side effects (blistering, hyperpigmentation, pain) drove the shift from very high initial doses to **medium doses**.
- **Course:** the source says "**average ~6 treatments delivered biweekly**" — i.e., **two sessions per week**, meaning ~3 weeks to notable response; in practice courses often run 8–15 sessions for full clearance. The efficiency (few sessions, high clearance) is the selling point.
- **Best indications (exactly as the source says):** **stable, localised, recalcitrant** plaques — classically **elbows, knees**, scalp margins, palmoplantar disease, and **limited plaques in patients you don't want to irradiate whole-body**. Also excellent for **vitiligo** and localised alopecia areata.
- **Limitations:** impractical for extensive disease (spot-by-spot treatment), costly, operator-dependent.

**Analogy:** Whole-body NB-UVB is **floodlighting a stadium** to illuminate a few players — you must keep the brightness low enough not to dazzle the crowd. The excimer laser is a **spotlight** — full brightness on the target, darkness for everyone else.

> **Resolving an apparent contradiction (a favourite exam trap):** How can UVB dosing be *sub*erythemogenic in a cabinet but *supra*erythemogenic with the excimer laser? Because the constraint is **collateral exposure**, not the plaque. Same photon, same chromophore, same mechanism — different geometry.

### Step 7: Photodynamic Therapy (PDT) in psoriasis

The source positions PDT as "useful in chronic stable psoriasis." Let me give you the mechanism carefully, then be honest about its real-world place.

**The three obligatory ingredients of PDT** (remember these three and you can derive everything):
1. A **photosensitiser** that accumulates preferentially in target cells
2. **Light** of a wavelength matching the photosensitiser's absorption peak
3. **Molecular oxygen** in the tissue

**The mechanistic chain:**
1. A **prodrug** — **5-aminolaevulinic acid (5-ALA)** or its lipophilic ester **methyl aminolevulinate (MAL)** — is applied topically.
2. It enters the haem biosynthesis pathway, bypassing the rate-limiting enzyme ALA synthase. Cells convert it to **protoporphyrin IX (PpIX)**, the actual photosensitiser.
3. **Selectivity arises here:** hyperproliferative, metabolically active cells (psoriatic keratinocytes, tumour cells) take up more ALA (leaky barrier, altered transporters) and have **relatively deficient ferrochelatase**, the enzyme that would insert iron into PpIX to make haem. So PpIX **accumulates** in the diseased cells and not in normal ones.
4. Light (**red ~630 nm** for depth, or **blue ~415 nm** — the Soret band — for superficial lesions) is absorbed by PpIX, exciting it to a triplet state.
5. The excited PpIX transfers energy to **O₂**, generating **singlet oxygen (¹O₂)** and other **reactive oxygen species** — a **Type II photodynamic reaction**.
6. ROS oxidise membrane lipids, mitochondrial proteins, and DNA ⇒ **apoptosis and necrosis of the hyperproliferative keratinocytes**, plus vascular shutdown and a local immune-modulating effect.

**Analogy:** PDT is a **guided munition with a two-stage fuse**. The prodrug is inert until the cell's own metabolism arms it (stage 1 — biochemical targeting), and it remains inert until you shine light on it (stage 2 — spatial/temporal targeting). Nothing happens where there is no PpIX *and* no light. Elegant.

**Photosensitiser generations (as the source structures it):**

| Generation | Example | Problem/advantage |
|---|---|---|
| **1st** | **Haematoporphyrin derivative / porfimer sodium** | Effective but causes **prolonged generalised cutaneous photosensitivity lasting weeks** — patients had to avoid sunlight for 4–6 weeks. Impractical. |
| **2nd** | **Verteporfin** (the source's "verteporphyrin"; correct INN is **verteporfin**, trade name Visudyne), plus **5-ALA / MAL** | **Much shorter photosensitivity**, better tissue selectivity, defined chemistry. Verteporfin is a benzoporphyrin derivative best known for ocular use in choroidal neovascularisation, and has been explored in psoriasis. |

**Light sources:** **lasers** (coherent, monochromatic, fibre-deliverable), **LED arrays** (the practical workhorse — uniform, cheap, large-field), broadband filtered lamps, and **daylight PDT** (used for actinic keratoses).

**What actually happens in psoriasis:** the source notes "notable improvement with 5-aminolaevulinic acid" — true in small studies, and PDT is mechanistically attractive. **But you must know the caveats:**
- **Pain is the limiting factor.** ALA-PDT of psoriatic plaques is often intensely painful (ROS-induced nociceptor activation in a densely innervated, inflamed plaque).
- **Results are inconsistent** and response rates are inferior to NB-UVB; plaque thickness impairs both ALA penetration and light penetration.
- Therefore **PDT is *not* a standard, first-line, or even commonly used treatment for plaque psoriasis.** It remains a **niche/experimental option** for limited, recalcitrant plaques. Its established dermatological indications are **actinic keratoses, Bowen's disease, superficial BCC**, and acne.

**Side effects (as listed):** warmth, stinging, burning during illumination; a **sunburn-like phototoxic reaction** with erythema, oedema, crusting; post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation. Patients must **avoid bright sunlight for 2–3 days** and wear **sunglasses** because residual photosensitiser makes skin and eyes vulnerable.

## PART 2 — THE NOTE ON PHOTOTHERAPY (in full depth)

### 2.1 The founding observation

The source opens with the key clinical fact: **sunlight benefits psoriasis.** Patients have known for millennia that psoriasis improves at the seaside and in summer (**climatotherapy**, classically at the Dead Sea — low altitude means a longer atmospheric path that filters harmful short UVB while transmitting therapeutic wavelengths, plus high mineral content and low humidity).

Phototherapy is simply the **rationalisation of that observation**: isolate the wavelengths that heal, discard those that merely burn and mutate, and deliver a measured, reproducible dose.

**And the important exception:** the source notes that **~5% of psoriasis patients are photosensitive** — their psoriasis is *worsened* by sunlight. This subgroup:
- Often has coexisting **polymorphic light eruption** or a photoaggravated pattern
- May show **Koebner-like induction** of plaques in sun-exposed sites
- **Must be identified before starting phototherapy**, or you will make them worse

Hence the pre-treatment history should always ask: *"Does sunlight help or harm your skin?"* If the answer is "harm," think photosensitivity, consider phototesting, and screen for **connective tissue disease (ANA, anti-Ro)** and **porphyria** before irradiating anyone.

### 2.3 UVB Phototherapy — the workhorse

#### Why narrowband beat broadband: the action-spectrum story

This is one of the most instructive stories in dermatologic therapeutics, so let me walk you through the logic.

Researchers irradiated psoriatic plaques with narrow wavebands and asked two separate questions:
- **Which wavelengths clear psoriasis?** → **Peak efficacy at 311–313 nm.** Wavelengths below ~295 nm cleared nothing.
- **Which wavelengths burn the skin?** → Erythemogenic potency **rises steeply as wavelength falls** — 297 nm burns far more readily than 313 nm.

Put the two curves on the same axes and you get a **divergence**: at ~311 nm you retain near-maximal therapeutic effect while shedding a disproportionate amount of the burning capacity.

**Broadband UVB (300–320 nm)** delivers the whole band — including the short, highly erythemogenic 295–300 nm photons that contribute **burn and mutagenesis but not clearance**. It is therapeutic *contamination*.

**Narrowband UVB (311 ± 2 nm)** — produced by **TL-01 fluorescent lamps** (and now LEDs) — delivers essentially only the useful photons.

**Consequences, exactly as the source states ("NB-UVB has been found superior to conventional broadband UVB"):**
- **Higher clearance rates** and **faster clearance**
- **Fewer treatments** needed
- **Longer remissions**
- **Less erythema/burning per unit of benefit** → better **therapeutic index**
- Safer for **children** and in **pregnancy**

**Analogy:** Broadband UVB is a **shotgun** — some pellets hit the target, many hit bystanders. NB-UVB is a **rifle chambered for exactly the right calibre**. Same aim, far less collateral damage.

#### Mechanism of action — the full cause→effect chain

The source gives the core in one sentence: *"UVB induces apoptosis in T cells leading to a shift from a proinflammatory to an anti-inflammatory response; depletion of epidermal T cells contributes to clearing of psoriatic lesions."* Let me expand that into the full chain, because this is the part that earns marks.

**Step 1 — Photon absorption (the chromophore).**
UVB at 311 nm is absorbed directly by **nuclear DNA**, whose absorption peak is ~260–300 nm with substantial absorption into the low 310s. Other chromophores include **urocanic acid** (trans → **cis-urocanic acid**, itself immunosuppressive), **tryptophan**, and **7-dehydrocholesterol**.

**Step 2 — DNA photoproduct formation.**
Absorbed energy drives formation of **cyclobutane pyrimidine dimers (CPDs)** and **pyrimidine–pyrimidone (6-4) photoproducts** between adjacent pyrimidines. These are bulky, helix-distorting lesions that **block replication and transcription**.

**Step 3 — Damage sensing and apoptosis.**
CPD load activates the **ATR/ATM–p53** pathway. In cells with a low apoptotic threshold — and **activated T lymphocytes have a notoriously low threshold** — p53 upregulates **Bax, PUMA, Fas/FasL**, mitochondrial cytochrome-c is released, caspase-9 and -3 are activated, and the cell dies by apoptosis.
> **Why T cells and not keratinocytes?** Selectivity here is quantitative, not absolute. Activated lymphocytes are highly proliferative, have less efficient nucleotide-excision repair capacity per unit damage, and are primed for activation-induced cell death — so a dose that a keratinocyte survives (with repair) **kills the T cell**. This is the single most important idea in UVB phototherapy: ⭐ **UVB works by selectively deleting the pathogenic effector lymphocytes from the plaque.**

**Step 4 — Depletion of the epidermal/dermal T-cell infiltrate.**
Sequential biopsies during a NB-UVB course show progressive **loss of CD3⁺/CD8⁺ T cells from the epidermis** and CD4⁺ cells from the dermis, and this depletion **temporally precedes and predicts clinical clearance**. Remove the cytokine source and the feed-forward loop collapses.

**Step 5 — Antigen-presenting cell knockdown.**
UVB depletes and functionally paralyses **Langerhans cells** (they retreat from the epidermis, lose co-stimulatory molecules), while recruiting UV-damaged macrophages that present antigen tolerogenically. Result: **no new T-cell priming.**

**Step 6 — Cytokine repolarisation (the "shift" the source mentions).**
- **Down:** IL-23, IL-17A, IL-22, IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-12, IL-8, ICAM-1 — i.e., the entire **Th1/Th17** program
- **Up:** **IL-10** (from keratinocytes and macrophages), **IL-4**, prostaglandin E₂, α-MSH, **cis-urocanic acid**
- **Induction of regulatory T cells (Tregs)** — a genuinely *active* immunosuppressive state, not just deletion
> This explains the source's phrase "shift from a proinflammatory immune response to an anti-inflammatory one." It is both **subtractive** (kill Th17) and **additive** (induce Treg/IL-10).

**Step 7 — Direct keratinocyte effects.**
Antiproliferative (cell-cycle arrest) and normalising effects on differentiation; plus **cutaneous vitamin D₃ synthesis** from 7-dehydrocholesterol, which itself feeds back to suppress Th17 and promote differentiation — the endogenous version of your topical calcipotriol.

**Step 8 — Clinical resolution.**
Erythema fades (↓VEGF, ↓vascular dilatation), scale normalises (restored differentiation), induration flattens (↓proliferation, ↓infiltrate). Plaques often clear **centrally first**, leaving annular rings, then resolve to macular post-inflammatory hyper- or hypo-pigmentation.

**Analogy for the whole mechanism:** The psoriatic plaque is a **protest rally that keeps recruiting new marchers**. UVB doesn't negotiate with the crowd or block the loudspeakers — it **removes the organisers (activated T cells)** and **closes the recruiting office (Langerhans cells)**, then **hands out leaflets promoting calm (IL-10, Tregs)**. The rally disperses. But note: the *predisposition* to rally again remains — hence relapse, hence maintenance.

#### Dosing: the doctrine of suberythemogenic escalation

Here is where the source is dense; let me unpack every element.

**First, define MED.**
**Minimal Erythema Dose (MED)** = the **lowest UV dose that produces just-perceptible erythema with clearly defined borders** at a fixed time after exposure (**24 hours** for UVB), assessed on uninvolved skin. It is determined by irradiating a template of small windows (e.g., on the buttock or inner forearm) with a geometric dose series. Units: **mJ/cm²** for UVB.

*Why we bother:* MED individualises the dose. It captures **constitutive pigmentation, tanning history, photosensitising drugs, and inter-lamp variability** in a single number. Alternatively (and more commonly in busy units), the starting dose is derived from **Fitzpatrick skin phototype** (e.g., ~130 mJ/cm² for type I–II, ~250–400 mJ/cm² for type V–VI) using a standard table.

**The protocol, as the source describes it:**

| Element | Source statement | Explanation & verification |
|---|---|---|
| **Dose character** | "gradually increasing **suberythemogenic** doses" | Each dose sits **just below the erythema threshold** — enough photons to kill T cells, not enough to burn. Cumulative sub-threshold hits achieve clearance without phototoxicity. |
| **Starting dose** | "**80% of MED**" | ✅ Within accepted practice. Guidelines commonly use **50–70–80% of MED**; 80% is the **more aggressive** end. Lower starting fractions (50–70%) are safer in fair skin, drug-photosensitised patients, or when lamp output is uncertain. |
| **Increments** | "**20% increments if no erythema is seen**" | ✅ Standard increments are **10–20% per session**. 20% is again the brisker option. **The increment is *conditional* on the skin's response** — this is a **feedback-controlled**, not fixed, protocol. |
| **Course length** | "**20–30 sessions**" | ✅ Typical; sessions **3× weekly** (some units 2× or 5× weekly). |
| **The two failure modes** | "Poor results if low doses used; phototoxicity from high doses or rapid increases" | ✅ Exactly the two horns of the dilemma. |

**⭐ The central pharmacodynamic principle: the therapeutic window.**

Plot dose on the x-axis:
- **Too low** → insufficient DNA photoproduct formation → insufficient T-cell apoptosis → **no clearance**, wasted time and money, and (importantly) the patient **tans**, raising their MED further, so subsequent doses become relatively even weaker. Under-treatment is self-defeating.
- **Just right (suberythemogenic, escalating)** → maximal lymphocyte deletion with minimal keratinocyte injury → **clearance**.
- **Too high, or escalated too fast** → **phototoxic erythema, oedema, blistering, pain**. This is not merely uncomfortable: a UV burn is **trauma**, and trauma in psoriasis can trigger the **Koebner phenomenon** → a *flare of psoriasis at the burnt site*. You have made the disease worse with the treatment. Plus it forces a treatment holiday, losing momentum.

**Why escalation is necessary at all:** as treatment proceeds, the skin **adapts** — it tans (melanin absorbs and scatters UV) and the stratum corneum thickens (**UV-induced hyperkeratosis**). Both raise the MED. A fixed dose therefore delivers a *falling* effective dose to the target cells. Escalation simply keeps pace with adaptation.

**Analogy:** Suberythemogenic escalating dosing is like **building muscle by progressive overload**. Go too light and nothing adapts. Go too heavy too fast and you tear something and lose weeks. Increase steadily, just within tolerance, and you make continuous gains.

**Practical rules every unit follows:**
- **Missed sessions:** if a patient misses a week, the dose must be **held or reduced** (adaptation decays), because resuming at the escalated dose risks a burn.
- **Erythema encountered:** if asymptomatic mild erythema → hold dose. If symptomatic/painful erythema → **omit sessions until settled**, then resume at a reduced dose.
- **Eye protection:** **UV-opaque goggles inside the cabinet** — non-negotiable (UVB keratoconjunctivitis, "welder's flash"; long-term cataract risk).
- **Genital shielding:** ⭐ **male genitalia must be shielded** (jock strap/athletic support) — genital skin is exquisitely UV-sensitive and there is a documented excess of **genital squamous cell carcinoma** in men who received unshielded genital UV (best established for PUVA, but shielding is standard for all).
- **Medication review before every course:** photosensitising drugs (**thiazides, tetracyclines/doxycycline, fluoroquinolones, sulfonamides, NSAIDs (piroxicam), amiodarone, phenothiazines, voriconazole, St John's wort**) sharply lower the burn threshold.
- **Remove UV-blocking topicals:** ⭐ **salicylic acid absorbs UVB** and must be washed off; **sunscreens** obviously must be off; **calcipotriol is photodegraded** so it goes on after, not before.

#### Effectiveness and the maintenance problem

**The source's figures, carefully interpreted:**
- "**90% show clearance after 20–30 sessions**" — 🔶 *This is optimistic.* The more defensible modern figure is **~70–80% achieving PASI-75** (75% improvement) over 20–35 NB-UVB sessions, with a smaller proportion achieving complete clearance (PASI-90/100). Learn both: quote the source figure if reproducing it, but know the evidence-based number. The distinction between **"clearance"** and **"PASI-75"** is exactly the kind of nuance that separates a good answer from an excellent one.
- "**Requires more frequent treatment for maintenance… weekly/biweekly sessions**" — correct in principle, but note an important nuance: **maintenance phototherapy for psoriasis is controversial and is not universally recommended**, precisely because every additional session adds to the **lifetime cumulative UV dose** and hence to photoageing and carcinogenic risk. Many units instead: clear the patient, stop, and re-treat on relapse, while keeping a **lifetime session/dose log**. Where maintenance is used, it is tapered (e.g., weekly, then fortnightly).

**⭐ Why petrolatum before UVB?** The source says "to enhance penetration, psoriatic plaques are often treated with petrolatum before UVB exposure." The physics is lovely:
- Psoriatic scale is a **chaotic, air-filled array of loosely stacked corneocytes**. Each corneocyte–air interface has a large **refractive-index mismatch**, so UV photons are **scattered and reflected** rather than transmitted. The plaque behaves like frosted glass.
- Applying a **thin film of white petrolatum** (refractive index ~1.48, close to keratin's ~1.55) **fills the air gaps** and performs **optical index matching** — it abolishes the scattering interfaces.
- The scale becomes **optically clear**, and **UV transmission into the epidermis rises substantially**.
- ⚠️ **Thin film only.** A **thick** layer of petrolatum — and especially any **tar-, salicylate-, or sunscreen-containing** ointment — will *absorb* or *block* UV and **reduce** the delivered dose. Same substance, opposite effect, depending on thickness and composition. This is a classic exam nuance.

**Analogy:** Think of **wetting frosted glass** — or putting immersion oil on a microscope slide, or spitting in your swimming goggles. Filling the air gaps makes the medium transparent. That's all petrolatum is doing.

#### Adverse effects of UVB

**Acute:** erythema/burning, pruritus, xerosis, **reactivation of herpes simplex** (photo-induced), polymorphic light eruption, blistering, photo-onycholysis (rare with UVB), and in cabinet-treated patients, discomfort from heat/claustrophobia.

**Chronic:** **photoageing** (dermal elastosis, wrinkling, lentigines, telangiectasia), **cataract** (if eye protection is omitted), and a **theoretical increase in non-melanoma skin cancer**. Reassuringly, large cohort studies have **not** demonstrated a clear, significant increase in skin cancer attributable to NB-UVB at conventional cumulative doses — a major safety advantage over PUVA. Still, **cumulative session counts are logged** and long-term skin surveillance is advised, especially in fair-skinned patients or those with prior PUVA/arsenic/ciclosporin exposure.

**Contraindications to UVB phototherapy:**
- **Absolute/strong:** genetic photosensitivity/DNA-repair disorders (**xeroderma pigmentosum**, Cockayne, Bloom), **lupus erythematosus** and other photosensitive connective tissue disease, **porphyrias**, **melanoma** history, **unstable/erythrodermic psoriasis**, **concurrent ciclosporin**, prior arsenic or ionising radiation
- **Relative:** multiple prior NMSC, photosensitising drugs, immunosuppression/transplant, pemphigus/pemphigoid, epilepsy or claustrophobia (standing cabinet safety), inability to stand or comply, cataract without lens protection

### 2.4 Photochemotherapy — PUVA

#### The concept

**PUVA = Psoralen + UVA.** Unlike UVB, **UVA alone is a poor therapy for psoriasis** — it is weakly absorbed by DNA and weakly erythemogenic, so you would need punishing doses. The solution is to **add an exogenous chromophore that absorbs UVA avidly and sits inside the DNA of the target cells**. That chromophore is the **psoralen**.

**Analogy:** UVA is a **key that fits no lock**. The psoralen is a **lock you install inside the diseased cell's DNA**, so that suddenly the key works — and works only where you installed the lock. PUVA is therefore a **two-component ("photochemo") therapy**: harmless drug + harmless light = potent cytotoxic effect only at their intersection.

#### The psoralens

**Furocoumarins** — natural tricyclic compounds found in *Psoralea corylifolia*, *Ammi majus*, bergamot, figs, celery, limes, parsnips (the source of **phytophotodermatitis**, which is essentially accidental PUVA).

| Psoralen | Notes |
|---|---|
| **8-methoxypsoralen (8-MOP, methoxsalen)** | The standard agent — oral 0.6 mg/kg, or topical/bath |
| **5-methoxypsoralen (5-MOP, bergapten)** | **Less nausea, less phototoxic** — better tolerated; not available everywhere |
| **4,5′,8-trimethylpsoralen (TMP)** | Mainly for **bath-PUVA** |

**Light:** **UVA, 320–400 nm**, with psoralen absorption peaks around **330–380 nm**, dosed in **J/cm²** (note the unit difference from UVB's mJ/cm² — a 1000-fold difference, another exam trap).

#### Mechanism of action — a three-pronged attack

The source structures this beautifully; let me flesh out each prong.

**Prong 1 — DNA cross-linking (the dominant mechanism; Type II, oxygen-*independent*).**
1. The psoralen is a **flat, planar tricyclic molecule** that **intercalates** between DNA base pairs, preferentially at **5′-TA-3′** sequences.
2. UVA excitation triggers a **[2+2] cycloaddition** between the psoralen's **4′,5′-furan** ring and a **thymine** on one DNA strand → a **monofunctional adduct**.
3. A **second photon** excites the psoralen's **3,4-pyrone** ring, which cycloadds to a thymine on the **opposite strand**.
4. The result is a **bifunctional photoadduct = an interstrand cross-link** — the two DNA strands are **covalently stapled together**.
5. **Consequence:** the helix **cannot unwind**, so **DNA replication and transcription are blocked** ⇒ **cell-cycle arrest and apoptosis** in proliferating cells.
> The source's phrase "**bifunctional photo adducts that inhibit DNA synthesis**" is precisely this.

**Analogy:** DNA replication requires the double helix to **unzip**. A psoralen cross-link is a **rivet through the zipper**. The zip is permanently jammed; the cell cannot copy itself and dies.

**Prong 2 — Apoptosis of cutaneous lymphocytes and immunosuppression.**
The DNA damage is most lethal to **activated, proliferating T cells** in the plaque (same logic as UVB, different chemistry). PUVA also:
- Depletes **Langerhans cells** and dermal dendritic cells
- **Suppresses IL-2, IFN-γ, IL-17/IL-23** signalling
- Induces **IL-10** and regulatory T cells
- Produces a state of **localised cutaneous immunosuppression** (the source's wording) — because psoralen is systemically distributed but light is applied only to the skin, the immunosuppression is **anatomically confined to irradiated skin**. ⭐ This spatial confinement is the elegance of photochemotherapy: systemic drug, topical effect.

**Prong 3 — Free radicals, membranes, and eicosanoids (oxygen-*dependent*, "Type I/II photodynamic" component).**
Excited psoralens also transfer energy to oxygen, generating **singlet oxygen and superoxide**. These:
- **Peroxidise membrane lipids** → damage cell and organelle membranes
- **Cross-link membrane proteins** and damage mitochondria
- **Liberate arachidonic acid** → activation of **eicosanoid mediators** (prostaglandins, leukotrienes) → this contributes to the characteristic **delayed erythema** of PUVA and to modulation of inflammation
- Drive keratinocyte cytostasis directly

> Notice: the *same* radical chemistry that contributes to therapy also underlies PUVA's phototoxic erythema. Therapy and toxicity share a mechanism — which is exactly why careful dosimetry is mandatory.

#### Practical delivery — details that matter

| Route | How | When useful |
|---|---|---|
| **Oral PUVA** | 8-MOP 0.6 mg/kg (or 5-MOP 1.2 mg/kg) **with food/milk** (reduces nausea, though food slows absorption), then UVA at **peak plasma level ~1.5–2 h** later | Widespread disease |
| **Bath PUVA** | Immersion in dilute 8-MOP/TMP solution 15–20 min, then immediate UVA | ⭐ **No systemic absorption ⇒ no nausea, no need for 24 h eye protection**; excellent dose uniformity; more logistically demanding |
| **Topical/paint/cream PUVA** | Psoralen applied locally to plaques | Localised disease; **risk of irregular uptake → unpredictable blistering** at edges |
| **Hand-and-foot PUVA** | Local soak + small UVA unit | ⭐ **Palmoplantar psoriasis and palmoplantar pustulosis** |

**⭐ Dosimetry:** the starting UVA dose is set by **MPD (minimal phototoxic dose — assessed at 72–96 h, because PUVA erythema is *delayed*, unlike UVB's 24 h peak)** or by skin phototype. Increments are made every 2–3 sessions, typically 2–3 sessions per week. **Crucial:** PUVA erythema peaks **late (48–96 h)**, so treating on consecutive days risks stacking doses onto an erythema that has not yet declared itself. Hence **never treat two days in a row** with PUVA — conventionally leave ≥48 hours.

**⭐ Eye protection — the point students most often get wrong:**
Psoralens are distributed into the **lens**, where they can form photoadducts with lens proteins → **cataract**. Therefore after **oral** psoralen, the patient must wear **UVA-opaque wraparound sunglasses for the remainder of the day (≥12–24 hours)** whenever exposed to daylight, indoors near windows included — not just in the cabinet. The skin similarly remains photosensitive for the day, so sun avoidance and clothing/sunscreen are required. **Bath-PUVA avoids this** because there is no systemic drug.

**⭐ Genital shielding:** mandatory in men. The classic PUVA follow-up cohort (Stern et al.) demonstrated a dose-dependent excess of **genital squamous cell carcinoma** in men with high cumulative exposure and unshielded genitalia.

#### Indications, contraindications, and the age question

**Indications (source):** **moderate-to-severe psoriasis**; also thick plaque disease resistant to NB-UVB, **palmoplantar** psoriasis, and other UVA-responsive dermatoses (mycosis fungoides, vitiligo, lichen planus, chronic hand eczema, granuloma annulare, PLEVA, morphoea).

**🔶 "≥50 years":** The source's age qualifier is **not a standard criterion** in Rook's, Bolognia, or Andrews'. PUVA is indicated by *disease severity and phototype*, not by reaching a birthday. However, there is a **defensible clinical instinct behind it**: because PUVA's principal long-term hazard is **cumulative, dose-dependent carcinogenesis with a latency of 15–25 years**, clinicians are understandably **more reluctant to commit young patients** to a treatment whose cancer bill falls due decades later. So PUVA is *preferentially* used in **older patients**, where lifetime cumulative dose will remain modest and latency exceeds life expectancy, and NB-UVB or biologics are favoured in the young. **Learn it that way** — as a risk-latency argument, not a numerical rule. (Also note: PUVA is contraindicated in **children** for the same reason.)

**Contraindications (expanding the source's list, with reasons):**

| Contraindication | Why |
|---|---|
| **Photosensitive disorders** (LE, porphyria, XP, PLE, photoallergy) | UV will provoke or catastrophically worsen |
| **Pregnancy and lactation** | Psoralens are **teratogenic/mutagenic**; oral PUVA absolutely avoided (NB-UVB is the phototherapy of choice in pregnancy) |
| **Severe hepatic disease** | Psoralens are **hepatically metabolised** — impairment → prolonged plasma levels → unpredictable, severe phototoxicity |
| **Severe renal disease** | Altered clearance/handling; frailty |
| **Cardiovascular disease** | Practical: the patient must **stand in a hot cabinet for many minutes** — a genuine problem in unstable angina or severe heart failure |
| **History of skin cancer / melanoma / multiple NMSC** | PUVA is a **proven cutaneous carcinogen** — adding more mutagenic load is indefensible |
| **Aphakia / lens removed / significant cataract** | No lens filter → retinal damage |
| **Concurrent ciclosporin or prior arsenic/ionising radiation** | Multiplicative carcinogenic risk |
| **Unstable/erythrodermic psoriasis** | Koebner/flare risk |
| **Poor compliance with eye protection, or inability to stand** | Safety |
| **Photosensitising medications** | Unpredictable severe burn |

**Mandatory precautions:** **regular eye protection** and **shielding of the genitalia** — as the source emphasises — "to prevent long-term damage."

#### Adverse effects

**Acute (source + expansion):**
- **Nausea and vomiting** — the most common reason patients abandon oral 8-MOP; mitigate by taking with food/milk, splitting the dose, antiemetics, switching to **5-MOP**, or moving to **bath-PUVA**
- **Headache, dizziness**
- **Phototoxic erythema** — remember it is **delayed (48–96 h)**, dose-dependent and can be severe
- **Pruritus** — including the characteristic deep, prickling "**PUVA itch**," which can be intractable
- **Blistering**, oedema, pain
- **Photo-onycholysis** and **subungual haemorrhage** (relatively characteristic of PUVA)
- **Friction blisters**, herpes simplex reactivation

**Long-term / chronic (source + expansion):**
- **⭐ Squamous cell carcinoma** — the headline risk. The evidence is strong and **dose-dependent**: risk rises appreciably beyond ~**150–200 treatments** (or ~**1000–1500 J/cm² cumulative**). Hence **lifetime dose logging** and a practical ceiling (~200 treatments), plus lifelong skin surveillance. **Basal cell carcinoma** risk rises less steeply; **melanoma** risk is increased in the highest-exposure, longest-follow-up cohorts.
- **Genital SCC in men** if unshielded (hence the mandate)
- **⭐ Cataract and ocular damage** — if eye protection is neglected (the source lists this twice, under both side effects and the additional side-effect note — the repetition tells you it's examinable)
- **PUVA lentigines** — stellate, dark, persistent macules on treated skin; a marker of heavy cumulative exposure
- **Persistent hyperpigmentation**, mottled pigmentation, **poikiloderma**
- **Hypertrichosis** (as the source notes)
- **Photoageing** — elastosis, wrinkling, dryness
- Rarely: **lichenoid eruptions**, bullous pemphigoid, PUVA-induced lupus-like reactions

#### Advances in PUVA

- **Liquid/topical methoxsalen formulations** — engineered for **better, more uniform cutaneous absorption and efficacy** with less systemic exposure than tablets.
- **⭐ Psoralen baths (bath-PUVA)** — the source's key point: *localised treatment with fewer systemic side effects.* Because the psoralen never enters the circulation in meaningful quantity, you get **no nausea, no hepatic dependence, no lens exposure, no 24-hour eye-protection requirement**, and a more reproducible cutaneous drug concentration. Bath-PUVA is genuinely the refinement that kept PUVA clinically alive.
- **Hand-and-foot units** for palmoplantar disease.
- **Turbo-PUVA / low-dose combination regimens** designed explicitly to reduce cumulative UVA exposure.

#### Combination therapy — the "dose-sparing" strategy

Why combine at all? Two reasons, both about **cumulative dose**:
1. **Synergy** — two mechanisms clear faster than one.
2. **Dose sparing** — a smaller total UVA dose and a smaller drug dose achieve the same result, **reducing long-term carcinogenic and organ toxicity**.

**With topicals (source's list):**
- **Coal tar** → the **Goeckerman** principle; tar is antiproliferative *and* photosensitising
- **Urea** and **salicylic acid** → **keratolytics** that **descale**, improving UV transmission (⚠️ salicylic acid must be **removed before** irradiation because it absorbs UV)
- **Dithranol** → the **Ingram** principle; potent antiproliferative
- Also: **calcipotriol** (apply **after** UV, as it is photodegraded) and topical steroids

**With systemics (source's list):**
- **⭐ Acitretin → "Re-PUVA"** — the best-validated combination. The retinoid **thins the plaque** (improving light penetration) and normalises differentiation, so Re-PUVA achieves clearance with roughly **30–40% less cumulative UVA** and **fewer sessions** than PUVA alone. Prescribe acitretin for ~2 weeks *before* starting PUVA for maximum benefit.
- **Methotrexate + PUVA** — effective, ⚠️ **but this combination carries a documented increase in skin cancer risk** and both agents are photosensitising/hepatotoxic; use cautiously and briefly if at all.
- **🔶 Etretinate ("Re-PUVA with retinoids")** — historically the original Re-PUVA partner, **but etretinate has been withdrawn from most markets** (including the USA since 1998) because it is stored in adipose tissue with an elimination half-life of ~120 days (detectable for up to 3 years) and is severely teratogenic. **Acitretin has replaced it** — though note the sting in the tail: acitretin is re-esterified back to etretinate in the presence of alcohol, which is why acitretin's own pregnancy-avoidance period is 2–3 years. Mention etretinate for historical completeness; **prescribe acitretin**.
- **⛔ Never combine PUVA with ciclosporin** — additive immunosuppression plus mutagenesis is an unacceptable skin-cancer risk.

### 2.5 NB-UVB vs PUVA — the comparison table you should be able to draw

| Feature | **NB-UVB (311 ± 2 nm)** | **PUVA (psoralen + UVA 320–400 nm)** |
|---|---|---|
| Photosensitiser | **None** | **Required** (8-MOP/5-MOP/TMP) |
| Chromophore | Endogenous **DNA** (and urocanic acid) | **Intercalated psoralen** in DNA |
| Molecular lesion | **Cyclobutane pyrimidine dimers**, (6-4) photoproducts | **Bifunctional interstrand cross-links** + ROS |
| Depth of action | Epidermis / upper dermis | **Deeper dermis** |
| Dose units | **mJ/cm²** | **J/cm²** |
| Erythema peak | **24 h** | **48–96 h (delayed)** |
| Dose reference | **MED** | **MPD** |
| Frequency | 3× weekly | 2–3× weekly, **never consecutive days** |
| Efficacy per session | Good | **Slightly higher; better for thick plaques** |
| Remission duration | Shorter | **Longer** |
| Nausea | No | **Yes** (oral) |
| Post-treatment eye protection | Goggles in cabinet only | **24 h UVA sunglasses after oral psoralen** |
| Use in **pregnancy** | ⭐ **Safe — treatment of choice** | **Contraindicated** |
| Use in **children** | Acceptable | **Avoid** |
| Skin cancer risk | **Low / not clearly increased** | ⭐ **Clearly increased, dose-dependent (SCC)** |
| Cumulative dose ceiling | Log sessions; no hard limit | ⭐ **~200 treatments / ~1000–1500 J/cm²** |
| Convenience | High | Lower (drug, timing, eye protection) |
| Current position | ⭐ **First-line phototherapy** | **Second-line**; niche (thick plaques, palmoplantar, MF) |

> **One-line summary:** *NB-UVB is safer and simpler; PUVA is stronger and deeper but carries a genuine, dose-dependent cancer bill.* That single trade-off explains every guideline recommendation in this field.

## PART 3 — COMMON MISUNDERSTANDINGS (the traps)

1. **"Broadband UVB is a wider, therefore better, band."**
   ❌ No — the *extra* wavelengths in BB-UVB are the *shorter, more erythemogenic, less therapeutic* ones. Broader ≠ better; **more selective = better therapeutic index.**

2. **"UVB dosing is suberythemogenic, so the excimer laser must be too."**
   ❌ The excimer laser deliberately uses **supra-erythemogenic (~3 MED)** doses. The limit in cabinet therapy is the **normal skin between plaques**, not the plaque. Targeted delivery removes that limit.

3. **"UVA is just a weaker version of UVB."**
   ❌ They are **mechanistically different**. UVB is absorbed *directly by DNA*; UVA is barely absorbed by DNA and needs a *photosensitiser*, acting largely through **oxygen radicals** (and, with psoralen, through cross-links). Different depth, different units, different erythema kinetics, different risk profile.

4. **"Petrolatum protects the skin, so applying lots before UVB is good."**
   ❌ A **thin** film improves transmission by **optical index matching**. A **thick** layer, or anything containing **tar, salicylate, or sunscreen**, **blocks** UV and under-doses the patient. Thin, bland, and thin again.

5. **"Sunscreen and salicylic acid are inert — leave them on."**
   ❌ Both **absorb UV**. Salicylic acid is a genuine UV filter. They must be **washed off** before irradiation.

6. **"Apply calcipotriol just before going into the cabinet for synergy."**
   ❌ **Calcipotriol is photodegraded by UV.** Apply it **after** treatment.

7. **"PUVA eye protection means wearing goggles in the machine."**
   ❌ That covers UVB. For **oral PUVA**, the psoralen is **in the lens all day**, so the patient needs **UVA-blocking wraparound sunglasses for 12–24 hours** in any daylight. (Bath-PUVA does not require this.)

8. **"PUVA erythema, like sunburn, peaks the next day, so I can treat daily."**
   ❌ PUVA phototoxicity peaks at **48–96 hours**. Treating on consecutive days stacks doses onto undeclared erythema and causes serious burns. **Never consecutive days.**

9. **"Steroids are the best long-term treatment because they work fastest."**
   ❌ Speed ≠ suitability for maintenance. Steroids cause **atrophy, tachyphylaxis, and rebound**. Use them for **induction**, then hand over to a **vitamin D analogue** or combination for **maintenance**.

10. **"Oral prednisolone will settle a bad psoriasis flare."**
    ❌⛔ This is dangerous. Systemic steroids in psoriasis produce transient improvement followed by **severe rebound, sometimes generalised pustular psoriasis or erythroderma** on withdrawal. **Avoid systemic corticosteroids in psoriasis.**

11. **"Phototherapy is fine for any psoriasis."**
    ❌ It is for **stable** disease. In **unstable, erythrodermic, or pustular** psoriasis it can trigger a catastrophic flare. And in the **~5% who are photosensitive**, it makes things worse.

12. **"Ciclosporin plus phototherapy will clear them faster."**
    ❌⛔ **Contraindicated combination.** Chronic immunosuppression + UV mutagenesis = substantially increased skin cancer risk.

13. **"PASI-75 means the patient is clear."**
    ❌ PASI-75 = **75% improvement** from baseline, not clearance. Complete clearance is PASI-100. This is why the "90% clearance" figure in the source should be read cautiously.

14. **"Psoriasis is only a skin disease, so topicals and light are enough."**
    ❌ It is a **systemic inflammatory disease**. Neither topicals nor phototherapy touch **psoriatic arthritis** or cardiometabolic risk. If joints are involved, you must step up to **methotrexate or a biologic** regardless of how good the skin looks.

15. **"Verteporphyrin, hematoporphyrin — the names don't matter."**
    ⚠️ They do: the 1st vs 2nd generation distinction is precisely about **duration of generalised photosensitivity** (weeks vs days), which is the whole clinical difference. And the correct drug name is **verteporfin**.

16. **"PDT is a mainstream psoriasis treatment."**
    ❌ It is mechanistically elegant but **painful and inconsistent in plaque psoriasis**; it remains **investigational/niche** there. Its established indications are **actinic keratosis, Bowen's disease, superficial BCC**.

## PART 4 — THE CORE IDEA IN ONE PICTURE

Here is the single mental image to carry away.

> **Psoriasis is a self-reinforcing loop between an activated T cell and a hyper-proliferating keratinocyte, wired together by IL-23 and IL-17.** Everything we do is an attempt to cut one wire in that loop — and we choose *which* wire, and *how invasively* we reach it, according to how much disease there is and how much risk the patient can afford.
>
> - **Emollients and keratolytics** work on the **outside** of the loop — the scale and barrier.
> - **Steroids** silence the loop's **transcriptional amplifiers** — fast, but they thin the wall they are painted on.
> - **Vitamin D analogues, retinoids, tar, and dithranol** re-educate the **keratinocyte** — slow, but safe for the long haul.
> - **Phototherapy reaches into the plaque with photons and deletes the T cell itself.** UVB does it by writing pyrimidine dimers directly into lymphocyte DNA; PUVA does it by first installing a psoralen and then stapling the DNA strands shut. In both cases, the activated lymphocyte — proliferative, apoptosis-prone, poorly repaired — dies while the keratinocyte survives and repairs. The loop loses its organiser, and the plaque dissolves.
> - **Systemics and biologics** do the same job from the **inside**, via the bloodstream — necessary when the disease is too extensive, too deep (joints), or too stubborn for light.
>
> And the discipline that governs phototherapy is a **single trade-off**: *every photon that heals is also a photon that mutates.* Hence **MED-based individualised dosing, suberythemogenic escalation, feedback-controlled increments, eye and genital shielding, dose-sparing combinations, and lifetime cumulative-dose logging.* Under-dose and nothing clears; over-dose and you burn the patient, Koebnerise the plaque, and mortgage their skin's future.
>
> **NB-UVB is where that trade-off is most favourable — which is why, for stable plaque psoriasis, narrowband UVB at 311 nm is the phototherapy of first choice, PUVA is the deeper but costlier reserve, the 308 nm excimer laser is the sniper for stubborn elbows and knees, and PDT remains an elegant idea still waiting for its psoriasis indication.**

### 📌 Twelve high-yield facts for the exam

1. **"Stable"** = well-demarcated, non-spreading, non-pustular, non-erythrodermic — **and it is the prerequisite for phototherapy and dithranol.**
2. Ladder: **lifestyle → topicals → phototherapy → conventional systemics → biologics/small molecules**; combine and rotate at every rung.
3. **Rule of tens** (BSA/PASI/DLQI >10) for severe disease — plus **site-based severity** and **psoriatic arthritis** as independent escalators.
4. **NB-UVB = 311 ± 2 nm**; superior to broadband UVB (300–320 nm) because the action spectrum for clearance (311–313 nm) is separable from the erythema spectrum.
5. **UVB MOA:** DNA pyrimidine dimers → p53 → **apoptosis of activated T cells** → Th1/Th17 → Treg/IL-10 shift → plaque clears.
6. **UVB protocol:** start at **80% MED** (range 50–80%), **20% increments** (range 10–20%) if no erythema, **suberythemogenic**, **20–30 sessions**, 3× weekly; **thin petrolatum** before treatment for optical index matching.
7. **Excimer 308 nm:** monochromatic, coherent, **supra-erythemogenic ~3 MED**, spares normal skin, ideal for **stable recalcitrant elbow/knee plaques**.
8. **PUVA MOA (three prongs):** psoralen intercalates and forms **bifunctional interstrand cross-links** with thymine → ↓DNA synthesis; **lymphocyte apoptosis** → local immunosuppression; **free radicals** → membrane damage + **eicosanoid** activation.
9. **PUVA safety essentials:** delayed erythema (48–96 h, never consecutive days), **24 h UVA sunglasses after oral psoralen**, **genital shielding**, contraindicated in pregnancy/photosensitivity/hepatic disease/skin cancer history, **never with ciclosporin**, cumulative ceiling ~200 treatments; **bath-PUVA** eliminates systemic effects.
10. **Re-PUVA (acitretin + PUVA)** is the key dose-sparing combination (etretinate is obsolete).
11. **PDT:** ALA/MAL → **protoporphyrin IX** (selectivity from ↑uptake + ↓ferrochelatase) → light → **singlet oxygen** → apoptosis; 1st gen **haematoporphyrin** (prolonged photosensitivity) vs 2nd gen **verteporfin**; niche/painful in psoriasis; avoid bright sun 2–3 days.
12. **Absolute don'ts:** no systemic corticosteroids for chronic plaque psoriasis; no phototherapy in unstable/erythrodermic/photosensitive disease; no ciclosporin + UV; no methotrexate or acitretin in pregnancy; no salicylic acid or sunscreen on the skin at the time of irradiation.
