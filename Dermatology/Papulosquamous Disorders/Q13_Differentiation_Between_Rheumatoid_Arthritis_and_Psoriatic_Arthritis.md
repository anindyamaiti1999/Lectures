# Rheumatoid Arthritis vs. Psoriatic Arthritis
### A Mini-Lecture on Two Inflammatory Arthritides That Look Alike and Behave Completely Differently

---

## Part 1 — The Big Picture: Why This Question Exists at All

Let me start with the clinical scenario that makes this question worth asking.

A 45-year-old walks into your clinic with painful, swollen, stiff hands. The joints are warm. Morning stiffness lasts an hour. Inflammatory arthritis — that much is obvious. But *which* inflammatory arthritis?

Here's why it matters enormously: **the two diseases respond to almost opposite drugs.** If you give this patient rituximab and they have psoriatic arthritis, you will achieve nothing — and you may actually flare their skin. If you give them secukinumab (an IL-17A blocker) and they have rheumatoid arthritis, again, nothing. Same joint, same swelling, same pain — completely different biology underneath.

So the real intellectual content of this question is not a list of differences. It's a **single organizing idea**, which I want you to carry through everything that follows:

> **Rheumatoid arthritis is a Class II MHC / CD4⁺ T-cell / B-cell / autoantibody disease centred on the SYNOVIUM, and it DESTROYS bone.**
>
> **Psoriatic arthritis is a Class I MHC / CD8⁺ T-cell / IL-23–IL-17 axis disease centred on the ENTHESIS, and it both destroys AND builds bone.**

Every single row of the comparison table — the genetics, the joint distribution, the nails, the eyes, the X-rays, the drug responses — falls out of those two sentences. If you understand *those*, you don't need to memorise the table. You can regenerate it.

Let's build it up properly.

---

## Part 2 — Definitions, Stated Carefully

**Rheumatoid arthritis (RA)** is a chronic, systemic autoimmune inflammatory disease whose defining lesion is **synovitis** — inflammation of the synovial membrane lining the joint — which progresses to **bony erosion** and **cartilage destruction**.

Notice the emphasis: the synovium is the *primary* target. Everything else in RA is downstream of a synovium that has become an inflammatory organ.

**Psoriatic arthritis (PsA)** is a chronic, heterogeneous, systemic autoimmune (more precisely, *immune-mediated*) inflammatory disease occurring in association with psoriasis, with famously **diverse clinical and radiographic manifestations**.

That word **heterogeneous** is doing real work. RA is one disease with one look. PsA is a family. Moll and Wright classically described **five patterns**:

1. **Asymmetric oligoarthritis** (≤4 joints) — the classic pattern
2. **DIP-predominant** arthritis
3. **Symmetric polyarthritis** — the "RA-mimic" pattern (this is the one that catches you out)
4. **Axial/spondylitic** disease (spine + sacroiliac joints)
5. **Arthritis mutilans** — the severely destructive, digit-shortening form

Both diseases share the same surface presentation: **chronic inflammatory pain and swelling of joints.** That shared surface is exactly why a structured comparison is needed.

## Part 3 — Epidemiology: Who Gets Which?

| | **PsA** | **RA** |
|---|---|---|
| Overall frequency | Less common | **More common** |
| Peak age of onset | **30–50 years** (earlier) | **40–60 years** (later) |
| Sex ratio | **M > W** (roughly equal to slight male excess) | **W > M** (~3:1 female) |
| Ethnicity | Predominantly **Caucasians** | **All ethnic groups**, globally distributed |

**How to make this stick:** PsA tracks the epidemiology of *psoriasis*, and psoriasis is most prevalent in Northern European Caucasian populations with earlier onset. RA tracks the epidemiology of *autoantibody-mediated autoimmunity*, and almost every classic autoantibody disease (lupus, Sjögren's, Hashimoto's, RA) shows a female predominance and is globally distributed.

**A useful one-liner:** *"PsA follows the skin; RA follows the antibody."*

**Why the female predominance in RA?** Oestrogen modulates B-cell selection and survival, lowering the threshold for autoreactive B cells escaping tolerance. Since RA's engine is a B cell making anti-CCP antibodies, a hormonal environment favouring B-cell autoreactivity favours RA. PsA's engine is a T cell, so this sex skew largely disappears.

## Part 4 — Etiopathogenesis: Where the Two Roads Diverge

This is the conceptual heart of the answer. Take your time here.

### 4a. Genetics — Class I vs Class II MHC

**PsA:**
- **HLA-B27** → associated with **enthesitis** and **symmetric sacroiliitis** (axial disease)
- **HLA-B08 (B8)** → associated with **dactylitis** and symmetric sacroiliitis
- *(Also widely cited in standard references: **HLA-C\*06:02 / Cw6** for skin psoriasis and peripheral arthritis; **HLA-B38** and **HLA-B39** for polyarticular PsA)*

**RA:**
- **HLA-DR1** → associated with **RF and anti-CCP seropositivity**
- **HLA-DR4** → associated with **increased susceptibility and disease severity**
- *(Both DR1 and DR4 are subtypes within the **HLA-DRB1 "shared epitope"** alleles — a pocket in the peptide-binding groove that preferentially binds citrullinated peptides)*

**Now — why does this single fact predict everything else?**

HLA-B molecules are **MHC Class I**. Class I presents peptides to **CD8⁺ cytotoxic T cells**. Class I is expressed on *virtually every nucleated cell* in the body — including the fibrocartilage cells of the enthesis and the keratinocytes of the epidermis.

HLA-DR molecules are **MHC Class II**. Class II presents peptides to **CD4⁺ helper T cells**, and CD4⁺ T cells are the cells that license **B cells** to make high-affinity class-switched **autoantibodies**.

> **Analogy:** Think of Class I as a **security camera on every building in the city** — when it flags something, a CD8⁺ "SWAT team" arrives at that specific building and attacks it directly, locally. Class II is the **central intelligence office** — it briefs CD4⁺ "commanders," who then commission the B-cell "arms factory" to mass-produce antibody "munitions" that circulate everywhere and deposit wherever they find their target.

So immediately:
- **PsA** = local, tissue-targeted, CD8-driven, **seronegative**, patchy/asymmetric, hitting Class-I-rich structures like enthesis and skin.
- **RA** = systemic, antibody-driven, CD4/B-cell-dependent, **seropositive**, symmetric (because circulating antibody reaches both hands equally), hitting synovium-rich joints.

**And the RA antibody story mechanistically:** The shared-epitope HLA-DRB1 pocket binds **citrullinated** peptides (arginine converted to citrulline by peptidyl-arginine deiminase). Smoking and periodontal infection increase citrullination in mucosal tissues. Citrullinated peptides get presented to CD4⁺ cells → B cells make **anti-CCP (ACPA)** → immune complexes form in the synovium → complement activation and Fc-receptor engagement on macrophages → cytokine storm → synovitis. That is RA in one sentence, and it explains why HLA-DR1 is the allele "associated with positive RF and CCP antibodies."

### 4b. Triggers — Different Doors Into the Same Corridor

| **PsA triggers** | **RA triggers** |
|---|---|
| Smoking | Smoking |
| Air pollution | Alcohol |
| Infections (e.g., streptococcal, HIV) | Gut dysbiosis |

Mechanistic reading:
- **Infection and physical/biomechanical stress** trigger PsA because the enthesis is a site of repetitive **mechanical microdamage**, and damaged tissue releases innate alarmins that engage the IL-23/IL-17 pathway. This is the **"deep Koebner phenomenon"** — just as skin trauma triggers a psoriatic plaque at that site, mechanical stress at an enthesis triggers enthesitis there. *This is why PsA is asymmetric: it goes where the mechanical stress went.*
- **Gut dysbiosis and smoking** trigger RA because mucosal surfaces (gut, lung, gum) are where citrullination and initial loss of tolerance occur. The **"mucosal origins hypothesis"** of RA holds that anti-CCP antibodies appear in blood *years before* any joint symptoms — the disease begins outside the joint and later lands in it.

> **Note on alcohol:** it appears here as a listed trigger/modifier. In the broader epidemiological literature moderate alcohol is often reported as mildly *protective* for RA risk, but relevant clinically as a hepatotoxicity risk with methotrexate. Know it as listed; understand the nuance.

### 4c. Immunopathology — The Cytokine Signatures

This is where the therapeutic differences are born.

**PsA:**
- **Expansion of T cells, especially CD8⁺ T cells → IL-17 production** (consistent with the Class I/HLA-B27 genetics)
- Proinflammatory cytokine profile: **IL-17, IL-23, IL-22, IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, and IFN-γ** — i.e., a combined **Th17/Th1 axis**, with **IL-17 and IL-23 dominant**
- **Elevated IL-9**, driving pathological T-cell proliferation (and mast-cell activation in synovium) — a less commonly quoted but genuinely described feature

**RA:**
- **Autoantibodies — rheumatoid factor (RF) and anti-CCP** — forming **immune complexes**
- Proinflammatory cytokine profile: **TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1, IL-18, IL-23, CXCL-8** driving joint damage
- **Chronic synovitis → pannus formation → joint destruction and cartilage erosion**

⚠️ **A scan-artefact warning for this table.** Printed versions of this comparison carry OCR garbling in the cytokine rows. The correct readings are:
- "TNF-2" → **TNF-α**; "PL-6" → **IL-6**; "UL-28" → **IL-18**; "12-3" → **IL-23**; "CXCL-B" → **CXCL-8** (also called IL-8, a neutrophil-recruiting CXC chemokine)
- The line ends "...drive joint damage in **RA**", not "PSA" — it is the RA column.
- Similarly, PsA's list should be read with full prefixes: **IL-17, IL-23, IL-22, IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, IFN-γ**.
- "11-12/23 inhibitor" → **IL-12/23 inhibitor** (ustekinumab).

**Now the crucial mechanistic payoff — why RA only erodes bone while PsA erodes AND builds it.**

*In RA:* TNF-α and IL-6 upregulate **RANKL** on synovial fibroblasts and T cells → massive **osteoclast** activation → erosion. Simultaneously, TNF-α upregulates **Dickkopf-1 (Dkk-1)**, an inhibitor of Wnt signalling. Wnt is the master pathway for **osteoblast** differentiation. So RA switches bone *resorption* ON and bone *formation* OFF. Result: **pure destruction** — marginal erosions, juxta-articular osteopenia, no new bone anywhere.

*In PsA:* osteoclasts are also activated (hence erosions, and in the extreme, arthritis mutilans), **but Dkk-1 is functionally inhibited**, leaving Wnt signalling free to drive osteoblasts. Result: **destruction happening side-by-side with proliferation** — periostitis, juxta-articular new bone, syndesmophytes, enthesophytes, and ultimately **ankylosis**.

> **Analogy:** RA is a **demolition crew with no builders** — the building comes down and stays down. PsA is a **demolition crew and a rogue construction crew working the same site at the same time** — walls come down in one place while ugly unplanned concrete gets poured in another. That is precisely what a PsA radiograph looks like: erosion *and* new bone in the same hand, sometimes the same joint ("pencil-in-cup").

And the **anatomical starting point** differs:
- **RA begins in the synovium.** Inflamed synovium proliferates into a thick, invasive, tumour-like tissue called **pannus**, which creeps over the cartilage and erodes bone at the "bare area" (the marginal zone where bone is intrasynovial but uncovered by cartilage). Hence **marginal erosions**.
- **PsA begins at the enthesis** — the insertion of tendon/ligament/capsule into bone. Inflammation then spreads *from* the enthesis into adjacent synovium and bone. This is why the enthesis, not the synovium, is PsA's hallmark, and why **synovitis in PsA is a secondary phenomenon** (a fact you'll see reflected in the imaging numbers later).

## Part 5 — Clinical Features: Reading the Body

### 5a. Which Criteria Set Do You Use?

- **PsA → CASPAR criteria** (ClASsification criteria for Psoriatic ARthritis)
- **RA → EULAR/ACR (2010) criteria**

**CASPAR, worth knowing in detail** because it *is* the differentiation logic in scoring form. Entry requirement: established **inflammatory articular disease** (joint, spine, or entheseal). Then score **≥3 points** from:

| Feature | Points |
|---|---|
| Current psoriasis | **2** |
| Personal history of psoriasis (if no current) | 1 |
| Family history of psoriasis (if neither above) | 1 |
| Psoriatic **nail dystrophy** (pitting, onycholysis, hyperkeratosis) | 1 |
| **Negative rheumatoid factor** | 1 |
| Dactylitis — current or documented history | 1 |
| Radiographic **juxta-articular new bone formation** | 1 |

Notice what's embedded in there: *RF negativity is itself a scoring criterion*, and *new bone formation* is a criterion. CASPAR is essentially "RA-not-ness" plus psoriasis features. Sensitivity ~91%, specificity ~98%.

**ACR/EULAR 2010 for RA:** score **≥6 of 10** across four domains — joint involvement (0–5, weighted toward *many small* joints), serology (0–3, weighted toward *high-titre* RF/ACPA), acute-phase reactants (0–1), and symptom duration ≥6 weeks (0–1). Notice this one is *built around serology and small-joint symmetry* — the mirror image of CASPAR.

### 5b. Joint Distribution — The Single Most Useful Bedside Clue

| | **PsA** | **RA** |
|---|---|---|
| Pattern | **Asymmetric** | **Symmetrical** |
| Hand joints | **DIP** joints involved | **MCP and PIP**; **DIP characteristically spared** |
| Other joints | Large joints of **lower extremities**, axial spine, **SI joints** | Shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles, **MTP** joints |

**Why is PsA asymmetric and RA symmetric?** Because RA's effector is a **circulating antibody**, which reaches the right and left second MCP in identical concentration. PsA's effector is a **locally recruited CD8⁺/IL-17 response at mechanically stressed entheses**, and mechanical stress is never perfectly symmetric.

**Why does PsA hit the DIP while RA spares it?** This is beautiful, and it's my favourite fact in this whole topic. The **nail apparatus is anatomically continuous with the DIP joint** — the nail matrix and bed are tethered by collagen fibres to the extensor tendon enthesis and periosteum of the distal phalanx. The nail is effectively *part of the DIP entheseal organ*. So in an entheseal disease, nail disease and DIP arthritis are **two views of the same lesion**. That is why PsA patients with nail involvement have far more DIP arthritis. RA, being synovium-driven, goes where the synovium is richest — MCPs, PIPs, wrists, MTPs — and the DIP simply isn't a major synovial target.

**The examiner's trap:** first MTP and forefoot involvement occurs in both. And remember pattern 3 of PsA is a *symmetric polyarthritis that looks exactly like RA*. Distribution is powerful but not absolute — which is why you also check nails, serology, and films.

### 5c. Axial Involvement — Both Diseases Touch the Spine, Differently

| | **PsA** | **RA** |
|---|---|---|
| Axial disease | **~50%** of cases — spine **and SI joints**, any segment | **Cervical spine in ~80%** of longstanding disease |

**This difference is mechanistically clean.** PsA is a **spondyloarthropathy**: it inflames the SI joints and the entheseal attachments of spinal ligaments, producing **sacroiliitis and syndesmophytes** anywhere along the spine — cervical, thoracic, or lumbar.

RA, in contrast, **does not cause sacroiliitis and does not affect the thoracolumbar spine**, because those are largely non-synovial, fibrocartilaginous, entheseal structures. RA attacks the *one* part of the spine that is richly synovial: the **cervical spine**, specifically the **atlanto-axial joint** and facet joints.

🚨 **Clinical significance you must not skip.** RA synovitis of the atlanto-axial joint erodes the **transverse ligament** and the odontoid peg, producing **atlantoaxial subluxation** — radiographically present in roughly a quarter of RA patients. The C1 ring slides forward on C2, narrowing the canal around the cervical cord. Consequences:
- Occipital headache, neck pain, myelopathy, painless loss of hand dexterity, bladder dysfunction
- **Catastrophic risk during intubation** — neck extension for laryngoscopy can compress the cord. This is why **every long-standing RA patient going for general anaesthesia needs cervical spine assessment** (flexion–extension lateral films or MRI). This single fact is worth more marks than half the table.

PsA's axial equivalent of catastrophe is different: bony **ankylosis** producing a rigid, fused, fracture-prone spine.

### 5d. Dactylitis — The "Sausage Digit"

| | **PsA** | **RA** |
|---|---|---|
| Dactylitis | Up to **50%** | **~5%** |

**What it is:** uniform, diffuse swelling of an *entire* digit — not a single joint — so the finger or toe loses its normal contours and looks like a sausage.

**Why it happens in PsA and not RA:** dactylitis is the sum of **flexor tenosynovitis + entheseal inflammation at multiple tendon/ligament insertions along the digit + soft-tissue oedema + synovitis of all three joints simultaneously.** It is a *whole-digit entheseal event*. RA, which inflames discrete synovial cavities, produces **fusiform swelling of an individual PIP joint**, not a uniformly swollen digit.

> **Analogy:** RA swelling is like **beads on a string** — discrete lumps at each involved joint. PsA dactylitis is like the **whole string having been dipped in wax** — everything from base to tip is thickened.

Dactylitis is a **CASPAR criterion** and a marker of more erosive PsA.

### 5e. Enthesitis — PsA's Signature Lesion

| | **PsA** | **RA** |
|---|---|---|
| Enthesitis | **~35%** | **Rare** |

The enthesis is where tendon, ligament, or capsule inserts into bone. Classic sites: **Achilles insertion, plantar fascia at the calcaneus, patellar tendon, lateral epicondyle, greater trochanter, iliac crest, costochondral junctions.**

**Why PsA?** Entheseal fibrocartilage is packed with resident innate and innate-like immune cells (γδ T cells, mucosal-associated invariant T cells, mast cells) that constitutively express the **IL-23 receptor**. Give them IL-23 and they pour out **IL-17**. Add mechanical microtrauma and you have a self-perpetuating local inflammatory loop. The enthesis is, in effect, **a tissue pre-wired for the exact pathway PsA uses.**

**Why not RA?** No synovium at the enthesis, no immune complex deposition, no pannus. RA has nothing to do there.

**Practical tip:** heel pain in a patient with inflammatory arthritis is a *very* strong nudge toward PsA/spondyloarthritis. RA patients get heel pain from retrocalcaneal *bursitis*, not true enthesitis.

### 5f. Nail Changes — Free Diagnostic Information

| | **PsA** | **RA** |
|---|---|---|
| Nails | **SUHK** — **S**ub**U**ngual **H**yper**K**eratosis, **pitting, dystrophy, onycholysis** | **No changes** |

The full PsA nail spectrum: **pitting** (matrix disease), **onycholysis** and **subungual hyperkeratosis** and the **oil-drop / salmon patch sign** (nail bed disease), **splinter haemorrhages**, and **crumbling/dystrophy** (severe matrix disease).

**Frequency matters:** nail disease is present in **80–90% of PsA patients**, versus roughly 50% of psoriasis patients *without* arthritis. That's not a coincidence — remember Part 5b: **the nail is an enthesis.** Nail disease is a visible marker of subclinical distal entheseal inflammation, which is why it's both a **CASPAR criterion** and a **predictor of developing PsA** in someone with psoriasis.

**RA nails:** unaffected by the disease itself. (You may see nailfold vasculitic infarcts in severe seropositive vasculitic RA, and clubbing from associated lung disease — but no psoriasiform nail dystrophy.)

> **Teaching point:** In a seronegative polyarthritis, **examine all twenty nails and the scalp, natal cleft, umbilicus and retroauricular skin.** Hidden psoriasis converts an undifferentiated arthritis into a confident PsA diagnosis in thirty seconds and changes your entire drug plan.

### 5g. Ocular Manifestations

| | **PsA** | **RA** |
|---|---|---|
| Eye | **Uveitis** (especially if **HLA-B27 positive**) | **Keratoconjunctivitis sicca**, **episcleritis** (and scleritis) |

**PsA:** HLA-B27-associated **anterior uveitis** — acute, painful, red, photophobic eye with blurred vision. The uvea is a Class-I-rich, HLA-B27-linked immune-privileged tissue; this is the same uveitis seen across the spondyloarthritis family. *An emergency: same-day ophthalmology referral for topical steroid and cycloplegic, or you risk synechiae and glaucoma.* (Note: PsA uveitis tends to be more insidious, bilateral and chronic than the classic AS pattern.)

**RA:** two distinct mechanisms.
1. **Keratoconjunctivitis sicca / Sicca syndrome** — **secondary Sjögren's syndrome** from lymphocytic infiltration of lacrimal and salivary glands. Dry, gritty eyes and dry mouth. Common and under-diagnosed.
2. **Episcleritis and scleritis** — from RA's **vasculitic** tendency. Episcleritis is uncomfortable and benign; **scleritis is severe, boring pain and sight-threatening**, and in its worst form **scleromalacia perforans** thins the sclera until the uvea shows through. Scleritis signals aggressive systemic seropositive RA.

> **Mnemonic:** **P**sA → **P**ainful red **U**veitis. **R**A → **R**ubbing dry eyes (sicca) and **red sclera** (scleritis/episcleritis).

### 5h. Skin and Extra-Articular Findings

| | **PsA** | **RA** |
|---|---|---|
| Skin/systemic | **Psoriatic plaques** | **Rheumatoid nodules**, **vasculitis**, **granulomatous dermatoses** |

**PsA:** well-demarcated erythematous plaques with silvery scale; check scalp, extensors, natal cleft, umbilicus, retroauricular folds, palms/soles, genitalia. Skin usually **precedes** arthritis by ~10 years (~70% of cases), but in ~15% arthritis comes first (*psoriatic arthritis sine psoriasis*) — hence the CASPAR credit for family history.

**RA:** **rheumatoid nodules** are the cardinal cutaneous sign — firm, subcutaneous, non-tender, over extensor surfaces (olecranon, finger extensors, Achilles, occiput, and in the lungs). Histologically a **palisading granuloma**: central fibrinoid necrosis, palisaded histiocytes, surrounding lymphocytes. Present in ~25% of RA, overwhelmingly in **seropositive** disease, and a marker of severity.

Also: **rheumatoid vasculitis** (nailfold infarcts, digital ulcers, purpura, mononeuritis multiplex), **granulomatous dermatoses** including **palisaded neutrophilic and granulomatous dermatitis**, **interstitial granulomatous dermatitis**, **rheumatoid neutrophilic dermatitis**, and **pyoderma gangrenosum**. Plus the dermatology-relevant systemic set: interstitial lung disease, Felty's syndrome, secondary amyloidosis.

**Nodules do not occur in PsA. Psoriasis does not occur because of RA.** These are clean, reliable discriminators.

### 5i. Synovial Vascularity — The Arthroscopic/Histological Pearl

| | **PsA** | **RA** |
|---|---|---|
| Synovial vessels | **Tortuous, bushy** vessels | **Straight, branching** vessels |

This is a genuine high-yield examination detail. On arthroscopy or synovial biopsy microvascular imaging:
- **PsA** — **tortuous, elongated, bushy** vessels, reflecting the **IL-17/TNF-driven, VEGF/angiopoietin-2-rich** angiogenic environment that PsA shares with psoriatic skin. In fact **this is the same vascular morphology as the dilated tortuous capillary loops in the dermal papillae of a psoriatic plaque** — the skin and synovium are using the identical angiogenic programme.
- **RA** — **straight, regularly branching** vessels.

> **Take-home:** *PsA's synovium looks like psoriatic skin from the inside.* That single sentence tells you they are one disease in two organs, and is the most elegant way to remember this row.

## Part 6 — Serology: The Cleanest Discriminator You Have

| | **PsA** | **RA** |
|---|---|---|
| **Rheumatoid factor (RF)** | **Negative** | **Positive in ~80%** |
| **Anti-CCP (ACPA)** | **Negative** | **Positive in ~80%** |
| **ESR / CRP** | Raised, but **lower** than RA | **Much higher** |

**What these antibodies actually are:**
- **RF** = an antibody (usually IgM) directed against the **Fc portion of IgG**. An antibody against antibodies. RF–IgG complexes are large, fix complement efficiently, and are excellent at driving synovitis and vasculitis. RF is *sensitive but not specific* — it appears in Sjögren's, hepatitis C, endocarditis, sarcoidosis, and in healthy elderly people.
- **Anti-CCP / ACPA** = antibodies against **citrullinated peptides**. **Highly specific (~95%+)** for RA, present **years before symptoms**, and a strong predictor of erosive disease. This is *the* RA antibody.

**Why is PsA seronegative?** Because PsA has no B-cell/autoantibody arm. It is CD8⁺ T-cell and IL-17 driven from start to finish. Seronegativity in PsA isn't an absence of information — it is a **positive finding** that confirms the underlying immunology, which is precisely why CASPAR **awards a point for a negative RF**.

**Why are ESR and CRP higher in RA?** **IL-6.** IL-6 is *the* hepatic inducer of CRP and fibrinogen. RA is an IL-6-dominant disease, so it generates a fierce systemic acute-phase response. PsA is an IL-17/IL-23-dominant disease in which IL-6 is present but not the conductor — so inflammatory markers are elevated but more modestly. Keep this in mind: **a PsA patient can have very active joint disease with a nearly normal CRP**, so never use CRP alone to judge PsA activity.

⚠️ **Exam-versus-reality caveat.** For exam purposes, treat this as absolute: PsA = seronegative, RA = seropositive. In real life, ~5–10% of PsA patients carry a **low-titre** RF, and ~20% of RA patients are **seronegative**. So a low-titre RF in a patient with plaque psoriasis, dactylitis, nail pitting and asymmetric DIP disease does **not** make it RA. The ACR/EULAR criteria wisely weight **high-titre** serology more heavily than low.

## Part 7 — Radiology: The Bone Tells the Truth

| | **PsA** | **RA** |
|---|---|---|
| Overall signature | **Juxta-articular bony proliferation**, bone resorption, **ankylosis** | **Osteodestructive lesions** and **joint erosions** |
| Synovitis on imaging | **60%** | **90%** |
| Dactylitis on imaging | **++** | Rare |
| DIP changes | **+** | Rare |
| Periosteal new bone | **++** | **Absent** |
| MRI bone marrow oedema | Near **entheses** | Near **capsular attachments** |

### The master contrast

> **PsA = PROLIFERATIVE (with destruction). RA = PURELY DESTRUCTIVE.**

This is the Dkk-1/Wnt story from Part 4c made visible. Look for:

**In PsA:**
- **Periostitis / periosteal new bone** — fluffy, ill-defined new bone along shafts. **Never seen in RA.** This is the single most specific radiographic discriminator, and it's a CASPAR criterion.
- **Enthesophytes** — bony spurs at Achilles/plantar insertions
- **"Pencil-in-cup" deformity** — a whittled proximal phalanx telescoping into an expanded, cupped base of the distal phalanx: erosion and proliferation in the same joint
- **Ankylosis** — bony fusion; RA fuses only the wrist/carpus late, PsA fuses digits and spine
- **Sacroiliitis** (often asymmetric) and **chunky non-marginal syndesmophytes**
- **Acro-osteolysis** and, at the extreme, **arthritis mutilans**
- **Bone density preserved** — no periarticular osteopenia

**In RA:**
- **Marginal ("bare area") erosions** at MCPs, PIPs, wrists, MTPs — especially the **5th MTP**, often the earliest site
- **Uniform joint space narrowing** (diffuse cartilage loss, not focal)
- **Periarticular osteopenia** — early, from local RANKL-driven bone loss plus disuse
- **Soft-tissue swelling** and **no new bone anywhere**
- **Deformities** from ligament/tendon failure: ulnar deviation, swan-neck, boutonnière, Z-thumb, hitchhiker's thumb
- **Atlantoaxial subluxation** on flexion lateral cervical views

> **Analogy for the films:** RA is **rust** — it eats metal away smoothly and evenly, and nothing grows back. PsA is **coral growing on a shipwreck** — the hull is being eaten away in places while irregular new material accretes in others.

### Why is imaging synovitis 60% in PsA but 90% in RA?

Because in RA **synovitis IS the disease** — pannus is the primary lesion, present in essentially every affected joint. In PsA, **enthesitis is the primary lesion**, and synovitis is a **secondary spill-over** from the inflamed enthesis into the adjacent joint. So it's present often, but not obligatorily. The numbers aren't arbitrary trivia — they are a *quantitative readout of which tissue is the true target*.

### Why the MRI bone marrow oedema pattern differs

Bone marrow oedema on MRI marks where inflammation is invading bone. So it maps directly onto each disease's port of entry:
- **PsA → oedema near entheses**, because the enthesis is the gateway (this is "osteitis" and can be seen in the spine as **Romanus lesions/shiny corners**).
- **RA → oedema near capsular attachments**, because inflamed synovium and its capsular reflection are the gateway.

**Practical value:** in the first months of disease, plain films are often normal in *both* diseases. MRI oedema pattern can distinguish early PsA from early RA **before any radiographic change appears** — enabling correct drug selection at the point where it matters most for preventing damage.

## Part 8 — Comorbidities: The Systemic Company Each Disease Keeps

| | **PsA** | **RA** |
|---|---|---|
| Comorbidities | **Obesity, diabetes, metabolic syndrome, NASH**, systemic inflammation, **CVD** | **CVD** |

**PsA — the metabolic disease.** PsA carries a heavy **cardiometabolic** burden: central obesity, insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes, dyslipidaemia, full metabolic syndrome, and **NASH** (non-alcoholic steatohepatitis). Two reinforcing mechanisms:
1. **Adipose tissue is an immune organ.** Visceral fat secretes TNF-α, IL-6, leptin and resistin, priming the same Th17 pathway that drives PsA. Obesity is an independent **risk factor for developing** PsA, and weight loss measurably improves treatment response.
2. **The inflammation feeds back.** TNF-α and IL-6 induce hepatic and peripheral insulin resistance. Skin/joint inflammation → metabolic dysfunction → more inflammation. A closed loop.

📝 **Nomenclature update:** by the 2023 Delphi consensus, **NASH is now MASH** (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis) and **NAFLD is now MASLD** (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease). Both terms are currently accepted in examinations; use the new one and acknowledge the old.

⚠️ **Why MASLD/MASH matters practically in PsA:** these patients frequently need **methotrexate** — a hepatotoxic drug — in a liver already fatty and inflamed. Hence the emphasis on baseline liver assessment, non-invasive fibrosis scoring (FIB-4, transient elastography), alcohol counselling, and weight management before and during MTX.

**RA — the cardiovascular disease.** RA's dominant comorbidity is **accelerated atherosclerosis**. Cardiovascular risk approaches that of diabetes, and CVD is the leading cause of death in RA. Mechanism: sustained systemic IL-6/TNF-α exposure drives endothelial dysfunction, unstable plaque, and a paradoxical lipid picture (inflammation *lowers* total cholesterol while *raising* risk — so a "reassuring" lipid panel misleads you). RA also brings **osteoporosis** (inflammation + glucocorticoids), **ILD**, **anaemia of chronic disease**, **secondary Sjögren's**, **amyloidosis** and increased **lymphoma** risk.

**Shared imperative:** **both diseases require active cardiovascular risk management** — EULAR recommends multiplying calculated CVD risk by **1.5** in both. Controlling the inflammation is itself cardioprotective.

## Part 9 — Treatment Response: The Ultimate Proof of Different Biology

This is the most examination-relevant section, and also the most intellectually satisfying, because **the drug responses are a live experiment confirming the pathogenesis.**

| Agent | **PsA** | **RA** | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Methotrexate** | **Mixed results** | **Efficacy established** | Anchor drug in RA; in PsA helps skin and peripheral joints but weak evidence for axial disease, enthesitis, dactylitis |
| **IL-6 inhibitors** (tocilizumab, sarilumab) | **Limited efficacy** | **Effective** | IL-6 is a dominant RA cytokine; peripheral in PsA |
| **IL-17A inhibitors** (secukinumab, ixekizumab) | **Effective** | **NOT effective** | IL-17 is the PsA effector; not a driver in RA |
| **Ustekinumab (IL-12/23, anti-p40)** | **Effective** | **NOT effective** | IL-23/Th17 axis is specific to psoriatic disease |
| **Anakinra (IL-1 receptor antagonist)** | **Limited efficacy** in spondyloarthropathy | **Effective** | IL-1's role is modest in PsA |
| **Rituximab (anti-CD20)** | **Ineffective** | **Effective** | RA is B-cell/autoantibody-driven; PsA is T-cell-driven |
| *(TNF-α inhibitors)* | *Effective* | *Effective* | *The one shared pathway — TNF is upstream in both* |

### Read the table as an argument, not a list

**IL-17A blockade works in PsA and fails in RA.** Not a pharmacological quirk — **proof** that IL-17 sits on the critical path in PsA and not in RA. Large RA trials of secukinumab were disappointing despite excellent psoriasis and PsA results.

**IL-6 blockade works in RA and fails in PsA.** The mirror image, proving IL-6 is on RA's critical path. And it neatly explains the serology row: IL-6 dominance → high CRP/ESR in RA; IL-6 non-dominance → modest CRP in PsA. *One cytokine explains both a lab finding and a drug response.*

**Rituximab works in RA (especially RF⁺/anti-CCP⁺) and fails in PsA.** Direct confirmation that B cells and autoantibodies are mechanistically necessary in RA and irrelevant in PsA. And there's a sting: rituximab has occasionally caused **paradoxical worsening of skin psoriasis**. So it's not merely useless in PsA — it can be counterproductive.

**Ustekinumab works in PsA and fails in RA.** Blocking the shared **p40** subunit of IL-12 and IL-23 shuts down the Th17-generating axis. Effective for PsA skin and peripheral joints; data in axial disease are limited. Failure in RA again isolates the IL-23/Th17 axis as psoriatic-disease-specific.

**Anakinra works in RA and disappoints in spondyloarthropathies including PsA.** IL-1 is a genuine but secondary player in PsA. (Anakinra's real home today is autoinflammatory disease — CAPS, adult-onset Still's, gout flares.)

**Methotrexate — the asymmetry is instructive.** In RA it is the **undisputed first-line anchor DMARD** with hard evidence for reducing radiographic progression. In PsA it is widely used and clearly helps skin and peripheral arthritis, but randomised evidence is underwhelming and it does **not** convincingly treat **axial disease, enthesitis, or dactylitis** — exactly the entheseal domains. That's not a failure of the trials; it's a clue that **MTX doesn't reach PsA's core lesion.**

**And TNF-α inhibitors work in both** — the exception that clarifies the rule. TNF-α sits *upstream*, common to both cytokine cascades. Blocking a shared trunk helps both diseases; blocking a branch (IL-17, IL-6, CD20) only helps the disease that uses that branch.

> **Analogy:** Both diseases are fires, but with different fuel. RA burns on **IL-6/TNF and antibody**; PsA burns on **IL-23/IL-17 at the enthesis**. TNF is the **oxygen** both need — remove it and both fires dim. But dousing IL-17 only extinguishes the PsA fire, and dousing IL-6 only the RA fire. **The drug that works tells you what the disease was made of.**

### A practical corollary for the ward

The **treat-to-target** logic differs. In RA you target remission using **DAS28/CDAI**, which are joint-count-and-CRP-based. In PsA, joints are only one of **six domains** — peripheral arthritis, axial disease, enthesitis, dactylitis, skin, nails — and you must select a drug that covers your patient's active domains (hence **MDA/VLDA** and GRAPPA domain-based recommendations). A PsA patient in "joint remission" with severe nail disease and plantar enthesitis is **not** in remission.

## Part 10 — Common Misunderstandings, Corrected

**1. "Seronegative means it can't be RA."**
False. **~20% of RA is seronegative.** Seronegative RA is diagnosed on symmetric small-joint synovitis, raised acute-phase reactants, duration, and marginal erosions on imaging. RF negativity *supports* PsA (and scores a CASPAR point) but doesn't clinch it — look for **positive** PsA features: psoriasis, nail disease, dactylitis, enthesitis, DIP disease, new bone.

**2. "PsA is always asymmetric."**
False. **Symmetric polyarthritis is one of the five Moll & Wright patterns** and is a genuine RA-mimic. Distribution is a probability, not a rule.

**3. "No psoriasis, so it can't be PsA."**
False. In ~15%, arthritis **precedes** skin disease (*psoriatic arthritis sine psoriasis*). Hunt hidden sites — scalp, natal cleft, umbilicus, retroauricular, genital, nails — and take a **family history** (both are CASPAR-credited).

**4. "Dactylitis is just several swollen joints in one finger."**
Conceptually wrong. Dactylitis is a **whole-digit entheseal and tenosynovial event**, which is why the swelling is diffuse and uniform rather than nodular-at-each-joint.

**5. "Both diseases erode bone, so radiology can't help."**
This misses the whole point. **Both erode; only PsA builds.** Find **periosteal new bone, enthesophytes, ankylosis, or pencil-in-cup** and you have PsA. Find **marginal erosions with periarticular osteopenia and no new bone** and you have RA.

**6. "RA affects the spine, so spinal pain doesn't help differentiate."**
Wrong, and clinically dangerous. RA affects the **cervical** spine only (it is the only richly synovial spinal region) and **never** causes sacroiliitis. **Inflammatory low back pain + sacroiliitis = spondyloarthritis, not RA.**

**7. "IL-17 inhibitors are good arthritis drugs, so they'll help RA."**
No. The RA trials failed. Cytokine blockade is **pathway-specific**, and this failure is the strongest available evidence that RA and PsA are different diseases.

**8. "A normal CRP means the PsA is controlled."**
No. PsA is not IL-6-dominant, so CRP frequently stays near-normal despite active enthesitis, dactylitis and skin disease. Assess **all six domains** clinically.

**9. "Rheumatoid nodules and psoriatic plaques could be confused."**
They shouldn't be. Nodules are **firm subcutaneous masses** over extensor points with palisading granuloma histology. Plaques are **epidermal, scaly, well-demarcated** with parakeratosis, Munro microabscesses and suprapapillary thinning. Different compartment, different histology.

**10. Don't be fooled by garbled printed tables.** Read "TNF-2" as TNF-α, "UL-28" as IL-18, "CXCL-B" as CXCL-8, "11-12/23" as IL-12/23. Understanding the biology protects you from typographical traps.

## Part 11 — The Core Idea in One Picture

Picture **two hands**.

**The RA hand.** Both hands are affected, **mirror-image**. The swelling sits at the **MCPs and PIPs**; the **DIPs and the nails are pristine**. There are ulnar deviation and swan-neck deformities. A firm **nodule** sits over the olecranon. The eyes are **dry**. The film shows **marginal erosions and thin, osteopenic bone — and not one speck of new bone.** The blood is **RF⁺, anti-CCP⁺, CRP high**. Inside the joint: a thick invasive **pannus** fed by **straight branching vessels**. The cervical spine hides an **atlantoaxial subluxation** the anaesthetist must know about. It responds to **methotrexate, IL-6 blockade, and rituximab** — because this is a **Class II MHC → CD4⁺ T cell → B cell → autoantibody → IL-6/TNF → synovitis → pure bone destruction** disease.

**The PsA hand.** Only **some** digits, **asymmetrically**. One whole finger is a **sausage**. A **DIP** is swollen — and the **nail above it is pitted, onycholytic and hyperkeratotic**, because *the nail is that joint's enthesis*. There's a **psoriatic plaque** on the elbow and in the natal cleft. The heel hurts where the **Achilles inserts**. The **sacroiliac joints** are inflamed. The film shows **erosion sitting right next to fluffy new bone**, a **pencil-in-cup** deformity, and **ankylosis**. The blood is **RF⁻, anti-CCP⁻, CRP only modestly raised**. Inside the joint: **tortuous, bushy vessels — the synovium looks like psoriatic skin turned inside out.** The waistline, the glucose and the liver all tell a **metabolic** story. It responds to **IL-17A inhibitors and ustekinumab, not** to rituximab or IL-6 blockade — because this is a **Class I MHC → CD8⁺ T cell → IL-23/IL-17 → enthesitis → simultaneous destruction and proliferation** disease.

**And the two sentences that generate both pictures:**

> **RA = antibody in the SYNOVIUM → symmetric, seropositive, high-CRP, purely EROSIVE disease. Treat the antibody and IL-6 (rituximab, tocilizumab, MTX).**
>
> **PsA = T cells at the ENTHESIS → asymmetric, seronegative, nail-and-skin-linked, EROSIVE *plus* PROLIFERATIVE disease. Treat IL-17/IL-23 (secukinumab, ustekinumab).**

Everything else is detail hanging off those hooks. **TNF-α is the one branch they share — which is why anti-TNF agents are the only class that works in both.**
